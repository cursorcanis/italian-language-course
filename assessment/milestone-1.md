## What this instrument is for, and what it decides

Milestone 1 sits after Unit 6, at the end of the course's front half, and
answers one question: **has the learner arrived at B1 lower on the ground Units
1–6 cover?** The map fixes that ground
([§Assessment points](../curriculum/syllabus/a2-b1-syllabus-map.md#assessment-points)):
past narrative, description and comparison, direct pronouns and `ne`, and
transactional speaking.

It has three jobs, and every design choice below serves one of them:

1. **A decision for the learner** — pass, or a named list of what to repair
   before Unit 7 leans on it.
2. **The placement check's confirmation step.** A learner placed in Band 3
   sits Parts C, D and E of this instrument before skipping Units 1–6
   ([`placement-check.md` §Band 3](placement-check.md#band-3--the-confirmation-step)).
   This is specified in [§Use as the placement confirmation
   step](#use-as-the-placement-confirmation-step) and the two documents must
   stay in step.
3. **Evidence for the course.** Decision 1 of the
   [shape review](../docs/syllabus-shape-review.md#decision-1--twelve-units-or-fourteen-hold-twelve-escalated-for-ratification)
   holds twelve units on the condition that data can reopen it. M1 is the first
   point at which the front half's spiral can be checked on real learners. See
   [§What goes back to the Chief](#what-goes-back-to-the-chief).

### What it deliberately does not measure

- **Anything from Unit 7 onward.** No imperativo item, no indirect pronoun as a
  target, no `si` passivante as a target. The Unit 6 market and the Unit 7
  office share a register; the Italian Content Editor should keep U7's
  lexical imperatives (`mi dica`, `si accomodi`) **out** of the M1 scripts
  rather than rely on learners recognising them.
- **The congiuntivo, in any form.** Seed 2 chunks
  ([`grammar-progression.md` §Cross-unit seeding](../curriculum/syllabus/grammar-progression.md#cross-unit-seeding))
  may occur in a reading or listening text because they occur in the course's
  input; no item may turn on them.
- **The condizionale as a structure.** `vorrei` and `potrebbe` are Seed 3
  chunks the learner has used since U3 and will use in the E1 role-play. They
  are credited on TA as register, never targeted on TS.
- **Culture-note content.** A milestone item on `Ferragosto` would be trivia.
  Culture shows up where it should: register on TA in E1 (brisk is polite at the
  counter).

## Delivery, and the audio problem

As in the placement check, Part B is a **script** until audio exists (format
brief §4). Unlike the placement check, a milestone pass is never final without a
listening sample: "arrived at B1 lower" is a four-skill claim. A deferred Part B
gives a provisional pass that becomes final when Part B is sat.

| Path | How | Effect on the result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Recorded** (once audio exists) | Learner plays each file twice. | Full result. |
| **Partner-read** | A partner reads each script aloud twice at the marked pace, without showing it. | Full result. |
| **Deferred** | Part B skipped. | **Provisional.** Scored out of 76: condition 1 becomes total ≥ 46 / 76 and condition 4 becomes Part A ≥ 6 / 12; conditions 2 and 3 are unchanged. A pass is recorded as "M1 passed, listening outstanding" and Part B is sat before `U8 L2`. A deferred Part B never blocks Unit 7. |

**Speaking.** E1 needs a counterpart. Partner path: the partner plays the vendor
from a role card. Solo path: the learner speaks into the pauses of a scripted
vendor recording (or, until audio exists, reads each vendor turn silently from a
card, covers it, and answers aloud). E2 is a monologue and needs no partner.
Both are recorded.

**Who marks what.** As [`rubrics.md` §Marker
calibration](rubrics.md#marker-calibration): the learner may mark Parts A–C, and
TA and CC on productive tasks. **TS, GA and FD go to a partner or teacher.** A
milestone with those scales unmarked is a **provisional** result, and the learner
is told so: it can say they did the tasks; it cannot say the forms were right.

## Part 0 — Progress review (10 minutes, unscored)

Unit 1 asks the learner to keep their first forty-second recording from `U1 L1`
and their final `U1 L5` attempt, because "the milestone review after Unit 6
asks for the first and the last"
([`unit-01-lesson-05.md`](../curriculum/units/unit-01/unit-01-lesson-05.md#after-the-lesson)).
This is that review.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Task type** | Guided self-review in English. The learner listens to the `U1 L1` recording, the `U1 L5` final attempt, and their E2 recording from this milestone (Part 0 is therefore done *after* Part E), and answers four questions on a sheet. |
| **Questions** | (1) What can you say now that you could not say then? Name two things. (2) Where do you still stop and restart? (3) Which of your six checkpoint profiles had the lowest Part B recycled score, and has that structure come right since? (4) Re-read the ten Unit 1–6 can-do statements you rated at the placement check: re-rate them. |
| **What it measures** | Nothing. It is a **washback** device: it shows the learner the spiral working on their own voice, which is the only evidence a self-study learner will believe. |
| **Why unscored** | Comparing a learner to their own past is motivating and invalid as measurement — the two recordings were not elicited under the same conditions. |

## Part A — Reading (15 minutes, 12 marks)

**Construct-validity rule for every item** (same as the placement check): an item
is answerable from the propositions in the text, never from one low-frequency
word, never from world knowledge, never by matching a keyword in the stem.

### A1 — A personal anecdote (6 marks)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | ⟨220–260 words. A blog post or long message telling a mishap story — something that went wrong on a day out, a move, a dinner. Background/foreground structure throughout; at least two background-plus-event pairs; at least five narrative connectives from [Set 5 subgroup A](../curriculum/syllabus/lexis-progression.md#a-narrative-signposts-and-connectives-10); one `stare + gerundio` in the past; riflessivi in the passato prossimo. Lexis Sets 1–5.⟩ |
| **Task type** | 3 × three-option multiple choice + 3 × sequencing (place three stated events in the order they happened, against the text's order of telling). |
| **Sequencing scoring** | *(ITA-27, ruling 1)* The learner numbers the three events 1–3. Each of the three marks is one ordered pair fixed in the key, given if the learner's numbers put that pair in the right order (the placement check's [ITA-23 ruling 6](placement-check.md#d2--a-short-monologue-4-marks-b1-probe)). A missing or duplicated number scores 0 for every pair it is in. **No key pair may be in the order of telling**, so a learner who numbers the events as they read them scores 0. |
| **Items** | MC: 1 gist (why the writer tells the story) · 1 "what was going on when…" item that turns on a background imperfetto · 1 inference from a stated reaction (Set 5 subgroup C). Sequencing: three events, presented in the text out of chronological order, recoverable only from tense and connectives. |
| **Construct note** | The sequencing items are the discriminating ones. If the text tells events in chronological order the items test nothing; the Editor must write a text with at least one flashback (`mentre … ` / `quando …` recovering an earlier moment) using only Units 1–5 tenses — **no trapassato**, which is `U9`. |

### A2 — Six short texts, five readers (6 marks)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | ⟨Six short texts of 40–60 words each — three flat or room adverts and three market/shop notices or short recipe intros. Language: relative clauses with `che` and `in cui / di cui / con cui / a cui`, comparatives, `ci` locativo, quantifiers, `si` impersonale (input only: no match may turn on `si`, because no `si` frame excludes an answer that ignores it — ITA-23 ruling 2, ITA-27 ruling 8). Lexis Sets 3 and 6.⟩ Plus five one-line descriptions of people and what they need, in English. |
| **Task type** | **Matching**, 5 items: which text suits each person. One text is a distractor that suits nobody. Plus 1 item: **which of two offers within one shop text is presented as the better value, and why** (one to four words, English). *(ITA-27, ruling 3.)* |
| **Value item** | The two offers sit in one text, so the item turns on how the text compares them (a comparative, here with `ne` and a quantity), not on price arithmetic across texts. **One mark, and both parts are needed for it:** the offer, and the reason the text gives. An offer with no reason, or with a reason the text does not present as value (taste alone, price alone), scores 0. |
| **Construct note** | Each person's need must be matchable only by parsing a relative clause or a comparison — e.g. the distractor and the key share content words and differ in what `in cui` attaches to. A matching item solvable by spotting one word is rejected at review. **Nor may a match be reachable from text type alone** *(ITA-27)*: if a person's need points to one kind of text (a flat, a recipe, a shop), at least one other text must compete for it on type and content words and be ruled out only by what a relative clause or comparison attaches to. |

> **ICE dependency:** both A1 and A2 texts, stems, options, keys and accepted

### Version 1 — Italian (ITA-16)

#### A1 — *Il trasloco (quasi) perfetto*

**Level: B1 lower** (U5 genre, Units 1–5 grammar). These choices pitch it there:
- **Tenses:** presente frame, then passato prossimo against imperfetto
  throughout. One `stare + gerundio` in the past (`stavamo scaricando`).
  **No trapassato** (`U9`): both flashbacks are a PP recovered by a time
  anchor (`quella mattina, appena…`; `alle otto, prima di partire`), which is
  how spoken Italian does it anyway.
- **Background-plus-event pairs:** four (`stavamo scaricando… quando ha
  cominciato`; `Mentre io cercavo… si è rotto`; `ero distrutta e volevo… mi
  sono accorta`; `Era tutto pronto… ha cominciato`).
- **Riflessivi in the PP:** `mi sono trasferita`, `si è rotto`, `mi sono
  accorta`, `non mi sono arrabbiata`, `mi sono seduta`.
- **Connectives from Set 5 A:** all ten (`all'improvviso`, `mentre`, `appena`,
  `così`, `a un certo punto`, `il fatto è che`, `allora`, `insomma`, `però`,
  `comunque`). The spec floor is five.
- **Lexis:** Sets 1–5 (`trasferirsi`, `il trasloco`, `il palazzo`,
  `l'ascensore`, `la cantina`, `guasto`, `rompersi`, `accorgersi`,
  `arrabbiarsi`, `disponibile`, `trovarsi bene`) plus A2 core.
  `materassino da campeggio` and `furgone` are context words no item turns on.
- **Spoken syntax:** a blog post, so it is written to sound told — left
  dislocations (`Il letto lo carichiamo per ultimo`; `la prima notte… l'ho
  passata per terra`), verb–subject order (`Mi ha aperto Rosa`), a question
  answered by its own writer (`E l'ascensore? Niente da fare`).

**Register:** a personal blog, first person, addressed to readers as `voi`
(`Vi racconto`). The writer is female (`trasferita`); no item depends on it.
**Length:** 257 words.

> **Il trasloco (quasi) perfetto**
>
> Il mese scorso mi sono trasferita da un monolocale in periferia a un bilocale
> vicino al centro. Vi racconto com'è andato il trasloco: una giornata in cui è
> andato storto quasi tutto.
>
> Era tutto pronto: mio fratello Matteo aveva un furgone per tutto il giorno, e
> due amici ci davano una mano. Nel pomeriggio stavamo scaricando gli ultimi
> scatoloni quando all'improvviso ha cominciato a piovere. Mentre io cercavo un
> ombrello, uno scatolone di libri si è rotto sul marciapiede. E l'ascensore?
> Niente da fare: quella mattina, appena siamo arrivati al palazzo nuovo,
> abbiamo scoperto che era guasto. Così, già prima di pranzo, abbiamo portato
> su la lavatrice a piedi, fino al quarto piano!
>
> Verso le nove di sera gli amici sono andati via. Io ero distrutta e volevo
> solo andare a letto. Ho cercato i pezzi del letto tra gli scatoloni… niente.
> A un certo punto mi sono accorta che il letto non c'era. Il fatto è che alle
> otto, prima di partire, abbiamo svuotato la cantina della casa vecchia, e
> Matteo ha detto: «Il letto lo carichiamo per ultimo». E poi l'abbiamo
> dimenticato lì, tutti e due.
>
> Non mi sono neanche arrabbiata: mi sono seduta su uno scatolone e ho
> cominciato a ridere. Allora ho bussato alla porta di fronte. Mi ha aperto
> Rosa, una signora di ottant'anni, che mi ha portato un materassino da
> campeggio e una coperta. Insomma, la prima notte nella casa nuova l'ho
> passata per terra, però ho conosciuto la vicina più disponibile del palazzo.
> E comunque, nel bilocale mi trovo benissimo.

**Items 1–3** (choose A, B or C):

1. *Why does the writer tell this story?*
   A. To complain that her brother ruined the whole move by forgetting things.
   B. To tell readers about a moving day when almost everything went wrong.
   C. To warn readers about the problems she has since found with her new flat.
2. *What were they doing when it started to rain?*
   A. Emptying the cellar of the old building before they left.
   B. Carrying the washing machine up four floors on foot.
   C. Unloading the last boxes from the van.
3. *What does the writer's reaction show, when she realises the bed is missing?*
   A. She is furious with Matteo for leaving the bed behind.
   B. By then, she can see the funny side of it.
   C. She goes straight back to the old flat to fetch it.

**Items 4–6.** *Number these three events 1–3 in the order they happened.*

- a. It starts to rain.
- b. Matteo says they will load the bed last.
- c. They find out that the lift is broken.

| Item | Key | The clause that contradicts each distractor, or the scoring rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **B** | gist. A: `l'abbiamo dimenticato lì, tutti e due` — the blame is shared. C: `nel bilocale mi trovo benissimo`. |
| 2 | **C** | background imperfetto (`stavamo scaricando… quando ha cominciato a piovere`). A: the cellar was `alle otto, prima di partire`. B: the washing machine went up `già prima di pranzo`; the rain is `Nel pomeriggio`. B is the keyword lure: the stairs sentence follows the rain sentence. |
| 3 | **B** | inference from a stated reaction (Set 5 C: `Non mi sono neanche arrabbiata… ho cominciato a ridere`). A: `Non mi sono neanche arrabbiata`. C: `la prima notte… l'ho passata per terra`. |
| 4 | **b before c** — 1 mark | `alle otto, prima di partire` (old cellar) against `appena siamo arrivati al palazzo nuovo` (the lift). |
| 5 | **c before a** — 1 mark | The rain is told first, but `quella mattina, appena siamo arrivati… abbiamo scoperto` puts the lift in the morning and the rain is `Nel pomeriggio`. |
| 6 | **b before a** — 1 mark | The bed is told last, recovered by `Il fatto è che alle otto…`. |

**Scoring rule** (ITA-23 ruling 6, extended to three events): the learner
numbers a–c 1–3; each mark is one ordered pair fixed in the key, given if the
learner's numbers put that pair in the right order. A missing or duplicated
number scores 0 for every pair it is in.

**Full chronological order:** b – c – a. **Order of telling:** a – c – b, which
is also the order of last mention. **A learner who numbers the events in the
order they read them scores 0 / 3.** One adjacent swap from the key costs one
mark.

#### A2 — *Sei annunci, cinque persone*

**Level: A2+ / B1 lower** (U3 and U6 payload). Grading choices:
- **Relatives:** ten, across `che`, `in cui`, `di cui` and `con cui` (plus
  `per chi fa`). No `a cui` in these texts: it is gapped in C2.4.
- **Comparatives:** `più tranquilla del resto della casa`; `più luminoso e
  spazioso di quello di…`; `costa più di quello fresco… molto più saporito`;
  `più dolci che mai`; `più veloce di una pizza`.
- **`ci` locativo:** `all'università ci si arriva a piedi` (with `si`
  impersonale, U4). **`si` impersonale** also in D (`si assaggia`) and F
  (`si torna`, `si ha poca voglia`).
- **Quantifiers (U6 L3):** `un po' di`, `qualche`, `ne… la metà`, `ne…
  qualche cassetta`.
- **Lexis:** Set 3 (`camera`, `cortile`, `bollette`, `spese condominiali`,
  `arredato`, `luminoso`, `spazioso`, `rumoroso`, `tranquillo`) and Set 6
  (`il banco`, `di stagione`, `senza conservanti`, `assaggiare`,
  `condire`, `un barattolo`, `saporito`, `la pentola`). One
  false-friend item: `la fattoria` (U2) in E.

**Register:** A–C are private listings on a portal (telegraphic facts,
discursive description, `Siamo a pochi minuti…` as landlords write). D and E
are notices at a market stall and a greengrocer's, addressing customers as
`voi` (`trovate`). F is the intro to a recipe on a food blog.

**Cultural currency:** Padova's piazza delle Erbe holds a market every morning
except Sunday; the city's tram runs through Arcella to the station. Rents are
in line with Padova in 2026 (room 360 €, central furnished bilocale 820 €,
Arcella 650 €). **Lengths:** A 59, B 60, C 60, D 52, E 59, F 55.

> **A. Camera singola — Padova**
>
> Affittasi camera singola in un appartamento al secondo piano: le coinquiline
> sono due studentesse con cui dividere cucina e bagno. La camera dà sul
> cortile ed è più tranquilla del resto della casa. Il mercato rionale, che c'è
> solo il sabato, è a dieci minuti; all'università ci si arriva a piedi. 360 €
> al mese, bollette comprese. Niente animali.

> **B. Bilocale — Padova, piazza delle Erbe**
>
> Bilocale arredato al primo piano, in pieno centro. La camera da letto dà sulla
> piazza, in cui c'è il mercato tutte le mattine tranne la domenica; il
> soggiorno, invece, dà sul cortile ed è tranquillissimo. Siamo a pochi minuti
> dai giardini pubblici, in cui la mattina mezzo quartiere porta a spasso il
> cane. 820 € al mese, più spese condominiali.

> **C. Bilocale con giardino — Padova, Arcella**
>
> Bilocale al piano terra in un palazzo tranquillo, con un piccolo giardino
> privato in cui può stare un cane. La camera da letto, che dà sulla strada, di
> giorno è un po' rumorosa, ma il soggiorno è più luminoso e spazioso di quello
> di molti appartamenti in centro. Il tram per la stazione passa qui davanti.
> 650 € al mese.

> **D. Banco 12 — Formaggi di nostra produzione**
>
> Il nostro pecorino lo facciamo noi, con il latte delle pecore di cui ci
> occupiamo in famiglia da tre generazioni. Quello stagionato costa più di
> quello fresco, ma è molto più saporito: per condire la pasta ne basta la
> metà. Senza conservanti. Al nostro banco, come sempre, si assaggia prima di
> comprare.

> **E. Frutta e verdura — Da Rita**
>
> Nel nostro negozio trovate solo frutta e verdura di stagione e, il venerdì, la
> ricotta fresca di una fattoria che conosciamo da vent'anni. Questa settimana
> le pesche sono più dolci che mai: ne abbiamo qualche cassetta anche per chi
> fa la marmellata. Il giovedì è l'unico giorno in cui siamo aperti fino a
> tardi, fino alle otto di sera.

> **F. Pasta e ceci in mezz'ora**
>
> Questa è la ricetta per le sere in cui si torna a casa tardi e si ha poca
> voglia di cucinare. Servono una pentola sola, un barattolo di ceci, uno
> spicchio d'aglio, un po' di rosmarino e qualche cucchiaio di passata. È più
> veloce di una pizza a domicilio e, secondo me, molto più saporita.

7. Mark only buys cheese from people who keep their own animals.
8. Anna is a nurse who works nights, so she needs a bedroom that is quiet

## Part C — Language in use (12 minutes, 14 marks)

**Task type:** two connected texts with numbered gaps — **contextual cloze**,
one to three words per gap, **no options supplied**. **Verb gaps show the
infinitive in brackets, in C1 and C2**, so the item tests the choice and the
form, not recall of the verb. **Where a gap targets a periphrasis or an
adverb's position, the bracket shows both words** (`stare` + the infinitive;
the adverb + the infinitive), because otherwise a correct non-target form
(a plain imperfetto; a different adverb) fills the gap. Non-verb gaps have no
cue. *(ITA-27, ruling 5.)*

This is the same mechanic as the placement check's Part C and every checkpoint's
Part B, by design (format brief §5): at a milestone the learner should be
spending attention on Italian, not on a new task format.

#### E1 — Scripted vendor audio (solo path) · `assets/audio/milestone-1/m1-e1-vendor.mp3`

**Speaker:** the vendor (m), `Lei`, brisk, falling terminals, about 150 words a
minute. **Pauses** are silent and timed; the learner speaks into them. Until
audio exists, the learner reads each vendor turn silently from a card, covers
it, and answers aloud (§Delivery).

The script cannot know what the learner buys, so it **never names the
learner's items**. It uses `ne` (which has one form for every noun) and `Va
bene così?`, and it names only the foods it offers itself (the mozzarella and
the strawberries). `Quanto ne vuole?` is used for any item: with `quanto`
invariable, it asks "how much / how many of it" and fits weights and counts
alike.

**Solo-path cue card (English, the learner may look at it):** *Buy one of your
things at each of pauses 1, 4, 7 and 12. At pause 6, the vendor offers you
something you already bought elsewhere. At pause 9, ask what is good today. At
pause 15, query or confirm the total.*

## Scoring and pass criterion

### Mark allocation

| Part | Marks | Duration | Nature | Self-markable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 — Progress review | 0 | 10 min | unscored | — |
| A — Reading | 12 | 15 min | objective | yes |
| B — Listening | 10 | 12 min | objective | yes |
| C — Language in use | 14 | 12 min | objective (cloze) | yes |
| D — Writing | 20 | 22 min | TA 5 + TS 5 + GA 5 + CC 5 | TA, CC |
| E — Speaking | 30 | 16 min | E1: TA 5 + TS 5 · E2: TA 5 + TS 5 + CC 5 + FD 5 | TA, CC |
| **Total** | **86** | **77 min** | | |
| *of which C–E* | *64* | *50 min* | | |

Productive (D + E) is **50 of 86 (58%)**; productive plus language in use is
**64 of 86**. That is a **washback** decision: the half of the course that
teaches learners to narrate and transact is assessed by making them narrate and
transact.

### Pass — all four conditions

1. **Total ≥ 52 / 86** (60%).
2. **Parts C–E ≥ 39 / 64** (60%).
3. **No productive scale below 3** — all ten scale scores across D, E1 and E2.
   This contains the standing floor of TA ≥ 3 on every productive task
   ([`rubrics.md` §Aggregation](rubrics.md#aggregation-and-the-no-compensation-rule))
   and extends it: at a milestone, a 2 on TS means a front-half structure is not
   yet usable, and a 2 on FD or CC means the learner cannot yet sustain a turn.
   Neither is compensable by a strong reading score.
4. **Parts A–B ≥ 11 / 22** (50%). A lower receptive floor than productive, on
   purpose: this course's risk is learners who understand and cannot produce,
   not the reverse.

Condition 3 is the strict one, and it is meant to be. Band 3 is the pass band on
every scale ([`rubrics.md` §The band meanings are
fixed](rubrics.md#the-band-meanings-are-fixed)): *did the thing, with errors
that do not obscure the message*. Requiring it on every scale is requiring B1
lower, not B2.

### Reporting — a profile, not a number

Reported as: **A / B / C1 / C2 / D (TA, TS, GA, CC) / E1 (TA, TS) / E2 (TA, TS,
CC, FD)** plus pass or not yet. The profile is what the carry-forward list is
written from.

### What a "not yet" does

The checkpoint rule holds: **a milestone result does not block Unit 7**, for the
reasons given in [`unit-checkpoint-template.md` §A fail does not block
progression](unit-checkpoint-template.md#a-fail-does-not-block-progression).
What it does instead is more specific than a checkpoint fail, because the
evidence is richer.

| Result | Action |
| --- | --- |
| **Pass** | Continue to Unit 7. Any C gap scored 0 still writes a carry-forward entry. |
| **Not yet — one or two scales below 3, totals met** | Carry-forward entry per scale, with its return point (table below). The learner does the repair set for each TS below 3 before `U7 L2`. **Resit only the failed task** in a parallel version after `U8 L4`, which is where PP/imperfetto and direct pronouns next return. |
| **Not yet — a total missed, or three or more scales below 3** | As above, plus a resit of **Parts C–E** in a parallel version after `U8 L4`. Whether a learner in this band should instead repeat Units 5–6 before Unit 7 is a **course-shape decision** for the Chief Italian Linguist — see [§Open](#open-and-who-owns-it). Until it is decided, the default is the checkpoint rule: continue, with the list. |

## Use as the placement confirmation step

The placement check's Band 3 is confirmed, not assumed
([`placement-check.md` §Band 3 — the confirmation
step](placement-check.md#band-3--the-confirmation-step)). The learner sits:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Parts** | **C, D and E** — language in use, writing, speaking. Parts 0, A and B are not sat: placement has already sampled reading and listening, and Part 0 needs a course history the learner does not have. |
| **Marks / time** | **64 marks, ≈ 50 minutes** (12 + 22 + 16). |
| **Threshold** | **≥ 60% — 39 / 64 — with no scale below 3** across D, E1 and E2. Identical to M1 pass conditions 2 and 3, so "confirmed for Unit 7" means exactly "would have passed the productive half of Milestone 1". |
| **Marker** | **A partner or teacher must mark TS, GA and FD.** A self-marked result cannot confirm Band 3, for the same reason a deferred listening cannot award it. |
| **Version** | The **parallel version**, not the one learners in the course sit at the end of Unit 6. |
| **If missed** | Enter at Unit 4, as the placement check says. |

Any change to Parts C–E — marks, duration, scales, threshold — is a change to
the placement check too, and must be made in both files in the same commit.

## What goes back to the Chief

Decision 1 holds twelve units on condition that data can reopen it
([shape review](../docs/syllabus-shape-review.md#decision-1--twelve-units-or-fourteen-hold-twelve-escalated-for-ratification)).
M1 cannot show the compressed tail failing — that is M2's job — but it supplies
the **baseline** M2 is compared against, and it is the first check on whether
learners entered at solid A2.

For every learner who sits M1, the anonymised profile goes to the Chief with
their placement band. Two patterns are worth acting on, and both are stated so
they can be argued with:

- **Band 2 entrants failing M1 condition 3 at a high rate** (proposed trigger:
  more than a third of the first cohort, or of the first twelve solo learners) is

## Construct-validity review checklist

Run before M1 is used, and again on the parallel version.

- [ ] Nothing in any text, script or item requires a structure introduced after `U6` — checked against `grammar-progression.md`. No trapassato in A1's flashback.
- [ ] No item turns on a congiuntivo chunk, a `Lei` imperative or a condizionale form.
- [ ] A1 tells its events out of chronological order; B2 mentions each classified feature in both halves.
- [ ] B1 and E1 scripts refer to at least two items by pronoun or `ne` only, after naming them once.
- [ ] No A2 match is reachable from one word or from text type alone; every person's need has a competing text ruled out only by a relative clause or comparison (ITA-27).
- [ ] Every cloze gap has an alternatives list and needs the co-text to fill.
- [ ] Every verb gap carries its bracket cue, and the periphrasis and adverb-position gaps show both words (ITA-27, ruling 5).
- [ ] Every productive prompt lists its required elements in English and names no structure.
- [ ] Every elicitation floor is reachable in the time: 3 relatives in 150 words; 4 substitutions in a four-item purchase; 2 background-plus-event pairs in two minutes.
- [ ] Band-3 and band-5 anchors exist for D, E1 and E2 before the first learner sits it.
- [ ] Parts C–E sum to 64 marks and ≈ 50 minutes, matching `placement-check.md`.
- [ ] A parallel version exists — Band 3 placement learners and resitting learners need it.
