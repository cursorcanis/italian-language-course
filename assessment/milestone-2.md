## What this instrument is for, and what it carries

Milestone 2 is the course's exit. It answers: **has this learner reached B1?** —
in a form recognisable outside this repo
([map §Assessment points](../curriculum/syllabus/a2-b1-syllabus-map.md#assessment-points);
the **externally recognisable exit** lens).

It also carries **structural weight**, not only assessment weight, and that is
stated first because it shapes everything below. Holding the course at twelve
units ([shape review, Decision
1](../docs/syllabus-shape-review.md#decision-1--twelve-units-or-fourteen-hold-twelve-escalated-for-ratification))
means M2 is where the compressed tail gets its last encounter:

- **Six structures** reach free production **only here** — their third
  encounter is `M2` in the
  [master table](../curriculum/syllabus/grammar-progression.md#units-912--b1-and-the-compressed-tail):
  pronomi combinati, trapassato prossimo, periodo ipotetico della realtà,
  futuro epistemico, condizionale presente, `ci` pronominale.
- **Two more** get their **second** encounter here and no third inside the
  course: congiuntivo presente (supported) and connettivi argomentativi.

[`grammar-progression.md` §What this hands to
ITA-4](../curriculum/syllabus/grammar-progression.md#what-this-hands-to-ita-4)
turns that into seven numbered requirements and makes them binding. Decision 1
adds three conditions. How this instrument meets each is in
[§Binding requirements, and where each is met](#binding-requirements-and-where-each-is-met).

### Task type, duration, what it measures, pass criterion

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Task type** | Five-section B1 exam on the CILS UNO-B1 section pattern (CELI 2 has the same skills in four components): reading, listening, language in use (*structures*), two writing tasks, a four-part oral. Self-administered with a stated partner path; the oral and the productive marking need a second person for a confirmed result. |
| **Duration** | **Written paper (A–D): 145 minutes** — A 35, B 25, C 25, D 60 — in one sitting with a break after C, or two sittings within a week. **Oral (E): ≈ 18 minutes**, on a separate day. |
| **What it measures** | The B1 can-dos of Units 9–12, with the whole course underneath them; the seven tail requirements; Lexis Sets 1–12 with Sets 9–12 dominant. |
| **Pass criterion** | **Every section ≥ 60%**, **and** every TA, CC, GA and FD score ≥ 3, **and** no more than **one** of the five TS scores below 3. See [§Scoring and pass criterion](#scoring-and-pass-criterion). |

### Model: what is borrowed from CILS and CELI, and what is not

Verified against the publishers' current specifications on 2026-10-12 (ITA-17).
The evidence, with sources and the full external formats, is in
[`docs/cils-celi-verification.md`](../docs/cils-celi-verification.md). Exams
modelled: **CILS UNO-B1** (Siena) and **CELI 2** (Perugia). They are not the
citizenship variants; see that file's §B1 cittadinanza.

| This instrument | CILS UNO-B1 section | CELI 2 component | Task types borrowed (verified) | Course's own design (in neither B1 exam) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — Reading | *Comprensione della lettura* | *Prova di comprensione della lettura* (Parte A) | A1 multiple choice on one text (CILS; CELI uses MC on short texts) · A2 text reordering (CILS only) · A3 true/false information selection (CILS reading 2; ITA-22) | — |
| B — Listening | *Ascolto* | *Prova di comprensione dell'ascolto* (Parte C) | B1 multiple choice on short messages (both) · B2 multiple choice on one longer text (CILS) · B3 true/false information selection on a radio broadcast (CILS listening 3; ITA-22) | — |
| C — Language in use | *Analisi delle strutture di comunicazione* | **no separate component at CELI 2**: its grammar items (MC cloze, sentence completion) sit inside Parte A | C1 verb-form cloze with the infinitive given (CILS) · C3 multiple-choice cloze (CILS; CELI inside reading) | C2 pronoun transformation |
| D — Writing | *Produzione scritta* | *Prova di produzione di testi scritti* (Parte B) | D1 a letter or email with a practical purpose (both; neither fixes formal register at B1, and both are shorter: CILS 50–80 or 100–120 words, CELI 90–100) | D2 argued opinion text. Neither B1 exam sets one; CILS's longer task is a set-topic text, currently a description or narrative |
| E — Oral | *Produzione orale* | *Prova orale* / *Prova di produzione orale* | E1 conversation about oneself (both, **scored** in both) · E2 picture-based monologue (both) · E3 role-play (CELI only) | E4 as specified: a sustained 3-minute argued monologue plus a reply to a counterargument. CILS's ~1½-minute topic monologue is the nearest |

**Borrowed:** the five-way section split (CILS's; CELI 2 has four components),
the task types in the "borrowed" column, and **CILS's** principle that each
skill is passed separately rather than averaged, so a strong reader cannot carry
a learner who cannot write. CELI 2 does **not** work this way: reading, writing
and listening compensate for each other inside one written threshold, and the
oral has its own threshold. M2's rule is the stricter of the two.

**Course's own design:** the three tasks in the right-hand column (C2, D2, E4;
50 of 150 marks), kept by the Chief's ITA-17 ruling because each carries an
elicitation floor no external B1 format provides (requirements 2, 6, 7). A3
and B3 were moved to verified CILS formats by ITA-22. Learners are told that
C2, D2 and E4 are the course's own tasks, never that they are CILS or CELI task
types ([ITA-17 ruling](../docs/assessment-framework-review.md#ita-17-ruling-external-exam-alignment-2026-10-13)).

**Not borrowed:** exact word counts, timings, mark totals and pass marks. The
external exams set those for a general B1 population; this instrument sets them
against this course's constructs and its rubric bank. A learner who passes M2
has met a B1 standard **on task types they will recognise** in the external
exam. This is true of the borrowed tasks (100 of 150 marks); the three
course-designed tasks are not external formats. M2 is not a mock exam and does not predict a score on
one. The external figures are recorded in the
[verification file](../docs/cils-celi-verification.md) for reference. M2's
written paper (145 min) is shorter than CILS UNO-B1's (about 3 h 45′). Its oral
(about 18 min) and its writing lengths (120–150 and 160–200 words) are longer
than either exam's.

## Delivery, and who marks what

**Listening** is scripts until audio exists (format brief §4). Partner-read, twice,
at the marked pace, is a full result. **Deferred listening is not available at
M2**: "reached B1" is a four-skill claim. A learner with no partner and no audio
sits the other sections, has them reported as passed or not, gets "Section B
outstanding", and receives no exit statement until B is sat.

**The oral** needs a counterpart for E3 and the E4 follow-up. Partner path: the
partner reads the interlocutor card. Solo path: scripted interlocutor
recordings with pauses (`assets/audio/milestone-2/m2-e3-agent.mp3`,
`m2-e4-counter.mp3`); until audio exists, the learner reads each interlocutor
turn silently, covers it, and answers aloud. Everything is recorded.

**Marking.** As [`rubrics.md` §Marker
calibration](rubrics.md#marker-calibration): learner marks A–C and TA/CC on
productive tasks; **TS, GA and FD go to a partner or teacher**. At M2 this is
not a convenience but a condition: **a self-marked M2 is reported as
"provisional — not confirmed"**, and no exit statement is issued on it. A
cohort decision on M2 additionally needs the inter-marker step that
[`rubrics.md` §Open](rubrics.md#open-and-who-owns-it) leaves with the Chief.

## Section A — Reading (35 minutes, 15 marks)

**Construct-validity rule for every item:** answerable from the propositions in
the text; never from one low-frequency word, world knowledge, or keyword
matching.

### A1 — An opinion article (5 marks)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | ⟨350–400 words. A newspaper or magazine opinion piece on an everyday public question (city traffic, remote work, tourism in small towns). The writer takes a position, concedes one point, and separates what they are sure of from what they suspect. Language: congiuntivo after the taught opinion triggers, `anche se` + indicative concession, at least three argumentative connectives from [Set 12 subgroup C](../curriculum/syllabus/lexis-progression.md#c-argumentative-connectives-and-evaluation-10), `si` passivante in news register, one futuro epistemico. Lexis Sets 10–12 dominant.⟩ |
| **Task type** | 5 × four-option multiple choice, in text order. |
| **Items** | 1 gist (the writer's position) · 1 detail · 1 **concession** item (what the writer accepts from the other side) · 1 **hedged-vs-asserted** item: which of four claims the writer presents as fact rather than as opinion or likelihood · 1 reference item (what a pronoun or `ne` refers back to). |
| **Construct note** | The hedged-vs-asserted item is **congiuntivo at recognition level** (requirement 6). The key and the strongest distractor make the same claim; one is introduced by a taught trigger with congiuntivo, the other is stated in the indicative. A learner who reads the content words without the frame gets it wrong. |

### A2 — Reordering a story (5 marks)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | ⟨A narrative of 260–300 words in six paragraphs, the first given in place: a favour that went wrong between friends or neighbours, told from the end. The remaining five are presented shuffled. Language: trapassato prossimo for events before the narrative's past, combined pronouns referring across paragraph boundaries, narrative connectives. Lexis Set 9.⟩ |
| **Task type** | **Text reordering** — place five paragraphs. 1 mark per paragraph in the correct position. |
| **Construct note** | Each paragraph boundary must be recoverable from **grammar and reference**, not from topic: the trapassato tells the learner that an event was earlier than the one just narrated; a combined pronoun in paragraph *n* needs its referents in paragraph *n − 1*. Requirement 3 (trapassato) at recognition. The Editor must ensure no boundary is recoverable from a repeated content word alone. |

### A3 — Offers and their conditions: information selection (5 marks)

*Format: CILS UNO-B1 reading task 2, true/false information selection
([verification](../docs/cils-celi-verification.md), CILS table). Re-specified
by ITA-22; the construct is unchanged.*

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | ⟨One informational text of 350–420 words: a page from a town's noticeboard or portal with several offers under short headings. It includes job adverts, course listings and accommodation offers, and each offer states its conditions. Language: `si` passivante (`si richiede`, `si offre` as in U10), relative clauses, comparatives, futuro for conditions of the offer. Lexis Sets 10–11.⟩ Plus ⟨ten statements in Italian, one line each, about who or what the offers suit and on what terms⟩, in text order. |
| **Task type** | **Information selection**: for each statement, the learner marks *vero* if the text says it and *falso* if not. Five are true. 0.5 mark per correct answer; 10 × 0.5 = 5 marks. |
| **Construct note** | Every statement must be decidable only by **parsing a condition** in the text: a relative clause, a comparison, a requirement in `si` passivante, a futuro condition. A single content word must never decide it. **Each false statement shares content words with the offer it is about and fails exactly one condition.** For example, it reverses a comparative, or attaches a relative clause to the wrong offer. **Each true statement paraphrases its condition and never repeats the text's wording.** The construct is the one the matching task carried: reading conditions, not spotting words. |

> **ICE dependency:** all three texts, stems, options and keys.

## Section B — Listening (25 minutes, 15 marks)

Each recording is played (or partner-read) **twice**; the learner reads the
questions first. Assets under `assets/audio/milestone-2/`.

### B1 — Five short messages (5 marks)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | ⟨Five independent recordings of 15–30 seconds: voicemails, a station or office announcement, a radio trailer. Natural pace.⟩ `m2-b1-1.mp3` … `m2-b1-5.mp3`. |
| **Task type** | 5 × three-option multiple choice, one per message. |
| **Required items** | One message whose key turns on **futuro epistemico** — the speaker is guessing about the present (as in `saranno le otto`), and the distractor is the anglophone misparse, a prediction about the future (requirement 4, recognition). One whose key turns on a **condizionale request** versus a statement of fact (requirement 1, recognition). One whose key turns on a **`ci` pronominale** chunk from the U11 set (`non ci riesco`, `ci penso`) — requirement 5, recognition. Two free. |

### B2 — A phone call about a booking (5 marks)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | ⟨2–2½ minutes. A guest calls an agriturismo or hotel: what had been booked, what has gone wrong, what each side has already done about it. Formal register, `Lei` throughout. Combined pronouns referring to documents and payments (`me lo conferma?`, `gliel'ho già detto` type); trapassato for the earlier arrangement; conditional requests; one `Lei` imperative set against a conditional request. Lexis Set 11.⟩ `m2-b2.mp3`. |
| **Task type** | 5 × three-option multiple choice. |
| **Required items** | 1 **sequencing** item that turns on the trapassato: which of two arrangements was made first. 1 **referent** item that turns on a combined pronoun: who sent, or is to send, what to whom. 1 item on the remedy finally agreed. 2 free. |

### B3 — A radio discussion: information selection (5 marks)

*Format: CILS UNO-B1 listening task 3, true/false information selection on a
radio broadcast ([verification](../docs/cils-celi-verification.md), CILS
table). Re-specified by ITA-22; the construct is unchanged.*

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | ⟨3 minutes. A host and two guests discuss a local issue on a radio programme. The guests are named and introduced by their role (for example, a shopkeeper and a resident). Each guest holds a clear position, concedes one point, and hedges one claim. Overlap is permitted and natural (U12 culture note).⟩ `m2-b3.mp3`. Plus ⟨ten statements in Italian, one line each, in the order of the recording. Each statement names the guest it is about.⟩ |
| **Task type** | **Information selection**: for each statement, the learner marks *vero* if the programme says it and *falso* if not. Five are true. 0.5 mark per correct answer; 10 × 0.5 = 5 marks. |
| **Required items** | **2 hedged-vs-asserted statements** (requirement 6, recognition). Each one reports a guest's claim as a firm assertion. One is *falso*: the guest only hedged it, with a trigger + congiuntivo. The other is *vero*: the guest asserted it in the indicative. Both are answerable only by hearing the frame. **2 concession statements** (requirement 7, recognition). One correctly reports what a guest conceded with `anche se` before answering (*vero*). The other turns the concession into the guest's own position (*falso*). **1 attribution statement** credits a guest with the other guest's view (*falso*); it keeps the old task's "who said what" construct and the U12 can-do "respond to what they actually said". The remaining 5 are free. |
| **Construct note** | Naming the guest in each statement keeps the speaker attribution that the matching format tested. The true/false format then also tests whether the learner heard the claim's **status** (asserted, hedged or conceded), which is what requirements 6 and 7 ask of recognition. |

> **ICE dependency:** seven scripts, marked for speaker, pace and contour, with
> items and keys.

## Section C — Language in use (25 minutes, 20 marks)

Two of the three formats are external: C1 (verb-form cloze, infinitive given)
is a CILS UNO-B1 *strutture* task, and C3 (multiple-choice cloze) appears in
both CILS and CELI 2, inside CELI's reading component. Both are cloze, the
course's own mechanic in every checkpoint Part B and in Milestone 1 Part C; C3
only adds options. The third, C2 transformation, is **the course's own format**:
neither B1 exam uses it ([verification](../docs/cils-celi-verification.md),
claim 19). It is kept because it is the only objective item that forces
pronomi combinati in production (requirement 2). It is new to the learner at
M2, so **the Lesson Designer should introduce it once in `U12 L4`** as an activity
format inside the whole-course recycling, not as test practice.

### C1 — Verb forms in context (10 marks)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | ⟨One connected text, 160–190 words: an email to a friend about a trip that has just gone wrong and what happens next. Each gap shows the infinitive in brackets.⟩ |
| **Gaps** | **Trapassato prossimo** ×2 (an event before another past event; one with `essere` and agreement) · **condizionale** ×2 (one polite request, one softened opinion or advice) · **futuro semplice** ×1 (a plan) · **futuro epistemico** ×1 (a guess about the present) · **periodo ipotetico della realtà** ×1 (the main-clause verb after a `se` clause given in the text) · **congiuntivo presente** ×2, **supported** — the trigger is printed immediately before the gap and the infinitive is given, so the learner supplies morphology, not the decision to subordinate (as at the [U12 checkpoint](unit-checkpoint-template.md#the-unit-12-exception-and-why-it-is-not-a-softening)) · **PP / imperfetto** ×1. |
| **Marking** | 1 mark each, all-or-nothing, with an alternatives list per gap. Where both PP and trapassato are grammatical Italian at a gap, the gap is rejected at review: the co-text must force the anterior reading. |

### C2 — Pronoun transformation (5 marks)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | ⟨Five short exchanges forming one conversation between flatmates. In each, the learner rewrites a reply replacing the underlined nouns with pronouns.⟩ |
| **Items** | **Pronomi combinati** ×3 — one `glie-` form, one in the passato prossimo with participle agreement, one attached to an infinitive or imperative (requirement 2, **production**) · direct-vs-indirect choice ×1 · `ne` with a quantity ×1. |
| **Marking** | 1 mark each, all-or-nothing: cluster form, position and agreement are all part of the key. |

### C3 — Multiple-choice cloze (5 marks)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | ⟨One connected text, 130–150 words: a reader's comment replying to an article. Four options per gap.⟩ |
| **Gaps** | **Argumentative connectives** ×2 (Set 12 subgroup C; the distractors are other connectives that are grammatical but reverse or break the argument) · **concession** ×1 (`anche se` + indicative against distractors that are not concessive) · **`ci` pronominale** ×1 — recognition by selection, the distractors being `lo`, `ne` and no pronoun · **`si` passivante agreement** ×1. |
| **Why options here** | Requirement 5 is explicit: `ci` pronominale is accepted **at recognition level**, because it cannot be forced in production without cueing it. Connectives are chosen, not produced, here because their production is marked where it belongs — on CC in D2 and E4. |

> **ICE dependency:** all three texts, keys, alternatives lists and distractors.

## Section D — Writing (60 minutes, 50 marks)

Two tasks, both compulsory, **25 marks each**. Suggested split: 25 minutes D1,
35 minutes D2, each with planning time inside it.

### D1 — A formal complaint (25 marks)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Task type** | Formal email, **120–150 words**, `Lei` register. |
| **Prompt spec (English)** | You stayed three nights at a small hotel or agriturismo. The prompt gives, in English, a booking confirmation (what you had booked and paid for) and a note of what you found on arrival, which was different. Write to the owner. Required elements: (1) say what you had booked and what you had already done before arriving (for example: paid a deposit, sent your arrival time); (2) say what you found, and what happened when you raised it; (3) ask for a specific remedy, politely enough that the owner wants to help; (4) say what you would like to happen next. |
| **What it measures** | The U11 can-dos: complaining about a service and asking for it to be put right in writing; making a request politely enough for a formal situation. The map's own line: *a complaint is always about what had already been arranged*. |
| **Scales** | **TA ×1 + TS-a ×1 + TS-b ×1 + GA ×1 + CC ×1** = 25. Two TS targets, scored separately ([`rubrics.md` §TS](rubrics.md#scale-ts--range-and-control-of-the-target-structure-05)). CC applies (≥ 120 words). |
| **TS-a — condizionale presente** (requirement 1) | **Floor: 3 conditional forms across ≥ 2 of the four taught uses** (request, wish, advice, softened opinion). A complaint made entirely in the indicative and imperative is achievable and rude; it caps TS-a at 2 and, if the register fails, TA too. |
| **TS-b — trapassato prossimo** (requirement 3) | **Floor: 2 trapassato forms where the sequence genuinely requires them.** Element (1) is built so the arrangement precedes a narrated past arrival — the anterior event is in the situation, not in an instruction. A passato prossimo where Italian would accept it is not an error; it simply does not count toward the floor. |
| **Combined pronouns** | Credited on TS of E3, not here; in D1 they fall under GA. |
| **Register** | `tu` anywhere in the email fails TA band 3 ([`rubrics.md` §TA](rubrics.md#scale-ta--task-achievement-05) band 2 example). |

### D2 — An opinion piece (25 marks)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Task type** | Argued text, **160–200 words**, for a local newspaper's readers' page. |
| **Stimulus** | ⟨Two short reader comments, 40–50 words each, taking opposite sides of an everyday public question — not the A1 topic.⟩ Plus a **phrasebox**: the U12 trigger list as printed in the map (`penso/credo che`, `mi sembra/mi pare che`, `spero che`, `dubito che`, `è possibile/probabile che`, `può darsi che`, `è giusto che`), with no example sentences. |
| **Required elements** | (1) your position, stated in the first two sentences; (2) two reasons, at least one with an example; (3) one point from the comment you disagree with, acknowledged and answered — not just restated against; (4) what you think is likely to happen, or should be done; (5) a conclusion that follows from what precedes it. |
| **What it measures** | All four U12 can-dos. |
| **Scales** | **TA ×1 + TS ×1 + GA ×1 + CC ×2** = 25. |
| **TS — congiuntivo presente, supported** (requirement 6) | **Floor: 3 congiuntivo forms after phrasebox triggers, and ≥ 1 deliberate `penso di` + infinitive where the subject is the same** — the same floor as the U12 checkpoint, because the construct is the same. Scored exactly as there: correct supported use is band 3–4 and **passes**; correct unsupported use (a trigger not in the phrasebox, a form not in the taught list) is band 5. An item demanding unsupported congiuntivo would test B2, and none does. |
| **CC ×2 — connettivi argomentativi** (requirement 7) | Weighted double because cohesion is the B1 claim this task exists to make. **Floor: ≥ 3 different argumentative connectives used correctly, including one concession with `anche se`.** Below the floor, CC cannot exceed 2. |

> **ICE dependency:** the D1 booking confirmation and arrival note (in English,
> but naming Italian places and services), the D2 stimulus comments, and band-3
> / band-5 anchors for both tasks.

## Section E — Oral (≈ 18 minutes, 50 marks)

Four parts in a fixed order, recorded throughout. Preparation times are inside
the 18 minutes.

### E1 — Warm-up conversation (2 minutes, unscored)

The interlocutor asks about the learner's work or study and their plans for the
next year. **Unscored**: its job is to settle the learner and get the voice
working, and scoring a warm-up penalises nerves rather than Italian.

### E2 — What's going on here? (1 minute preparation + 1½ minutes + 1 minute follow-up, 15 marks)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Task type** | Picture-based monologue, then two follow-up questions. |
| **Stimulus** | > [visual: a crowded regional railway platform in the late afternoon; the departures board shows one train delayed and one cancelled; a man in a suit on the phone looking at his watch; a family with two children and large suitcases; a young woman running down the stairs with a cake box.] Asset: `assets/images/milestone-2/m2-e2-binario.png`. The same scene is printed as an **English description** beside the image, so no meaning is carried by the image alone (format brief §4). |
| **Prompt (English)** | Describe what you can see. Then **guess**: who these people are, where they are going, why. Then say what will happen to two of them if the delayed train does not come — and what will happen if it does. |
| **Follow-up (interlocutor card)** | Two questions inviting further speculation and consequence, e.g. about the woman with the cake box. ⟨Editor: in Italian, on the card.⟩ |
| **What it measures** | Requirement 4 — `periodo ipotetico della realtà` and `futuro epistemico`, together, in the speculation task the requirement asks for. The U10 can-do "I can say what will happen if something else happens". |
| **Scales** | **TA ×1 + TS ×1 + FD ×1** = 15. CC not used: 90 seconds is below the two-minute sustained-turn threshold ([`rubrics.md` §CC](rubrics.md#scale-cc--coherence-and-cohesion-05)). |
| **TS — speculation** | **Floor: ≥ 2 futuro epistemico forms for guesses about the present, and ≥ 2 `se` sentences with presente/futuro.** `forse` + presente is good Italian and is never marked as an error, but it does not count toward the floor: the prompt was built for the epistemic future, and avoidance is a result. |

### E3 — Role-play: moving out (1 minute preparation + 4 minutes, 15 marks)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Task type** | Formal role-play of short turns with an interlocutor — a letting agent, `Lei` both ways. |
| **Role card (English)** | You are leaving a rented flat. Required elements: (1) arrange how and when to hand over the keys; (2) ask about your deposit and when they will return it; (3) ask the agent to send you a document you need for your new address; (4) when the agent says they never received the meter readings, explain that you had already sent them, and offer to send them again. |
| **Interlocutor card** | ⟨Editor: eight scripted agent turns. Each of elements (1)–(4) must be met by an agent turn that names the object first — keys, deposit, document, readings — so that the natural reply refers back to the object *and* a person at once. One turn uses a `Lei` imperative, one a conditional request, so the learner hears both politeness levels and chooses theirs.⟩ |
| **What it measures** | Requirement 2 — pronomi combinati, in "a task where repeating full noun phrases would be unnatural": every element is about handing a named thing to a named person. The U9 can-do "I can refer back to a person and a thing at the same time". |
| **Scales** | **TA ×1 + TS ×1 + FD ×1** = 15. CC not used (short turns). |
| **TS — pronomi combinati** | **Floor: ≥ 3 combined pronouns, ≥ 1 `glie-` form.** Repeating *the keys … to you* in full is the avoidance this floor exists to catch. |
| **Credited, not targeted** | Condizionale in the requests and trapassato in element (4) are credited on TA as register and task completion. `ci` pronominale (`ci penso io` type) is logged in the structure tally. |

### E4 — Opinion, and a reply (2 minutes preparation + 3 minutes + 1½ minutes reply, 20 marks)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Task type** | Sustained opinion monologue on one of two topics (different from A1 and D2), then a response to one counterargument. |
| **Prompt (English)** | Give your opinion. Required elements: (1) your position; (2) two reasons, with an example; (3) one thing that is likely to change, and what should be done about it; then (4) **answer** the counterargument the interlocutor puts to you — respond to what was actually said, not restate your position. |
| **Support** | The D2 phrasebox may be on the table during preparation and speaking. Congiuntivo is **credited, not targeted** here: it was targeted, supported, in D2. |
| **What it measures** | The U12 spoken can-dos: opinion with reasons, disagreeing politely and responding to what was said. Requirement 7 in speech. |
| **Scales** | **TA ×1 + CC ×2 + GA ×1** = 20. CC applies (≥ 2 minutes, sustained). GA is scored here because this is the oral's longest sample. FD is scored on E2 and E3 instead — twice is enough for a reliable fluency score, and adding it here would let one long turn decide most of the oral. |
| **CC ×2 floor** | **≥ 3 different argumentative connectives, including one concession**, as in D2. CC is also where `ci` pronominale earns credit in free production: as a device that refers back to the other speaker's point (`non ci credo`, `ci tengo`), it is exactly the "reference back" the CC descriptor rewards ([`rubrics.md` §CC](rubrics.md#scale-cc--coherence-and-cohesion-05)). |

> **ICE dependency:** the E2 follow-up card, the E3 interlocutor card and audio,
> the E4 topics and counterarguments (card and audio), and band-3 / band-5
> anchors for E2, E3 and E4.

## Scoring and pass criterion

### Mark allocation

| Section | Marks | Duration | Scales / nature | Self-markable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — Reading | 15 | 35 min | objective | yes |
| B — Listening | 15 | 25 min | objective | yes |
| C — Language in use | 20 | 25 min | objective | yes |
| D — Writing | 50 | 60 min | D1: TA, TS-a, TS-b, GA, CC · D2: TA, TS, GA, CC×2 | TA, CC |
| E — Oral | 50 | ≈ 18 min | E2: TA, TS, FD · E3: TA, TS, FD · E4: TA, CC×2, GA | TA, CC |
| **Total** | **150** | **≈ 163 min** | | |

Productive is **100 of 150**. The weight sits there on purpose (**washback**):
the course's last units teach learners to request, complain, speculate and argue,
and the exit rewards doing those things.

### Pass — all three conditions

1. **Every section ≥ 60%** (A and B may total in half-marks, from A3 and B3;
   the thresholds are unchanged): A ≥ 9 / 15 · B ≥ 9 / 15 · C ≥ 12 / 20 ·
   D ≥ 30 / 50 · E ≥ 30 / 50. Sections are not averaged.
2. **Every TA, CC, GA and FD score ≥ 3** — thirteen scale scores across five
   tasks (TA ×5, CC ×3, GA ×3, FD ×2). This includes the standing floor
   ([`rubrics.md` §Aggregation](rubrics.md#aggregation-and-the-no-compensation-rule)),
   and holds the connected-discourse and fluency claims that define B1.
3. **At most one of the five TS scores below 3** (D1 TS-a, D1 TS-b, D2 TS,
   E2 TS, E3 TS).

**Why condition 3 allows one.** Milestone 1 allows none. The difference is the
shape review's stated cost of holding twelve units: *the late structures will be
weaker at the end of the course than the front-half structures*. Every TS at M2
targets a tail structure, each with compressed spacing and two of them without a
third encounter. Requiring all five at 3 would make the pass rest on the
container's weakest point; requiring none would let the tail be skipped. One is
allowed, it is **named on the result**, and it becomes the learner's one
carry-forward item after the course. Two or more is "not yet".

### The result

| Result | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| **B1 reached** | All three conditions met, marked by a partner or teacher. Reported with the full profile and any one named TS gap. |
| **B1 reached — provisional** | Conditions met on the learner's own marking, or with listening outstanding. Not an exit statement until confirmed. |
| **Not yet** | Any condition missed. The profile names the section(s) or scale(s). **Resit the failed section(s) only**, in a parallel version, no sooner than four weeks later. Passed sections stand for six months. |

Reported as a profile — **A / B / C (C1, C2, C3) / D1 (TA, TS-a, TS-b, GA, CC) /
D2 (TA, TS, GA, CC) / E2 (TA, TS, FD) / E3 (TA, TS, FD) / E4 (TA, CC, GA)** —
never as a bare total.

### Closing carry-forward entries

M2 is the closing point for every carry-forward entry whose return is
"Milestone 2" — including congiuntivo and connettivi argomentativi, for which
it is the **only** return point
([`unit-checkpoint-template.md` §What a fail does
instead](unit-checkpoint-template.md#what-a-fail-does-instead--the-carry-forward-list)).
An entry closes when its structure scores ≥ 3 on the TS or CC that targets it
here, or — for a structure M2 tests objectively only — when its items in A–C are
all correct.

## Binding requirements, and where each is met

### The seven requirements from `grammar-progression.md`

"Elicit" here means: there is a task the learner **cannot complete as specified
without attempting the structure**, and the marking makes avoidance visible
(a TS or CC floor), plus at least one objective item. "Permit" — a task in which
the structure would be nice — is not counted.

| # | Structure | Encounter at M2 | Elicited by (scored floor) | Also tested | Level |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Condizionale presente | 3rd | **D1 TS-a** — a formal complaint cannot be made politely without it | C1 ×2, B1 | production |
| 2 | Pronomi combinati | 3rd | **E3 TS** — every element hands a named thing to a named person | C2 ×3, B2, A2 | production |
| 3 | Trapassato prossimo | 3rd | **D1 TS-b** — the arrangement precedes the narrated arrival | C1 ×2, A2, B2 | production |
| 4 | Periodo ipotetico + futuro epistemico | 3rd (both) | **E2 TS** — the speculation task | C1 ×2, B1 | production |
| 5 | `ci` pronominale | 3rd | Recognition: **C3, B1**. Free production: credited on E4 CC and logged in E3 | — | recognition + credit |
| 6 | Congiuntivo presente | 2nd | **D2 TS, supported** (phrasebox) · C1 ×2 supported | A1, B3 recognition | recognition + supported production |
| 7 | Connettivi argomentativi | 2nd | **D2 CC ×2, E4 CC ×2**, each with a connective floor | C3 ×3, A1, B3 | production, marked for cohesion |

No item anywhere demands **unsupported** congiuntivo, congiuntivo after
conjunctions, congiuntivo imperfetto, or the unreal conditional
([§Deliberately deferred](../curriculum/syllabus/grammar-progression.md#deliberately-deferred)).

### Decision 1's three conditions

| Condition | How M2 honours it |
| --- | --- |
| **M2 must elicit, not permit, the tail structures** | Table above: every requirement has a scored floor or (for `ci` pronominale, as the requirement itself specifies) recognition plus credit. |
| **Unit 12's `L4` stays a whole-course recycling lesson** | M2's washback is built not to pull `U12 L4` toward congiuntivo. Congiuntivo carries **9 of 150 marks** (C1 ×2, D2 TS 5, A1 one item, B3 two half-mark statements) — 6%. The U12 `L4` list (condizionale, futuro and epistemico, real conditional, `si`, combinati, relativi and comparativi, `ne`, `ci` pronominale, connectives) carries 14 of the 20 Section C marks and 35 of the 50 TS and CC marks in Sections D and E. A learner preparing for M2 is rewarded for doing exactly what `U12 L4` does. The one thing M2 asks `U12 L4` to add is a single introduction of the transformation format (Section C2). |
| **The placement check ships before Unit 1, and its results go to the Chief** | Not an M2 matter directly — but M2 closes the loop the placement check opens: see below. |

### Units 1–8 underneath

M2 is a whole-course exam, not a tail exam. The front half appears where B1 use
needs it: PP/imperfetto (C1, D1, A2), relativi and comparativi (A3, D2), `si`
impersonale/passivante (A1, A3, C3), `ne` (C2), direct and indirect pronouns
(C2, E3), `Lei` imperatives (B2, E3), avverbi in `-mente` (GA range in D2, E4).
It is weighted to the tail, as the map requires
([§5](../curriculum/syllabus/a2-b1-syllabus-map.md#5-deliberate-compression-at-the-end-of-the-course-stated-plainly)).

## What goes back to the Chief

Decision 1 names the trigger for fourteen units as data: *milestone data showing
the tail failing*. M2 is where that data is produced, so it is designed to
produce it.

For every learner, the anonymised profile goes to the Chief **with their
placement band and their M1 profile**. The comparison that matters:

| Front half (M1) | Tail (M2) |
| --- | --- |
| E2 TS — PP/imperfetto | D1 TS-a — condizionale |
| E1 TS — diretti + `ne` | D1 TS-b — trapassato |
| D TS — relativi | E2 TS — speculation |
| | E3 TS — combinati |

**Proposed trigger for reopening Decision 1:** across the first cohort (or the
first twelve solo learners with partner-marked results), the share of TS scores
≥ 3 on the four M2 tail targets is **more than 20 percentage points below** the
same learners' share on the three M1 targets; **or** more than a third of
learners who fail M2 fail it on condition 3 alone. Congiuntivo (D2 TS) is
reported but excluded from the trigger: it is a first look by design, and its
weakness is expected, not evidence.

The thresholds are proposals. Setting them is the Chief's call.

## Construct-validity review checklist

- [ ] No item demands unsupported congiuntivo, or any structure in [§Deliberately deferred](../curriculum/syllabus/grammar-progression.md#deliberately-deferred).
- [ ] A1's hedged-vs-asserted item: key and strongest distractor make the same claim and differ only in the frame.
- [ ] A2: every paragraph boundary is recoverable from grammar or reference; none from a repeated content word alone.
- [ ] A3: every false statement shares content words with its offer and fails exactly one condition; no true statement repeats the text's wording; 5 true / 5 false.
- [ ] B1: the futuro-epistemico item's distractor is the future-prediction misparse.
- [ ] B3: the hedged-vs-asserted pair (one *vero*, one *falso*) differs only in the frame the guest used; every statement names its guest; 5 true / 5 false.
- [ ] C1: no trapassato gap where the passato prossimo is also good Italian.
- [ ] C2 and E3: every combined-pronoun item names both referents in the co-text.
- [ ] C3: no `ci` pronominale gap where `ne` or `lo` is also grammatical.
- [ ] Every productive prompt lists its required elements in English and names no structure. The D2 phrasebox lists triggers only — no example sentences.
- [ ] Every elicitation floor is reachable in the time and length: 3 conditionals and 2 trapassati in 150 words; 3 combined pronouns in four elements; 2 + 2 speculation forms in 90 seconds plus follow-up.
- [ ] The E2 image carries no meaning absent from its English description.
- [ ] No text, item or prompt reproduces or closely paraphrases CILS or CELI material.
- [ ] Congiuntivo totals ≤ 9 / 150; recount after the Editor's texts land.
- [ ] Band-3 and band-5 anchors exist for D1, D2, E2, E3 and E4.
- [ ] A parallel version exists before the first learner sits it — resits are by section.
