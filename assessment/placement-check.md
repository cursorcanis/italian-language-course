## What this instrument is for, and what it decides

The format brief (§3) plans **90 guided hours** for A2→B1 against a published
norm of 150–200. That figure is only honest if entry is **solid** A2 rather than
weak A2. This check is the thing that makes that assumption falsifiable. It has
one job: put a learner into one of three entry points, and say so in a way that
is arguable rather than vibes.

| Band | Decision | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Band 1** | **Not yet ready** | The A2 spine this course re-enters is not in place. Starting at Unit 1 would mean learning A2 from a course that assumes it. |
| **Band 2** | **Start at Unit 1** | Solid A2. The course is built for this learner. |
| **Band 3** | **Start at Unit 7** | Units 1–6 would be revision. Entry after Milestone 1 coverage, with a confirmation step. |

**Band 3 is deliberately hard to reach**, and the reason is **construct
validity**: skipping six units on the strength of a reading score would be
inferring production from comprehension. A learner who recognises the
PP/imperfetto contrast but cannot narrate is exactly the learner Units 4–6 were
written for. So Band 3 requires productive evidence, not just a high total.

### Task type, duration, what it measures, pass criterion

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Task type** | Mixed-skill diagnostic: reading comprehension, contextual cloze, script-read listening, short guided writing, short guided monologue. Self-administered with a stated partner path. |
| **Duration** | **Stage 1: 23 minutes** (Parts A–C, paper only). **Stage 2: 23 minutes** (Parts D–F). Total **≈46 minutes** of learner time, one sitting or two. A stop rule ends it at Stage 1 for learners already placed in Band 1. |
| **What it measures** | Control of the A2 spine the course re-enters (present including high-frequency irregulars, riflessivi, passato prossimo with auxiliary choice and agreement, imperfetto, comparatives, past-time expressions) and, separately, whether Units 3–6 payload (relatives with `cui`, direct pronouns and `ne`, the PP/imperfetto contrast, `ci` locativo) is *already* productive. `si` impersonale is not probed: no free gap can force it (ITA-23, ruling 2). |
| **Pass criterion** | There is no pass. This is a **placement** instrument: every learner gets a band, and each band names an action. Band thresholds are in [§Scoring](#scoring-and-bands) and are hard. |

### Why not shorter

A 15-minute check can separate "beginner" from "not beginner". It cannot
support a decision to skip six units, because that decision needs a writing
sample and a speaking sample. The stop rule is the compromise: a learner who is
not ready finishes in 23 minutes and is told so; the 46 minutes are spent only
where a finer decision is actually being made.

## Delivery, and the audio problem

Recorded audio is out of scope for v1 (format brief §4), so Part D exists as a
**script** and has three delivery paths. This is stated here because it changes
how the result is reported, and an unreported change to an instrument is a
broken instrument.

| Path | How | Effect on the result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Recorded** (once audio exists) | Learner plays the file twice. | Full result. |
| **Partner-read** | A partner — teacher, language partner, any fluent reader — reads the script aloud twice at the marked pace, without showing it. | Full result. |
| **Deferred** (solo learner, no partner) | Part D is skipped. | **Provisional result.** Score out of 52, renormalised thresholds in [§Scoring](#scoring-and-bands). Band 3 **cannot** be awarded on a deferred result — the skip decision is not made without a listening sample. |

Part F (speaking) has no such problem: the learner records themselves on a phone
and either self-scores TA only or sends the recording to a partner. See
[`rubrics.md` §Marker calibration](rubrics.md#marker-calibration) for what a
learner may and may not self-score.

## Part A — Self-assessment (3 minutes, 0 marks)

**Task type:** learner-facing can-do checklist, four-point response
(`not yet` / `with difficulty` / `yes` / `easily`).

**What it measures:** nothing, by design. It is a **calibration** input and a
learner-orientation device, and it is explicitly **unscored** so that nobody is
placed on their own opinion of themselves.

**Content:** ten statements, lifted verbatim from the syllabus map so the
learner meets the course's own language immediately — four **(A2)** statements
from Units 1–2, three **(A2+)** from Units 3–4, three **(B1 lower)** from Units
5–6. Each is tagged with its unit so the learner can see where it is taught.

**How it is used:** compared against the scored result and reported back as one
of three notes.

| Pattern | Note reported to the learner |
| --- | --- |
| Self-assessment much higher than score | The most common pattern for anglophone learners with good passive Italian. Named explicitly, with the specific gap (usually production) identified, because a learner who thinks they are being under-placed will disengage. |
| Self-assessment much lower than score | Under-confidence. The learner is told the score stands and that the course's first two units are consolidation by design. |
| Aligned | Noted, and the band is reported without comment. |

> **ICE dependency:** none. Part A is in English (format brief §1 — metalanguage
> is English), quoting the can-do statements already drafted in the map.

## Part B — Reading (10 minutes, 10 marks)

Two texts, graded. **Construct validity note applying to every item below:** an
item is answerable from the propositions in the text. No item turns on a single
low-frequency word; no item can be answered from world knowledge without
reading; no item's key differs from its distractors only by a synonym the
learner has not met. A reading item that is really a vocabulary trap is a broken
item and is rejected at review, not patched.

### B1 — Text 1: a personal message (5 marks, **A2-core**)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | 90–110 words. A message from a friend describing a recent change — a move, a new job, a new routine. Register informal, `tu`. Language: presente, riflessivi, passato prossimo, past-time expressions. Lexis drawn from [Set 1](../curriculum/syllabus/lexis-progression.md#unit-1--routine-carattere-rapporti) and [Set 2](../curriculum/syllabus/lexis-progression.md#unit-2--eventi-e-svolte) only. |
| **Task type** | 5 × three-option multiple choice, in text order. |
| **Items** | 1 gist (why the writer is writing) · 2 detail (what happened, when) · 1 inference from stated fact (how the writer feels about it, signalled by an evaluation item from Set 2 subgroup C) · 1 reference (what a pronoun in the text refers to). |
| **Distractor rule** | Each wrong option must be true-sounding and contradicted by exactly one identifiable clause in the text. No "not mentioned" distractors at this level. |

### B2 — Text 2: a rental advert plus a short reply (5 marks, **B1-probe**)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | Advert 70–90 words in advert register; reply 50–60 words asking practical questions. #### B1 — Text 1: *Un messaggio da Chiara*

**Level: A2.** These choices pitch it there:
- **Tenses:** presente, riflessivi and passato prossimo only. There is no
  imperfetto and no object pronoun.
- **Time:** three explicit time anchors (`l'anno scorso`, `il mese scorso`,
  `due settimane fa`), each tied to a different event.
- **Sentences:** short. The only subordinate clauses are `scusa se…` and
  `ti ricordi che…`. Everything else is joined with `e`, `così`, `però` and a
  colon.
- **Lexis:** topic words are from Sets 1–2 (`licenziarsi`, `trasferirsi`,
  `il colloquio`, `alzarsi`, `in gamba`, `socievole`, `soddisfatto`,
  `valere la pena`). Everything else is A1–A2 core.

**Register:** informal written message, `tu`. Chiara writes to Sara, a friend she
has not contacted for a month. That is why it opens with an apology (`Scusa se
non mi sono fatta sentire`). **Length:** 109 words in the body.

> Ciao Sara!
>
> Scusa se non mi sono fatta sentire, ma è stato un mese pieno di novità. Ti
> ricordi che l'anno scorso mi sono licenziata dal negozio? Ecco: il mese scorso
> ho fatto un colloquio in un'agenzia di viaggi a Bologna, ed è andato bene!
> Così mi sono trasferita qui, e due settimane fa ho cominciato a lavorare. Il
> lavoro è faticoso: mi alzo alle sei e torno a casa tardi. Però sono
> soddisfatta: vale proprio la pena. La mia responsabile, Marta, è in gamba.
> Abito con Giulia, una ragazza di Lecce molto socievole. Con lei esco quasi
> tutte le sere, così conosco un sacco di gente. Quando vieni a trovarmi?
>
> Un abbraccio,
> Chiara

**Items** (in text order; choose A, B or C):

1. *Why is Chiara writing to Sara?*
   A. To tell Sara she has moved to Bologna for a new job.
   B. To ask Sara to help her find a job in Bologna.
   C. To tell Sara she is going to move to Bologna soon.
2. *What happened last year?*
   A. Chiara moved to Bologna for work.
   B. Chiara had an interview at a travel agency.
   C. Chiara left her job at the shop.
3. *When did Chiara start her new job?*
   A. Two weeks ago. B. A month ago. C. A year ago.
4. *How does Chiara feel about the change?*
   A. She likes Bologna, but the new job has disappointed her.
   B. The work is hard, but she's glad she moved.
   C. She likes the job, but she feels lonely in Bologna.
5. *"Con lei esco quasi tutte le sere." Who is* lei*?*
   A. Marta, her manager. B. Sara, her friend. C. Giulia, her flatmate.

| Item | Key | Function | The clause that contradicts each distractor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **A** | gist | B: `due settimane fa ho cominciato a lavorare` (she already has the job). C: `mi sono trasferita qui` (the move has already happened). |
| 2 | **C** | detail: what | A: the move comes after the interview `il mese scorso` (`Così mi sono trasferita`). B: the interview was `il mese scorso`. |
| 3 | **A** | detail: when | B: `il mese scorso` was the interview. C: `l'anno scorso` was when she resigned. |
| 4 | **B** | inference from `soddisfatta` / `vale proprio la pena` (Set 2 C), set against `faticoso` | A: `sono soddisfatta`. C: `conosco un sacco di gente`. |
| 5 | **C** | reference | A: Marta is mentioned in the sentence before Giulia; `lei` follows `Abito con Giulia…`. B: Sara is the addressee (`tu`), so she cannot be `lei`. |

**ICE notes.**
- **Item 2 says "left her job".** It does not say "resigned" or "was fired".
  `licenziarsi` (resign) and `essere licenziato` (be fired) are a known
  learner trap. A key that turned on that distinction would be a vocabulary
  item, not a time-anchoring item.
- **Item 5 uses a stressed subject pronoun (`lei`).** It deliberately avoids
  `lo/la`. Direct object pronouns are introduced at `U6 L2`, and an A2-core
  item must not depend on them.
- **"Sets 1–2 only" is read as *topic* lexis.** No 100-word text can be built
  from sixty items plus nothing else. The extra words (`negozio`, `agenzia di
  viaggi`, `faticoso`, `un sacco di gente`) are all A2 high-frequency.

#### B2 — Text 2: *Un annuncio e una risposta*

**Level: A2+ / B1 lower** (U3 payload).

**Grading choices:**
- **Relatives:** two relative clauses with `cui` after a preposition,
  `a cui` and `in cui`, inside the U3 cap.
- **Comparison:** one double comparison (`più piccola e meno luminosa del
  soggiorno, ma più tranquilla`).
- **`ci` locativo:** `alla stazione ci si arriva a piedi`. It is a dislocation
  with a resumptive `ci`, plus `si` impersonale (U4).
- **`si` passivante:** `si possono lasciare`. It is input only, and no item
  turns on it (it is a U7 structure).
- **Lexis:** Set 3 (`palazzo`, `ascensore`, `balcone`, `cortile`,
  `riscaldamento autonomo`, `spese condominiali`, `bollette`, `cauzione`,
  `arredato`, `luminoso`, `tranquillo`, `ben collegato`).

**Register:**
- **The advert** is a private landlord's listing on a property portal. It is
  telegraphic in the facts (`Cucina abitabile, riscaldamento autonomo`) and
  discursive where it describes. That is how such adverts are actually written.
- **The reply** uses `Lei` (`il Suo annuncio`). Anna is writing to a landlord
  she does not know.

**Lengths:** advert 88 words, reply 57 words (body).

**Cultural currency:**
- 780 € for a furnished bilocale in San Salvario is in line with Turin in
  2026.
- A deposit of two months' rent is standard; the legal maximum is three.

> centro e ci vado sempre in bici. Vorrei vedere l'appartamento questa
> settimana, se possibile.
>
> Grazie e buona giornata,
> Anna Ferri

**Items.** Items 1–3 are multiple choice (A, B or C). Items 4–5 are answered in
English, in one to four words.

1. *Which room has a balcony?*
   A. The bedroom has a small balcony.
   B. The living room has a small balcony.
   C. The eat-in kitchen has a small balcony.
2. *What does the advert present as the bedroom's advantage?*
   A. It is bigger than the living room.
   B. It is brighter than the living room.
   C. It is quieter than the living room.
3. *What does the 780 € a month include?*
   A. The rent and the building's service charges.
   B. The rent and the gas and electricity bills.
   C. The rent and the deposit.
4. *What does Anna want to know about the courtyard?*
5. *Anna asks about the heating. What information is missing from the advert?*

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **B** | — | **A** is the keyword trap: `La camera da letto` opens the sentence, and `balcone` closes it. `in cui` attaches to `soggiorno`, the nearest antecedent. ## Part C — Language in use (10 minutes, 12 marks)

**Task type:** two short connected texts with numbered gaps — **contextual
cloze**, one to three words per gap. **Verb gaps show the infinitive in
brackets; other gaps are bare. No options are supplied.** Not discrete
sentences. (The infinitive cue was ratified by ITA-23. Without it, a verb gap can
be filled with a different verb and the gap stops testing its form.)

**Why cloze and not multiple choice on isolated sentences.** A discrete-point
grammar item tells you whether a learner can pick a form when the target is
flagged. This course's whole claim is that learners *use* structures. A gap
inside a connected text requires the learner to read the co-text to choose the
form, which is nearer the real construct — and it makes avoidance impossible,
which multiple choice does not.

### C1 — A2-core cloze (7 marks, **A2-core**)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | 100–120 words, first-person, "how my week goes and what I did last weekend" — a text whose topic forces the present/past alternation. |
| **Gaps** | 1 present-tense irregular from the U1 list · 1 reflexive in the present · 1 passato prossimo with `avere` · 1 passato prossimo with `essere` **plus agreement** (the gap covers auxiliary and participle together, and both must be right for the mark) · 1 imperfetto (descriptive use) · 1 past-time expression from [Set 2 subgroup A](../curriculum/syllabus/lexis-progression.md#a-time-anchors-10) · 1 preposizione articolata. |
| **Marking** | 1 mark each, all-or-nothing. Spelling and accents not marked ([`rubrics.md` §Spelling](rubrics.md#spelling-accents-and-tolerance)). Every gap has a stated list of acceptable alternatives — a gap with one permitted answer in a free cloze is a guessing item. |

### C2 — B1-probe cloze (5 marks, **B1-probe**)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | 100–120 words, a short anecdote about a shopping trip that went wrong — a topic that makes all five targets natural rather than decorative. |
| **Gaps** | 1 `che` or `cui` relative (the co-text decides which; both must not be possible) · 1 direct object pronoun replacing a noun named in the previous clause · 1 `ne` partitivo with a quantity · 1 imperfetto-vs-passato-prossimo choice at a genuine background/foreground boundary · 1 `ci` locativo (the gap sits in the clitic slot, so no non-clitic answer fits). |
| **Marking** | 1 mark each, all-or-nothing. Where the target is agreement with a direct object pronoun (`l'ho comprata`), the agreement is part of the key. No gap's key turns on participle agreement after partitive `ne`, which is optional in current usage. |

**Learner instruction (English):** *Write one to three words in each gap. Where

**Marking rules for both texts:**
- Each gap is worth 1 mark and is all-or-nothing.

#### C1 — *La mia settimana* (A2-core)

**Level: A2.** These choices pitch it there:
- **Narrator:** first-person and male (`io e Marco`). No gap depends on the
  narrator's gender.
- **Structure:** the first paragraph is in the habitual present, the second in
  a past narrative. That alternation is the spec's point.
- **Agreement gap:** subject `io e Marco` gives masculine plural, so the gap
  has one answer whatever the narrator's gender.
- **Lexis:** A2 core plus Set 1 (`svegliarsi`) and Set 2 (`trasferirsi`,
  `[quantity] + fa`).
- **Leaks:** the co-text avoids models for the gaps. No `siamo + participle`
  form appears outside gap 4, and gap 5's verb appears nowhere else.
- **Length:** 110 words.
- **Cultural note:** Bergamo's Città Alta is reached on foot or by funicular
  from the lower town. The walk is the ordinary weekend thing to do.

> Durante la settimana ho una vita abbastanza tranquilla. Lavoro in un ufficio
> in centro e la sera esco poco. Il giovedì, di solito, mia sorella
> **(1)** __________ *(venire)* a cena da me. La mattina **(2)** __________
> *(svegliarsi)* presto, verso le sette, perché vado al lavoro a piedi.
>
> Il fine settimana scorso, invece, niente routine. Sabato mattina io e Marco
> **(3)** __________ *(prendere)* il treno per Bergamo. Nel pomeriggio
> **(4)** __________ *(andare)* a piedi fino in Città Alta: che vista! Domenica,
> però, niente passeggiate: **(5)** __________ *(essere)* troppo stanchi.
> Abbiamo dormito fino a tardi a casa di Luca, un amico di Marco che si è
> trasferito a Bergamo due anni **(6)** __________. Luca abita
> **(7)** __________ quinto piano, senza ascensore!

| Gap | Target (spec) | Key | Also accept | Reject, and why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

- **`Perché↘?`** falls. Wh-questions fall in Italian; anglophone readers tend to

Apply the **FD cap**: if any silence on the recording is longer than five seconds, FD is at most 2. **Do not self-score GA or FD** — they go to a partner ([`rubrics.md` §Marker calibration](rubrics.md#marker-calibration)); until then the result is provisional, as for Part E. |

### Anchors — Italian (ITA-15)

These are transcripts of the recordings a marker should hear at each band. They
are not models for learners. Pauses are shown as `[pausa, n s]`, and `…` marks a
hesitation of under two seconds.

#### Band 5 — TA 5, GA 5, FD 5 → **15 / 15** (≈ 250 words, ≈ 2:00)

> Allora… una settimana normale. Di solito mi sveglio verso le sette, faccio
> colazione in fretta e vado al lavoro in bici: lavoro in una biblioteca, quindi
> le mie giornate sono abbastanza tranquille. Il martedì e il giovedì, dopo il
> lavoro, ho lezione di italiano, e il venerdì sera esco con i colleghi, di
> solito in un pub vicino all'ufficio. Il fine settimana mi riposo, oppure vado
> a trovare i miei genitori, che abitano in campagna.
>
> Una cosa che mi è successa quest'anno… beh, a marzo ho fatto il mio primo
> viaggio in Italia da sola. Sono stata una settimana a Bologna. All'inizio ero
> un po' nervosa, perché quando la gente parlava veloce non capivo quasi niente,
> però dopo due o tre giorni ho cominciato a rilassarmi. Una sera, mentre
> cercavo un ristorante, ho conosciuto una signora molto gentile che mi ha
> consigliato una trattoria fantastica. Ci sentiamo ancora!
>
> E dove vivo… abito in un appartamento al secondo piano, in un quartiere
> residenziale. È molto più tranquillo del centro, e c'è un parco in cui vado a
> correre quasi tutte le mattine. Il problema è che è un po' lontano da tutto:
> il centro è a quaranta minuti di autobus. Quindi vorrei una fermata più
> vicina… e mi piacerebbe tanto avere un balcone, perché l'appartamento è un
> po' buio.

**Why it scores 5:**
- **TA:** all three prompts, each developed.
- **Prompt 2:** a real PP/imperfetto narrative (`ero nervosa… ho cominciato`,
  `mentre cercavo… ho conosciuto`).
- **Prompt 3:** a comparative, a `cui` relative, and the change expressed with
  the seeded chunks `vorrei` and `mi piacerebbe`.
- **FD:** the pauses fall at clause boundaries (`Allora…`, `beh`), and list
  intonation is used on the routine.

#### Band 3 — TA 3, GA 3, FD 3 → **9 / 15** (≈ 170 words, ≈ 1:45; contains deliberate errors)

> Ehm… allora, la mia settimana. Io… mi sveglio alle sette e… e vado al lavoro
> con il treno. Lavoro in un ufficio, ehm, tutto il giorno. La sera cucino e
> guardo la TV. Il sabato vado al supermercato e… la domenica **io riposo**.
>
> Ehm… quest'anno… [pausa, 4 s] quest'anno ho cambiato lavoro. Prima lavoravo in
> una banca, ma… non mi piaceva. A settembre ho cominciato un nuovo lavoro in
> una scuola. Il primo giorno ero molto nervoso e… e **ho arrivato** tardi,
> perché il treno era in ritardo. Ma i colleghi sono molto **gentile**, e
> adesso… adesso sto bene.
>
> Ehm… la mia casa. Abito in una casa piccola, **vicino il centro**. È più
> tranquilla **che** il mio vecchio appartamento, ehm… perché non c'è traffico.
> Mi piace, ma… è un po' vecchia. Vorrei cambiare la cucina, perché è troppo
> piccola e… e non c'è la lavastoviglie. Sì. Basta, credo.

**TA 3:**
- All three prompts are addressed.
- Prompt 1 is a list of times and places.
- Prompt 3's "what would you change" gets one item.
- The length is within 20% of two minutes.

**GA 3:** five errors (bold): reflexive (`mi riposo`), auxiliary
(`sono arrivato`), agreement (`gentili`), preposition (`vicino al`) and
comparative (`del`). All are expected anglophone errors, and the message is
never lost.

**FD 3:**
- Audible planning and restarts (`e… e`, `adesso… adesso`).
- The longest silence is 4 seconds, so the five-second FD cap does not apply.
- Stress is right on `lavoravo`, `nervoso` and `ritardo`.

**Placement effect:** this sample meets the Band 2 speaking floor. It misses
Band 3 (TA ≥ 4 and FD ≥ 3).

**ICE note (for the Architect): prompt (3) invites the conditional.** The
wording "say what you would change about it" calls for the condizionale
(`cambierei`), which is introduced at `U11 L2`. Both anchors use the seeded
chunks `vorrei` and `mi piacerebbe` (Seed 3, from `U3 L1` / `U4 L1`) instead.
Proposed marker note for the Part F table: "Element (3) is fully met by
`vorrei…` / `mi piacerebbe…` or by any present-tense wish (`voglio cambiare`,
`mi serve`). A productive conditional is not expected, and its absence is not
penalised on GA."

**Architect ruling (ITA-23): accepted.** The marker note is added to the Part F
table. Prompt (3) is reworded to "what you would like to change", which points
to `vorrei` / `mi piacerebbe` and not to the U11 conditional. Both anchors already
meet it, so they stand unchanged.

## Scoring and bands

### Mark allocation

| Part | Marks | A2-core | B1-probe | Productive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — Self-assessment | 0 | — | — | — |
| B — Reading | 10 | 5 | 5 | — |
| C — Language in use | 12 | 7 | 5 | — |
| D — Listening | 8 | 4 | 4 | — |
| E — Writing | 15 | — | — | 15 |
| F — Speaking | 15 | — | — | 15 |
| **Total** | **60** | **16** | **14** | **30** |

Receptive-plus-language is 30 marks and productive is 30. That is a
**washback** decision, stated so it can be argued with: a placement check
weighted toward comprehension would tell learners that this is a comprehension
course, before lesson one.

### The bands

Read the conditions in order. The first band whose conditions are met is the
result.

#### Band 1 — Not yet ready

Awarded if **any** of these holds:

- A2-core subtotal ≤ 7 / 12 at the Stage 1 stop rule, **or** ≤ 10 / 16 overall;
- total < 30 / 60;
- writing TA ≤ 2 **and** speaking TA ≤ 2.

#### Band 2 — Start at Unit 1

Awarded if **all** of these hold:

- total 30–47 / 60;
- A2-core ≥ 11 / 16;
- writing TA ≥ 3 **or** speaking TA ≥ 3.

#### Band 3 — Start at Unit 7

Awarded if **all** of these hold:

- total ≥ 48 / 60;
- **B1-probe ≥ 11 / 14**;
- writing TA ≥ 4 **and** writing GA ≥ 3;
- speaking TA ≥ 4 **and** speaking FD ≥ 3;
- Part D was **not** deferred.

Every one of those five conditions is load-bearing. The total alone would let a
strong A2 learner with excellent exam technique skip the six units that build
the narrative past. The B1-probe threshold is what makes the band mean "already
past Unit 6" rather than "good at tests".

### Deferred listening — renormalised thresholds

If Part D was not taken, score out of **52** (A2-core out of 12, B1-probe out of
10) and use:

| Band | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| Band 1 | A2-core ≤ 7 / 12, or total < 26 / 52, or both productive TA ≤ 2 |
| Band 2 | total 26–41 / 52, A2-core ≥ 8 / 12, one productive TA ≥ 3 |
| Band 3 | **not available** — report "Band 2, provisional; re-check Part D before considering an accelerated start" |

### Borderline zones — what to do instead of rounding

Two zones where the instrument is not precise enough to carry the decision on
its own. Saying so is more useful than a spuriously confident band.

| Zone | Score | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 / 2 boundary** | total 27–32, or A2-core exactly 8–10 | Report **Band 1 with a bridge**: the named pre-work below, then re-take **Parts B, C and E only** (33 minutes) after it. Re-take uses a parallel version — the same specs, different content. A learner who has seen the items is no longer being measured by them. |
| **2 / 3 boundary** | total 44–50 but any Band 3 condition unmet | Report **Band 2**. Start at Unit 1, and treat the Unit 1 and Unit 2 checkpoints as diagnostic: if both are passed at ≥ 22 / 25, an accelerated route through Units 1–2 is worth considering. **Whether the course permits an accelerated route at all is a course-shape decision and belongs to Chief Italian Linguist** — it is not decided here. See [§Open](#open-and-who-owns-it). |

| Band | Reported as |
| --- | --- |
| **Band 1** | "This course starts by consolidating A2 and moves to B1 quickly. Right now some of the A2 foundation isn't in place, so you'd be learning two things at once. Here is exactly what to secure first, and here is when to come back." Followed by the named list below — not a generic "revise A2". |

### Band 1 — the named pre-work

Generated from the learner's own wrong answers, not from a template. The report
lists whichever of these the check found missing, with the unit that will assume
it:

| Gap found | Evidence | Assumed by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present-tense high-frequency irregulars | C1 gap 1 | U1 L2 |
| Reflexive paradigm | C1 gap 2, Part F | U1 L2 |
| Passato prossimo auxiliary choice | C1 gaps 3–4 | U2 L2 |
| Participle agreement with `essere` | C1 gap 4 | U2 L2, and U6 depends on it |
| Imperfetto forms | C1 gap 5 | U4 L2 |
| Placing events in time | C1 gap 6, Part E element 2 | U2 L3 |
| Sustaining two minutes of speech | Part F, FD ≤ 2 | Every unit's L5 |

**Re-check interval:** not before four weeks of work on the named gaps. Sooner
than that and the re-check measures short-term recall.

**Honesty note.** Band 1 is not a soft "nearly". Format brief §3 states that if
learners are entering weaker than assumed, the fix is **more units, not longer
lessons**. A Band 1 learner admitted to Unit 1 anyway is the case that makes the
90-hour figure false, and the course has no A2 remediation content — providing
one is out of scope for v1 and is named as an open item below.

### Band 3 — the confirmation step

Band 3 skips 30 lessons. That is too large a decision to rest on a 46-minute
check, so it is confirmed rather than assumed:

1. Sit the **Unit 6 checkpoint** ([`unit-checkpoint-template.md`](unit-checkpoint-template.md)) as a dry run. Pass criterion as published: ≥ 18 / 25.
2. Sit **Milestone 1 Parts C, D and E** — language in use, writing, speaking ([`milestone-1.md` §Use as the placement confirmation step](milestone-1.md#use-as-the-placement-confirmation-step)), 64 marks, ~50 minutes, in its parallel version. Threshold: ≥ 60% (39 / 64) with no scale below 3, marked by a partner or teacher — a self-marked result cannot confirm Band 3.
3. If either is missed, enter at **Unit 4** instead, not Unit 1: the placement
   evidence has already ruled out Units 1–3 as anything but revision, and Unit 4
   is where the narrative past begins in earnest.

The outcome of the confirmation step is also the learner's first carry-forward
list — the gaps Units 7–12 `L4` slots will need to cover for them specifically.

### What goes back to the Chief

Decision 1 of the
[shape review](../docs/syllabus-shape-review.md#decision-1--twelve-units-or-fourteen-hold-twelve-escalated-for-ratification)
holds the course at twelve units on three conditions, one of which is that this
check ships before Unit 1 is taught and **its results come back to the Chief
Italian Linguist**, because it is the trigger for reopening that decision.

For every learner who takes it: the band, the total, the A2-core and B1-probe
subtotals, the four productive scale scores, whether Part D was deferred, and
the Part A self-assessment pattern — anonymised. The pattern worth acting on is
the format brief §3 trigger: **a high share of Band 1 and 1/2-borderline
results** among learners who believed themselves to be A2. That is evidence that
entry is weaker than the ~90-hour figure assumes, and the honest response is
more units. The same profile travels forward to
[Milestone 1](milestone-1.md#what-goes-back-to-the-chief) and
[Milestone 2](milestone-2.md#what-goes-back-to-the-chief), where it is compared
against.

## Construct-validity review checklist

Run before this instrument is used on a real learner. Each line has failed in
real placement tests and each failure changes who gets placed where.

- [ ] No reading or listening item is answerable from the question stem alone.
- [ ] No reading or listening item turns on one low-frequency word; every key is supported by a full proposition.
- [ ] No multiple-choice key is the longest option, and no key is the only grammatically smooth option.
- [ ] No cloze gap has a single acceptable answer unless the co-text genuinely forces it; every gap carries its alternatives list.
- [ ] No cloze gap can be filled correctly by a learner who has not read the surrounding sentences.
- [ ] Every productive task's required elements are listed in the prompt, in English.
- [ ] Nothing in Parts B–D requires production of Italian, and nothing in Parts E–F is marked for comprehension.
- [ ] The A2-core and B1-probe tags on every item have been checked against `grammar-progression.md` introduction points.
- [ ] Two parallel versions exist before the check is used, because borderline learners re-take it.
