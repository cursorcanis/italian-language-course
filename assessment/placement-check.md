## What this instrument is for, and what it decides

The format brief (§3) plans **90 guided hours** for A2→B1 against a published
norm of 150–200. That figure is only honest if entry is **solid** A2 rather than
weak A2. This check is the thing that makes that assumption falsifiable. It has
one job: put a learner into one of three entry points, and say so in a way that
is arguable rather than vibes.

| Band | Decision | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Band 1** | **Not yet ready** | The A2 spine this course re-enters is not in place. Starting at Unit 1 would mean learning A2 from a course that assumes it. |
| **Band 2** | **Start at Unit 1** | Solid A2. The course is built for this learner. |
| **Band 3** | **Start at Unit 7** | Units 1–6 would be revision. Entry after Milestone 1 coverage, with a confirmation step. |

**Band 3 is deliberately hard to reach**, and the reason is **construct
validity**: skipping six units on the strength of a reading score would be
inferring production from comprehension. A learner who recognises the
PP/imperfetto contrast but cannot narrate is exactly the learner Units 4–6 were
written for. So Band 3 requires productive evidence, not just a high total.

### Task type, duration, what it measures, pass criterion

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Task type** | Mixed-skill diagnostic: reading comprehension, contextual cloze, script-read listening, short guided writing, short guided monologue. Self-administered with a stated partner path. |
| **Duration** | **Stage 1: 23 minutes** (Parts A–C, paper only). **Stage 2: 23 minutes** (Parts D–F). Total **≈46 minutes** of learner time, one sitting or two. A stop rule ends it at Stage 1 for learners already placed in Band 1. |
| **What it measures** | Control of the A2 spine the course re-enters (present including high-frequency irregulars, riflessivi, passato prossimo with auxiliary choice and agreement, imperfetto, comparatives, past-time expressions) and, separately, whether Units 3–6 payload (relatives with `cui`, direct pronouns and `ne`, the PP/imperfetto contrast, `si` impersonale, `ci` locativo) is *already* productive. |
| **Pass criterion** | There is no pass. This is a **placement** instrument: every learner gets a band, and each band names an action. Band thresholds are in [§Scoring](#scoring-and-bands) and are hard. |

### Why not shorter

A 15-minute check can separate "beginner" from "not beginner". It cannot
support a decision to skip six units, because that decision needs a writing
sample and a speaking sample. The stop rule is the compromise: a learner who is
not ready finishes in 23 minutes and is told so; the 46 minutes are spent only
where a finer decision is actually being made.

## Delivery, and the audio problem

Recorded audio is out of scope for v1 (format brief §4), so Part D exists as a
**script** and has three delivery paths. This is stated here because it changes
how the result is reported, and an unreported change to an instrument is a
broken instrument.

| Path | How | Effect on the result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Recorded** (once audio exists) | Learner plays the file twice. | Full result. |
| **Partner-read** | A partner — teacher, language partner, any fluent reader — reads the script aloud twice at the marked pace, without showing it. | Full result. |
| **Deferred** (solo learner, no partner) | Part D is skipped. | **Provisional result.** Score out of 52, renormalised thresholds in [§Scoring](#scoring-and-bands). Band 3 **cannot** be awarded on a deferred result — the skip decision is not made without a listening sample. |

Part F (speaking) has no such problem: the learner records themselves on a phone
and either self-scores TA only or sends the recording to a partner. See
[`rubrics.md` §Marker calibration](rubrics.md#marker-calibration) for what a
learner may and may not self-score.

## Item tagging — the two subscores that do the work

Every receptive and language-in-use mark carries one tag. The total alone cannot
distinguish the two learners this check exists to tell apart, so the tags are
not optional bookkeeping.

| Tag | Marks | Tests | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A2-core** | 16 | The A2 spine the course assumes on day one | The Band 1 / Band 2 boundary |
| **B1-probe** | 14 | Units 3–6 payload, at recognition and production | The Band 2 / Band 3 boundary |

Productive marks (Parts E, F — 30 marks) are untagged and count toward both
boundaries through their own floors.

## Part A — Self-assessment (3 minutes, 0 marks)

**Task type:** learner-facing can-do checklist, four-point response
(`not yet` / `with difficulty` / `yes` / `easily`).

**What it measures:** nothing, by design. It is a **calibration** input and a
learner-orientation device, and it is explicitly **unscored** so that nobody is
placed on their own opinion of themselves.

**Content:** ten statements, lifted verbatim from the syllabus map so the
learner meets the course's own language immediately — four **(A2)** statements
from Units 1–2, three **(A2+)** from Units 3–4, three **(B1 lower)** from Units
5–6. Each is tagged with its unit so the learner can see where it is taught.

**How it is used:** compared against the scored result and reported back as one
of three notes.

| Pattern | Note reported to the learner |
| --- | --- |
| Self-assessment much higher than score | The most common pattern for anglophone learners with good passive Italian. Named explicitly, with the specific gap (usually production) identified, because a learner who thinks they are being under-placed will disengage. |
| Self-assessment much lower than score | Under-confidence. The learner is told the score stands and that the course's first two units are consolidation by design. |
| Aligned | Noted, and the band is reported without comment. |

> **ICE dependency:** none. Part A is in English (format brief §1 — metalanguage
> is English), quoting the can-do statements already drafted in the map.

## Part B — Reading (10 minutes, 10 marks)

Two texts, graded. **Construct validity note applying to every item below:** an
item is answerable from the propositions in the text. No item turns on a single
low-frequency word; no item can be answered from world knowledge without
reading; no item's key differs from its distractors only by a synonym the
learner has not met. A reading item that is really a vocabulary trap is a broken
item and is rejected at review, not patched.

### B1 — Text 1: a personal message (5 marks, **A2-core**)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | 90–110 words. A message from a friend describing a recent change — a move, a new job, a new routine. Register informal, `tu`. Language: presente, riflessivi, passato prossimo, past-time expressions. Lexis drawn from [Set 1](../curriculum/syllabus/lexis-progression.md#unit-1--routine-carattere-rapporti) and [Set 2](../curriculum/syllabus/lexis-progression.md#unit-2--eventi-e-svolte) only. |
| **Task type** | 5 × three-option multiple choice, in text order. |
| **Items** | 1 gist (why the writer is writing) · 2 detail (what happened, when) · 1 inference from stated fact (how the writer feels about it, signalled by an evaluation item from Set 2 subgroup C) · 1 reference (what a pronoun in the text refers to). |
| **Distractor rule** | Each wrong option must be true-sounding and contradicted by exactly one identifiable clause in the text. No "not mentioned" distractors at this level. |

### B2 — Text 2: a rental advert plus a short reply (5 marks, **B1-probe**)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | Advert 70–90 words in advert register; reply 50–60 words asking practical questions. Language: relative clauses including at least two with `cui` after a preposition, comparatives, `ci` locativo, `si` passivante in the advert's register. Lexis from [Set 3](../curriculum/syllabus/lexis-progression.md#unit-3--casa-palazzo-quartiere). |
| **Task type** | 3 × three-option multiple choice + 2 × short written answer in English (one to four words). |
| **Items** | 1 detail requiring a relative clause to be parsed correctly (what the thing described *is*, where the modifier is attached by `in cui` / `di cui`) · 1 comparison (which of two stated features is presented as the better) · 1 detail on costs · 2 short answers: what the writer of the reply wants to know, and what the advert does *not* say about it. |
| **Why English answers** | The short-answer items test comprehension. Requiring an Italian answer would confound comprehension with production and make the item unmarkable against its own construct. Production is Parts E and F. |
| **Construct note** | The relative-clause item is the discriminating item of this part. It must be impossible to answer by matching a keyword — the key and the strongest distractor must both contain the same content words and differ only in what the relative clause attaches to. |

> **ICE dependency:** both texts, all stems, all options, and the key.

## Part C — Language in use (10 minutes, 12 marks)

**Task type:** two short connected texts with numbered gaps — **contextual
cloze**, one to three words per gap, no options supplied. Not discrete
sentences.

**Why cloze and not multiple choice on isolated sentences.** A discrete-point
grammar item tells you whether a learner can pick a form when the target is
flagged. This course's whole claim is that learners *use* structures. A gap
inside a connected text requires the learner to read the co-text to choose the
form, which is nearer the real construct — and it makes avoidance impossible,
which multiple choice does not.

### C1 — A2-core cloze (7 marks, **A2-core**)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | 100–120 words, first-person, "how my week goes and what I did last weekend" — a text whose topic forces the present/past alternation. |
| **Gaps** | 1 present-tense irregular from the U1 list · 1 reflexive in the present · 1 passato prossimo with `avere` · 1 passato prossimo with `essere` **plus agreement** (the gap covers auxiliary and participle together, and both must be right for the mark) · 1 imperfetto (descriptive use) · 1 past-time expression from [Set 2 subgroup A](../curriculum/syllabus/lexis-progression.md#a-time-anchors-10) · 1 preposizione articolata. |
| **Marking** | 1 mark each, all-or-nothing. Spelling and accents not marked ([`rubrics.md` §Spelling](rubrics.md#spelling-accents-and-tolerance)). Every gap has a stated list of acceptable alternatives — a gap with one permitted answer in a free cloze is a guessing item. |

### C2 — B1-probe cloze (5 marks, **B1-probe**)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | 100–120 words, a short anecdote about a shopping trip that went wrong — a topic that makes all five targets natural rather than decorative. |
| **Gaps** | 1 `che` or `cui` relative (the co-text decides which; both must not be possible) · 1 direct object pronoun replacing a noun named in the previous clause · 1 `ne` partitivo with a quantity · 1 imperfetto-vs-passato-prossimo choice at a genuine background/foreground boundary · 1 `si` impersonale or `ci` locativo. |
| **Marking** | 1 mark each, all-or-nothing. Where the target is agreement (`l'ho comprata`, `ne ho presi due`), the agreement is part of the key. |

> **ICE dependency:** both cloze texts, the key, and the acceptable-alternative
> list per gap.

## ⛔ Stop rule — end of Stage 1

Score Parts B and C now. **22 marks available, of which 12 are A2-core.**

**If the A2-core subtotal is 7 or below (out of 12): stop. The result is Band 1.**

Parts D–F add 38 marks that cannot change this decision, and asking a learner
who cannot hold the A2 spine to produce a writing and a speaking sample buys a
demoralising 23 minutes and no information. Report Band 1 and go to
[§Band 1](#band-1--not-yet-ready).

Everyone else continues to Stage 2.

## Part D — Listening (8 minutes, 8 marks)

Script-read; see [§Delivery](#delivery-and-the-audio-problem). Each task is read
**twice**, at the pace marked in the script, with 20 seconds between readings.
The learner reads the questions before the first reading — withholding them
would test memory, not listening.

### D1 — A short two-person exchange (4 marks, **A2-core**)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | 45–60 seconds. Two speakers arranging to meet: times, a change of plan, a reason. Natural speech features permitted (false starts, `allora`, `senti`), speed at the slow end of natural. Lexis Sets 1–2. |
| **Task type** | 4 × note completion — a short table (who / when / where / why changed), one to three words each. |
| **Why note completion** | It is the most authentic listening construct at A2: this is what a learner actually has to do with a spoken arrangement. Multiple choice at this length would be answerable by elimination. |

### D2 — A short monologue (4 marks, **B1-probe**)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Spec** | 60–75 seconds. Someone telling a small mishap story with a clear background/foreground structure and at least four narrative connectives from [Set 5 subgroup A](../curriculum/syllabus/lexis-progression.md#a-narrative-signposts-and-connectives-10). |
| **Task type** | 2 × three-option multiple choice (what was going on when the problem happened; how it ended) + 2 × sequencing (put four stated events in the order they occurred). |
| **Construct note** | The sequencing items are the discriminating ones: they cannot be done without hearing the tense contrast, and they cannot be done from vocabulary alone. The four events must be presented in the script in an order that differs from their chronological order, or the item tests nothing. |

> **ICE dependency:** both scripts, marked for pace and speaker, plus items and
> key. Scripts must be recordable as written (format brief §4) and name their
> eventual asset path — `assets/audio/placement/placement-d1.mp3`,
> `placement-d2.mp3`.

## Part E — Writing (10 minutes, 15 marks)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Task type** | Short guided message, 80–100 words. One prompt, no choice. |
| **Prompt spec** | An email or message to an Italian-speaking acquaintance with four **required elements**, stated in the prompt in English: (1) say what you have been doing recently, (2) describe one change in your life and when it happened, (3) describe where you live now, (4) ask two questions back. |
| **What it measures** | Whether the A2 spine survives in free production — the present/past alternation, and enough connected writing to be more than a list. Elements (1)–(2) force passato prossimo and time expressions; (3) admits comparatives and relatives without requiring them; (4) tests question formation. |
| **Scales** | **TA ×2** (0–10) + **GA ×1** (0–5). **CC is not used** — 80–100 words is below the threshold in [`rubrics.md` §CC](rubrics.md#scale-cc--coherence-and-cohesion-05), and marking cohesion on five sentences rewards padding. **TS is not used** — no single structure is the target; the elements are. |
| **Pass criterion** | None (placement). **Floor:** TA ≥ 3 is required for Band 2 or above; TA ≥ 4 **and** GA ≥ 3 are required for Band 3. |
| **Self-marking** | The learner scores TA against the four required elements as a checklist. GA is left blank and marked later by a partner; until then the result is provisional in the same way a deferred Part D is. |

## Part F — Speaking (5 minutes, 15 marks)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Task type** | Recorded monologue, 2 minutes, from three prompts shown together. One minute of preparation is allowed and should be taken — this is a placement check, not a stress test. |
| **Prompt spec** | (1) Describe your normal week. (2) Tell me about one thing that happened to you in the last year. (3) Describe the place where you live and say what you would change about it. |
| **What it measures** | Sustained speech at A2/A2+: whether the learner can keep going for two minutes, and whether the past-tense system holds up without a page to plan on. Prompt (3) is the B1-probe: extended description is where relatives and comparatives either appear or are visibly missing. |
| **Scales** | **TA ×1** + **GA ×1** + **FD ×1** (0–15). |
| **Pass criterion** | None (placement). **Floor:** TA ≥ 3 for Band 2 or above; TA ≥ 4 **and** FD ≥ 3 for Band 3. |
| **Solo path** | Record on a phone. Self-score TA and FD against the stated descriptors (did I cover all three prompts; was there a silence longer than five seconds). **Do not self-score GA** — see [`rubrics.md`](rubrics.md#marker-calibration). |

## Scoring and bands

### Mark allocation

| Part | Marks | A2-core | B1-probe | Productive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — Self-assessment | 0 | — | — | — |
| B — Reading | 10 | 5 | 5 | — |
| C — Language in use | 12 | 7 | 5 | — |
| D — Listening | 8 | 4 | 4 | — |
| E — Writing | 15 | — | — | 15 |
| F — Speaking | 15 | — | — | 15 |
| **Total** | **60** | **16** | **14** | **30** |

Receptive-plus-language is 30 marks and productive is 30. That is a
**washback** decision, stated so it can be argued with: a placement check
weighted toward comprehension would tell learners that this is a comprehension
course, before lesson one.

### The bands

Read the conditions in order. The first band whose conditions are met is the
result.

#### Band 1 — Not yet ready

Awarded if **any** of these holds:

- A2-core subtotal ≤ 7 / 12 at the Stage 1 stop rule, **or** ≤ 10 / 16 overall;
- total < 30 / 60;
- writing TA ≤ 2 **and** speaking TA ≤ 2.

#### Band 2 — Start at Unit 1

Awarded if **all** of these hold:

- total 30–47 / 60;
- A2-core ≥ 11 / 16;
- writing TA ≥ 3 **or** speaking TA ≥ 3.

#### Band 3 — Start at Unit 7

Awarded if **all** of these hold:

- total ≥ 48 / 60;
- **B1-probe ≥ 11 / 14**;
- writing TA ≥ 4 **and** writing GA ≥ 3;
- speaking TA ≥ 4 **and** speaking FD ≥ 3;
- Part D was **not** deferred.

Every one of those five conditions is load-bearing. The total alone would let a
strong A2 learner with excellent exam technique skip the six units that build
the narrative past. The B1-probe threshold is what makes the band mean "already
past Unit 6" rather than "good at tests".

### Deferred listening — renormalised thresholds

If Part D was not taken, score out of **52** (A2-core out of 12, B1-probe out of
10) and use:

| Band | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| Band 1 | A2-core ≤ 7 / 12, or total < 26 / 52, or both productive TA ≤ 2 |
| Band 2 | total 26–41 / 52, A2-core ≥ 8 / 12, one productive TA ≥ 3 |
| Band 3 | **not available** — report "Band 2, provisional; re-check Part D before considering an accelerated start" |

### Borderline zones — what to do instead of rounding

Two zones where the instrument is not precise enough to carry the decision on
its own. Saying so is more useful than a spuriously confident band.

| Zone | Score | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 / 2 boundary** | total 27–32, or A2-core exactly 8–10 | Report **Band 1 with a bridge**: the named pre-work below, then re-take **Parts B, C and E only** (33 minutes) after it. Re-take uses a parallel version — the same specs, different content. A learner who has seen the items is no longer being measured by them. |
| **2 / 3 boundary** | total 44–50 but any Band 3 condition unmet | Report **Band 2**. Start at Unit 1, and treat the Unit 1 and Unit 2 checkpoints as diagnostic: if both are passed at ≥ 22 / 25, an accelerated route through Units 1–2 is worth considering. **Whether the course permits an accelerated route at all is a course-shape decision and belongs to Chief Italian Linguist** — it is not decided here. See [§Open](#open-and-who-owns-it). |

### What each band means, in words a learner reads

| Band | Reported as |
| --- | --- |
| **Band 1** | "This course starts by consolidating A2 and moves to B1 quickly. Right now some of the A2 foundation isn't in place, so you'd be learning two things at once. Here is exactly what to secure first, and here is when to come back." Followed by the named list below — not a generic "revise A2". |
| **Band 2** | "Start at Unit 1. The first two units are consolidation by design, so the early going will feel familiar — that is the course working, not you being under-placed." |
| **Band 3** | "Units 1–6 would largely be revision for you. Start at Unit 7, after the confirmation step below." |

### Band 1 — the named pre-work

Generated from the learner's own wrong answers, not from a template. The report
lists whichever of these the check found missing, with the unit that will assume
it:

| Gap found | Evidence | Assumed by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present-tense high-frequency irregulars | C1 gap 1 | U1 L2 |
| Reflexive paradigm | C1 gap 2, Part F | U1 L2 |
| Passato prossimo auxiliary choice | C1 gaps 3–4 | U2 L2 |
| Participle agreement with `essere` | C1 gap 4 | U2 L2, and U6 depends on it |
| Imperfetto forms | C1 gap 5 | U4 L2 |
| Placing events in time | C1 gap 6, Part E element 2 | U2 L3 |
| Sustaining two minutes of speech | Part F, FD ≤ 2 | Every unit's L5 |

**Re-check interval:** not before four weeks of work on the named gaps. Sooner
than that and the re-check measures short-term recall.

**Honesty note.** Band 1 is not a soft "nearly". Format brief §3 states that if
learners are entering weaker than assumed, the fix is **more units, not longer
lessons**. A Band 1 learner admitted to Unit 1 anyway is the case that makes the
90-hour figure false, and the course has no A2 remediation content — providing
one is out of scope for v1 and is named as an open item below.

### Band 3 — the confirmation step

Band 3 skips 30 lessons. That is too large a decision to rest on a 46-minute
check, so it is confirmed rather than assumed:

1. Sit the **Unit 6 checkpoint** ([`unit-checkpoint-template.md`](unit-checkpoint-template.md)) as a dry run. Pass criterion as published: ≥ 18 / 25.
2. Sit **Milestone 1 Parts C, D and E** — language in use, writing, speaking ([`milestone-1.md`](milestone-1.md)), 64 marks, ~50 minutes. Threshold: ≥ 60% with no scale below 3.
3. If either is missed, enter at **Unit 4** instead, not Unit 1: the placement
   evidence has already ruled out Units 1–3 as anything but revision, and Unit 4
   is where the narrative past begins in earnest.

The outcome of the confirmation step is also the learner's first carry-forward
list — the gaps Units 7–12 `L4` slots will need to cover for them specifically.

## Construct-validity review checklist

Run before this instrument is used on a real learner. Each line has failed in
real placement tests and each failure changes who gets placed where.

- [ ] No reading or listening item is answerable from the question stem alone.
- [ ] No reading or listening item turns on one low-frequency word; every key is supported by a full proposition.
- [ ] No multiple-choice key is the longest option, and no key is the only grammatically smooth option.
- [ ] No cloze gap has a single acceptable answer unless the co-text genuinely forces it; every gap carries its alternatives list.
- [ ] No cloze gap can be filled correctly by a learner who has not read the surrounding sentences.
- [ ] Every productive task's required elements are listed in the prompt, in English.
- [ ] Nothing in Parts B–D requires production of Italian, and nothing in Parts E–F is marked for comprehension.
- [ ] The A2-core and B1-probe tags on every item have been checked against `grammar-progression.md` introduction points.
- [ ] Two parallel versions exist before the check is used, because borderline learners re-take it.
