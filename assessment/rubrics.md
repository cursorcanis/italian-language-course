This file exists so that the four instruments do not each invent their own
scales. Four documents with four near-identical rubrics drift within two
revisions, and a learner's "3" then means something different in the placement
check than at Milestone 2. There is one set of scales, defined here, and the
instruments say which ones they use and how they weight them.

---

## How to read this document

### The five scales

| Scale | Code | Used for | Range |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Task achievement | **TA** | Did the learner do what the task asked? | 0–5 |
| Range and control of the target structure | **TS** | The structure the task was built to elicit | 0–5 |
| General accuracy | **GA** | Everything else — agreement, tense, word order | 0–5 |
| Coherence and cohesion | **CC** | Does it hold together as a text or a turn? | 0–5 |
| Fluency and delivery *(spoken only)* | **FD** | Pace, stalling, pronunciation that carries meaning | 0–5 |

No instrument uses all five on every task. Each names its scales and its
weights. **TA is compulsory on every productive task** — a piece of writing that
does not answer the question cannot pass on accuracy.

### The band meanings are fixed

A **3** is the pass band on every scale: *the learner did the thing, with
errors that do not obscure the message*. This is deliberate and it is a
**CEFR alignment** decision — B1 is defined by successful communication under
strain, not by accuracy. A rubric where 3 means "some weaknesses" and 4 means
"good" invites markers to treat 4 as the real pass and quietly raises the exit
level to B2.

### Two rules that protect construct validity

1. **Do not double-count.** If the task's target structure is the PP/imperfetto
   contrast, contrast errors are marked on **TS** and are *not* also marked on
   **GA**. GA covers the rest of the language. Marking one error twice turns a
   grammar scale into a grammar multiplier.
2. **Mark what the task asked for.** A beautifully written answer to a different
   question scores 1 on TA. A learner who answered the question in rough
   Italian scores 3–4 on TA and low on GA. Those are different learners and the
   profile has to show it.

## Scale TA — Task achievement (0–5)

Applies to every productive task.

| Band | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| **5** | Every element of the task is addressed, with the right amount of detail. Register and audience are right throughout. Length is within range. A reader or listener gets what they needed with nothing missing and nothing padded. |
| **4** | All elements addressed; one is thin, or the register slips once. Length within range. |
| **3** | **Pass.** All the *required* elements are addressed, or all but one minor one. The reader gets what they needed even if they have to work slightly for it. Register is broadly right, with one or two lapses. Length within 20% of range. |
| **2** | One required element missing or misunderstood, or register wrong for the situation (e.g. `tu` throughout a formal complaint), or length well short. |
| **1** | Answers a related but different question; or two or more required elements missing; or so short it cannot be judged. |
| **0** | No response, or no relationship to the task. |

**Marking note.** "Required elements" are listed explicitly in every task spec
in the four instruments. If a task's elements are not listed, the task is not
finished and cannot be marked reliably.

---

## Scale TS — Range and control of the target structure (0–5)

Applies where a task was built to elicit a named structure. The instrument says
which structure, and the task spec states the **elicitation floor** — the
minimum number of attempts a passing answer needs. Below that floor the learner
cannot score above 2, however accurate the rest is: **avoidance is a result,
not a neutral outcome.**

| Band | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| **5** | Used more often than the floor, in more than one of its taught functions, and essentially correct. Choices look deliberate. |
| **4** | Meets the floor comfortably; correct with at most one slip that the learner would likely self-correct. |
| **3** | **Pass.** Meets the floor. The majority of attempts are correct, and where they are wrong the intended meaning is still recoverable. |
| **2** | Below the floor, **or** at the floor with the majority of attempts wrong. Includes systematic avoidance — repeating full noun phrases where a pronoun was the point, or staying in the present through a past-narrative task. |
| **1** | One attempt, or attempts so malformed that the structure is not recognisable. |
| **0** | Structure absent. |

**Why avoidance is penalised.** **Washback.** A learner who discovers that a
task can be passed by avoiding the structure it was built for will avoid it
every time, and the course will have taught avoidance. This is also why the
elicitation floor belongs in the task spec and not in the marker's judgement.

## Scale GA — General accuracy (0–5)

Everything the task was *not* built to elicit. The question is always
**intelligibility under load**, not error count.

| Band | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| **5** | Errors are rare and are the kind a fluent speaker makes. Nothing has to be re-read. |
| **4** | Errors are noticeable but confined to low-level agreement, prepositions and article choice. Never obscures meaning. |
| **3** | **Pass.** Regular errors, including in structures taught earlier, but the message survives. A sympathetic reader never has to guess. |
| **2** | The reader has to re-read or guess at least twice. Errors reach into basic forms — subject–verb agreement, auxiliary choice, adjective agreement in short phrases. |
| **1** | Meaning is frequently lost. L1 word order and English calques dominate. |
| **0** | Not assessable as Italian. |

**Anglophone-specific marking note** (format brief §1: L1 is English). These
error types are *expected* at B1 and are marked at band 3, not below, unless
they obscure meaning: auxiliary choice with `essere`/`avere`; participle
agreement; adjective agreement at distance; `piacere`-family subject inversion;
preposition choice. These are *not* marked leniently when they are the task's
**TS** target — there, they are the thing being measured.

**False friends** (see
[`lexis-progression.md` §The false-friend thread](../curriculum/syllabus/lexis-progression.md#the-false-friend-thread))
are marked as lexical errors on GA, not as comprehension failures — unless the
item was written to test the pair, in which case it is a TS item.

## Scale CC — Coherence and cohesion (0–5)

Used on all extended writing (120 words or more) and on all spoken tasks of two
minutes or more. Not used on short-turn checkpoint tasks: there is not enough
text to judge, and marking it there would just reward padding.

| Band | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| **5** | A clear shape the reader can feel — opening, development, close. Connectives are varied and load-bearing. Reference back (pronouns, `ne`, `ci`) is unambiguous. |
| **4** | Clear shape; connectives correct but repetitive, or one referent momentarily unclear. |
| **3** | **Pass.** Recognisable as a connected text, not a list. At least a handful of different connectives, used correctly. Order is followable. |
| **2** | Reads as a list of sentences on the same topic. Connectives are limited to `e`, `ma`, `poi` or are used wrongly. |
| **1** | No discernible organisation; the reader has to reconstruct the order. |
| **0** | Not applicable / no text. |

**Why this scale exists at all.** At A2, accuracy is the whole game. At B1 it
stops being: a learner can produce accurate sentences and still fail to tell a
story or make an argument. **CEFR alignment** — the B1 descriptors are about
connected discourse. This is the scale that makes the course teach connectives
as a thread (map §3) rather than as decoration.

## Scale FD — Fluency and delivery (0–5, spoken only)

| Band | Descriptor |
| --- | --- |
| **5** | Speech runs at a natural pace. Pauses fall at clause boundaries. Intonation carries the meaning the words carry. |
| **4** | Mostly even pace with occasional reformulation. Isolated mispronunciations that never cause confusion. |
| **3** | **Pass.** Audible effort — pauses to plan, some restarts — but the listener stays with it. No silence longer than about five seconds. Stress and double consonants are right often enough not to distract. |
| **2** | Frequent long pauses or restarts; the listener has to work to stay engaged. Systematic errors in word stress or double consonants that force re-listening. |
| **1** | Halting to the point of breaking down; or so heavily L1-accented in stress and rhythm that comprehension fails. |
| **0** | No usable speech sample. |

**Marking note.** FD marks *delivery*, not accent. An audibly anglophone accent
with correct stress placement and length contrasts scores 4–5. The things that
cost marks are the ones the course teaches as meaning-bearing: word stress (U1),
double consonants (U2), stress under clitic attachment (U7), and request /
narrative contour (U5, U11).

## Aggregation and the no-compensation rule

Every instrument that uses these scales states:

1. Which scales apply to each task.
2. The **weight** of each (a multiplier onto the 0–5 score).
3. A **no-compensation floor**: the scale scores that cannot be traded away by a
   high total.

The floors are not decoration. Without them, a learner can pass Milestone 2 on
receptive sections while producing nothing — which would make "reached B1" a
claim about reading comprehension. Each instrument's floor is stated with its
pass criterion.

**Standing floor across all four instruments:** **TA ≥ 3 on every productive
task.** A task not attempted, or answered off-brief, is not compensable.

## Marker calibration

Every instrument is designed to be markable by (a) the learner alone, (b) a
teacher, or (c) a teacher who did not teach the course (format brief §2 —
self-study-first). That imposes three requirements, and they are binding on
every assessment document in this folder:

- **Every receptive item has a single unambiguous key**, plus a stated list of
  acceptable variants where a short answer is open.
- **Every productive task carries two anchor scripts**: one worked answer at
  band 3 (bare pass) and one at band 5. A rubric without anchors is marked
  differently by every marker who reads it. Anchors are Italian, so they are
  written by the **Italian Content Editor** against the specification in each
  task — they are named as a dependency in each instrument's open items.
- **Self-marking path.** Where a learner marks their own productive work, they
  score TA and CC only, against a checklist derived from the task's required
  elements, and are told explicitly not to self-score GA or TS — self-assessment
  of one's own accuracy is unreliable in exactly the direction that matters.
  Those two scales go to a teacher, a language partner, or are deferred until
  one is available, and the instrument says what the result means in the
  meantime.

## Spelling, accents and tolerance

One policy, applied everywhere, so markers do not have to decide:

| Feature | Receptive items | Productive tasks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spelling | Not marked. Any recognisable spelling of the right word is correct. | GA, and only when it changes the word. |
| Written accents | Not marked. | Not marked, **except** where the accent distinguishes the target form (`parlò`/`parlo`, `è`/`e`, `dà`/`da`) — then TS. |
| Double consonants in writing | Not marked. | GA, once, as a pattern — not per instance. |
| Capitalisation of `Lei` / `Le` | Not marked. | Not marked. Register is marked on TA; orthography of the pronoun is not a B1 construct. |
| Missing apostrophe / elision | Not marked. | GA only where it obscures the form. |

**Why.** A **construct validity** rule: an item that claims to test listening
comprehension and is failed on a missing accent tested orthography. If spelling
is worth testing, it gets its own item and says so.
