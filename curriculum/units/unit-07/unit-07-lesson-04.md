## Stages in full

### 1 · Opener — the errand that went wrong — 5 min · pairs → lockstep

**Purpose.** Put the theme on the table from the learners' own experience, and
collect the problems the culture note is about to solve.

**Instruction.** "With your partner, three minutes. **Think of the last time you
had to go to an office** — any office, any country: a bank, a town hall, a
consulate. Tell your partner where it was, how you got there, and **what went
wrong**. In Italian if you can. If you need an English word, say it in English
and keep going."

**CCQ.** "Am I asking about a good experience or a problem?" → *A problem — what
went wrong.*

**Then, lockstep, 2 minutes.** Collect "what went wrong" on the left of the
board, **in English**, one line each: *wrong documents · wrong queue · no
appointment · it was closed · had to come back*. Leave the list up. Stage 2 ends
by ticking it off against the text.

**Correction stance.** None.

### 2 · Culture note as input — *Allo sportello* — 15 min · solo → pairs → lockstep · `A10` `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** The unit's culture note — **bureaucracy as a system, not an
obstacle** — and, at the same time, the lesson's only input text. All four
recycled structures are in it, so the culture note is not competing with the
recycling for time: it *is* where the recycling starts.

**This note is in Italian, deliberately.** Unit 1's was in English because its
takeaway could not risk a confident misreading. At B1 lower, and with the
takeaway written up in English at the end of this stage, the Italian text earns
its place twice: as culture, and as the source the rest of the lesson mines.
Six opaque terms are glossed under it.

**Instruction.** "This is from a guide for people who have just moved to Italy.
Read it — five minutes — and then do exercise 1, part A: three questions, four
minutes. **Don't
look up every word.** The six hard ones are glossed underneath."

#### *Allo sportello: istruzioni per l'uso* `[ITA?]`

> In Italia quasi tutto comincia da un numero: il **codice fiscale**. Ti serve per
> il telefono, per un conto in banca, per andare dal medico. Si chiede
> all'Agenzia delle Entrate ed è gratuito. Vacci per primo: senza codice fiscale,
> gli altri uffici non possono fare niente.
>
> Poi c'è la **residenza**, che non è solo l'indirizzo: è un fatto legale. Si chiede
> all'anagrafe del Comune. Dopo qualche settimana, può darsi che un vigile passi a
> casa tua per controllare che ci abiti davvero.
>
> Tre regole che nessuno ti spiega:
>
> 1. **Prima di uscire, controlla se serve l'appuntamento.** In molti uffici si
>    entra solo su prenotazione, e gli appuntamenti si prenotano online. Controlla
>    anche come ci si arriva.
> 2. **La marca da bollo non si compra allo sportello.** Si compra dal tabaccaio.
>    Comprala prima di andarci, e porta i moduli già compilati: si scaricano dal
>    sito del Comune.
> 3. **Il numeretto è sacro.** Appena entri, prendilo dalla macchinetta — ogni
>    servizio ha la sua lettera, quindi scegli quella giusta — e non perderlo.
>    Quando il tuo numero appare sullo schermo, vai allo sportello indicato.
>
> La fila sembra lenta, ma funziona: si aspetta il proprio turno e non si passa
> davanti a nessuno. Chi arriva preparato esce prima.

**Glossed** — `l'Agenzia delle Entrate` the tax office · `l'anagrafe` the
registry office (in the town hall) · `un vigile` a municipal police officer ·
`su prenotazione` by appointment only · `la macchinetta` the ticket machine ·
`passare davanti a qualcuno` to jump the queue.

**Recognition only, and say so if asked — do not explain:**

- `Ti serve`, `se serve l'appuntamento` — `piacere`-family chunks.
  **`piacere` chunks are recognition-only until `U8 L2`; do not explain the
  construction.** "It means *you need* / *is needed*. Unit 8 takes it apart."
- `può darsi che un vigile passi` — *a police officer may come round*. A fixed
  hedging chunk (cross-unit Seed 2). The verb form after it is not this lesson's
  business and is never asked for.

**Then, pairs, 3 minutes.** Exercise 1 part B — the two comparison questions.

**Then, lockstep, 3 minutes — this is the stage, not a wrap-up.** Go back to the
stage 1 list on the board and tick each problem the text would have prevented.
Then write the behavioural takeaway up, **in English**, and leave it there for
stage 7:

> **Takeaway — before any Italian office.**
> 1. Check whether you need an appointment. Book it online.
> 2. Ask — by phone, online, or at the information desk — **`Cosa devo portare?`**
>    and get the list.
> 3. Buy the `marca da bollo` **first**, at the `tabaccaio`.
> 4. Take the `numeretto` for the **right letter**, and keep it.
>
> The queue rewards preparation. It does not reward insistence.

**CCQ.** "Where do you buy a `marca da bollo` — at the office or before?" →
*Before, at the tabaccaio.* · "What do you ask before you go?" → *`Cosa devo
portare?`*

**Watch the clock.** This text invites stories about bureaucracy in English, and
the stories are good. Fifteen minutes, then stop: the takeaway on the board is
what stage 7 needs.

**Correction stance.** Accuracy on part A. Part B and the discussion are content
only — no correction.

### 3 · Sort — four structures in the text — 6 min · solo → pairs · `A2` (recycling use) `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** Get learners to *retrieve* all four contract structures out of the
text before working on any of them, so that the next three stages build on
something they have just found rather than something they are told.

**`A2` used in its recycling variant — say so to yourself, not to the class.**
`A2`'s discipline is that no rule is stated, because in `L1` the form is new.
Here none of the four is new, so learners may say what each form *does* — they
may not be **taught** it. The grid still has two model rows per column, and it
is still collection first.

**Instruction.** "Exercise 2. Four columns, four things you already know, all
hiding in the text. Find **as many as you can** for each column in four minutes —
the first one in each column is done. Then compare with your partner: **did you
put anything in a different column?**"

**CCQ.** "Do you need to find new grammar?" → *No — things we already know.* ·
"How many columns?" → *Four.*

**Then, pairs, 2 minutes.** Compare. Then one lockstep question, thirty seconds:
"Column 4 — where are the pronouns? Before the verb or stuck on the end?" →
*Stuck on the end.* "**Why?**" → *Because the text says `tu`.* Leave it there;
stage 5 opens from that answer.

**Correction stance.** Accuracy on the collection. A form in the wrong column is
corrected by a question — "who is doing it?" — not by a rule.

### 4 · Recycle A — getting there: `ci` and prepositions with movement — 14 min · lockstep → solo → pairs · `A4` `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** The two **mandated** recyclings. U3 used `ci` and the place
prepositions to say *where something is*; this stage uses them to say *how you
get there* — a route, a means of transport, a destination named once and then
replaced by `ci`.

**Part 1 — lockstep, 4 minutes.** Two board panels. Elicit each line rather than
writing it cold: every one is recycled.

**(a) `ci` = *there*, once the place is named.**

> `All'anagrafe? **Ci** vado domani.`
> `**Ci** devo andare oggi.` = `Devo andar**ci** oggi.` — two equal options, as in Unit 1.
> `Come **ci si** arriva?` — **`ci` first, then `si`. Always.**

"`Come ci si arriva` is two things you already have: U3's `ci`, and U4's `si`
for *people in general*. The only new thing is the order."

**(b) The prepositions you already have, now moving.**

| Job | Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where to — same as *where* | `a` / `in` | `vado in posta, in banca, a Bologna` |
| To a person, or a business named after one | `da` | `vado dal medico, dal tabaccaio` |
| Where from | `da` | `dalla fermata, dalla stazione` |
| As far as | `fino a` | `vai fino al semaforo, fino in fondo` |
| Along, through | `per` | `sempre dritto per Corso Italia` |
| How | `a piedi` · `in autobus / in metro / in bici` · `con il 12` | **never `per autobus`** |

"First row: **Italian doesn't change the preposition when you start moving.**
`Sono in banca`, `vado in banca`. English does — *at* the bank, *to* the bank.
That is why English speakers reach for the wrong one."

**CCQ.** "`Come ci si arriva` or `come si ci arriva`?" → *`Ci si`.* · "*By bus* —
`per autobus`?" → *No — `in autobus`.* · "I'm going to the doctor's: `al medico`
or `dal medico`?" → *`Dal` — it's a person.*

**Part 2 — solo, 4 minutes.** "Exercise 3. Ten questions. Answer each one
**using `ci`** and the cue in brackets. The last three need a decision — read
the instruction in italics."

**Part 3 — pairs, 6 minutes: the map.** Point at the map on the board.

**The map** `[ITA?]` — copy onto the board before class.

The route is drawn **going up the page** on purpose: the walker faces the top, so
left on the page is left in the street. Bus 12 runs up Corso Italia; the

**Instruction.** "You're both at the bus stop — `sei qui`. Partner A: choose a
place on the map and ask **`Come ci si arriva?`** Partner B: give directions in
**`tu`** — how to get there, which way, as far as where, and on foot or by bus.
A follows with a finger. If the finger ends up in the wrong place, B tries again.
Then swap. **Four places each.**"

**CCQ.** "Who asks the question — the one who knows, or the one who doesn't?" →
*The one who doesn't.* · "`tu` or `Lei`?" → *`tu`.*

**Model directions** (key for the map — give one as a model before the pairs
start; the rest are for checking) `[ITA?]`:

- **Al Comune.** `Dalla fermata vai sempre dritto per Corso Italia. Supera il
  semaforo, e alla rotonda gira a destra in Via Manzoni. Il Comune è di fronte
  alla scuola. Ci si arriva a piedi in dieci minuti.`
- **In posta.** `Vai dritto fino al semaforo, gira a destra in Via Dante e vai
  fino in fondo: la posta è sulla sinistra.`
- **Dal tabaccaio.** `È a due passi: la tabaccheria è sulla sinistra, accanto al
  Bar Centrale.`
- **All'Agenzia delle Entrate.** `Prendi il 12 e scendi alla seconda fermata.
  Oppure vacci a piedi: sempre dritto per Corso Italia, supera la rotonda, ed è
  sulla destra. Ci vuole un quarto d'ora.`
- **In banca.** `Vai dritto, supera il semaforo: la banca è sulla sinistra, prima
  della rotonda.`

**Correction stance.** Exercise 3: accuracy, immediate. The map pairs: fluency —
the finger is the correction. Collect two things for stage 8.

### 5 · Recycle B — at the counter: `si` and the pronoun with the imperative — 14 min · lockstep → solo → pairs · `A4` `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** The two unbolded recyclings, both operating on an **older**
structure. U4's `si` (*people in general*) is now the voice of every sign in an
office. U6's direct pronoun — the choice of `lo / la / li / le` by the noun's
gender and number — now travels with an imperative, and **where it goes depends
on who you are talking to**. The pronoun choice is U6's; the register switch is
what is new to the learner's ear.

**Part 1 — lockstep, 4 minutes.** Two board panels.

**(a) `si`, from U4 to the office wall.**

> `Una volta **si viveva** meglio.` — U4: people in general.
> `Qui **si parla** inglese.` · `Non **si entra** senza numeretto.` — same job, no *thing*: verb stays singular.
> `**Si accettano** carte.` · `I moduli **si scaricano** online.` — there is a *thing*: the verb **agrees with the thing**.

"One question decides it: **is there a thing?** If there is, count it."

**(b) The pronoun from Unit 6, and who you are talking to.**

> `Il modulo? — **Lo** compili qui.` — **`Lei`: before.** Always.
> `Il modulo? — Compila**lo** qui.` — **`tu`: stuck on the end.**
> `Il modulo? — Non **lo** perdere. / Non perder**lo**.` — `tu`, negative: either. Two equal options.
> `All'anagrafe? — **Ci** vada domani. / Vac**ci** domani.` — `ci` follows the same rule. `va'` + `ci` doubles: `vacci`.

"The pronoun is still Unit 6's: **`lo` for `il modulo`, `la` for `la ricevuta`,
`li` for `i documenti`, `le` for `le fotocopie`.** What changes is where it sits.
The clerk talks to you in `Lei`, so the pronoun goes in front. You tell your
flatmate in `tu`, so it goes on the end." Say `compìlalo`, `prèndilo`, `pòrtali`
once each, with the stress where it was before the pronoun arrived — L3's
pronunciation point, maintained for ten seconds.

**CCQ.** "`Si accetta carte` or `si accettano carte`?" → *`Accettano` — `carte` is
plural.* · "The clerk says *fill it in*. `Compililo` or `lo compili`?" → *`Lo
compili`.* · "Your flatmate: *take them* (`i documenti`)?" → *`Portali` /
`prendili`.*

**Part 2 — solo, 5 minutes.** "Exercise 4. Part A: six notices to rewrite in the
`si` form you'd see on an office wall. Part B: eight things a clerk said to you
in `Lei` — tell your flatmate in `tu`. **The last two in each part need a
decision.**"

**Part 3 — pairs, 5 minutes: the chain.** Write the six objects up:

> `il modulo · la marca da bollo · il numeretto · i documenti · le fotocopie · la ricevuta`

**Instruction.** "Partner A: you're new in town. Ask about each thing:
**`E il modulo?`**, **`E la ricevuta?`** Partner B: you're a friend, so `tu`. Tell
A what to do with it — **one imperative with the pronoun on the end**, and then,
if you can, **one rule with `si`**. Like this: `Il modulo? Scaricalo dal sito — si
compila a casa.` Six things, then swap. **Second time round, B is the clerk, and
it's `Lei`.**"

**CCQ.** "First round, `tu` or `Lei`?" → *`tu`.* · "Second round, where does the
pronoun go?" → *In front.*

**Correction stance.** Exercise 4: accuracy, immediate. The chain: fluency —
listen for attached pronouns on `Lei` forms (`*compililo`) and collect them for
stage 8.

### 6 · Error clinic — all four structures — 8 min · pairs → lockstep · `A8` `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** The four structures' predicted English-L1 errors, side by side, so
that learners have to decide *which* rule applies rather than apply the last one
they practised.

**Instruction.** "Exercise 5. Eight sentences. Some have **one** mistake. **Some
are already correct** — I'm not saying how many. With your partner: decide, fix
the wrong ones, and for each one, name **which of the four** it is: `ci`,
preposition, `si`, or pronoun-with-imperative. Five minutes."

**CCQ.** "Does every sentence have a mistake?" → *No.* · "What do you say as well
as the correction?" → *Which of the four it is.*

**Then, lockstep, 3 minutes.** Go through all eight. Spend the time on items 2
and 8 — the register-position errors — and do not re-explain anything that
stage 4 or 5 already put on the board: point at the board line.

**Correction stance.** Accuracy, and metalanguage welcome.

### 7 · Activate — the information desk, `Lei` then `tu` — 22 min · lockstep → pairs → new pairs · `A7` `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** The lesson's one goal, performed. Round 1 is a real information gap
at a counter in `Lei` — the citizen does not know the answers, the clerk has them
only as notes and must **turn them into sentences**. Round 2 relays the same
information to someone who also doesn't know it, in `tu`, which moves every
pronoun. All four contract structures are needed to complete the task, and none
of them is asked for by name.

**Seed 4 lives here.** The clerk's `Lei` imperatives (`ci vada`, `lo compili`,
`li porti`) are the production habit the course needs from U7 to U12 (see
[`grammar-progression.md` §Cross-unit seeding](../../syllabus/grammar-progression.md#cross-unit-seeding)).
They are taught as imperatives; they are not called anything else.

**Setup — lockstep, 4 minutes.** Two things on the board, then the model.

**The repair strip** — the same idea as Unit 1's five keep-going words, and it
stays up for both rounds:

> `Scusi, può ripetere?` · `Più piano, per favore.` · `Cioè?` · `Quindi ci vado in…, giusto?` · `Come si scrive?`

**The standing rule:**

> **The citizen may not leave the desk without checking back once: `Quindi…`**

**Model exchange** — you are the clerk; a confident learner reads the citizen.
Card 3 (the post office), which nobody uses in the rounds. Ninety seconds.

> **Cittadino:** Buongiorno. Devo pagare una multa. Dove si paga?
> **Impiegata:** In posta. L'ufficio postale è in Via Dante.
> **Cittadino:** E come ci si arriva da qui?
> **Impiegata:** Vada dritto per Corso Italia fino al semaforo, giri a destra in
> Via Dante e prosegua fino in fondo. È sulla sinistra. Ci si arriva in cinque
> minuti.
> **Cittadino:** Scusi, più piano: fino al semaforo, e poi?
> **Impiegata:** A destra, in Via Dante. Fino in fondo.
> **Cittadino:** Cosa devo portare?
> **Impiegata:** La multa — la porti con sé — e un documento.
> **Cittadino:** Si paga con la carta?
> **Impiegata:** Sì, si accettano contanti e carte. Il numeretto lo prenda
> all'ingresso: lettera P.
> **Cittadino:** Quindi: Via Dante, in fondo a sinistra, la multa e un documento,
> lettera P. Giusto?
> **Impiegata:** Esatto.

Point at three things in it, ten seconds each: `ci si arriva` · `la porti`
(`Lei`, pronoun in front) · `si accettano` (there is a thing, so it agrees).

#### Round 1 — at the desk, `Lei` (8 min + 1 min check, pairs)

**Instruction.** "Pairs. One of you is the citizen with **card 1**, the other the
clerk with **card 1-clerk**. **Don't show your card.** Citizen: ask your five
questions and take notes — you'll need them in round 2. Clerk: your card is in
notes, not sentences. **Turn every note into a sentence, in `Lei`.** Four
minutes. Then swap roles and use **card 2**. Remember the rule: no leaving the
desk without `Quindi…`."

**CCQ.** "Clerk — can you read the card aloud?" → *No — I have to make sentences.*
· "Is the clerk's pronoun before or after the verb?" → *Before — it's `Lei`.* ·
"What do you need at the end of round 1?" → *My notes.*

**Check, 1 minute.** "Clerks: now show the citizen the card. Citizens: check your
notes and correct them." The notes have to be right — round 2 depends on them.

#### Round 2 — relay to a flatmate, `tu` (7 min, new pairs)

**Before round 2, rub out stage 5's board panel (b).** Leave the repair strip and
the takeaway. This is the scaffold removal: round 2 is the only stage in the
lesson where the attached pronoun is produced with no model in sight.

**Instruction.** "New partner — **find someone who did the other errand**.

### 8 · Delayed feedback + close — 6 min · lockstep · `A13`

**Purpose.** Deal with the round 1 and round 2 errors without having interrupted

**What to listen for in stage 7** — the likely six come from these:

## Exercises and answer keys

### Exercise 1 — *Allo sportello* `[ITA?]`

**Part A — on the text.** Answer in English or Italian.

1. Why should you get the `codice fiscale` before anything else?
2. Where do you buy a `marca da bollo`, and when?
3. Why does the text tell you to choose carefully when you take the `numeretto`?

**Key.** 1 Other offices **can't do anything for you without it** — `senza codice
fiscale, gli altri uffici non possono fare niente`. Accept also: you need it
for a phone contract, a bank account, the doctor. · 2 At the **tabaccaio**,
**before** going to the office — `Si compra dal tabaccaio. Comprala prima di
andarci.` · 3 **Each service has its own letter** — `ogni servizio ha la sua
lettera` — so the wrong letter puts you in the wrong queue. **3 marks.**

**Part B — on you.** No key; for discussion, nothing is marked.

4. Where you live, what is the one number or document everything else depends on?
   Who issues it, and how long did it take you to get?
5. Think of the stage 1 story — yours or your partner's. Which of the four
   takeaway points would have changed what happened?

### Exercise 2 — Sort: four things you already know `[ITA?]`

Find each form in the text and write it in the right column. The first row is
done.

| 1 · `ci` = *there* | 2 · Preposition + place, with movement | 3 · `si` + verb | 4 · Imperative + pronoun |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Vacci` per primo | andare **dal** medico | `si chiede` all'Agenzia | `Vacci` per primo |
| | | | |

**Key.**

| Column | Instances | The point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · `ci` | `vacci` (= all'Agenzia) · `ci abiti` (= a casa tua) · `ci si arriva` (= all'ufficio) · `andarci` (= allo sportello) | Four. Each one replaces a place the text has just named. |
| 2 · movement | `dal medico` (to a person's) · `a casa tua` (to) · `dal tabaccaio` (at a person's business) · `dal sito` (from) · `dalla macchinetta` (from) · `allo sportello` (to) | Six. Accept `all'Agenzia`, `all'anagrafe` and label them **position** — *where you ask*, not *where you go*; that is U3's job, and it is the same preposition, which is the point of stage 4. |
| 3 · `si` | **No thing:** `si entra` · `ci si arriva` · `si passa` (davanti a nessuno). **A thing — verb agrees:** `si chiede` (il codice fiscale / la residenza, ×2) · `si prenotano` (gli appuntamenti) · `non si compra` / `si compra` (la marca da bollo) · `si scaricano` (i moduli) · `si aspetta` (il proprio turno) | Sorting them into two groups is stage 5's opening question. A learner who has done that already is ahead. |
| 4 · imperative + pronoun | `vacci` · `comprala` · `prendilo` · `non perderlo` | Four, all `tu`, all attached. `andarci` is an infinitive, not an imperative — accept it with that note. The imperatives **without** a pronoun (`controlla`, `porta`, `scegli`, `vai`) do not belong here. |

`vacci` and `ci si arriva` legitimately sit in two columns each. A learner who
noticed that has noticed the lesson.

6. Sei mai stato/a all'Agenzia delle Entrate? *(sì, la settimana scorsa)*

9. *Decide: you are telling a friend, in `tu`.* In posta? *(andare, subito)*
10. *Decide.* Torni all'anagrafe domani? *(no)*

**Key.**

| # | Answer | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |

| 2 | Ci vado domani mattina. | |

### Exercise 4 — At the counter `[ITA?]`

**Part A — the notice on the wall.** Rewrite each one in the `si` form. Two
models:

> `Vendiamo biglietti.` → **`Si vendono biglietti.`**
> `Parliamo inglese.` → **`Si parla inglese.`**

1. Accettiamo solo contanti.
2. Qui chiudiamo alle 13.
3. Potete ritirare i documenti dalle 9 alle 12.
4. Prenotate gli appuntamenti online.
5. *Decide.* Compilate il modulo in stampatello. *(`in stampatello` = in block
   capitals)*
6. *Decide.* Non entrate senza numeretto.

**Key.** 1 Si accettano solo contanti. · 2 Qui si chiude alle 13. · 3 I documenti
si ritirano dalle 9 alle 12. · 4 Gli appuntamenti si prenotano online. · 5 **Il
modulo si compila** in stampatello — *one* thing, so singular; learners who have
just written three plurals write `*si compilano`. · 6 **Non si entra** senza

being entered. **6 marks.**

4. I documenti? Li porti in originale.

6. All'anagrafe? Ci vada la mattina.

| 2 | Comprala dal tabaccaio. | `la` — `la marca`. |
| 3 | Prendilo all'ingresso. | |
| 4 | Portali in originale. | `li` — `i documenti`, U6's choice. |

| 6 | Vacci la mattina. | `va'` + `ci`: `vacci`. |
| 7 | Non perderla! / Non la perdere! | Negative `tu`: **both**, equal. |

### Exercise 5 — Error clinic `[ITA?]`

Some have **one** mistake. Some are correct. For each one, say which of the four
it is: `ci` · preposition · `si` · pronoun-with-imperative.

1. Si ci arriva in metro.
2. Signora, compililo qui, per favore.
3. Qui si accetta solo carte.
4. Il numeretto? Prendilo all'ingresso e non perderlo.
5. Con la tessera sanitaria vado al medico.
6. Ci vado per autobus.
7. Il Comune? Ci vada a piedi, è vicino.
8. Vaci subito, chiude alle dodici.

**Key.**

| # | Verdict | Correction | Which, and why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | ✗ | **Ci si** arriva in metro. | **`ci`.** `ci` always comes before `si`. The learner put `si` first because U4 taught it as the word that opens the sentence. |
| 2 | ✗ | Signora, **lo compili** qui. | **Pronoun-with-imperative.** `Signora` means `Lei`, and with `Lei` the pronoun goes in front — never on the end. The attachment rule from L2 is right for `tu`, `noi`, `voi` and wrong here. |
| 3 | ✗ | Qui si **accettano** solo carte. | **`si`.** There is a thing — `carte` — and it is plural, so the verb is plural. |
| 4 | ✓ | — | Correct. `tu`, attached twice; negative `non perderlo` is one of the two equal options. Here so that "every sentence has a mistake" is not a winning strategy. |
| 5 | ✗ | vado **dal** medico. | **Preposition.** Going to a *person* — or their surgery — is `da`. English says *to the doctor's*, which pulls toward `a`. |
| 6 | ✗ | Ci vado **in** autobus. | **Preposition.** English *by bus* is not `per`. Transport is `in` (`a piedi` on foot). |
| 7 | ✓ | — | Correct. `Lei`, so `ci` in front. The partner of item 2. |
| 8 | ✗ | **Vacci** subito. | **`ci` / imperative.** `va'` + `ci` doubles the consonant and is written as one word. |

**8 marks**, for verdict plus correction. Items 4 and 7 score only if left alone.

### Exercise 6 — The message to your flatmate (homework, written) `[ITA?]`

Write the message you would send your flatmate about **your** errand from stage 7
— or a real errand of your own. `tu`. 80–100 words.

**Model answer** `[ITA?]`

> Ciao Luca! Sono stato all'anagrafe oggi: ti scrivo tutto per domani. Ci si
> arriva a piedi in dieci minuti. Dalla fermata del 12 vai dritto per Corso Italia,
> supera il semaforo e alla rotonda gira a destra in Via Manzoni. Il Comune è di
> fronte alla scuola, l'anagrafe è al secondo piano. Prima però prenota
> l'appuntamento: si prenota online. Il modulo scaricalo dal sito e compilalo a
> casa. Porta il documento, il codice fiscale e il contratto d'affitto — il
> contratto portalo anche in fotocopia. All'ingresso prendi il numeretto, lettera
> A, e non perderlo! A domani!

**What it has to do** — four things, and these are the only four:

1. **`ci` at least once** for a place already named (`ci si arriva`).
2. **A route** with at least **three** different movement prepositions (`dalla
   fermata`, `per Corso Italia`, `alla rotonda`, `in Via Manzoni`).
3. **At least one `si` rule** (`si prenota online`).
4. **At least three `tu` imperatives with an attached pronoun**, each pronoun the
   right one for its noun (`scaricalo` — il modulo; `portalo` — il contratto;

*What the model also does, and a learner need not:* it fronts the noun and then
picks it up with the pronoun — `Il modulo scaricalo dal sito` — which is how
Italians write this kind of message, and which makes the pronoun choice visible.
A learner who writes `Scarica il modulo dal sito` has made no error; they have
just not used the pronoun, and criterion 4 needs three elsewhere.

## Teacher notes

Unit-level error profile and pacing: `unit-07-teacher-notes.md` — **not yet
written** (spike; see the assumptions section). Until it exists, this section
carries the lesson's notes in full.

**Predictable errors**

1. **Attaching the pronoun to a `Lei` form.** `*Compililo`, `*vadaci`,
   `*prendalo`. L2 drilled attachment for ninety minutes, and `Lei` is the one
   form where it doesn't happen — so the better a learner learned L2, the more
   likely this is. Expect it in stage 5 part 3 and from most clerks in stage 7
   round 1. **Do:** point at the board line `Lo compili qui — Lei: before` and say
   the right form; no explanation. If it is still there in round 1, it goes first
   on the stage 8 board. Say to the group that it is a sign L2 worked.
2. **`*Si ci arriva`.** U4 taught `si` as the word that starts the sentence, so
   learners keep it at the front and drop `ci` in after it. Expect it in stage 4
   part 3 and in every citizen's question 2. **Do:** "`ci si` — never the other
   way", and nothing else. It is a fixed order, not a reasoned one, and reasoning
   about it costs more than it buys.
3. **English prepositions of movement carried over.** `*per autobus` (*by bus*),
   `*vado al medico` (*to the doctor's*), `*vado a la posta`. Expect it in the map
   pairs and in round 2's routes. **Do:** one-word correction — "`in` autobus",
   "`dal` medico" — and move on. Stage 4's first table row is the answer, and
   pointing at it is enough.

**The `piacere` seed.** `piacere` chunks are recognition-only until `U8 L2`; do
not explain the construction. In this lesson that means `ti serve` and `se serve
l'appuntamento` in the stage 2 text: gloss them as *you need* / *is needed* and
move on. The cards are written so no one needs to produce them.

**Correction stance per stage.** Stages 2 (part A), 3, 4 (exercise 3), 5
(exercise 4), 6 — **accuracy**, immediate; metalanguage welcome at 6. Stages 1,
2 (part B and discussion), 4 (map pairs), 5 (chain), 7 — **fluency**, delayed,
collected and dealt with at stage 8.

**To shorten (−12 min).** Set stage 3 (the sort) as the first homework task
(−6); set exercise 1 part B as home reading and keep only the takeaway (−3);
run stage 5's chain once, in `tu` only (−3). You lose the explicit retrieval of
all four structures before work starts, so open stages 4 and 5 by asking for the
forms rather than writing them up. **Never cut stage 7 round 2**: it is the only
place in the lesson where the attached pronoun is produced with the scaffold gone,
and without it two of the four contract rows have no free-production evidence.
And **never cut a stage that is the only production site of a mandated
structure** — the contract ledger in *At a glance* says which those are.

**To extend (+10 min).** Reverse the culture note. In pairs, learners explain to
an Italian who has just moved to **their** country how one office there works —
where you go, what you bring, what the rule is — in `Lei`, as the clerk would.
Same four structures, the comparison from exercise 1 part B turned into
production, and no preparation needed. Card 3 (the post office) is a second,
shorter option: run it as a round 3 without the model.

### The contract and "this unit's grammar"

Template README §2 says L4 "is not more practice of this unit's grammar". Unit 7's
contract row names two structures that this unit **extends**: `si` impersonale
*extended to passivante* (the extension is U7 L3's secondary point) and direct
pronouns *attached to imperatives* (attachment is U7 L2's main point). Read
literally, the README forbids half of the contract.

The reading this lesson uses: **L4 practises the earlier structure as the
current unit's operation acts on it**, and never re-presents the operation.
Concretely, stage 5 does not teach imperative forms or the passivante agreement
rule; it makes the learner choose U6's `lo/la/li/le` by gender and number and U4's
`si` by "is there a thing?", under a register switch, in a new theme. The test of
whether the line held: nothing in this file is a paradigm, and every item in
exercise 4 depends on a choice the learner first made in U4 or U6.

The rule needs writing into the template — see the ITA-11 report.
