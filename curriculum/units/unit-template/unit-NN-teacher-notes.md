## Shape

### 1. If you are teaching this unit cold

Five lines: what the unit is for, the one thing that must land, the one stage
that always overruns, the lesson most likely to be cut short, and what the next
unit needs this one to have delivered.

### 2. Error profile

The table `A8` items are built from. One row per predicted error, and the
prediction has to be **specific to an English L1** (format brief §1) — which
means it names *why* the English speaker produces it, not just that it is
wrong.

| # | Error | The wrong sentence | Why an English L1 produces it | Where it shows | Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

**Rules.** Six to ten rows, no more — a profile with twenty rows is a grammar
of Italian, not a teaching tool. Every row must appear in at least one `A8`
clinic in the unit, or it is an observation rather than a plan. And the
**Response** column names a teaching action, not a diagnosis: "recast and put
two on the feedback board" is a response; "needs more practice" is not.

### 3. Register decisions

Which register the unit's materials use and why, and what learners are told
about it. This course runs a `tu`/`Lei` axis from Unit 1 onward, so any unit
whose materials use one and not the other is making a decision that the learner
will generalise from. Write it down.

### 4. Known weak spots in this unit

The honest list: where the design is thin, where it depends on something not yet
built, where a reasonable teacher will disagree with a choice. A unit with no
weak spots listed has not been read carefully by its author.

### 5. Pacing reality

Which stages overrun, in practice, and the standing instruction for what to cut.
This duplicates each lesson's "to shorten" line on purpose: a teacher who is
already running late reads one file, not five.

## Filled example (Unit 1, sections 1 and 2 abridged)

### 1. If you are teaching Unit 1 cold

- **What it is for.** Re-entry, not instruction. The learner arrives with most
  of this half-owned; the unit's job is to make it usable at length.
- **The one thing that must land.** The reflexive pronoun is **obligatory**.
  Everything else in the unit is recoverable later; this is the error that
  survives to B1 if it survives Unit 1.
- **The stage that always overruns.** L3 stage 5 (character adjectives, 14 min).
  Learners want to talk about the people, which is the right instinct and the
  wrong stage.
- **Most likely to be cut short.** L4, because its culture note invites
  discussion in English. Protect stage 7: it is the only stage in the unit where
  the fourth can-do goal is practised.
- **What Unit 2 needs from this one.** Secure reflexives in the present, because
  U2 puts them in the past and uses them to teach `essere` + agreement. If they
  are shaky here, U2's main grammar point has no foundation.

### 2. Error profile (3 of 8 rows)

| # | Error | The wrong sentence | Why an English L1 produces it | Where it shows | Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Dropped reflexive pronoun | `*Sveglio alle sette.` | English *I wake up* has no pronoun, so there is nothing to translate. | Everywhere, L1–L5 | Recast, never explain in L1. In L2 make it rule 1 of three. `A8` items 1, 4. |
| 2 | Pronoun after the verb | `*Alzo mi presto.` | Maps *I get **myself** up* onto Italian word order. | L2 onward | Write correct next to wrong; say both aloud. `A8` item 2. |
| 3 | Pronoun between modal and infinitive | `*Devo mi alzare.` | Both correct Italian options (`mi devo alzare`, `devo alzarmi`) are unlike English, so the learner builds a third. | L2 stage 5, L5 | Teach both correct options as a **pair**, not one as primary. `A8` item 6. |
