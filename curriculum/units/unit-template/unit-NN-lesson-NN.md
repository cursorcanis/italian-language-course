## Stages in full

> **Shape.** One block per stage. Every block carries, without exception:
> purpose · timing · pattern · **the exact instruction in quotation marks** ·
> a CCQ with its expected answer wherever the task could be misread · the
> materials inline. A stage that says "do the gap-fill" without the gap-fill
> printed in the file is unfinished.
>
> Write the instruction the way you would actually say it. "Explain that they
> should work in pairs and discuss their routines" is a note to yourself.
> "Work with the person next to you. Tell them three things you do every
> morning, in order. You have two minutes each — I'll tell you when to swap" is
> an instruction.

**Filled example (Unit 1 L1, stage 6)**

### 6 · Listen 3 + noticing grid — 10 min · solo → pairs · `A2`

**Purpose.** Get learners to *collect* the reflexive pattern out of the text
before anyone names it. This is the stage L2 is built on: next lesson opens by
asking what they found, not by presenting a paradigm.

**Instruction.** "Now you've got the transcript. Listen one more time and read
along. While you listen, fill in this grid: every time Giulia says a verb about
herself with a little word in front of it — `mi`, `ci` — write the pair in the
grid, and write who it's about. I've done the first two for you. Don't worry
about why the little word is there. Just collect them. Solo first, then compare
with your partner."

**CCQ.** "Am I asking you to explain the little words, or to find them?"
→ *To find them.* · "How many do you think you'll find — about five, or about
fifteen?" → *About fifteen* (there are 14 to find, plus the 2 given).

**Grid** (models in the first two rows):

| Little word | Verb | Who it's about |
| --- | --- | --- |
| mi | sveglio | Giulia |
| ci | vediamo | Giulia and Sara |
| | | |

**Discipline.** If a learner asks *why* it is `mi`, say: "Good question — that's
exactly what we do next lesson. For now, just collect them." Answering it turns
L1 into L2 and throws the noticing away.

**Correction stance.** Accuracy, but only on the collection. No rule is stated,
asked for, or given.

## Exercises and answer keys

> **Shape.** Every exercise, numbered, with its key **immediately after it** —
> not in an appendix and not in another file. A teacher mid-lesson should never
> have to scroll or open a second document.
>
> Open tasks get a **model answer** or a **rubric**. "Answers will vary" is not
> a key; "answers will vary — model answer below, and the three things it has
> to do" is.
>
> Keys for `A8` error clinics explain **why**, in one line of English, not just
> what the correction is.

**Filled example (Unit 1 L1, exercise 1)**

### Exercise 1 — Giulia's week: true or false? `[ITA?]`

1. Giulia si alza subito quando si sveglia.
2. Fa colazione al bar.
3. Il martedì deve sbrigarsi.
4. Il sabato si sveglia con la sveglia.
5. Vede Sara una volta al mese.
6. La domenica si addormenta davanti alla televisione.

**Key.** 1 **F** — *non mi alzo subito, resto a letto dieci minuti.* · 2 **F** —
*faccio colazione in piedi, in cucina.* · 3 **V** · 4 **F** — *il sabato non mi
sveglio con la sveglia.* · 5 **F** — *ci vediamo quasi ogni settimana.* ·
6 **V**.

*The quoted justification is part of the key, not decoration: a solo learner who
got an item wrong needs to know where in the text the answer was.*

## Teacher notes

> **Shape.** Four things, all of them specific to this lesson:
>
> 1. **Two or three errors this lesson will predictably produce**, from an
>    English-L1 learner (format brief §1), each with what to do about it. Not
>    "they may struggle with reflexives" — the actual wrong sentence, and the
>    response.
> 2. **Correction stance per stage** — which stages get on-the-spot correction
>    and which get delayed feedback. Name them.
> 3. **One way to shorten**, for the session that starts late.
> 4. **One way to extend**, for the group that finishes early.
>
> Shortening and extending are both compulsory and both specific: which stage
> you cut and what you lose by cutting it.

**Filled example (Unit 1 L1)**

**Predictable errors**

1. **Dropped pronoun.** `*Sveglio alle sette` for *I wake up at seven* — the
   English verb has no pronoun, so the learner produces none. Expect this in
   stages 7–9 from most of the group. **Do:** recast, don't explain — "mi
   sveglio alle sette, sì" — and write two instances on the delayed-feedback
   board. The explanation is L2's job, one day away; explaining it now burns
   L2's discovery stage.
2. **Pronoun after the verb.** `*Alzo mi alle sette`, by analogy with *I get
   myself up*. Less frequent than (1) but stickier. **Do:** on the board, write
   the correct pair next to the wrong one and say the two aloud. Do not draw a
   rule.
3. **`ogni giorni`.** Plural agreement on `ogni`. **Do:** one-word correction,
   `ogni giorno`, and move on. It is a maintenance error, not this lesson's
   business.

**Correction stance.** Stages 3, 6, 7 — accuracy, immediate. Stages 2, 8, 9 —
fluency, delayed, collected at stage 10.

**To shorten (−10 min).** Cut stage 9 and run stage 8 rung 2 only. You lose the
third-person retell, which is where agreement gets stretched; pick it up in L2
stage 8.

**To extend (+10 min).** After stage 9, learners write six sentences comparing
their week with Giulia's (`anche io` / `io invece`). This pre-loads the contrast
language L4 needs.

## Self-study version

> **Shape.** A real 45–60-minute solo route, staged like the lesson, with a
> **stated solo equivalent for every pair or group stage** (format brief §2).
> Use the solo equivalents in `activity-repertoire.md` rather than reinventing
> them. "Do this alone" is not a solo equivalent and neither is "imagine a
> partner".
>
> The test: could a learner with this file, an audio file and a phone complete
> the lesson and know whether they had succeeded? If the answer depends on
> having someone to talk to, the solo version is not finished.
