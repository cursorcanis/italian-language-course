# Activity Repertoire

Owner: Lesson Designer · Task: ITA-5

The shared set of activity formats this course uses. Lessons reference a format
by code (`A7`) and then supply only the *content* — the cards, the sentences,
the prompts. The mechanics live here, once.

**Why a repertoire at all.** Format brief §5: an activity format used three
times is worth more than three novel formats. Two reasons, both practical.
Learners who already know how a task works spend their attention on Italian
rather than on decoding instructions — that is **cognitive load** spent where it
earns something. And a teacher meeting `A3 Paradigm sprint` for the fifth time
needs one line of set-up instead of a paragraph, which is what makes a
90-minute plan actually fit into 90 minutes.

**Rule:** a lesson that needs a format not listed here either adds it to this
file with a new code, or uses one that is here. No one-off formats.

---

## How to read an entry

- **Shape** — what happens, mechanically.
- **Pattern** — the default interaction pattern. A lesson may override it, but
  must say so.
- **Default timing** — the honest range. Lessons may deviate; if a lesson gives
  `A5` six minutes, the stage is doing something smaller than `A5` normally is.
- **Solo equivalent** — the self-study route. Every format has one, because
  format brief §2 requires one and because half the audience has no partner.
- **Correction stance** — accuracy stages get on-the-spot correction; fluency
  stages get delayed feedback. Stated per format so a lesson doesn't have to
  re-litigate it.
- **Recurs at** — where else in the course this format is expected. Kept
  approximate on purpose; it is a planning note, not a contract.

## Input and noticing

### `A1` — Gist → detail → noticing listen

Three passes over one recording, each with a different task.

- **Shape.** Pass 1: one global question, answered in a phrase (gist). Pass 2:
  five to eight detail items — true/false, short answer, or a table to
  complete. Pass 3: with the transcript visible, a collection task (see `A2`).
- **Pattern.** Solo listening → pairs to compare → lockstep check.
- **Default timing.** 6 + 10 + 10 minutes.
- **Solo equivalent.** Identical; "compare with your partner" becomes "write
  your answers, then check the key, then listen once more to the items you got
  wrong". The third pass is the one a solo learner must not skip.
- **Correction stance.** Accuracy — answers are right or wrong, checked openly.
- **Why three passes.** One listen tests memory; three teach listening. Pass 1
  removes the panic that stops learners hearing anything, pass 2 is the actual
  work, pass 3 turns listening into **noticing**.
- **Recurs at.** Every unit's L1. The course's single most-used format.

### `A2` — Noticing grid

Learners collect instances of a target form out of a text into a table, without
being told what the form *is*.

- **Shape.** A table with column headings that make the pattern visible
  (`pronoun | verb | who it refers to`) and two rows completed as models.
  Learners scan the transcript and fill it. **No rule is stated, asked for, or
  given.**
- **Pattern.** Solo, then pairs to pool findings.
- **Default timing.** 8–12 minutes.
- **Solo equivalent.** Identical, with the completed grid as the key.
- **Correction stance.** Accuracy, but on the *collection*, not on any rule.
- **Discipline.** If a learner asks "so why is it `mi`?", the answer is "we do
  that next lesson — for now just collect them." Answering it converts L1 into
  L2 and throws away the noticing.
- **Recurs at.** Every unit's L1, feeding that unit's L2.

### `A10` — Read and compare (culture)

A short text about how something works in Italy, read against how it works
where the learner is.

- **Shape.** 120–200 words of accessible Italian or English (state which), three
  comprehension questions, then two comparison prompts the learner answers from
  their own life. Ends with one **behavioural** takeaway — what to actually do
  or say, not a fact to know.
- **Pattern.** Solo read → pairs compare → lockstep on the takeaway.
- **Default timing.** 12–15 minutes.
- **Solo equivalent.** Written comparison in the learner's notebook instead of
  spoken; the takeaway is turned into a written "what I'll say next time" line.
- **Correction stance.** Comprehension questions: accuracy. Comparison: content
  only, no correction.
- **Recurs at.** Every unit's L4.

## Form-focused practice

### `A3` — Paradigm sprint

Fast, low-stakes, oral or written filling of a conjugation or agreement grid.

- **Shape.** A grid with one row or column given. Learners complete it against
  the clock (90 seconds is the standard), then check against the key, then do
  the same grid again for a different verb without looking.
- **Pattern.** Solo, timed, then lockstep check.
- **Default timing.** 6–10 minutes for two or three rounds.
- **Solo equivalent.** Identical — this format needs no partner. Round 2 from
  memory is the part that makes it work.
- **Correction stance.** Accuracy, self-checked against the key.
- **Honest limit.** This builds retrieval speed on form only. It is never the
  last stage of a lesson, and it never counts as practice of *use*.
- **Recurs at.** Every unit's L2, and any L4 recycling a paradigm.

### `A4` — Transform line

Learners rewrite given sentences under one stated change.

- **Shape.** Eight to ten sentences, one transformation, stated once and
  modelled twice: *make it negative*, *add `devo`*, *change `io` to `mia
  sorella`*, *put it in the past*. Items are ordered so the first three are
  mechanical and the last three require a decision.
- **Pattern.** Solo, then pairs to compare, then lockstep on the last three only.
- **Default timing.** 8–12 minutes.
- **Solo equivalent.** Identical; key supplied.
- **Correction stance.** Accuracy, immediate.
- **Recurs at.** Every unit's L2; standard homework format.

### `A8` — Error clinic

Learners correct sentences that contain the errors this course *predicts* they
will make.

- **Shape.** Six to ten sentences. Each carries exactly one error drawn from the
  unit's error profile in `unit-NN-teacher-notes.md`. **One or two items are
  already correct** and are not flagged as such — otherwise learners learn to
  "find the error" rather than to judge.
- **Pattern.** Pairs (arguing about it is the point), then lockstep.
- **Default timing.** 8–12 minutes.
- **Solo equivalent.** Solo with the key, where the key explains *why* each
  error is an error — one line, in English — and not merely what the correction
  is.
- **Correction stance.** Accuracy, and the only stage where metalanguage is
  encouraged.
- **Why errors the learner will actually make.** Generic error-correction
  exercises teach learners to spot typos. Items built from the unit's L1-English
  error profile (format brief §1) teach them to catch themselves.
- **Recurs at.** L2 and L4 of every unit.

### `A9` — Sound discrimination and production

The unit's pronunciation point, practised as meaning-bearing rather than as
decoration.

- **Shape.** Three steps, always in this order. (1) **Discriminate**: hear
  minimal pairs, say which one you heard. (2) **Mark**: write the feature on the
  page — stress mark, double letter, intonation arrow. (3) **Produce**: say
  pairs to a partner, who identifies which one was said. Step 3 is where
  learners find out whether step 2 was real.
- **Pattern.** Lockstep for (1), solo for (2), pairs for (3).
- **Default timing.** 12–15 minutes.
- **Solo equivalent.** Step 3 becomes record-and-compare: the learner records
  the pairs, listens back against the model, and marks where they differ.
  Self-hearing is weaker than a partner's ear — so the solo version does five
  pairs rather than ten and repeats the recording twice.
- **Correction stance.** Accuracy, immediate, generous — recasts rather than
  explanations.
- **Recurs at.** Every unit's L3.

## Meaning-focused and communicative practice

### `A5` — Survey / find someone who

Learners ask the same small set of questions round the room and record answers.

- **Shape.** Six to eight prompts on a grid with a column for names and a column
  for the answer. Learners must ask **one follow-up question** per person — this
  is the part that stops the activity being a form-filling exercise.
- **Pattern.** Mingle, or small group of three or four for a small class.
- **Default timing.** 12–18 minutes including feedback.
- **Solo equivalent.** **Predict-then-check.** The learner writes what they
  think the answers would be for two real people they know, then — if at all
  possible — actually asks one of them, in any language, and writes the answer
  up in Italian. Where that is impossible, the prompts are answered against a
  supplied set of three short profile texts, and the learner writes the
  follow-up questions they *would* have asked. Never "imagine a partner".
- **Correction stance.** Fluency — delayed feedback (`A13`).
- **Recurs at.** L1 and L4 across the course.

### `A6` — Scaffolded output ladder

The lesson's bridge between model and free production: **gradual release**.

- **Shape.** Three rungs, and the third is compulsory.
  1. **I do** — a worked model, on the page, complete.
  2. **We do** — the same task with sentence frames and prepared lexis supplied.
  3. **You do** — the same task again, frames **removed**, notes reduced to five
     words maximum.
- **Pattern.** Lockstep for rung 1, pairs for rung 2, pairs or solo for rung 3.
- **Default timing.** 14–20 minutes across the three rungs.
- **Solo equivalent.** Rung 2 is written, rung 3 is spoken and recorded. The
  removal of the scaffold is what makes the format work, so a solo learner is
  told explicitly to turn the page over.
- **Correction stance.** Rung 2 accuracy; rung 3 fluency, delayed.
- **Non-negotiable.** A lesson that jumps from model to free production has a
  hole in the middle. If the scaffold is never removed, the learner never
  produces — they read aloud.
- **Recurs at.** Every unit's L3 and L5.

### `A7` — Information gap

Two learners hold different halves of the information and must talk to complete
the task.

- **Shape.** Card A and Card B, printed or on screen, each with gaps the other
  can fill. The gap must be **real**: if either learner can guess the answer, or
  if showing each other the cards would be quicker, it is not an information
  gap, it is a worksheet with a partner.
- **Pattern.** Pairs, facing, cards not shown.
- **Default timing.** 10–15 minutes.
- **Solo equivalent.** Card B is supplied as a short recording or text; the
  learner asks the questions aloud, pauses, and checks each answer against it.
  The asking is the target skill, so it must still be spoken.
- **Correction stance.** Fluency during; delayed feedback on the two or three
  question forms that broke.
- **Recurs at.** L3 and L4 across the course; the backbone of the transactional
  units (U6, U7, U11).

### `A11` — Rehearse, record, re-record

The production-task engine, and the format that makes a solo learner's speaking
improve at all.

- **Shape.** Learner performs the task once against a scaffold and times it;
  gets or self-generates feedback on **two** named things; performs it again
  with the scaffold reduced. The second performance is the deliverable. Both
  recordings are kept — the improvement between them is the evidence.
- **Pattern.** Pairs for live classes (partner as audience and timer), solo with
  a phone otherwise.
- **Default timing.** 18–25 minutes.
- **Solo equivalent.** This *is* the solo format. In a live class the partner
  supplies the two feedback points; solo, the learner listens back once with a
  three-item checklist drawn from the task's success criterion.
- **Correction stance.** Delayed and selective: two points, named before the
  second attempt, chosen from the success criterion. Not a list.
- **Recurs at.** Every unit's L5.

### `A12` — Checkpoint

The ~10-minute end-of-unit check, run inside L5.

- **Shape.** Fixed proportions, so learners know what they are walking into:
  ~40% the unit's lexis, ~40% its main grammar point, ~20% at least one recycled
  structure from an earlier unit (syllabus map §Assessment points). Ends with
  the learner self-rating each can-do goal on the unit's three-point scale.
- **Pattern.** Solo, silent, timed.
- **Default timing.** 10 minutes, plus 5 to check and self-rate.
- **Solo equivalent.** Identical. Self-study learners mark their own, which is
  why every item has a single unambiguous answer.
- **Correction stance.** Accuracy. Marked, then the self-rating is what the
  learner acts on.
- **Recurs at.** Every unit's L5. Full spec: ITA-4.

### `A13` — Delayed feedback board

How fluency-stage errors get dealt with without interrupting fluency.

- **Shape.** During a fluency stage the teacher writes down five or six things
  heard — anonymised, verbatim. Afterwards they go on the board, learners work
  out in pairs what is wrong with each, then the teacher confirms. Two of the
  six should be **good** examples worth copying, not errors.
- **Pattern.** Teacher collects during; pairs then lockstep after.
- **Default timing.** 5–8 minutes.
- **Solo equivalent.** The learner listens back to their own recording once with
  one question only — "where did I stop, and why?" — and writes two lines. Not
  a full self-transcription; that costs forty minutes and gets abandoned.
- **Correction stance.** This format *is* the delayed-correction stance.
- **Recurs at.** After every fluency stage in the course.
