## Stages in full

### 1 · Opener — *tre cose* — 5 min · pairs → lockstep

**Purpose.** Get Italian out of mouths in the first ninety seconds, and put the
theme on the table before any vocabulary is taught, so that the lexis stage
answers a question the learner has already asked themselves.

**Instruction.** "Work with the person next to you. Tell them **three things you
do every morning**, in order. In Italian, and if you don't know a word, say it in
English and keep going — I want the order, not the vocabulary. Ninety seconds
each. I'll tell you when to swap."

**CCQ.** "Do I want perfect Italian, or do I want three things in order?"
→ *Three things in order.* · "What do you do if you don't know a word?"
→ *Say it in English and keep going.*

**Then, lockstep, 90 seconds.** Ask two or three pairs for one thing their partner
said. Write nothing on the board yet. Writing here pre-empts stage 3.

**Correction stance.** None. This stage is fluency and nothing is at stake.

---

### 2 · Engage — my week on a clock — 7 min · solo → pairs

**Purpose.** Make the learner discover, in writing, the exact points where their
Italian runs out. A learner who has found a gap learns the word that fills it;
a learner handed a word list memorises it.

**Instruction.** "On your own, five minutes. Draw this grid — four rows for a
**weekday** and three for **Saturday**. In the left column write a time. In the
right column write what you do at that time, in Italian if you can. **When you
can't say it in Italian, draw a box around the English.** The boxes are the
important part — they're what we do next."

**Grid** (learners copy it; it is four lines, not a handout worth printing):

| Che ora | Che cosa faccio |
| --- | --- |
| | |

**CCQ.** "What do you do when you don't know how to say it?" → *Write the English
and draw a box around it.* · "Is the box a mistake?" → *No — it's the point.*

**Then, pairs, 2 minutes.** "Show your partner your boxes. Did you box the same
things?" Boxed items overlap heavily across a group, which is the first evidence
learners get that the lexis set was chosen rather than invented.

**Solo equivalent.** See the self-study table.

**Correction stance.** None. Nothing is marked.

### 3 · Lexis set A — the 11 reflexive routine verbs — 13 min · lockstep → solo `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** Deliver the eleven items set A owes this lesson, as *sound first,
spelling second, own-life example third*. These eleven are the whole unit's
foundation: the grammar point is unteachable without them.

**The set**, in rough order of the day. Put it on the board, or hand it out, in
this order and not alphabetically — the order of the day is a free mnemonic.

| Italian | English |
| --- | --- |
| `svegliarsi` | to wake up |
| `alzarsi` | to get up |
| `farsi la doccia` | to have a shower |
| `vestirsi` | to get dressed |
| `prepararsi` | to get ready |
| `sbrigarsi` | to hurry up |
| `occuparsi di` | to deal with, to look after |
| `riposarsi` | to rest |
| `rilassarsi` | to relax |
| `addormentarsi` | to fall asleep |
| `trovarsi (bene / male)` | to be comfortable, to be settled somewhere |

**Instruction, part 1 (4 min, lockstep).** "Listen first, don't write. I'll say
each one twice: the infinitive, then the *io* form. Repeat the *io* form after me,
all together." Then: `svegliarsi — mi sveglio`, `alzarsi — mi alzo`, and on down
the list. Learners repeat the `io` form only.

**Instruction, part 2 (3 min, lockstep).** "Now look at the list. Two questions.
One: which three of these do you already know? Two: **which two did you draw a box
around in the last activity?**" Take answers quickly. The second question is the
one that matters: it closes the loop from stage 2.

**Instruction, part 3 (6 min, solo).** "On your own. Choose **five** of these
eleven and write one true sentence about yourself with each. Not a dictionary
sentence — a true one, with a time in it. Mine is: *Mi addormento sul divano quasi
ogni sera.* Five sentences, six minutes. I'll come round."

**CCQ.** "Five sentences about Giulia, or five about you?" → *About me.* · "Does
it have to be true?" → *Yes.*

**Teacher's job during part 3.** Circulate. Correct **only** the reflexive pair
and the time expression; ignore everything else. You will see `*Sveglio alle
sette` repeatedly: recast it — "mi sveglio alle sette, sì" — and move on. Do
**not** explain why. The explanation is L2, one lesson away, and giving it here
destroys stage 6.

**Correction stance.** Accuracy, immediate, narrow — the reflexive pair only.

---

### 4 · Listen 1 — gist — 6 min · solo → lockstep `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** One global question, so that learners hear the whole recording once
without a task heavy enough to stop them hearing it. `A1` pass 1.

**Instruction.** "You're going to hear a voice message. Giulia is telling a
friend what her week is like. **Books down, pens down.** One question only, and
you can answer it in English: **does Giulia like her week, or not?** Listen."

Play cue 1 at normal pace — or read the script aloud, keeping the `///` pauses.

**CCQ.** "How many things am I asking you to catch?" → *One.* · "Can I answer in
English?" → *Yes.*

**Expected answer.** Yes — she is content with it. The evidence is the last line
(`mi trovo bene così`) and `Non posso lamentarmi`; the phrase `niente di speciale`
is not a complaint. Take the answer and the reason in English, 90 seconds, then
move on. **Do not** start checking details here; that is stage 5, and doing it
twice costs four minutes you need at stage 8.

**Correction stance.** None.

---

### 5 · Listen 2 — detail — 10 min · solo → pairs → lockstep `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** The real listening work. `A1` pass 2. Two exercises, one closed and
one open, so that learners who found pass 1 easy are not idle.

**Instruction.** "Now the detail. Exercise 1 is six true-or-false. Exercise 2 is a
grid — Giulia's week, day by day. You'll hear it **twice**, and the second time I
won't stop. Answer what you can the first time; fill the gaps the second. On your
own first, then compare with your partner, then we check."

**CCQ.** "Do you compare with your partner before or after you listen?"
→ *After, and after I've written my own answers.*

Play twice at normal pace, no pause between. Then two minutes in pairs, then check
in lockstep. Exercises 1 and 2 with their keys are below.

**Correction stance.** Accuracy. Right or wrong, checked openly. When an answer is
wrong, give the paragraph number and play those eight seconds again rather than
telling them the answer — that is the listening skill, not the answer.

---

### 6 · Listen 3 + noticing grid — 10 min · solo → pairs · `A2` `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** Get learners to **collect** the reflexive pattern out of the text
before anyone names it. This is the stage L2 is built on: next lesson opens by
asking what the group found, not by presenting a paradigm.

**Instruction.** "Now you get the transcript. Listen one more time — slowly — and
read along. While you listen, fill in this grid. **Every time Giulia says a verb
about herself with a little word in front of it — `mi`, `ci` — write the pair in
the grid, and write who it's about.** I've done the first two for you. Don't worry
about why the little word is there. Just collect them. On your own first, then
compare with your partner and add what you missed."

Read the script aloud at **slow** pace, or play the slow take. Hand out or display
the transcript.

**CCQ.** "Am I asking you to explain the little words, or to find them?"
→ *To find them.* · "How many do you think you'll find — about five, or about
fifteen?" → *About fifteen.* (There are 14 to find, plus the 2 given.)

**Grid** — exercise 3 below, with its key.

**Discipline.** If a learner asks *why* it is `mi`, say: **"Good question — that's
exactly what we do next lesson. For now, just collect them."** Answering it turns
L1 into L2 and throws the noticing away. If you want to reward the question,
write the learner's name next to it on the board and come back to it at the start
of L2 — which costs you nothing and buys the question's energy for a day.

**Correction stance.** Accuracy, but only on the collection. No rule is stated,
asked for, or given.

---

### 7 · Controlled practice — supported gap-fill — 10 min · solo → pairs `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** First accurate use. The gap-fill supplies **complete** reflexive
phrases in a box and asks learners to choose by *meaning*, not to build a form —
because the form has not been taught and will not be until L2. A gap-fill that
asked learners to conjugate here would be asking them to do L2's work without
L2's lesson.

**Instruction.** "Exercise 4. Ten sentences, and a box with twelve phrases. Put
the right phrase in each gap. **Each phrase is used once, and two of the twelve
are not needed** — so don't force the last two in. Six minutes on your own, then
two minutes comparing with your partner, then we check."

**CCQ.** "How many phrases in the box?" → *Twelve.* · "How many gaps?" → *Ten.* ·
"So what's true about two of the phrases?" → *They're not used.*

**Correction stance.** Accuracy, immediate.

---

### 8 · Routine ladder — 15 min · lockstep → pairs · `A6` rungs 1–2 `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** Gradual release. Turn eleven items and a text into the learner's own
spoken routine, with the support that makes it possible — and then plan the
support's removal, which happens in stage 9.

#### Rung 1 — *I do* (3 min, lockstep)

Say this model aloud, twice, at normal speed. Do not write it up; they have the
transcript, and reading it defeats the purpose.

> **Model.** "Durante la settimana mi sveglio alle sei e mezza. Non mi alzo
> subito: resto a letto dieci minuti. Mi faccio la doccia, mi vesto, e alle sette
> e mezza esco di casa. Il martedì devo sbrigarmi, perché alle sei ho il corso di
> inglese. Il sabato invece non mi sveglio con la sveglia e mi rilasso tutto il
> giorno."

Then: "Five sentences. Count them with me. **What does every sentence have?**"
→ *A time or a day, and a verb about herself.* That is the only analysis this
stage does.

#### Rung 2 — *we do* (12 min, pairs)

**Instruction.** "Now yours. You have five frames on the board. Fill them with
your own life, **out loud, not in writing** — your partner is listening, and then
they'll do theirs. Two and a half minutes each, then swap. When you're the
listener, your job is to ask **one** question at the end. One, not five."

**Frames** (write these on the board; learners do not copy them down):

1. `Durante la settimana mi sveglio alle ______ .`
2. `Poi ______ e ______ .`
3. `Il ______ devo sbrigarmi, perché ______ .`
4. `Il sabato invece ______ .`
5. `La domenica sera ______ .`

**Prepared lexis for the frames** (also on the board): `alle sette · alle sette e
mezza · presto · tardi · subito · perché ho il corso di … · perché lavoro ·
tutto il giorno · non faccio niente`

**CCQ.** "Writing or speaking?" → *Speaking.* · "How many questions do you ask
your partner at the end?" → *One.*

**Correction stance.** Rung 2 is accuracy — but circulate and correct **only** the
reflexive pair. Write down two things you hear for stage 10. Do not interrupt a
learner mid-frame; wait for the end of their turn.

---

### 9 · Activate — *la settimana di qualcun altro* — 10 min · pairs → lockstep · `A6` rung 3 `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** Remove the scaffold — the frames come off the board — and stretch the
task one notch, into the third person. This is the stage that **creates the need
for L2**: learners will reach for `si sveglia` and mostly produce `mi sveglia` or
`sveglia`, and those attempts are what L2's presentation is built out of.

**Before you start, write this strip on the board** (and rub out the stage-8
frames):

> `si sveglia · si alza · si prepara · si riposa · si rilassa · si addormenta`

Introduce it in **one sentence** and no more: "When you talk about someone else,
the little word is `si`. That's all you need for now." This is prepared lexis, not
a rule. Do not conjugate anything else. Do not draw a table.

**Instruction.** "Stand up and find a **new** partner — not the person you just
worked with. The frames are gone. Tell your new partner about the week of the
person you **just** talked to: *Marco si sveglia alle sei…* Two minutes each. You
can look at the strip on the board, not at your notes."

**CCQ.** "Whose week are you describing — yours or the person you just spoke to?"
→ *The person I just spoke to.* · "Can I use my notes?" → *No — the board strip
only.*

**Then, lockstep, 3 minutes.** Take three or four reports: "One thing about
Marco's week?" Accept everything. Recast the reflexive pair without comment —
"ah, si alza tardi, anche lui" — and write what you heard down for stage 10.

**Success criterion for this stage.** The learner produces at least **four**
sentences about another person and is understood. Accuracy of the pronoun is
explicitly **not** the criterion, and you should say so if asked: "Next lesson is
about exactly that."

**Correction stance.** Fluency. Delayed, collected, dealt with at stage 10.

---

### 10 · Delayed feedback + close — 4 min · lockstep · `A13`

**Purpose.** Deal with the fluency-stage errors without having interrupted
fluency, and set homework that L2 opens with.

**Instruction.** "Six things I heard. Two of them are **good** — worth stealing.
Four have something small wrong. Thirty seconds with your partner: which two are
the good ones?"

Write up six items you actually heard in stages 8 and 9, anonymised and verbatim —
four with one error, two correct and worth copying. If you did not collect six,
write four; inventing them wastes the format. Confirm, briefly, in under two
minutes. Where the error is the reflexive pronoun, **give the correct form and do
not explain it** — "`mi alzo`, not `alzo`. Why, next lesson."

**Homework.** Say it, and write it on the board:

1. "Finish the grid from exercise 3. There are **sixteen** altogether; most of you
   have twelve or thirteen. The key is in your file."
2. "Exercise 5: eight sentences about your own weekday. Written."
3. "**Record forty seconds** on your phone: *il mio martedì*. Keep it — you'll
   compare it with your final recording in Lesson 5. Don't script it."

**Next lesson opens with** the noticing grid from stage 6, and with the wrong
third-person forms from stage 9. Bring the grid: L2's presentation is built out of
what this group collected, not out of a table.

## Exercises and answer keys

### Exercise 1 — *La settimana di Giulia*: vero o falso? `[ITA?]`

1. Giulia si alza subito quando si sveglia.
2. Fa colazione al bar.
3. Il martedì deve sbrigarsi.
4. Il sabato si sveglia con la sveglia.
5. Vede Sara una volta al mese.
6. La domenica sera si addormenta davanti alla televisione.

**Key.** 1 **F** — *non mi alzo subito: resto a letto dieci minuti* [2]. ·
2 **F** — *faccio colazione in piedi, in cucina* [2]. · 3 **V** [4]. · 4 **F** —
*il sabato non mi sveglio con la sveglia* [7]. · 5 **F** — *ci vediamo quasi ogni
settimana* [6]. · 6 **V** [8]. **1 mark each, 6 marks.**

*The quoted justification and the paragraph number are part of the key, not
decoration: a learner who got an item wrong needs to know where in the text the
answer was, and a teacher needs to be able to replay eight seconds instead of
explaining.*

### Exercise 2 — Giulia's week, day by day `[ITA?]`

Complete the grid. Two cells are already done.

| Giorno | Che cosa fa |
| --- | --- |
| lunedì – venerdì, mattina | si sveglia alle 6.30, si alza dopo 10 minuti |
| martedì e giovedì | |
| mercoledì | |
| giovedì sera | |
| sabato | |
| domenica sera | si addormenta davanti alla TV alle 10 |

**Key.** martedì e giovedì — *deve sbrigarsi: alle sei ha il corso di yoga; quei
giorni non si riposa* [4]. · mercoledì — *torna a casa presto, si rilassa sul
divano con una serie, non fa niente* [5]. · giovedì sera — *esce con Sara* [6]. ·
sabato — *non si sveglia con la sveglia, si alza quando vuole, va al mercato,
pulisce; il pomeriggio vengono i genitori* [7]. **1 mark per row, 4 marks.**
Accept any answer that carries the main fact; this is a listening check, not a
dictation.

*Note the shift.* The grid asks about Giulia in the **third person** while the
recording is in the first. That is deliberate and it is receptive only: it puts
`si sveglia` in front of learners four stages before stage 9 asks them to say it.

**Items 7 and 16 are the interesting ones** and the key must say so, because a
learner marking their own work will think they are wrong: in `devo sbrigarmi` and

of standing in front of it. Both are correct, both are Italian, and **why** is
L2 stage 5. If a learner found them, they have found L2's hardest point on their
own. If they did not, they have lost nothing.

*A learner who collects 12 or more of the 16 has done this stage successfully.*

### Exercise 4 — Supported gap-fill `[ITA?]`

Giulia is talking about her week. Put one phrase from the box in each gap. Each
phrase is used **once**, and **two are not needed**.

> **Box:** `mi sveglio` · `mi alzo` · `mi faccio la doccia` · `mi vesto` ·
> `mi preparo` · `devo sbrigarmi` · `mi occupo` · `mi riposo` · `mi rilasso` ·
> `mi addormento` · `mi trovo` · `ci vediamo`

1. Durante la settimana ______ alle sei e mezza.
2. Non ______ subito: resto a letto dieci minuti.
3. La mattina ______ e poi faccio colazione in cucina.
4. ______ in venti minuti: non ho tempo per altro.
5. In ufficio ______ dei clienti stranieri.
6. Il martedì ______, perché alle sei ho il corso di yoga.
7. Quei giorni non ______ per niente.
8. Il mercoledì ______ sul divano con una serie.
9. Io e Sara ______ quasi ogni settimana.
10. La domenica sera ______ davanti alla televisione.

**Key.** 1 mi sveglio · 2 mi alzo · 3 mi faccio la doccia · 4 mi preparo ·
5 mi occupo · 6 devo sbrigarmi · 7 mi riposo · 8 mi rilasso · 9 ci vediamo ·
10 mi addormento. **Not needed:** `mi vesto`, `mi trovo`. **1 mark each, 10
marks.**

*Item 3 is the one that catches people: `mi vesto` fits the grammar and not the
meaning — you do not get dressed and then have breakfast in the shower. If a
learner puts `mi vesto` in 3, the problem is reading, not Italian, and the
response is to ask them to read the second half of the sentence aloud.*

### Exercise 5 — *Il mio giorno tipo* (homework, written) `[ITA?]`

Write **eight** sentences about a normal weekday of yours. Rules: use at least
**six** different verbs from the list in stage 3; every sentence has a time or a
day in it; all eight are about **you**.

**Model answer** (this is what eight good sentences look like — not a ceiling):

> Mi sveglio alle sette meno un quarto, ma mi alzo alle sette. Mi faccio la doccia
> e mi vesto in dieci minuti. Alle otto esco di casa e vado al lavoro in autobus.
> In ufficio mi occupo delle email e dei clienti. Il lunedì devo sbrigarmi, perché
> alle sei ho il corso di italiano. La sera mi riposo e non faccio niente. Il
> giovedì mi rilasso con gli amici al bar. La domenica sera mi addormento presto,
> verso le dieci.

**What it has to do** — the three things to check, and the only three:

1. **Eight sentences**, each with a time or a day.
2. **Six or more** different verbs from the stage-3 list.
3. Every reflexive verb has its little word, and the little word is `mi`.

Nothing else is marked. A learner self-checking at home checks those three and
stops.

## Teacher notes

Unit-level error profile, register decisions and pacing:
[`unit-01-teacher-notes.md`](unit-01-teacher-notes.md). The three errors below are
the ones **this lesson** will produce.

**Predictable errors**

1. **Dropped pronoun.** `*Sveglio alle sette` for *I wake up at seven*. The
   English verb has no pronoun, so the learner produces none. Expect it in stages
   3, 8 and 9 from most of the group, and expect it in writing too. **Do:**
   recast, do not explain — "mi sveglio alle sette, sì" — and put two instances on
   the delayed-feedback board. The explanation is L2's job, one lesson away;
   explaining it now burns L2's discovery stage, which is the most valuable ten
   minutes in the unit.
2. **Pronoun after the verb.** `*Alzo mi alle sette`, by analogy with *I get
   **myself** up*. Less frequent than (1) and stickier, because it feels
   reasoned. **Do:** on the board, write the correct pair immediately next to the
   wrong one and say the two aloud, one after the other. Do not draw a rule.
3. **Third-person pronoun kept as `mi`.** At stage 9, `*Marco mi sveglia alle
   sei` — the strip on the board says `si sveglia`, and the learner says `mi`
   anyway, because `mi sveglio` has just been drilled for forty minutes. This is
   not a failure of stage 9; it is what stage 9 is **for**. **Do:** write two of
   them down verbatim and open L2 with them. Nothing else.

A fourth, minor and not worth a stage: `*ogni giorni`. One-word correction —
`ogni giorno` — and move on. It is maintenance, not this lesson's business.

**Correction stance per stage.** Stages 3, 5, 6, 7 — **accuracy**, immediate, and
in stage 3 narrow to the reflexive pair only. Stages 1, 2, 8, 9 — **fluency**,
delayed, collected and dealt with at stage 10. Stage 4 — no correction at all.

**To shorten (−10 min).** Cut stage 9 and run stage 8 rung 2 only, with the frames
left on the board. What you lose is real: stage 9 is the only place the third
person is produced, and it is what L2 opens with. If you cut it, open L2 with the
noticing grid alone and put the board strip (`si sveglia …`) up during L2's
presentation instead. Do **not** cut stage 6 to save time — everything after it
depends on it.

**To extend (+10 min).** After stage 9, learners write six sentences comparing

the contrast language L4 stage 7 needs, so the ten minutes are not spent twice.

## Self-study version — 50 minutes

Every stage has a solo route. Where the live stage needs a partner, the solo
equivalent is named and does the same job by other means.

| Live stage | Solo equivalent | Min |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Opener (pairs) | Say the three things **out loud** to yourself, then write them. Speaking aloud is not optional — it is the whole stage. | 3 |
| 2 Engage (solo → pairs) | Same grid, written. Then count your boxes and write the number down; you will check it again at the end of the unit. | 4 |
| 3 Lexis set A (lockstep → solo) | Read the eleven aloud twice: infinitive, then `io` form. Cover the English column and test yourself down the list. Then write the five true sentences. | 9 |
| 4 Listen 1 (solo → lockstep) | Identical. Read the script in the audio file **once silently**, then cover it and answer the one question from memory. | 3 |
| 5 Listen 2 (pairs compare) | Write your answers, check the key, then re-read **only** the paragraphs for the items you got wrong — the key gives the numbers. | 7 |
| 6 Noticing grid (solo → pairs) | Identical, with the transcript open. The completed grid is the key; compare only **after** you have stopped finding new ones. | 7 |
| 7 Gap-fill (solo → pairs) | Identical; key supplied. | 6 |
| 8 Routine ladder rung 2 (pairs) | Say all five frames **out loud** and record yourself on your phone. Then listen back once with one question: did every sentence have a time in it? | 7 |
| 9 Activate rung 3 (pairs) | Pick a real person you know. With the frames covered, record 90 seconds about **their** week, using the `si` strip. Then write the first two sentences down and check the little word. | 2 |
| 10 Delayed feedback (lockstep) | Listen to your rung-3 recording once, with one question only: **where did I stop, and why?** Write two lines. Not a transcription. | 2 |
| | **Total** | **50** |

**The test this passes.** A learner with this file, a phone and no partner can
complete every stage and **know whether they succeeded** — exercises 1–4 have
keys, exercise 5 has three checkable criteria, and stages 8 and 9 produce a
recording the learner judges against a single named question. Nothing in the
lesson depends on having someone to talk to.

## After the lesson

- **Homework.** (1) Finish exercise 3 to all sixteen. (2) Exercise 5, eight
  sentences, self-checked against its three criteria. (3) A 40-second phone
  recording, *il mio martedì*, unscripted and **kept** — L5 stage 1 plays it back
  and L5 compares it with the final recording. Total ~20 minutes.
- **Next lesson opens with** the noticing grid and the wrong third-person forms
  from stage 9. L2's presentation is guided discovery out of those two things; if
  learners arrive without the grid, L2 stage 2 has nothing to work on.
