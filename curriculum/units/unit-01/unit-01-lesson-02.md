## Stages in full

### 1 · Opener — pool the grid — 8 min · pairs → lockstep

**Purpose.** Make the class's own collected evidence the starting material, so
that the rule in stage 2 is something they find rather than something they are
handed. Also: find out how many they got, which tells you how hard to push
stage 2.

**Instruction.** "Grids out. With your partner: compare, and **count**. How many
did you find between you? Two minutes." Then: "Hands up — who has twelve? Fourteen?
Sixteen?"

**CCQ.** "Are we comparing answers, or adding them together?" → *Comparing, then
counting how many we have between us.*

**Then, lockstep, 5 minutes.** Build this on the board — a column of pairs as the
class calls them out. Do not sort them yet; sorting is stage 2.

> mi sveglio · mi alzo · mi faccio la doccia · mi vesto · mi preparo · mi occupo ·
> **sbrigarmi** · mi riposo · mi rilasso · **ci vediamo** · **ci sentiamo** ·
> mi addormento · mi trovo · **lamentarmi**

Then, next to it, write the two wrong third-person forms you collected at L1
stage 9, exactly as you heard them, unlabelled:

> `*Marco mi sveglia alle sei` · `*Anna alza alle otto`

Say: "We'll come back to these two in six minutes." Say nothing else about them.
Leaving them visible and unexplained for six minutes is what makes stage 2 land.

**Correction stance.** None yet. Nothing on this board is marked right or wrong.

### 2 · Presentation — guided discovery — 12 min · lockstep

**Purpose.** Derive the two rules that matter most from the board, in this order,
because it is the order of difficulty for an English-L1 learner. The third rule
has its own stage.

**Run it as four questions, in this order.** Each question has its expected answer
and what you do with it. Do not pre-empt: wait for the group.

**Question 1 (2 min).** "Look at the whole column. What does every single one
have?"
→ *A little word: `mi` or `ci`.*
Write at the top of the column: **PRONOME**.

**Question 2 (3 min).** "Try taking it away. *Sveglio alle sette.* Is that
Italian?"
→ *No.*
Push once: "What does `Sveglio alle sette` actually sound like to an Italian?"
→ Nothing — it is simply broken; `svegliare` without a pronoun means *to wake
someone else up*, so `Sveglio alle sette` is a sentence waiting for an object.
**Write rule 1 on the board and leave it there for the rest of the lesson:**

> **1. Il pronome è obbligatorio.** `mi sveglio` — never `*sveglio` for *I wake
> up*.

This is the single most important sentence in the unit. It is the error that
survives to B1 if it survives Unit 1.

**Question 3 (4 min).** "Three of them say `ci`, not `mi`. Which three, and
what's different about them?"
→ `ci vediamo`, `ci sentiamo` — and the answer is *they're about two people*, not
one.
Then: "So what decides which little word you use?"
→ *Who it's about.*
**Write rule 2:**

> **2. Il pronome cambia con il soggetto.**

**Question 4 (3 min).** "Now the two sentences on the right. What's wrong with
them?"
→ `*Marco mi sveglia` has the **wrong** pronoun for Marco; `*Anna alza` has
**none**. Fix them on the board to `Marco si sveglia`, `Anna si alza`.

**Then build the paradigm *with* the class (3 min).** Ask for each form; supply
only what they cannot produce. Write it up as you go:

| | `svegliarsi` |
| --- | --- |
| io | **mi** sveglio |
| tu | **ti** svegli |
| lui / lei / Lei | **si** sveglia |
| noi | **ci** svegliamo |
| voi | **vi** svegliate |
| loro | **si** svegliano |

**Then say exactly this, and nothing more, about the verb itself:** "Look at the
verb endings. `sveglio, svegli, sveglia…` — is there anything new?" → *No, they're
the normal present-tense endings.* "Right. The verb is ordinary. **Only the little
word is new.**" That sentence halves the perceived difficulty of the lesson and it
is true.

**CCQ.** "Which changes when the subject changes — the pronoun, the verb, or
both?" → *Both.* · "Which one is the new thing?" → *The pronoun.*

**Deferred, and say so if asked.** "Does `si` ever mean *one* or *people in
general*?" → Yes, and it looks identical. **That is Unit 4.** Write the learner's
name next to the question and move on; it is a good question and answering it here
costs you stage 3.

**Correction stance.** Accuracy, immediate, and metalanguage is welcome — this is
one of the two stages in the unit where it is.

### 3 · Practice — paradigm sprint — 10 min · solo, timed → lockstep · `A3` `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** Retrieval speed on the form, so that stages 4–8 are not spent
computing it. `A3` builds speed on form only; it is never the last stage of a
lesson and it does not count as practice of *use*.

**Instruction.** "Exercise 1. Three grids, three verbs. **Ninety seconds each.**
Round 1 you can look at the board. Round 2 the board is covered. Round 3 the board
is covered and the verb is an `-irsi` one, so think. Go."

Cover the paradigm table physically for rounds 2 and 3 — turn the board, or stand
in front of it. If it is visible, the format does nothing.

**CCQ.** "Can you look at the board in round two?" → *No.*

Check each round against the key immediately — thirty seconds, lockstep, learners
mark their own. Do not discuss errors during the sprint; collect them for stage 6.

**Correction stance.** Accuracy, self-checked against the key.

---

### 4 · Practice — transform line — 12 min · solo → pairs · `A4` `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** One stated change, applied ten times, so that "the pronoun follows
the subject" stops being a rule on a board and becomes a reflex.

**Instruction.** "Exercise 2. Ten sentences, all about **me**. Rewrite every one
so it is about **i miei coinquilini** — my flatmates. Two changes every time, and
I'll do the first two with you." Model items 1 and 2 aloud: *Mi sveglio alle sette*
→ *Si svegliano alle sette*; *Mi alzo subito* → *Si alzano subito*. "Seven minutes
on your own, then three minutes comparing with your partner. **We'll check the
last three together** — they each need a decision."

**CCQ.** "How many things change in each sentence?" → *Two — the little word and
the verb ending.* · "Are the flatmates one person or more than one?" → *More than
one.*

**Correction stance.** Accuracy, immediate, on items 8–10 in lockstep. Items 1–7
are self- and peer-checked against the key.

---

### 5 · Presentation 2 — pronoun placement with modals — 12 min · lockstep → solo `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** Rule 3, which is the one that produces the most durable wrong form,
because both of the correct Italian options are unlike English and a learner who
is given only one of them invents a third.

**Instruction, part 1 (4 min, lockstep).** "Back to your grid. Two of the sixteen
were odd. Which ones?" → `sbrigarmi` [4] and `lamentarmi` [7]. "Where's the little
word in those two?" → *Stuck on the end of the verb.* "And what's in front of the
verb in both?" → *`devo`, `posso` — a modal."

Write these three lines on the board, in this order, and leave them up:

> **Mi devo alzare presto.** ✓
> **Devo alzarmi presto.** ✓
> ~~Devo mi alzare presto.~~ ✗

Say: "Two correct options. **Both.** Not one proper one and one casual one — two
equally good ones. Italians use both, and so can you. The third is the one that
feels most logical in English and it is the one that does not exist."

**This is the whole teaching point of the stage.** Teaching `mi devo alzare` as
primary and `devo alzarmi` as a variant guarantees the error — the learner
remembers "pronoun before the modal" as a rule, meets `devo alzarmi` in the wild,
and concludes the rule is "pronoun somewhere near the front", which produces
`*devo mi alzare`.

**CCQ.** "How many correct places can the pronoun go?" → *Two.* · "Which one is
better?" → *Neither.* · "Where can it never go?" → *Between the modal and the
infinitive.*

**Instruction, part 2 (8 min, solo then checked).** "Exercise 3. Part A: four
sentences — write each one in **the other** correct order. Part B: four sentences —
tick the correct ones and fix the wrong ones. Six minutes, then we check."

**Correction stance.** Accuracy, immediate. Check part B in lockstep; part A can
be self-checked.

### 6 · Practice — error clinic — 10 min · pairs → lockstep · `A8` `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** Move from *producing* the form to *judging* it, which is the skill a
learner needs when nobody is correcting them. The items are the errors this course
predicts for an English L1, not generic mistakes.

**Instruction.** "Exercise 4. Eight sentences. Some have **one** thing wrong.
**Some are already right** — I'm not telling you how many. With your partner:
decide, and if it's wrong, fix it. Arguing about it is the point. Six minutes."

**CCQ.** "Does every sentence have a mistake?" → *No.* · "How many mistakes in the
ones that are wrong?" → *One.*

**Then, lockstep, 4 minutes.** Go through all eight. For each wrong one, ask "what
*rule* is it breaking?" and point at rule 1, 2 or 3 on the board. That is what the
three rules are still up there for.

**Correction stance.** Accuracy, and the only other stage in the unit where
metalanguage is encouraged.

---

### 7 · Reciprocals, and `Sbrigati!` — 10 min · lockstep → pairs · `A′` lexis `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** Show the same morphology doing a **second job**. `ci vediamo` is not
"we see ourselves"; it is "we see *each other*". One form, two functions — which
is why these two items are taught here, with the grammar, rather than as
vocabulary in L3.

**Instruction, part 1 (4 min, lockstep).** Write these two on the board:

> `Mi vedo allo specchio.` — I see **myself** in the mirror.
> `Io e Sara ci vediamo ogni venerdì.` — Sara and I see **each other** every
> Friday.

"Same little word system. Different job. With `noi`, `voi` and `loro`, it usually
means *each other*." Then give the two items properly:

| Italian | English | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `vedersi` | to see each other | `Ci vediamo!` is also just *See you!* |
| `sentirsi` | to be in touch | `Ci sentiamo!` — *Talk soon!* Both are everyday goodbyes. |

Teach `Ci vediamo!` and `Ci sentiamo!` **as farewells** as well as verbs. They are
the two most useful things in this lesson outside the classroom and learners will
use them on the way out.

**Instruction, part 2 (2 min, lockstep).** "One more, as a phrase, and I am
deliberately not explaining it. **`Sbrigati!`** — hurry up, to one person.
**`Sbrigatevi!`** — to more than one. Learn them as phrases. We take apart how
they're built in Unit 7." Write both up.

**Instruction, part 3 (4 min, solo then pairs).** "Exercise 5, six items. Three
minutes on your own, then say all six aloud to your partner."

**CCQ.** "`Ci vediamo domani` — am I seeing myself tomorrow, or seeing you?"
→ *Seeing you / each other.* · "Is `Sbrigati!` something we're analysing today?"
→ *No — it's a phrase.*

**Correction stance.** Accuracy on exercise 5; nothing marked on the farewells.

### 8 · Production — *Francesca e Dario* information gap — 12 min · pairs · `A7` `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** The lesson's one stage of real use. The gap is genuine: each learner
holds half the information, the questions must be asked in the **third person**,
and the answers come back in the third person — which is the form stage 2 built and
nothing before now has required in conversation.

**Instruction.** "In pairs. One of you is A, one is B. You each have **half** the
information: you know all about one person and nothing about the other. **Don't
show each other the cards.** Ask questions, fill your gaps. You'll need `A che ora
si sveglia…?` and `Di che cosa si occupa…?` — they're on your card. Ten minutes.
When you've both finished, one last question on the card."

**CCQ.** "Can you show each other the cards?" → *No.* · "Whose column are you
filling in — the one you know, or the one you don't?" → *The one I don't know.*

**Card A** `[ITA?]`

> **You know about FRANCESCA. Ask your partner about DARIO.**
>
> | | Francesca | Dario |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | si sveglia | alle 6.45 | |
> | si alza | alle 7.00 | |
> | si fa la doccia | la sera | |
> | esce di casa | alle 7.40 | |
> | si occupa di | i clienti stranieri | |
> | deve sbrigarsi il… | martedì — corso di yoga | |
> | si addormenta | verso le 23.00 | |
>
> **Useful questions:** `A che ora si sveglia…?` · `Quando si fa la doccia?` ·
> `Di che cosa si occupa?` · `Che giorno deve sbrigarsi? Perché?`
>
> **Last question, when you have both finished:** `Con chi preferisci vivere,
> Francesca o Dario? Perché?` Answer in one sentence.

**Card B** `[ITA?]`

> **You know about DARIO. Ask your partner about FRANCESCA.**
>
> | | Francesca | Dario |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | si sveglia | | alle 8.30 |
> | si alza | | subito |
> | si fa la doccia | | la mattina |
> | esce di casa | | alle 9.15 |
> | si occupa di | | il magazzino |
> | deve sbrigarsi il… | | giovedì — partita di calcio |
> | si addormenta | | verso l'una |
>
> **Useful questions:** `A che ora si sveglia…?` · `Quando si fa la doccia?` ·
> `Di che cosa si occupa?` · `Che giorno deve sbrigarsi? Perché?`
>
> **Last question, when you have both finished:** `Con chi preferisci vivere,
> Francesca o Dario? Perché?` Answer in one sentence.

**Success criterion.** Both grids complete and matching, in ten minutes, with the
questions asked in Italian. The final question is the one that connects to L3 —
"who would you rather live with" needs a character adjective the learner does not
have yet, and noticing that gap is useful on the day before L3.

**Correction stance.** Fluency during. Circulate with a pen and collect five or
six things for stage 9 — in particular, any question that broke. Do not interrupt.

### 9 · Delayed feedback + close — 4 min · lockstep · `A13`

**Purpose.** Deal with the production-stage errors, and set homework that is
genuinely consolidation rather than more of the same.

**Instruction.** "Six things from the last ten minutes. Two are **good** — worth
stealing. Thirty seconds: which two?"

Write up six items you actually heard, anonymised and verbatim: four with one error,
two correct and worth copying. Where the error is a placement error, point at the
three lines still on the board from stage 5 rather than re-explaining.

**Homework.** "Exercise 6 — twelve items, mixed, everything from today. Then one
thing out loud: **record ninety seconds about the week of someone you live with or
work with.** Third person. Keep it; L4 uses it." (~25 minutes.)

**Next lesson** leaves the grammar alone and spends ninety minutes on **people** —
which is what you will need in order to answer the last question on the
information-gap card properly.

## Exercises and answer keys

### Exercise 1 — Paradigm sprint, three rounds `[ITA?]`

**Round 1 — `alzarsi`** (board visible, 90 seconds)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| io | |
| tu | |
| lui / lei | |
| noi | |
| voi | |
| loro | |

**Key.** mi alzo · ti alzi · si alza · ci alziamo · vi alzate · si alzano.

**Round 2 — `rilassarsi`** (board covered, 90 seconds)

**Key.** mi rilasso · ti rilassi · si rilassa · ci rilassiamo · vi rilassate ·
si rilassano.

**Round 3 — `vestirsi`** (board covered, 90 seconds). *This one is `-irsi`, so the
verb endings are the `-ire` ones. The pronouns do not change at all — that is the
point of the round.*

**Key.** mi vesto · ti vesti · si veste · ci vestiamo · vi vestite · si vestono.

**6 marks per round, 18 total.** A learner who gets 16+ on round 3 has the form.
A learner below 12 on round 3 should redo this exercise before L5, and the
checkpoint will tell them the same thing.

### Exercise 2 — Transform line: `io` → `i miei coinquilini` `[ITA?]`

Rewrite each sentence so it is about **i miei coinquilini**.

1. Mi sveglio alle sette.
2. Mi alzo subito.
3. Mi preparo in dieci minuti.
4. Mi vesto in camera.
5. Mi occupo della cucina.
6. Mi addormento davanti alla televisione.
7. Mi trovo bene in questa casa.
8. Mi faccio la doccia la sera.
9. Esco di casa alle otto e mezza.
10. Il sabato mi riposo e non faccio niente.

**Key.** 1 Si svegliano alle sette. · 2 Si alzano subito. · 3 Si preparano in
dieci minuti. · 4 Si vestono in camera. · 5 Si occupano della cucina. ·
6 Si addormentano davanti alla televisione. · 7 Si trovano bene in questa casa. ·
8 **Si fanno** la doccia la sera. · 9 **Escono** di casa alle otto e mezza. ·
10 Il sabato **si riposano e non fanno** niente. **1 mark each, 10 marks.**

**Why the last three need a decision, in one line each** — this is the part of the
key that teaches:

- **8** — `fare` is irregular: `si fanno`, not `*si fano`. The pronoun was never
  the hard part here.
- **9** — `uscire` is **not reflexive**. There is no pronoun to change, and adding
  one (`*si escono`) is the commonest wrong answer on this exercise.
- **10** — two verbs, and **only one of them is reflexive**. `si riposano` takes
  the pronoun; `fanno` does not.

### Exercise 3 — Pronoun placement with modals `[ITA?]`

**Part A.** Rewrite each sentence in **the other** correct order.

1. Mi devo sbrigare.
2. Vuoi rilassarti un po'?
3. Non mi voglio alzare presto.
4. Possiamo vederci domani?

**Key.** 1 Devo sbrigarmi. · 2 Ti vuoi rilassare un po'? · 3 Non voglio alzarmi
presto. · 4 Ci possiamo vedere domani? **1 mark each.** Both versions in every
pair are equally correct and equally common; if a learner asks which one Italians
"really" say, the answer is *both*, and item 4 is the one where the attached form
is marginally more frequent in speech.

**Part B.** Tick the correct sentences. Fix the wrong ones.

5. Devo mi preparare.
6. Ti devi riposare.
7. Vogliamo ci sentire domani.
8. Non posso alzarmi presto.

**Key.** 5 ✗ → **Mi devo preparare** *or* **Devo prepararmi** — the pronoun can
never sit between the modal and the infinitive. · 6 ✓ · 7 ✗ → **Ci vogliamo
sentire domani** *or* **Vogliamo sentirci domani** — same error as 5. · 8 ✓.
**1 mark each, 8 marks for the exercise.**

### Exercise 4 — Error clinic `[ITA?]`

Some of these sentences have **one** thing wrong. Some are already correct. Decide,
and fix the wrong ones.

1. Sveglio alle sei e mezza.
2. Alzo mi subito.
3. Mia sorella mi alza alle nove.
4. Devo mi sbrigare, sono in ritardo.
5. Ci vediamo domani!
6. I miei coinquilini si rilassa in salotto.
7. Mi trovo bene in questa città.
8. Ogni giorni mi riposo dopo pranzo.

**Key**, with the reason in one line of English — the reason is the part that
transfers:

| # | Verdict | Correction | Why it is wrong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | ✗ | **Mi sveglio** alle sei e mezza. | The pronoun is obligatory. English *I wake up* has no pronoun, so there is nothing to translate — and nothing is exactly what gets produced. (Rule 1) |
| 2 | ✗ | **Mi alzo** subito. | The pronoun goes **before** the conjugated verb, not after it. *I get myself up* is the English model that causes this. (Rule 3) |
| 3 | ✗ | Mia sorella **si** alza alle nove. | The pronoun follows the **subject**, and the subject is *mia sorella*, not *io*. (Rule 2) |
| 4 | ✗ | **Mi devo sbrigare** *or* **Devo sbrigarmi**. | Two correct positions, and between the modal and the infinitive is neither of them. (Rule 3) |
| 5 | ✓ | — | Correct. `Ci vediamo!` is both "we see each other" and the everyday "see you!". |
| 6 | ✗ | I miei coinquilini **si rilassano** in salotto. | The pronoun is right; the **verb** is not. The subject is plural, so both parts have to be. |
| 7 | ✓ | — | Correct. |
| 8 | ✗ | **Ogni giorno** mi riposo dopo pranzo. | `ogni` is always followed by a singular. Nothing to do with reflexives — it is in here so that "find the reflexive error" is not a winning strategy. |

**8 marks**, 1 per item, and a learner only scores the mark if the verdict is
right — ticking a correct sentence as wrong loses the mark.

### Exercise 5 — Reciprocals, and two phrases `[ITA?]`

Complete with the right form.

1. Io e Luca ______ (vedersi) ogni venerdì.
2. ______ (sentirsi) domani, allora!
3. Marco e Anna non ______ (vedersi) da due mesi.
4. Tu e tua sorella ______ (sentirsi) spesso?
5. Ragazzi, ______! Il treno parte fra cinque minuti.
6. Dai, ______! Siamo in ritardo. *(to one friend)*

**Key.** 1 ci vediamo · 2 Ci sentiamo · 3 si vedono · 4 vi sentite ·
5 **Sbrigatevi** · 6 **Sbrigati**. **1 mark each, 6 marks.**

*Items 5 and 6 are the only imperatives in this unit and they are taught and
tested as fixed phrases. If a learner asks how `Sbrigati` is built, the honest
answer is "Unit 7". `sbrigarsi` is seeded here precisely because U7 needs it.*

### Exercise 6 — Mixed consolidation (homework) `[ITA?]`

Put the verb in brackets into the right form, in the right place.

1. La mattina io ______ (svegliarsi) alle sei, ma ______ (alzarsi) alle sei e
   venti.
2. Mio fratello ______ (prepararsi) in cinque minuti. Io no.
3. Noi ______ (trovarsi) molto bene in questo quartiere.
4. I miei vicini ______ (addormentarsi) sempre davanti alla televisione.
5. Tu a che ora ______ (alzarsi) la domenica?
6. Ragazzi, voi ______ (riposarsi) mai?
7. Prima di uscire, devo ______ (prepararsi). *(both correct orders)*
8. Non ______ (potere / rilassarsi, io) con questo rumore. *(both correct orders)*
9. Il martedì mia madre ______ (dovere / sbrigarsi), perché lavora alle sette.
10. Io e mia sorella ______ (sentirsi) ogni domenica.
11. Marta e Chiara ______ (vedersi) in palestra il giovedì.
12. Il sabato ______ (rilassarsi, io) e non faccio niente.

**Key.** 1 mi sveglio … mi alzo · 2 si prepara · 3 ci troviamo · 4 si addormentano ·
5 ti alzi · 6 vi riposate · 7 **prepararmi** *or* **mi devo preparare** ·
8 **posso rilassarmi** *or* **mi posso rilassare** · 9 **deve sbrigarsi** *or*
**si deve sbrigare** · 10 ci sentiamo · 11 si vedono · 12 mi rilasso.
**13 marks** (item 1 has two gaps).

**Self-check instruction for a solo learner, and it is part of the exercise:**
count your marks out of 13. Below 10, redo exercise 1 round 3 and exercise 3 before

## Teacher notes

Unit-level error profile and pacing:
[`unit-01-teacher-notes.md`](unit-01-teacher-notes.md). The three below are **this
lesson's**.

**Predictable errors**

1. **`*Devo mi alzare`.** The pronoun placed between the modal and the infinitive.
   An English-L1 learner produces it because *both* correct Italian orders are
   unlike English, so they build a third that splits the difference. Expect it in
   exercise 3 part B, in exercise 6 items 7–9, and in stage 8 whenever `deve
   sbrigarsi` comes up. **Do:** point at the three lines on the board from stage 5.
   Do not re-explain — the reason you wrote them up and left them there is so that
   this correction costs you four seconds and not ninety.
2. **Pronoun right, verb wrong, with plural subjects.** `*I miei coinquilini si
   rilassa` — the learner has learned "the pronoun follows the subject" so
   thoroughly that it absorbs all their attention and the verb ending is left
   behind. Expect it in exercise 2 and all through stage 8. **Do:** one question
   back, every time — "how many flatmates?" — and let them self-correct. It is an
   attention error, not a knowledge error, and explaining it does nothing.
3. **Adding a pronoun to a verb that does not take one.** `*si escono di casa`,
   `*mi faccio colazione`. This appears **only after** the lesson has worked, which
   is worth saying out loud to the group: it is a sign of learning, not of
   confusion. **Do:** exercise 2 item 9 exists for this. Name the three
   non-reflexive verbs the unit uses most — `uscire`, `fare colazione`, `andare` —
   and move on. Do not attempt a general rule about which Italian verbs are
   reflexive; there is not one worth an A2 learner's time.

**Correction stance per stage.** Stages 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 — **accuracy**,
immediate, with metalanguage welcome at 2 and 6. Stages 1 and 8 — **fluency**,
delayed, collected and dealt with at stage 9.

**To shorten (−12 min).** Cut stage 7 to four minutes — give the two reciprocals
and the two `Sbrigati` phrases from the board, drop exercise 5 into homework — and
take two minutes off stages 3 and 6. Do **not** cut stage 5 or stage 8. Stage 5 is
the only place the modal placement is taught and the error it prevents is the
unit's most durable; stage 8 is the lesson's only stage of real use, and a PPP
lesson with the third P removed is a worksheet.

**To extend (+12 min).** After stage 8, learners write the two information-gap
profiles up as continuous prose in the third person, about eighty words each, then
swap and check each other's against the card. It is the same content a third time,
which is the point: the form gets another twenty repetitions and the lesson needs
no new material.

## Self-study version — 55 minutes

This is the unit's most form-heavy lesson, so it is the one where a solo learner is
at **least** disadvantage — most of it needs no partner at all. The two stages that
do are named below.

| Live stage | Solo equivalent | Min |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Opener (pairs → lockstep) | Check your own grid against the L1 key and count. Then write out the fourteen pairs in one column, as the class would have built them on the board. The writing matters: stage 2 asks you to look down a column. | 5 |
| 2 Presentation (lockstep) | Work through the **four questions** in the stage above, in order, in writing, before you read the answer underneath each one. Cover the answer with your hand. Then fill in the `svegliarsi` paradigm yourself and check it against exercise 1. | 8 |
| 3 Paradigm sprint (solo, timed) | Identical — this format needs no partner. Time yourself: 90 seconds a round, three rounds, and cover the page for rounds 2 and 3. Round 2 from memory is the part that makes it work. | 8 |
| 4 Transform line (solo → pairs) | Identical; key supplied. Instead of comparing with a partner, do items 8–10 **again** on a fresh line without looking at your first attempt, then compare the two. | 8 |
| 5 Modal placement (lockstep → solo) | Copy the three board lines — two ticks, one cross — onto the inside cover of your notebook. You will look at them for the next six units. Then exercise 3. | 8 |
| 6 Error clinic (pairs) | Solo with the key, and the key explains **why** each error is an error, not only what the fix is. Arguing with a partner is what you are missing, so replace it: before you look at the key, write one line for each item saying *which of the three rules* it breaks. | 7 |
| 7 Reciprocals (lockstep → pairs) | Read the two contrast sentences aloud. Then exercise 5, written, and say all six aloud. Learn `Ci vediamo!` and `Ci sentiamo!` as goodbyes — use one of them in your next message to someone. | 5 |
| 8 Information gap (pairs) | **Card B is your answer sheet.** Cover it. Take Card A, and ask every question **out loud** — actually aloud, this is the target skill — then uncover one line of Card B at a time to check. Then answer the final question in one written sentence. | 4 |
| 9 Delayed feedback (lockstep) | Record yourself reading your six answers to exercise 6 items 7–12. Listen back with one question: did the pronoun land in one of the two legal places every time? | 2 |
| | **Total** | **55** |

## After the lesson

- **Homework.** Exercise 6, twelve items, self-checked against the key and its
  below-10 instruction. Then a 90-second phone recording about **the week of
  someone you live or work with** — third person, unscripted, kept. (~25 minutes.)
- **Next lesson opens with** the *people* in that recording — L3 stage 2 asks who
  is in it, and then spends the lesson giving you the words to describe them with.
  Keep the recording after that too: L4 stage 2 comes back to it. Nothing in L3 is
  grammar, and that is deliberate — the unit's heavy structure now needs three
  lessons of **use**, not more analysis.
