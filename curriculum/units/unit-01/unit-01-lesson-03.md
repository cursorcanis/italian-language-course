## Stages in full

### 1 · Opener — *chi c'è nella tua vita* — 5 min · pairs → lockstep

**Purpose.** Open the theme with a question the learner can answer badly in
Italian, so that the lesson's vocabulary arrives as a solution.

**Instruction.** "With your partner. **Name three people** who are in your life
every week — not family you never see, people you actually deal with. Say who they
are and one thing about them. In Italian, and if you don't have the word, say it in
English. Ninety seconds each."

**CCQ.** "Three people you know of, or three people you actually see every week?"
→ *Actually see every week.*

**Then, lockstep, 2 minutes.** "Who said a word in English? Which word?" Collect
three or four on the board **in English**, on the right-hand side, and leave them
there. You will cross them off in stages 3 and 5, which is the most satisfying
thing that happens in this lesson.

**Correction stance.** None.

---

### 2 · Engage — the people in your recording — 7 min · solo → pairs

**Purpose.** Connect to L2's homework and turn the gap from stage 1 into a written
list the learner owns. A learner who has written down *"my flatmate — I don't know
how to say what she's like"* will learn `coinquilina` and `socievole` in one
exposure.

**Instruction.** "Last night you recorded ninety seconds about someone's week. On
your own, four minutes: **write down every person who was in that recording**, and
next to each one, two things. One: **what they are to you** — flatmate, colleague,
neighbour. Two: **what they're like.** Italian where you can, English where you
can't, and put a box round the English."

**Grid** (three lines, copied, not printed):

| Chi | Che cosa è per me | Com'è |
| --- | --- | --- |
| | | |

**CCQ.** "Two columns to fill, or three?" → *Three — who, what they are to me, what
they're like.* · "English allowed?" → *Yes, in a box.*

**Then, pairs, 2 minutes.** "Count your boxes. Who has more than four?"

**If a learner did not do L2's homework** — likely in week one — the instruction
is the same but the source is their own life: "the three people from the last
activity." Nothing in the lesson depends on the recording existing.

**Correction stance.** None.

### 3 · Lexis set B (i) — the 8 people items — 10 min · lockstep → solo `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** Deliver the eight relationship items. These are the words that answer
the middle column of the stage-2 grid, and they are deliberately taught **before**
the listening, so that the listening is comprehensible rather than a vocabulary
test.

**The set.** Put it on the board, or hand it out.

| Italian | English | The thing worth saying about it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `il compagno` / `la compagna` | partner | The register-neutral word, and wider than `fidanzato`. If you do not know whether a couple is married, this is the safe word. |
| `il fidanzato` / `la fidanzata` | boyfriend, girlfriend | Literally *engaged*, but used far more loosely than the English *fiancé(e)*. |
| `la coppia` | couple | |
| `il coinquilino` / `la coinquilina` | flatmate | |
| `il collega` / `la collega` | colleague | **`il collega` is masculine and ends in `-a`.** Say this once, now, and point at it again in stage 9 when someone says `*il collego`. |
| `il vicino` / `la vicina` | neighbour | |
| `il conoscente` | acquaintance | English has the word and barely uses it; Italian uses it constantly. Someone you know but are not friends with. |
| `i parenti` | relatives | **Not** *parents* — that is `i genitori`. Full slot at stage 6. |

**Instruction, part 1 (4 min, lockstep).** "Listen first, don't write. I'll say each
one twice with its article. Repeat after me." Then the two questions that make it
stick: "Which of these words do you need for **your** middle column?" and
"Which ones can we now cross off the English list from the start of the lesson?"
Cross them off.

**Instruction, part 2 (6 min, solo).** "Go back to your grid. Fill in the middle
column in Italian, for everyone on your list. Then one more thing: write **one**
sentence with `conoscente` in it, about a real person. That's the word you're least
likely to use and most likely to need."

**CCQ.** "Is `i parenti` your mum and dad?" → *No — aunts, cousins, grandparents.*
· "What's the Italian for *parents*?" → `i genitori`.

**Correction stance.** Accuracy, immediate, on article and gender only.

### 4 · Input — *Tre persone intorno a me* — 12 min · solo → pairs → lockstep · `A1` `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** Hear the set in use by three different speakers before producing any
of it. This is a shortened `A1` — two passes, not three, because the noticing pass
belongs to L1's grammar point and this lesson has no form to notice.

**Instruction.** "Three people, each about a minute, telling you who's around them.
First time: **just listen**, and write down one word — is each person talking about
**family, work, or where they live**? Second time: exercise 2, six statements, and
you decide who said each one. You'll hear it twice."

Play or read cue 2. First pass: the one-word global task. Second pass: exercise 2.

**CCQ.** "First time — how many things am I asking for?" → *One word per speaker.*
· "Second time, am I writing what they said or **who** said it?" → *Who said it.*

**Expected answers to the global task.** Marta — where she lives. Davide — family.
Lina — work. Take these in English in thirty seconds and move on.

**Then, pairs 2 min, lockstep 3 min.** When an answer is wrong, re-read the speaker's
four relevant words rather than explaining. **Marta is deliberately fast**, and
learners should be told so: "The first one is the fastest. That's on purpose — she's
the one you'll meet in real life."

**Correction stance.** Accuracy on exercise 2, checked openly.

### 5 · Lexis set B (ii) — the 9 character items — 14 min · lockstep → solo → pairs `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** Deliver the nine items that let a learner say something **evaluative**
rather than merely factual. *"My sister is nice"* is A1. This stage is the
difference between the can-do goal and A1.

**This is the heaviest stage in the lesson and the one that always overruns.**
Learners want to talk about the people, which is the right instinct and the wrong
stage. Hold the fourteen minutes.

**The set.**

| Italian | English | The thing worth saying about it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `socievole` | sociable | |
| `riservato` | reserved | The useful, non-judgemental opposite of `socievole`. |
| `testardo` | stubborn | |
| `permaloso` | touchy, easily offended | **Very** high spoken frequency and no neat English equivalent. Someone who takes offence easily. Worth teaching properly: learners will hear it constantly. |
| `affidabile` | reliable | |
| `disponibile` | helpful, willing to help | Also *available*. Both senses are everyday. |
| `in gamba` | capable, on the ball | A fixed chunk — `in gamba`, never `*in gambe`, whoever you are talking about. |
| `andare d'accordo (con)` | to get on (with) | Not an adjective: a verb phrase. `Vado d'accordo con…` |
| `litigare` | to argue, to fall out | Covers both a single row and a lasting falling-out. |

**Instruction, part 1 (4 min, lockstep).** Say each with its article-free masculine
form, learners repeat. Then the agreement, in **one** pass and no more: "`riservato`,
`riservata`, `riservati`, `riservate` — normal adjective, normal endings. `socievole`
and `affidabile` and `disponibile` end in `-e`, so they do not change for gender,
only for number. `in gamba` never changes at all." Write the three groups on the
board as three columns. Do not drill it; agreement is L4's recycling slot and it is
being set up here, not taught.

**Instruction, part 2 (5 min, solo).** "Exercise 3 — eight short descriptions, and
you choose the adjective that fits. Then back to your grid: fill in the **third**
column for everybody on it. Five minutes."

**Instruction, part 3 (5 min, pairs).** "Tell your partner about **one** person from
your grid. Three sentences: who they are, what they're like, and how you get on.
Ninety seconds each. Listener: your job is to say whether you'd get on with that
person. One sentence."

**CCQ.** "Which of these three don't change for *masculine* and *feminine*?"
→ `socievole`, `affidabile`, `disponibile` — the `-e` ones. · "Is `in gamba` ever
`in gambe`?" → *Never.*

**Correction stance.** Accuracy in parts 1–2, on the agreement ending only. Part 3
is fluency — collect two things for stage 10 and say nothing.

### 6 · False-friend slot — `i parenti` — 3 min · lockstep `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** The running false-friend strand: one item per unit, in L3, for three
minutes, every time. Learners come to expect the slot, which is the whole reason it
is a strand and not a scatter of warnings.

**Instruction.** "Three minutes, and we do this in every unit. One word that looks
like an English word and isn't."

Write it up exactly like this — the four-column shape is identical in all twelve
units:

| Item | Actually means | It is **not** | That word is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `i parenti` | relatives | *parents* | `i genitori` |

Then play the evidence from cue 2, speaker B: *"andiamo dai parenti in campagna:
zii, cugini, i nonni di lei."* Ask: "Can `parenti` mean *parents* there?" → *No —
uncles, cousins, and her grandparents. Those are not parents.* The sentence was
built to make the wrong reading impossible, and pointing that out teaches learners
to use context rather than to memorise a warning.

**Then exercise 4**, four items, sixty seconds, checked immediately.

**CCQ.** "*My parents live in Leeds* — `parenti` or `genitori`?" → `genitori`.

**Correction stance.** Accuracy, immediate.

### 7 · The five `piacere` chunks — 8 min · lockstep → solo `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** Plant the seed. These five expressions are taught as **fixed phrases**
and **explicitly not explained**; they are analysed at U8 L2, after seven units of
exposure
([`grammar-progression.md` §Seed 1](../../syllabus/grammar-progression.md#seed-1--piacere-family-u1--analysed-u8)).

**Instruction, part 1 (3 min, lockstep).** "Five phrases. You already know the
first one. Learn all five **as phrases** — like `thank you`, not like a verb."

| Chunk | English |
| --- | --- |
| `mi piace` + singular | I like |
| `mi piacciono` + plural | I like |
| `mi interessa` | I'm interested in |
| `mi serve` | I need |
| `mi manca` | I miss |

Then say this, out loud, because a learner who is not told will spend the lesson
trying to work it out: **"These are back to front compared with English. The thing
you like is the subject, not you. That is genuinely strange, it is genuinely
important, and we take it apart properly in Unit 8. Between now and then you will
have said these five phrases several hundred times, which is exactly why Unit 8
will be easy. For today: phrases."**

**Instruction, part 2 (5 min, solo).** "Exercise 5, six items. Then write **two**
true sentences about a person on your grid using two different chunks. Mine:
*Mi manca mia sorella* and *Non mi piacciono le persone permalose.*"

**CCQ.** "Are we going to find out today why it's `mi piace` and not `io piaccio`?"
→ *No — Unit 8.* · "Singular thing or plural thing after `mi piacciono`?"
→ *Plural.*

**Discipline.** If a learner produces `*io piaccio il caffè`, give the chunk back —
"mi piace il caffè" — and do not explain. If they push, the answer is the one
scripted above, verbatim, a second time. The seed only works if it stays a seed.

**Correction stance.** Accuracy on the chunk's shape, immediate, by recast.

### 8 · Pronunciation — word stress — 14 min · lockstep → solo → pairs · `A9` `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** Word stress, practised as **meaning-bearing**. Italian stress is not
decoration: get it wrong in any of this stage's five pairs and the listener hears a
different word. And the `-ano` third-person plurals — which the learner started
producing yesterday — are the single most reliable anglophone stress error in the
language.

`A9` has three steps and they always run in this order.

#### Step 1 — Discriminate (5 min, lockstep)

**Instruction.** "Ten words, and they're five **pairs**. The two words in each pair
have exactly the same letters and a different meaning, and the only difference is
**which syllable is loud**. I'll say one of each pair. Write `A` or `B`. Exercise 6
part A — the pairs are on the page in front of you."

Read cue 3 part 1 as written. Same speed, same volume, every item.

**CCQ.** "What's different between the two words in a pair?" → *Which syllable is
loud.* · "Are they different words?" → *Yes — different meanings.*

Check immediately. Take the score, out loud: "Who got eight or more?"

#### Step 2 — Mark (4 min, solo)

**Instruction.** "Exercise 6 part B. Ten words. **Put a mark over the loud
syllable.** Here is the only rule you need: Italian usually stresses the
**second-to-last** syllable — `ca-SA`, `pa-RO-la`, `ri-SER-va-to`. But the
`loro` form of these verbs does not, and that is what you're about to hear."

Then read cue 3 part 2 — each `-ano` verb alone, then in its phrase. Learners mark
as they listen.

**CCQ.** "Where's the stress in Italian **usually**?" → *Second-to-last syllable.*
· "And in `abitano`?" → *Not there — three syllables earlier.*

Check in lockstep, 60 seconds. **Then say the one thing that makes this stick:** "You
all learned `àbito` — *I live*. The `loro` form keeps the stress in the **same
place** as the `io` form. `àbito`, `àbitano`. `telèfono`, `telèfonano`. `mi
svéglio`, `si svégliano`. The stress does not move. You already know where it is."
That reframe turns four separate facts into one, and it is true of every verb in
this group.

#### Step 3 — Produce (5 min, pairs)

**Instruction.** "In pairs, with exercise 6 part A in front of you. Say **one word**
from a pair — just one. Your partner says which one you said, A or B. Five each,
then swap. If your partner can't tell, say it again louder on the right syllable —
don't say it slower."

**CCQ.** "If your partner guesses wrong, do you say it slower or louder on the
stressed syllable?" → *Louder on the stressed syllable.*

**Step 3 is where learners find out whether step 2 was real.** Marking a stress on
a page and producing it are different skills and only the second one is useful.

**Correction stance.** Accuracy, immediate, and **generous** — recast, do not
explain. "`telèfonano`" said back once is worth more than a sentence about
syllables.

### 9 · Activate — *due persone* — 13 min · lockstep → pairs · `A6` `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** The lesson's goal, performed. Two people, described properly, with the
scaffold removed before the end.

#### Rung 1 — *I do* (3 min, lockstep)

Say this aloud twice. It is cue 2 speaker A, cut down to what the frames will carry.

> **Model.** "Chiara è la mia coinquilina. È molto socievole: conosce tutti nel
> palazzo. Io invece sono più riservata. Andiamo molto d'accordo, anche se a volte
> litighiamo per la cucina. È testarda, ma è anche la persona più affidabile che
> conosco."

Then: "Five sentences. What's in them? Who she is, two adjectives, how they get on,
and **one concrete detail** — the kitchen. The detail is what makes it a person
instead of a list."

#### Rung 2 — *we do* (6 min, pairs)

**Instruction.** "Your turn, out loud, with the frames on the board. **Two
people**, not one. Two and a half minutes each, then swap. Listener: at the end,
ask one question about one of the two people."

**Frames** (on the board):

1. `______ è il mio / la mia ______ .`
2. `È molto ______ , ma anche un po' ______ .`
3. `Andiamo d'accordo` / `Non andiamo molto d'accordo`, `perché ______ .`
4. `Ci vediamo ______ e ci sentiamo ______ .`

**Prepared lexis** (also on the board): `molto · un po' · abbastanza · ogni giorno ·
una volta alla settimana · quasi mai · al lavoro · in palestra`

#### Rung 3 — *you do* (4 min, pairs)

**Instruction.** "New partner. **Frames gone** — I'm rubbing them off. Describe
**one** person from your grid that you have not talked about yet. Ninety seconds
each. No notes: five words maximum on a piece of paper."

Rub the frames off the board. Physically. If they stay up, rung 3 is rung 2 again.

**CCQ.** "How many words can you write down?" → *Five.* · "Same person as last
time?" → *No, a new one.*

**Success criterion for the stage.** The learner describes a person for ninety
seconds, using **at least two** character adjectives and **one** sentence about how
they get on, without the frames. That is the lesson's can-do goal, and it is worth
reading out so learners know they have met it.

**Correction stance.** Rung 2 is accuracy, narrowed to adjective agreement. Rung 3
is fluency — delayed, collected, stage 10.

### 10 · Delayed feedback + close — 4 min · lockstep · `A13`

**Instruction.** "Six things from the last ten minutes. Two are **good**. Thirty
seconds with your neighbour: which two?"

Four with one error, two worth stealing. Expect the four to be agreement errors
(`*mia coinquilina è socievola`), `*il collego`, and `*in gambe`. Point at the
three adjective columns from stage 5 rather than re-teaching.

**Homework.** "Exercise 7 — write about two people, 100 words, and check it against
the three criteria printed with it. Then say it aloud once from memory. Twenty
minutes."

**Next lesson** is about **asking** rather than telling: the questions that get
someone to tell you all of this about themselves, and when to use `tu` and when to
use `Lei`.

## Exercises and answer keys

### Exercise 1 — The eight people items `[ITA?]`

Match the Italian to the description. *(Done at stage 3 as a 90-second check; it is
not a separate stage.)*

| | Italian | | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `il conoscente` | a | Someone I live with but am not related to |
| 2 | `il coinquilino` | b | My aunt, my cousins, my grandparents |
| 3 | `i parenti` | c | Someone I know, but we are not friends |
| 4 | `la collega` | d | The person I am in a relationship with — the neutral word |
| 5 | `il vicino` | e | A woman I work with |
| 6 | `la compagna` | f | The person who lives next door |

**Key.** 1-c · 2-a · 3-b · 4-e · 5-f · 6-d. **6 marks.** `la coppia` and
`il fidanzato` are not in the matching because they have no description that is not
circular; they are checked in exercise 7 instead.

### Exercise 2 — *Tre persone intorno a me*: who said it? `[ITA?]`

Write **M** (Marta), **D** (Davide) or **L** (Lina).

1. Vive con una persona molto socievole.
2. Durante la settimana non vede molto la sua compagna.
3. Parla di una persona del lavoro che risolve i problemi.
4. Il weekend va dai parenti in campagna.
5. A volte litiga per la cucina.
6. Dice che una persona è testarda, ma anche affidabile.

**Key.** 1 **M** · 2 **D** · 3 **L** · 4 **D** · 5 **M** · 6 **M**. **6 marks.**

*Four of the six are Marta or Davide, which is deliberate: Marta is the fast
speaker and Davide the slow one, so a learner who scores well on Marta's items has
done something real. If a learner gets 3 and 6 wrong but 1 and 5 right, their
problem is Lina's register, not their Italian.*

### Exercise 3 — Which adjective? `[ITA?]`

Choose the word that fits. Each is used once.

> `socievole` · `riservata` · `testardo` · `permaloso` · `affidabile` ·
> `disponibile` · `in gamba` · `litighiamo`

1. Non parla molto di sé e non le piacciono le feste. È molto ______ .
2. Conosce tutti, parla con tutti, esce ogni sera. È ______ .
3. Se dici una cosa su suo lavoro, si offende subito. È un po' ______ .
4. Dice sempre sì quando hai bisogno di una mano. È sempre ______ .
5. Se dice che viene alle otto, alle otto c'è. È ______ .
6. Ha deciso così e non cambia idea. È ______ .
7. Ha trentadue anni e gestisce due negozi. È una donna ______ .
8. Io e mio fratello ______ ogni volta che parliamo di politica.

**Key.** 1 riservata · 2 socievole · 3 permaloso · 4 disponibile · 5 affidabile ·
6 testardo · 7 in gamba · 8 litighiamo. **8 marks.**

*Items 4 and 5 are the pair learners confuse, and the key should be used to
separate them out loud: `disponibile` is about **willingness** — they'll help.
`affidabile` is about **predictability** — they'll do what they said. A person can
be one and not the other, and that is worth thirty seconds.*

### Exercise 4 — False friend: `parenti` or `genitori`? `[ITA?]`

1. My **parents** live in Scotland. → I miei ______ vivono in Scozia.
2. At Christmas all the **relatives** come. → A Natale vengono tutti i ______ .
3. I have a lot of **relatives** in Sicily. → Ho molti ______ in Sicilia.
4. Her **parents** are both teachers. → I suoi ______ sono entrambi insegnanti.

**Key.** 1 genitori · 2 parenti · 3 parenti · 4 genitori. **4 marks.**

### Exercise 5 — The five chunks `[ITA?]`

Complete with `mi piace`, `mi piacciono`, `mi interessa`, `mi serve` or `mi manca`.
One is used twice.

1. ______ molto i film italiani.
2. ______ la mia coinquilina quando va a casa dei suoi.
3. Dopo due giorni con i parenti, ______ un po' di silenzio.
4. ______ molto il tuo quartiere: è tranquillo.
5. ______ la storia di questa città. Hai un libro da consigliarmi?
6. Non ______ le persone permalose.

**Key.** 1 Mi piacciono · 2 Mi manca · 3 mi serve · 4 Mi piace · 5 Mi interessa ·
6 mi piacciono. **6 marks.**

*`mi piacciono` is the one used twice, and both times with a plural noun. If a
learner writes `mi piace` in 1 or 6, the correction is `piacciono` and the reason
is "the thing is plural" — which is as far as the explanation goes in this unit. Do
not go further. **Unit 8.***

### Exercise 6 — Word stress `[ITA?]`

**Part A — Discriminate.** You will hear **one** word from each pair. Write A or B.

| # | A | B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | pa**pà** (dad) | **pà**pa (pope) |
| 2 | **à**bito (I live) | a**bì**to (dress, suit) |
| 3 | an**cò**ra (still, again) | **àn**cora (anchor) |
| 4 | prin**cì**pi (principles) | **prìn**cipi (princes) |
| 5 | **sù**bito (immediately) | su**bì**to (undergone) |

*(Ten items — each pair is heard twice, in a mixed order.)*

**Key**, matching the order in cue 3 part 1: 1 **A** (papà) · 2 **B** (pàpa) ·
3 **A** (àbito) · 4 **B** (abìto) · 5 **A** (ancòra) · 6 **B** (àncora) ·
7 **A** (princìpi) · 8 **B** (prìncipi) · 9 **A** (sùbito) · 10 **B** (subìto).
**10 marks.** 8+ is a pass on this step; below 6, repeat part A before part B
rather than carrying on.

**Part B — Mark.** Put a mark over the stressed syllable.

1. abitano
2. telefonano
3. svegliano
4. si alzano
5. si preparano
6. caffè
7. perché
8. università
9. subito *(= immediately)*
10. Non è ancora pronto.

**Key.** 1 à-bi-ta-no · 2 te-lè-fo-na-no · 3 své-glia-no · 4 si àl-za-no ·
5 si pre-pà-ra-no · 6 caf-**fè** · 7 per-**ché** · 8 u-ni-ver-si-**tà** ·
9 sù-bi-to · 10 an-cò-ra. **10 marks.**

*Items 1–5 all keep the stress exactly where the `io` form has it — `àbito →
àbitano` — and the key exists to make that one fact visible rather than five
separate ones. Items 6–8 are written with the accent, so they are free marks;
their job is to show that Italian **tells you** when the stress is final.*

**Part C — Produce.** Pairs. Say one word from a pair; your partner says which. Five
each, then swap.

**Key.** None — the key is your partner's answer. **Success criterion:** your
partner identifies **4 out of 5** correctly. Below that, the problem is production,
not hearing, and part B needs doing again out loud.

### Exercise 7 — *Due persone* (homework, written) `[ITA?]`

Write about **two** people in your life. About 100 words in total, roughly 50 each.

**Model answer** (~100 words — this is the standard, not a ceiling):

> La mia coinquilina si chiama Petra. È tedesca e lavora in ospedale. È molto
> riservata — non parla tanto — ma è la persona più affidabile che conosco. Andiamo
> d'accordo: non litighiamo quasi mai, solo per il riscaldamento. Ci vediamo ogni
> sera in cucina.
>
> Poi c'è Gianni, un collega. È in gamba e molto disponibile: se hai un problema
> con il computer, lo risolve in cinque minuti. È un po' permaloso, però: non
> scherzare sul suo lavoro. Non siamo amici, siamo conoscenti — ci vediamo solo in
> ufficio, e non ci sentiamo mai il weekend.

**What it has to do** — three things, and nothing else is marked:

1. **Two people**, each named as something — `coinquilina`, `collega`, `vicino`,
   `conoscente`, `compagno`.
2. **At least two character adjectives each**, agreeing correctly.
3. **One sentence per person** about how you get on — `andare d'accordo`,
   `litigare`, `vedersi`, `sentirsi`.

**Then say it aloud once, from memory, without the page.** Thirty seconds. That is
not optional and it is not extra: the production task in L5 is spoken, and writing
something you cannot say is how a learner arrives at L5 unable to do it.

## Teacher notes

Unit-level error profile and pacing:
[`unit-01-teacher-notes.md`](unit-01-teacher-notes.md).

**Predictable errors**

1. **Adjective agreement, on the `-o` adjectives only.** `*Mia coinquilina è
   socievola` — overgeneralising the `-o/-a` pattern onto an `-e` adjective — and
   `*le mie colleghe sono riservato`, where the learner has the word and loses the
   ending under the load of a sentence. Expect it from stage 5 part 3 onward and
   all through stage 9. **Do:** point at the **three columns** on the board from
   stage 5 — `-o/-a/-i/-e`, `-e/-i`, and `in gamba` which never changes. One
   gesture per error. Agreement is L4's recycling slot, so you are not fixing it
   today, you are flagging it for tomorrow.
2. **`*il collego`.** The learner knows `il` is masculine and `-a` is feminine, so
   when they need a masculine colleague they repair the word. It is a *rule-driven*
   error, which is why it survives correction. **Do:** say it once in stage 3 —
   "`il collega` is masculine and ends in `-a`" — and then every time you hear it,
   give the article and the noun together and nothing else: "il collega." Add
   `il problema` and `il programma` only if someone asks; a list of four is
   memorable and a list of twenty is not.
3. **`*io piaccio…` or `*mi piace i film`.** The first is English word order
   reasserting itself; the second is the plural trap. **Do:** recast. `mi piace il
   film` / `mi piacciono i film`. Then **stop**, even though you can see the
   learner wants the rule and you have forty seconds. Explaining it here spends a
   seed that seven units of exposure are meant to grow, and U8 L2 is built on the
   assumption that it has not been spent.

**Correction stance per stage.** Stages 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 — **accuracy**, immediate,
and in stage 8 generous and by recast. Stage 5 — accuracy in parts 1–2, fluency in
part 3. Stages 1, 2, 9 — **fluency**, delayed, dealt with at stage 10.

**To shorten (−12 min).** Move stage 7 to self-study entirely (−8), take two
minutes off stage 5 part 3, and run stage 8 step 3 for three minutes rather than
five. The `piacere` chunks are the safest thing in this lesson to move out of the
room, because they are explicitly not being analysed and so nothing is lost by
meeting them on paper — provided you still say the "back to front, Unit 8"
sentence in class, out loud, which takes twenty seconds. Do **not** cut stage 8 to
save time: it is the unit's only pronunciation stage, and the `-ano` error it
prevents is already being produced, since L2 taught the `loro` form yesterday.

**To extend (+12 min).** After stage 9 rung 3, run a `A5` mingle: six prompts —
*trova qualcuno che… va d'accordo con i suoi vicini / litiga spesso con un
fratello / ha un collega in gamba / ha un coinquilino permaloso* — with one
compulsory follow-up question per person. It recycles set B under time pressure and
rehearses exactly the question forms L4 opens with.
