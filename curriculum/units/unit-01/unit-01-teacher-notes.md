## 1. If you are teaching this unit cold

- **What it is for.** **Re-entry, not instruction.** The learner arrives at solid A2
  with most of this half-owned. The unit's job is not to teach reflexive verbs from
  scratch — it is to make them **usable at length**, which is a different and harder
  thing.
- **The one thing that must land.** **The reflexive pronoun is obligatory.**
  Everything else in the unit is recoverable later. This is the error that survives
  to B1 if it survives Unit 1, and it is why L2 teaches it first and alone, before
  agreement and before placement.
- **The stage that always overruns.** **L3 stage 5** — the nine character adjectives,
  fourteen minutes. Learners want to talk about the people, which is the right
  instinct and the wrong stage. Hold the fourteen minutes; L3 stage 9 is where the
  talking belongs.
- **Most likely to be cut short.** **L4**, because its culture note invites
  discussion in English and the discussion is genuinely interesting. Protect **stage
  7 round 3** — the role cards — because it is the only place in the unit where a
  learner *chooses* a register rather than being told one, and without it can-do goal
  4 is not met.
- **What Unit 2 needs from this one.** **Secure reflexives in the present.** U2 puts
  them in the past and uses them to teach `essere` plus participle agreement. If they
  are shaky here, U2's main grammar point has no foundation — which is why the
  checkpoint's below-18 band says "do not move on" and means it.

## 2. Error profile `[ITA?]`

The table `A8` error-clinic items are drawn from. Every row appears in at least one
clinic in the unit; the right-hand column says where. All ten are predictions for an
**English L1** specifically, and the "why" column is the part that makes them
predictions rather than observations.

| # | Error | The wrong sentence | Why an English L1 produces it | Where it shows | Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Dropped reflexive pronoun | `*Sveglio alle sette.` | English *I wake up* has no pronoun, so there is nothing to translate — and nothing is what gets produced. | Everywhere, L1–L5 | **Recast, never explain, in L1.** In L2 make it rule 1 of three and the first thing on the board. `A8`: L2 ex 4 item 1. |
| 2 | Pronoun after the verb | `*Alzo mi subito.` | Maps *I get **myself** up* onto Italian word order. Feels reasoned, so it survives correction. | L1 s8–s9, L2 onward | Write the correct pair **immediately next to** the wrong one and say both aloud. Do not draw a rule. `A8`: L2 ex 4 item 2. |
| 3 | Pronoun not following the subject | `*Mia sorella mi alza alle nove.` | `mi sveglio` has just been drilled for forty minutes, so `mi` has become part of the word. | L1 s9, L2 s4, L5 s6 | One question back — "who gets up at nine?" — and let them self-correct. `A8`: L2 ex 4 item 3. |
| 4 | Pronoun between modal and infinitive | `*Devo mi sbrigare.` | **Both** correct Italian options (`mi devo sbrigare`, `devo sbrigarmi`) are unlike English, so the learner builds a third that splits the difference. | L2 s5 onward, L5 | Teach both correct options as an **equal pair**, never one as primary. Then point at the three board lines rather than re-explaining. `A8`: L2 ex 4 item 4. |
| 5 | Pronoun right, verb wrong | `*I miei coinquilini si rilassa in salotto.` | The learner has learned "the pronoun follows the subject" so thoroughly that it absorbs all available attention and the verb ending is left behind. | L2 s4, s8 | "How many flatmates?" and let them fix it. It is an **attention** error, not a knowledge error; explaining it achieves nothing. `A8`: L2 ex 4 item 6. |
| 6 | `-o` agreement over-applied to `-e` adjectives | `*La mia coinquilina è socievola.` | Over-generalising the `-o/-a` rule, which they have just learned correctly. A sign of learning, not of confusion. | L3 s5 onward, L4, L5 | Point at the **three columns** — `-o`, `-e`, `in gamba`. One gesture per error. `A8`: L4 ex 3 items 1, 2, 6. |
| 7 | `il collega` repaired to a "proper" masculine | `*il collego`, `*il mio collega è riservata` | `il` is masculine and `-a` is feminine, so the learner fixes the word. **Rule-driven**, which is why it survives correction. | L3 s3 onward, L5 | Say it once in L3 s3, then give article + noun together and nothing else: "il collega." Add `il problema` and `il programma` only if asked. `A8`: L4 ex 3 item 8. Checkpoint C5. |
| 8 | `in gamba` inflected | `*Chiara e Petra sono in gambe.` | It looks like an adjective and it is a prepositional chunk. | L3 s5, L5 | "It never changes." Three words. `A8`: L4 ex 3 item 7. |
| 9 | `tu` possessive surviving into `Lei` | `*Va d'accordo con i tuoi vicini?` | The verb switched and the possessive did not: the verb is where the learner's attention is. | L4 s4, s7 | One-word correction — "suoi" — and move on. Late-acquired detail; getting the verb right is the main thing at A2+. L4 ex 2 item 7. Checkpoint C3. |
| 10 | `ogni` + plural | `*Ogni giorni mi riposo.` | English *every day* / *all days* interference, plus `ogni` looking plural. | Anywhere | One-word correction and move on. It is maintenance, not this unit's business. It is in `A8` (L2 ex 4 item 8) **only** so that "find the reflexive error" is not a winning strategy. |

**Two notes on using this table.**

- Rows 1–5 are the **main grammar point**'s error set and they are the unit's real
  work. Rows 6–10 are maintenance and register, and the response column for all five
  of them is deliberately short: a one-word correction and no explanation. A teacher
  who explains row 10 at the same length as row 4 has misallocated the lesson.
- Errors **3 and 5 appear only once the lesson has worked.** Say that to the group
  when they appear. A learner producing `*si rilassa` with a plural subject is a
  learner who has acquired the pronoun rule, and telling them so is both true and
  worth more than the correction.

## 3. Register decisions

This course runs a `tu`/`Lei` axis from Unit 1 onward, so any unit whose materials
use one and not the other is making a decision the learner will generalise from.
Unit 1's decisions, written down:

| Material | Register | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cue 1, *Un messaggio vocale da Giulia* (L1) | `tu` | A voice message to a friend. The whole input is informal, which is correct for the theme and means L1 teaches nothing about register — deliberately, because register is L4's job. |
| Cue 2, *Tre persone intorno a me* (L3) | `tu`, three different ages | Marta is fast and informal, Lina careful and slightly formal, Davide in between. The **register spread is in the voices, not in the pronouns**: nobody addresses the listener, so there is no `tu`/`Lei` to choose. That keeps L3 clean for lexis. |
| *Due profili* (L4) | **One of each** | This is the unit's register teaching, and it is carried by two texts with the same content, so the difference is observable rather than asserted. |
| Culture note (L4 s6) | **English** | `A10` allows either. Unit 1's is in English because the takeaway has to be unambiguous: a learner who half-understands an Italian text about register makes a confident guess, and a confident guess about `tu` is precisely the error the note exists to prevent. Later units' culture notes move into Italian as the level rises. |
| Role cards (L4 s7 round 3) | **The learner chooses** | Three of the four cards are decided by the setting; card C (the sixty-year-old neighbour on the stairs) is decided by age alone, and it is the only genuinely ambiguous one. |
| Checkpoint item B10 | `Lei` | `Lei si trova bene in questa città?` — the form is identical for `lei` and `Lei`, so the mark is unaffected. It is there to put the register in front of the learner once more at no cost. |
| Production task (L5) | **Spoken: `tu`-neutral. Written: the learner states which.** | The spoken self-portrait addresses nobody, so no choice arises. The written profile **must** state its register at the top, and the rubric marks **consistency, not choice** — getting it "wrong" is not possible; drifting is. |

**What learners are told, explicitly, in L4 stage 6:** with someone you have not met
who is not obviously your peer, **start with `Lei` and let them downgrade it.** Being
slightly formal costs nothing; the other error is invisible to you and not to them.

## 4. Known weak spots in this unit

The honest list. A unit with no weak spots listed has not been read carefully by its
author.

1. **L4 is the least representative lesson in the course, and it is in the sample
   unit.** Unit 1 is the only unit whose L4 recycles *within A2* rather than from
   earlier units; from U2 onward the L4 content is dictated by the L4 contract in
   `grammar-progression.md` and is not chosen. So this unit demonstrates the L4
   **format** and proves nothing about whether the format survives the contract. **If
   the template is being judged for whether it scales to twelve, L4 is the stage to
   be sceptical about.** The honest test would be a second sample unit at U5 or U7,
   where the L4 contract is heaviest — U7's is four structures including clitic
   attachment to imperatives.
2. **The `piacere` seed is a liability this unit cannot discharge.** L3 stage 7 and
   this unit's materials keep it unanalysed correctly. Nothing in this unit can stop
   a teacher — or a later unit's writer — from explaining it in U3. The seed needs
   seven units of discipline and only has one unit's worth of instruction behind it.
   A one-line warning in every unit's teacher notes between U2 and U7 would fix this,
   and it does not exist yet.
3. **No audio is recorded**, so every listening stage depends on the teacher reading
   aloud. For cues 1, 2 and 4 that is merely less good. For **cue 3 it is a real
   risk**: the stress-discrimination task is only valid if the two members of each
   pair are said at identical speed and volume, and a teacher who is not a confident
   Italian speaker can invalidate the stage without noticing. The lesson says to
   rehearse it twice. That is mitigation, not a fix, and cue 3 should be near the
   front of the recording queue when recording is in scope.
4. **The checkpoint's mark boundaries are a judgement, not a measurement.** 18/25 to
   pass, with a three-band action table, is built to the `A12` proportions and to
   nothing else — ITA-4's full assessment spec has not landed, and no real learner
   has sat it. The proportions and the shape will survive; the boundary may not.
5. **L3 carries five separate things** — lexis set B, a listening, the pronunciation
   point, the secondary grammar point and the false-friend slot — and is therefore the
   lesson most likely to be under-delivered. Its "to shorten" line moves the `piacere`
   chunks out of the room, which is the safest cut available precisely because they
   are not being analysed. It is still a cut.
6. **Can-do goal 3 has no objective evidence anywhere in the unit.** It is self-rated
   only, which is defensible — testing recognition-only lexis would contradict what
   learners have been told — but it means one of four goals is assessed on the
   learner's word. The alternative is worse; the cost is real and it is stated in
   `unit-01-overview.md` §1 rather than hidden.
7. **Lexis counting.** `i parenti` is counted inside set B here, following
   `lexis-progression.md`, which elsewhere says the false-friend items sit outside the
   thirty. This is bookkeeping, it is already an open item on the Curriculum
   Architect from ITA-6, and nothing in the unit changes whichever way it goes.

## 5. Pacing reality

This duplicates each lesson's "to shorten" line **on purpose**: a teacher who is
already running late reads one file, not five.

| Lesson | Overruns at | Cut this | What you lose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **L1** | s3 (lexis set A, 13 min) and s8 (ladder, 15 min) | **s9** (−10), run s8 rung 2 only with frames up | The third-person production, which is what L2 opens with. Compensate by putting the `si` strip up during L2's presentation instead. **Never cut s6** — the noticing grid — everything after it depends on it. |
| **L2** | s2 (presentation, 12 min) — discovery takes longer than telling | **s7** down to 4 min (−6) and 2 min each off s3 and s6 (−4) | Exercise 5 moves to homework. **Never cut s5 or s8.** s5 is the only place modal placement is taught, and a PPP lesson with the third P removed is a worksheet. |
| **L3** | **s5, every time** (character adjectives, 14 min) | **s7** to self-study (−8), 2 min off s5 part 3, 2 min off s8 step 3 | The `piacere` chunks leave the room — safe, because they are explicitly not being analysed, **provided** you still say the "back to front, Unit 8" sentence aloud, which takes twenty seconds. **Never cut s8**: it is the unit's only pronunciation stage and the `-ano` error is already being produced. |
| **L4** | **s6** (culture note, 15 min) — it invites English discussion | **s7 round 1** (−6), culture note to 9 min with the text set as home reading (−6), 2 min off s5 | **Never cut s7 round 3.** It is the only place a learner chooses a register, and the lesson without it does not meet can-do goal 4. |
| **L5** | s3 (planner) and s6 (recording) both run long, and s6 must not | **s1** (−6), s3 to 8 min (−4), 2 min off s9's close | The before-and-after playback, which is the unit's best motivational moment and carries no language. **Never move the checkpoint out of the lesson** — a checkpoint set as homework is marked by nobody and acted on by nobody, and the action table is the only part of assessment in this course that changes what a learner does next. |

**The standing instruction, if you are more than ten minutes behind in any lesson:**
cut a **production** stage's length, never its existence, and never cut a noticing
stage, a discovery stage, or the checkpoint. A shortened production stage still
produces. A deleted noticing stage silently breaks the next lesson, which is a cost
you pay tomorrow and cannot see today.
