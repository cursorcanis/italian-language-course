## Stages in full

### 1 · Opener — your forty seconds from Lesson 1 — 6 min · solo → pairs

**Purpose.** Four lessons ago, before any of this was taught, every learner recorded
forty seconds about their Tuesday. Playing it back now is the single most motivating
six minutes in the unit, and it costs nothing to run.

**Instruction.** "Headphones in, or phone to your ear. **Listen to the forty seconds
you recorded after Lesson 1.** Two questions, and write the answers down: **what can
you hear that you'd say differently now? And what did you not say at all because you
couldn't?** Three minutes."

**CCQ.** "Am I asking what's wrong with it, or what you'd change?" → *What I'd
change.* · "Are you going to re-record it?" → *No — this is the before.*

**Then, pairs, 2 minutes.** "Tell your partner one thing from each question."

**If a learner did not make the recording or has deleted it** — which will happen —
give them this instead, and it takes the same three minutes: "Write down, from
memory, the five Italian words you did not have four lessons ago." It is a weaker
version of the stage and it still works.

**Correction stance.** None.

---

### 2 · Task framing and the model — 12 min · lockstep → solo · `A6` rung 1 `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** State the task, state the criterion, then play a model that meets the
criterion and no more. A model that outperforms the criterion teaches learners that
the criterion is a lie, so cue 4 is deliberately pitched **at** it.

**Instruction, part 1 (3 min, lockstep).** Write the task and the criterion on the
board, in these words, and leave them up all lesson:

> ### Il mio ritratto
> **Record two minutes:** who I am · what a normal week looks like · who is around
> me.
>
> **Done means all three of these:**
> 1. it runs **two minutes** with no stall longer than **five seconds**;
> 2. **six or more** reflexive verbs, correct, in the present;
> 3. **two people**, each with a character adjective.

Then say: "Those three are the whole assessment. There is nothing else. You can do
this task with the Italian you had yesterday afternoon."

**Instruction, part 2 (4 min, lockstep).** "Now listen to someone doing it. Elena,
two minutes. **Exercise 1 while you listen** — it asks you what she talks about and
in what order. Don't write sentences, write the order."

Play or read cue 4. **Time it in front of them** and say the time out loud when it
ends: "One minute fifty-four. That's what two minutes sounds like, and it is shorter
than you think."

**Instruction, part 3 (5 min, solo → check).** "Exercise 1, parts A and B. Part B is
the one that matters: **did she meet the three criteria?** Count them."

**CCQ.** "How long is two minutes of talking — about half a page, or about two
pages?" → *About half a page.* · "Does Elena do anything we haven't done?" → *No.*

**Correction stance.** Accuracy on exercise 1.

---

### 3 · The planner — 12 min · solo → pairs · `A6` rung 2 `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** The scaffold. Five frames that between them produce a two-minute turn,
filled in before anyone speaks. This stage is why the task is achievable; stage 6 is
where the scaffold comes off.

**Instruction (8 min, solo).** "Exercise 2 — the planner. Five frames, and the last
one twice because you need two people. **Write in note form, not sentences** — you
are not writing a script, and anyone who writes a script will read it aloud in stage
4 and it will sound like reading aloud. Eight minutes."

**The planner** `[ITA?]`

| # | Frame | Your notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `Mi chiamo ______ , ho ______ anni, e ______ .` | |
| 2 | `Durante la settimana mi sveglio ______ , poi ______ e ______ .` | |
| 3 | `Il ______ devo sbrigarmi, perché ______ .` | |
| 4 | `Il weekend invece ______ .` | |
| 5a | `______ è il mio / la mia ______ : è ______ e ______ . Andiamo d'accordo / Litighiamo ______ .` | |
| 5b | *(the same frame, a second person)* | |

**Instruction (4 min, pairs).** "Swap planners. **Don't read your partner's out
loud — read it silently and ask them one question about something that isn't there
yet.** One question. Then swap back."

**CCQ.** "Notes or full sentences?" → *Notes.* · "How many people in frame 5?"
→ *Two — 5a and 5b.* · "When you get your partner's planner, do you correct it or
ask about it?" → *Ask about it.*

**Why the partner asks instead of correcting.** At this point the learner's notes
are in note form and mostly not in sentences, so there is nothing to correct
usefully. A question about a gap — "what about Sunday?" — adds content, and content
is what makes the two minutes reachable. Correcting notes at this stage adds
accuracy to something that will be said differently anyway.

**Correction stance.** None on the notes. Circulate and check only that every learner
has **something in all six rows**; an empty row at the end of this stage is a stall
at minute ninety of the recording.

---

### 4 · Rehearsal 1, timed — 12 min · pairs · `A11` attempt 1

**Purpose.** Perform the task once, against the scaffold, and **find out the two
things that are wrong with it**. The first performance is not the deliverable; it is
the diagnostic.

**Instruction.** "In pairs. **A goes first, five minutes.** B does three jobs, and
only these three, so listen for them:
1. **time it** — tell A at the end how long they spoke;
2. **count the reflexive verbs** on your fingers. You need six;
3. **mark every stall longer than five seconds** on a piece of paper — just a tick.

A: planner in front of you, talk for two minutes. Don't stop to fix a mistake; go
round it. Then swap."

**CCQ.** "B, are you correcting A's Italian?" → *No — timing, counting, and ticking
stalls.* · "A, what do you do when you make a mistake?" → *Keep going.*

**The three jobs are the whole instruction.** A listener told to "give feedback"
gives a list of grammar corrections, which is both demotivating and useless here;
a listener told to count to six and tick the stalls produces exactly the two
measurements the criterion needs.

**Correction stance.** Fluency. Say nothing during. Circulate and collect six things
for stage 9's close — **not** for an immediate feedback stage, because stage 5
belongs to the partners.

---

### 5 · Two feedback points, named — 6 min · pairs → lockstep

**Purpose.** `A11`'s hinge. The second attempt improves because **two** things were
named before it, chosen from the criterion. Not a list. Two.

**Instruction (4 min, pairs).** "Exercise 3. Tell your partner their numbers — how
long, how many reflexives, how many stalls. Then **two things only**:
- **one thing they did that met the criterion** — say which one;
- **one thing to fix in the next attempt** — and it has to come from the three
  criteria on the board, not from anything else.

Write both on their planner. Two minutes each."

**CCQ.** "How many things to fix?" → *One.* · "Can it be anything you noticed?"
→ *No — it has to be one of the three criteria.*

**Then, lockstep, 2 minutes.** "Hands up: who was under ninety seconds?" Usually
most of the room. Say the thing that fixes it for everybody: **"The fix is almost
never more Italian. It's frame 5 twice. Two people with two adjectives each is
forty seconds on its own."** That one sentence adds thirty seconds to most learners'
second attempt.

**Correction stance.** Delayed and selective, by design — two points, named before
the second attempt, drawn from the success criterion.

---

### 6 · The recording — 14 min · solo · `A11` attempt 2 · `A6` rung 3 `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** The deliverable. Same task, **scaffold removed**, two named improvements
in hand.

**Instruction.** "Last step, and this is the one that counts. **Turn your planner
face down.** On a new piece of paper you may write **five words. Five.** Mine would
be: *settimana · martedì · weekend · Tom · Nadia*. Then find a corner, record two
minutes on your phone, and keep both recordings. Ten minutes — you may record it
twice if the first one breaks, and most people do."

Write on the board: **`5 parole. Due minuti. Due persone.`**

**CCQ.** "How many words can you write down?" → *Five.* · "Is the planner in front
of you?" → *No — face down.* · "What do you do if you get stuck?" → *Keep going and
come back to it. Stalls under five seconds don't count.*

**Turning the planner over is the stage.** `A6` is non-negotiable on this: if the
scaffold is never removed, the learner has not produced, they have read aloud. Walk
round and physically check that planners are face down. It feels fussy and it is the
difference between the format working and not.

**Success criterion.** The three on the board, applied by the learner to the
recording they just made — exercise 4 is the sheet they apply them with.

**If a learner has no recording device.** They perform live to their stage-4 partner,
who times it and counts on their fingers again. They lose the ability to listen back,
which is real, so they get the compensation: their partner tells them, afterwards,
**which** of the three criteria they met. Arrange this at the door, not here.

**Correction stance.** None. Nothing is corrected during or after the recording. The
learner's own judgement against the three criteria is the feedback, and that is the
skill `A11` exists to build.

### 7 · Checkpoint — 10 min · solo, silent, timed · `A12`

**Purpose.** The unit check. Not a test — a check: ten minutes, every item with one
unambiguous answer, so that a learner working alone can mark it and know what to do
next.

**Instruction.** "Papers out. **Ten minutes, on your own, in silence, no notes.**
Twenty-five marks: ten on the words from this unit, ten on the reflexive verbs, five
on questions and agreement. Everything in it has been taught and used in the last
five lessons. I'll tell you when there are two minutes left."

**CCQ.** "Notes?" → *No.* · "Is this a test?" → *No — you mark it yourself in five
minutes and the mark tells you what to do next.*

The instrument and its key: [`unit-01-checkpoint.md`](unit-01-checkpoint.md). Hand it
out face down and start everyone together. Call two minutes remaining. Stop at ten
minutes **even if people are not finished** — an unfinished checkpoint is itself
information, and a checkpoint that runs to fifteen minutes has become a test.

**Correction stance.** Accuracy, and the marking is the learner's own.

---

### 8 · Mark, self-rate, act — 7 min · solo → lockstep

**Purpose.** Put a consequence on the mark. A mark with no action attached teaches
nothing, and the self-rating is the part a solo learner actually acts on.

**Instruction (5 min, solo).** "Keys are in the checkpoint file. **Mark your own**,
out of 25. Then two things:
1. find your mark in the three-band table and **write down what it tells you to
   do**;
2. the self-rating — four goals, rate each **1, 2 or 3**. Be honest; nobody sees
   it and it's the only part of this that changes what you do next week."

**Then, lockstep, 2 minutes.** Do not ask for marks — ask for one thing only: "Which
of the four goals did most people rate lowest?" In most groups it is goal 4, keeping
a conversation going, because it is the A2+ one and the only one that needs another
person. Say so if it comes up, and say what to do about it: "That is the goal Unit 2
keeps working on. It is supposed to be a 2 at this point."

**Correction stance.** Self-marked against the key.

---

### 9 · The written task, and the unit close — 11 min · lockstep `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** Set the written half of the production task with its criteria, then close
the unit against the four can-do goals.

**Instruction, part 1 — the written task (4 min).** "The other half of the task, and
it's homework. **A language-exchange profile: about 120 words, the same ground as
your recording, written for someone who will read it rather than hear it.** You've
seen two of these — Claudia's and Ferri's, in Lesson 4. Exercise 5 has a model and
the four things yours has to do. **Choose your register and say which you chose at
the top of the page** — one word, `tu` or `Lei`. That choice is marked; getting it
'wrong' is not possible, but being inconsistent is."

**Instruction, part 2 — the close (7 min).** Put the four can-do goals on the board,
and go through them in order, out loud, with the group — one minute each:

| # | Goal | Where you did it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Describe my routine in detail | Today's recording. Compare it with your forty seconds from Lesson 1. |
| 2 | Describe the people closest to me | Frames 5a and 5b, and L3's homework. |
| 3 | Say what I like, need and miss | The five phrases. **Self-rated only** — they are not on the checkpoint, on purpose, because you have not been asked to understand them yet. |
| 4 | Ask the questions back and keep it going | L4's carousel. If you rated this a 1 or a 2, that is normal; it needs a person and it carries into Unit 2. |

Then the one sentence that frames Unit 2, and say it exactly: **"Everything you did
today was in the present. Next unit is the same content in the past — and the
reflexive verbs you just spent five lessons on are the hardest part of it, which is
why we did them first."**

**Correction stance.** None.

## Exercises and answer keys

### Exercise 1 — *Un ritratto in due minuti*: the model `[ITA?]`

**Part A.** In what order does Elena talk about these? Number them 1–5.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| ___ | the weekend |
| ___ | who she is and what she does |
| ___ | her colleague |
| ___ | a normal weekday |
| ___ | her partner |

**Key.** 1 who she is and what she does · 2 a normal weekday · 3 the weekend ·
4 her partner · 5 her colleague. **5 marks.**

*The order is the point of part A. It is the same order as the planner in stage 3,
and learners who notice that have been handed the structure of their own two
minutes.*

**Part B.** Did Elena meet the three criteria? Count.

1. How long did she speak?
2. How many reflexive verbs did she use? (You need six.)
3. How many people did she describe, and with what adjective each?

**Key.** 1 **1:54** — under two minutes, and worth saying out loud. · 2 **Eleven**:
`mi sveglio`, `mi faccio la doccia`, `mi vesto`, `mi riposo`, `sbrigarmi`, `mi
rilasso`, `mi addormento`, `mi alzo`, `ci vediamo`, `ci sentiamo`, `mi trovo`. Accept
any count of six or more — the task is to notice she clears the bar, not to
transcribe. · 3 **Two**: Paolo — `socievole`; Francesca — `in gamba`, `disponibile`.
**3 marks.**

### Exercise 2 — The planner

The six-row table in stage 3. **No key** — it is a planning document.

**Success criterion for the planner itself**, and it is the only thing checked: all
six rows have something in them, in note form, and rows 5a and 5b name **two
different people**. A planner with an empty row becomes a stall in the recording.

### Exercise 3 — The feedback sheet

Fill this in **about your partner**, after rehearsal 1.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| How long they spoke | ______ |
| Reflexive verbs counted | ______ (need 6) |
| Stalls over five seconds | ______ |
| **One thing they did well** — which criterion? | |
| **One thing to fix next time** — must be one of the three criteria | |

**Marking guidance** — the sheet is not marked, but it is checked, and these are the
two things that make it worth four minutes:

- The "fix" must come from the **three criteria**. "Your pronunciation of `gli`" is
  not acceptable feedback at this stage, however true, because it cannot be acted on
  in the next ten minutes.
- **One** thing, not three. A learner given three things to fix fixes none of them,
  and `A11`'s whole claim is that two named points before a second attempt beat a
  full correction after it.

### Exercise 4 — The spoken task: rubric

Apply this to **your own recording**, listening back once.

| Criterion | Met? | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Two minutes, no stall over five seconds | | Length: ______ · Longest stall: ______ |
| 2. Six or more reflexive verbs, correct, present | | Count: ______ |
| 3. Two people, each with a character adjective | | Who: ______ / ______ |

**Bands**, so that a learner alone knows what they are looking at:

| | Reading | Do this |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **All three met** | The unit's goal is met. | Keep both recordings. Play the first and the last to someone, in that order. |
| **Two of three met** | A pass. This is the normal outcome of a first unit. | Record **once more** this week, fixing only the missing one. Ten minutes. |
| **One or none met** | Not yet, and it is almost always criterion 1 — length. | Do not re-record yet. Go back to the planner, add a sentence to each of the six rows, and record again from the fuller planner. Length is a **content** problem, not a language problem, and it is fixed on paper. |

*That last line is the most useful sentence in this lesson. A learner who comes in
at seventy seconds concludes their Italian is not good enough; the actual cause is
six thin planner rows, and it is fixable in four minutes.*

### Exercise 5 — The written task (homework) `[ITA?]`

A language-exchange profile, ~120 words, the same ground as your recording, written
for a reader. State your register at the top: `tu` or `Lei`.

**Model answer** — register: `tu` `[ITA?]`

> **Sam, 38 anni, Manchester**
>
> Ciao! Mi chiamo Sam, ho trentotto anni e vivo a Manchester. Lavoro in un ufficio
> e studio italiano da due anni.
>
> Durante la settimana mi sveglio alle sei e mezza ed esco di casa alle sette e
> mezza. Il martedì devo sbrigarmi, perché alle sei ho il corso di italiano. La sera
> mi rilasso sul divano e verso le undici mi addormento davanti alla televisione. Il
> sabato invece non mi alzo prima delle dieci.
>
> Vivo con il mio compagno, Tom: è socievole e molto disponibile, e andiamo
> d'accordo quasi sempre. Ho anche una collega in gamba, Nadia: ci vediamo ogni
> giorno e ci sentiamo anche il weekend.
>
> E tu? Come ti chiami? A che ora ti svegli? Quando ci possiamo sentire?

**Rubric** — four things, 2 marks each, 8 marks:

| | Criterion | 2 | 1 | 0 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Coverage** — who I am, a normal week, two people | All three, with detail | All three, one of them in a single clause | One missing |
| 2 | **Reflexives** — six or more, correct, present | 6+, all correct | 6+, one or two wrong | Fewer than 6 |
| 3 | **People** — two, each with an agreeing character adjective | Two people, adjectives agree | Two people, one agreement error | One person, or no adjectives |
| 4 | **Register** — stated at the top and consistent throughout | Stated and consistent | Stated, one slip | Not stated, or drifts |

**6/8 is a pass.** The model scores 8: three questions back at the end are not
required by the rubric and are there because they are what a real profile does, and
because they recycle L4 at no cost.

**Two things to say about the model, out loud, when you hand it back.** It has **no
past tense in it at all** — the whole task is doable in the present, which is worth
knowing before Unit 2 makes the past compulsory. And `studio italiano da due anni`
uses the present for something that started in the past, which is a genuine Italian
pattern an English speaker will not reach for; it is in the model as **recognition
only** and nobody is expected to produce it here.

## Teacher notes

Unit-level error profile and pacing:
[`unit-01-teacher-notes.md`](unit-01-teacher-notes.md).

**Predictable errors** — and in this lesson, two of the three are not language
errors, which is normal for a TBLT lesson.

1. **Coming in at seventy to ninety seconds instead of two minutes.** Almost
   everybody, on attempt 1. The learner concludes their Italian is insufficient. It
   is not: they have six thin planner rows and no second person. **Do:** say the
   sentence from stage 5 to the whole room — "the fix is frame 5 twice, not more
   Italian" — and in feedback, make "length" the named fix only if you have also
   pointed at **which row** to grow. Never give "speak for longer" as a feedback
   point; it is not actionable.
2. **Reading the planner aloud in stage 6.** A learner who wrote sentences instead
   of notes in stage 3 will read them, and the recording will sound like reading
   because it is. **Do:** prevention, not correction — circulate in stage 3 and say
   "notes, not sentences" to anyone writing a paragraph, and in stage 6 physically
   check that planners are face down. If you catch it too late, the honest response
   is to let the recording stand and name it as the fix for the re-record: a lesson
   is not improved by making someone do the deliverable three times.
3. **Agreement collapsing under load, in frames 5a and 5b.** `*la mia collega è
   molto in gambe`, `*Tom è socievola`. The learner had this right in L3 and L4 and
   loses it here, because attention is on length and on the clock. **Do:** nothing,
   during. This is a fluency stage and the criterion does not mention agreement. If
   it appears in the **written** task, it costs a mark under rubric row 3, which is
   the right place for it to cost something.

**Correction stance per stage.** Stage 2 and stage 7 — **accuracy** (exercise 1, and
the checkpoint). Stage 8 — self-marked. Every other stage — **no correction at all**,
and that is a deliberate feature of the lesson rather than an oversight. The
learner's own judgement against three stated criteria is the feedback, and `A11`
exists to build exactly that.

**To shorten (−12 min).** Cut stage 1 (−6) and run stage 3 in eight minutes instead
of twelve (−4), and take two minutes off stage 9's close. You lose the before-and-
after, which is the unit's best motivational moment but carries no language. Do
**not** shorten stage 6, and do **not** move the checkpoint out of the lesson: a
checkpoint set as homework is marked by nobody and acted on by nobody, and the
three-band action table in stage 8 is the only part of assessment in this course that
changes what a learner does next.

**To extend (+12 min).** After stage 8, a listening gallery: learners swap phones in
groups of three and listen to each other's recordings with **one** job — tick the
three criteria on exercise 4's sheet for the person they are listening to. Eight
minutes to listen, four to compare. It is the only time in the unit learners hear a
peer perform the whole task, and judging someone else against a rubric is the
fastest way to internalise it.

## Self-study version — 60 minutes

The longest solo route in the unit, and the only one at the top of the 45–60-minute
band, because `A11` genuinely needs the time: two recordings and a checkpoint do not
compress. **Do it in one sitting if you can.** The gap between attempt 1 and attempt
2 is where the improvement happens, and a day between them loses it.

`selfstudy_min` in the header and the top of the 45–60 band. Stage 9's written profile
is the **homework**, a separate ~30-minute piece, exactly as it is for a learner in a
class — it is listed here so a solo learner knows it exists, not so they do it in the
same sitting. If you are short of time within the session, the two stages to compress
are 1 and 3, in that order. Never 4 and 6: they are the task.

## After the lesson

- **Homework.** Exercise 5 — the written profile, ~120 words, register stated,
  self-marked against the four-row rubric. (~30 minutes.)
- **Keep three things:** the forty seconds from L1, today's attempt 1, and today's
  attempt 2. They are the unit's evidence, and the milestone review after Unit 6
  asks for the first and the last.
- **Act on the checkpoint band before Unit 2's L3.** Below 18 means redoing L2's
  self-study and the checkpoint in a week, and **not moving on** — Unit 2 puts these
  same reflexive verbs into the past, so a shaky foundation here is not a small
  problem later.
- **Unit 2** is the same content in the past: *L'ultimo anno*. Its L4 comes back to
  this unit's riflessivi and sets the present against the past, which is the first
  turn of the spiral and the reason this unit was worth five lessons.
