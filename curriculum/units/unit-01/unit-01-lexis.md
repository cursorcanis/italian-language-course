**Two of these have something extra in them, and it is worth knowing which:**

- **`farsi la doccia`** is a reflexive verb **with a direct object** — the shower.
  Nothing to do today, but it is why `mi sono fatta la doccia` behaves the way it
  does when you meet the past in Unit 2, and it comes back properly at Unit 6.
- **`addormentarsi`** is not `dormire`. `Mi addormento alle undici` is the moment you
  fall asleep; `Dormo otto ore` is the eight hours. English uses *sleep* for both and
  Italian does not.

## Set A′ — the two reciprocals · taught in L2 · 2 items `[ITA?]`

These two are in L2 rather than L3 because they are the unit's **grammar** doing a
second job: the same pronouns, meaning *each other* instead of *myself*.

| Italian | English | Example sentence | Met in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `vedersi` | to see each other | Io e Sara ci vediamo quasi ogni settimana. | L1 s6 · L2 s7 · L3 s9 · L5 s3 · Ckpt B9 |
| `sentirsi` | to be in touch | Ci sentiamo tutti i giorni, anche solo un messaggio. | L1 s6 · L2 s7 · L3 s9 · Ckpt B3 |

**Learn these two as goodbyes as well as verbs.** `Ci vediamo!` is *See you!* and
`Ci sentiamo!` is *Talk soon!* — they are the two most useful things in Lesson 2
outside a classroom, and you can use one of them in your next message to someone.

`sentirsi` is also *to feel* (`mi sento bene`). That sense arrives in Unit 8; here it
only means being in touch.

| Italian | English | Example sentence | Met in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `il compagno` / `la compagna` | partner | Vivo con il mio compagno da tre anni. | L3 s3, s9 · L4 s3 · L5 s3 |
| `il fidanzato` / `la fidanzata` | boyfriend, girlfriend | Il fidanzato della mia coinquilina viene spesso a cena. | L3 s3, s4 |
| `la coppia` | couple | Siamo una coppia tranquilla: usciamo poco. | L3 s3, s4 |
| `il coinquilino` / `la coinquilina` | flatmate | Il mio coinquilino è molto disponibile. | L3 s3, s9 · L4 s3, s5 · L5 s3 · Ckpt A1 |
| `il collega` / `la collega` | colleague | Ho una collega in gamba che risolve tutto in cinque minuti. | L3 s3, s9 · L4 s5 · L5 s3 · Ckpt A2, C5 |
| `il vicino` / `la vicina` | neighbour | Vado d'accordo con i miei vicini, ma non li vedo mai. | L3 s3 · L4 s4, s5 · Ckpt A4, C3 |
| `il conoscente` | acquaintance | Non siamo amici, siamo conoscenti: ci vediamo solo in ufficio. | L3 s3, s4 · Ckpt A3 |
| `i parenti` | relatives | A Natale vengono tutti i parenti: zii, cugini, nonni. | L3 s3, s4, s6 · Ckpt A5 |

**Three things to get right:**

- **`il collega` is masculine and ends in `-a`.** `il collega` → `riservato`, not
  `*riservata`. The feminine is `la collega`. This is the unit's trap and it is on the
  checkpoint.
- **`il compagno` / `la compagna` is the word to reach for** when you do not know
  whether a couple is married. It is register-neutral and wider than `fidanzato`.
- **`il conoscente`** is a word English has and barely uses. Italian uses it
  constantly, and *"we're not friends, we're acquaintances"* is a normal thing to
  say rather than a cold one.

| Italian | English | Example sentence | Met in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `socievole` | sociable | La mia coinquilina è molto socievole: conosce tutti nel palazzo. | L3 s5, s9 · L4 s5 · L5 s3 · Ckpt A6 |
| `riservato` | reserved | Io invece sono abbastanza riservato: non parlo molto di me. | L3 s5, s9 · L4 s3, s5 · Ckpt C5 |
| `testardo` | stubborn | Mio padre è testardo: ha deciso e non cambia idea. | L3 s5 · L4 s5 |
| `permaloso` | touchy, easily offended | Non scherzare sul suo lavoro: è un po' permaloso. | L3 s5, s7 · Ckpt A7 |
| `affidabile` | reliable | È la persona più affidabile che conosco: se dice alle otto, alle otto c'è. | L3 s5, s9 · L4 s3 · Ckpt A8 |
| `disponibile` | helpful, willing to help | Il mio vicino è sempre disponibile se hai bisogno di una mano. | L3 s5, s9 · L4 s3, s5 · L5 s3 · Ckpt C4 |
| `in gamba` | capable, on the ball | Ha trentadue anni e gestisce due negozi: è davvero in gamba. | L3 s5, s9 · L4 s5 · L5 s3 · Ckpt A9 |
| `andare d'accordo (con)` | to get on (with) | Vado molto d'accordo con i miei vicini. | L3 s5, s9 · L4 s4, s5 · L5 s3 · Ckpt A10, C3 |
| `litigare` | to argue, to fall out | Io e mio fratello litighiamo ogni volta che parliamo di soldi. | L3 s5, s9 · L4 s5 · L5 s3 · Ckpt A10 |

**The agreement, in three groups.** Copy this into your notebook; it covers every
adjective in this unit and most of the ones in the next eleven.

| Group | Masc sg | Fem sg | Masc pl | Fem pl |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **`-o`** — `riservato`, `testardo`, `permaloso` | riservat**o** | riservat**a** | riservat**i** | riservat**e** |
| **`-e`** — `socievole`, `affidabile`, `disponibile` | socievol**e** | socievol**e** | socievol**i** | socievol**i** |
| **fixed chunk** — `in gamba` | in gamba | in gamba | in gamba | in gamba |

`-e` adjectives do **not** change for gender. They **do** change for number.
`in gamba` never changes at all, for anybody.

**Two pairs worth separating:**

- **`disponibile` vs `affidabile`.** `disponibile` is about **willingness** — they'll
  help. `affidabile` is about **predictability** — they'll do what they said. Someone
  can easily be one and not the other.
- **`permaloso` has no neat English word.** Not *annoying*, not *sensitive* —
  someone who takes offence easily, at small things. You will hear it constantly and
  it is worth owning properly rather than approximately.

## Recognition only — not counted in the thirty, not tested `[ITA?]`

### The five `piacere` chunks

**Learn these as phrases.** They are deliberately **not explained** in this unit, and
they are not on the checkpoint. Met in L3 s7 and L4 s3.

| Chunk | English | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `mi piace` + singular | I like | **Fixed chunk. Deliberately not explained.** These are back to front compared with English: the thing you like is the subject, not you. That is genuinely strange, genuinely important, and taken apart in **Unit 8**. For now, learn them as phrases. |
| `mi piacciono` + plural | I like | Same. `mi piace il film` / `mi piacciono i film` — the thing decides, not you. |
| `mi interessa` | I'm interested in | Same. |
| `mi serve` | I need | Same. |
| `mi manca` | I miss | Same. |

By the time Unit 8 takes these apart you will have said them several hundred times,
which is exactly why Unit 8 will be easy. Trying to work them out now is the one
piece of initiative in this unit that will cost you something.

### Other recognition-only items

| Item | Where | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `La ringrazio e La saluto cordialmente` | L4 s3, profile 2 | A fixed formal sign-off. The `La` in it is an object pronoun analysed at U8. Learn the whole line as a phrase. |
| `subìto` | L3 s8 | Stressed on the `i`, it means *undergone*. It is on the page only as the contrast partner for `sùbito` (*immediately*), which you do use. You are never asked to produce `subìto`. |
| `studio italiano da due anni` | L5, model answer | Present tense for something that started in the past. A real and very common Italian pattern that an English speaker will not reach for. Recognise it; nobody expects you to produce it in this unit. |

## False friend — Unit 1 of 12 `[ITA?]`

One per unit, two minutes in L3, and they are tested cumulatively at both
milestones. **Keep all twelve on one page of your notebook** — the whole point of the
strand is that they sit together.

| Item | Actually means | It is **not** | That word is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `i parenti` | relatives | *parents* | `i genitori` |

The sentence in Lesson 3's input was built so that the wrong reading is impossible:
*"andiamo dai parenti in campagna: zii, cugini, i nonni di lei."* Uncles, cousins and
her grandparents are not parents. Use the context, not the warning.
