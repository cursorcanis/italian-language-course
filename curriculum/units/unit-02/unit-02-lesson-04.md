**What is *not* re-taught.** The auxiliary rules and agreement (U2 L2), the
irregular participles (U2 L2), and `da allora` + present (U2 L3) are one to three
lessons old. This lesson presents none of them. Every item turns on a choice the
learner first made in **Unit 1**: **which little word for which subject** (`mi`,
`ti`, `si`, `ci`, `vi`), **whether the verb is reflexive at all** (`mi sono
trasferita` but `sono uscita`), and **the present-tense form for *now***, irregulars
included (`so`, `vado`, `esco`, `devo`). See
[§The contract and "this unit's grammar"](#the-contract-and-this-units-grammar).

## Stages in full

### 1 · Opener — *che cosa hai fatto ad agosto?* — 5 min · pairs → lockstep

**Purpose.** Put the theme on the table from the learners' own calendar, and set up
the culture note's surprise: that the Italian year has a different shape.

**Instruction.** "With your partner, three minutes. **What did you do last August,
and what did you do at Christmas?** Then: in your country, **when does the year
really start** — when does everyone go back to work? Italian if you can."

**CCQ.** "Two months, and one question about your country — what's the question?" →
*When the year really starts.*

**Then, lockstep, 2 minutes.** Collect answers to the last question on the board,
in English: *January · after Easter · September (schools)*. Leave them up; stage 2
ticks one.

**Correction stance.** None.

### 2 · Culture note as input — *Il mio primo anno italiano* — 15 min · solo → pairs → lockstep · `A10` `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** The unit's culture note — **the shape of the Italian year** — and, at
the same time, the lesson's only input text. Both contract structures are in it:
Hannah tells her first year in the past, much of it with reflexive verbs, and then
says how she does things **now**, in the present. The note is where the recycling
starts, so it cannot be cut quietly.

**This note is in Italian, deliberately.** Unit 1's was in English because its
takeaway could not risk a confident misreading. Here the takeaway is written up in
English at the end of the stage, five terms are glossed, and an English note could
not be the text the rest of the lesson mines.

**Instruction.** "Hannah is English and has lived in Bologna for a year. Read what
she wrote — five minutes. Then exercise 1 part A, three questions. Don't look up
every word: five are glossed underneath."

#### *Il mio primo anno italiano* — Hannah, 31 anni, da Manchester a Bologna

> Mi sono trasferita a Bologna un anno fa, a settembre, e ho scoperto subito una
> cosa: in Italia l'anno comincia a settembre, non a gennaio. Riaprono le scuole,
> tutti tornano al lavoro, gli uffici si riempiono.
>
> A dicembre mi sono resa conto che il Natale italiano è lungo: le vacanze finiscono
> il 6 gennaio, con l'Epifania. A febbraio i miei colleghi si sono presi una
> settimana per sciare — la *settimana bianca* — e io sono rimasta in ufficio quasi
> da sola.
>
> In primavera ho imparato la parola *ponte*: se il 25 aprile o il primo maggio è di
> giovedì, molti non lavorano neanche il venerdì. Io e il mio compagno ci siamo
> organizzati e siamo andati a Napoli per quattro giorni.
>
> Poi è arrivato agosto. I miei vicini si sono trasferiti al mare per tre settimane,
> il mio bar si è svuotato e sulla porta hanno messo un cartello: *Chiuso per
> ferie*. A Ferragosto, il 15, la città è sembrata un deserto. Io mi sono annoiata
> moltissimo.
>
> Da allora organizzo l'anno in un altro modo. Adesso so come funziona: a gennaio
> prenoto le vacanze, prima di luglio finisco tutte le pratiche, e a settembre torno
> al lavoro con tutti gli altri. E quando un'amica mi chiede: «Com'è andato
> l'anno?», rispondo come un'italiana: «A Natale…, a Pasqua…, ad agosto…».

**Glossed** — `l'Epifania` 6 January, the end of the Christmas holidays · `la
settimana bianca` a winter week off to go skiing · `il ponte` literally *bridge*: the
working day between a holiday and the weekend, which people take off · `Ferragosto`
15 August, the peak of the summer shutdown · `svuotarsi` to empty.

**Then, pairs, 3 minutes.** Exercise 1 part B — the two comparison questions.

**Then, lockstep, 3 minutes — this is the stage, not a wrap-up.** Tick *September*
on the stage 1 list if anyone said it. Then write the takeaway up, **in English**,
and leave it there for stage 7:

> **Takeaway — the Italian year.**
> 1. **Tell your year by the landmarks**: `a settembre`, `a Natale`, `a Pasqua`,
>    `ad agosto`. That is how Italians tell theirs, and it is how yours will sound
>    Italian.
> 2. **Nothing administrative happens in the second half of August.** Do it by the
>    end of July, or after the first week of September.
> 3. **Before a `ponte`, ask if the office is open.**

**CCQ.** "When does the Italian year really start?" → *September.* · "You need a
document from the `Comune`. Is 16 August a good day?" → *No.*

**Watch the clock.** Learners will want to tell holiday stories in English. Fifteen
minutes, then stop: the takeaway is what stage 7 needs.

**Correction stance.** Accuracy on part A. Part B and the discussion are content
only.

### 3 · Sort — then and now in the text — 6 min · solo → pairs · `A2` (recycling use) `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** Learners **retrieve** both contract structures out of the text before
any work on them, so stage 4 builds on what they have just found.

**`A2` in its recycling variant — say so to yourself, not the class.** Neither
structure is new, so learners **may say what each form does**. Nothing is
**taught**: no rule presented, no paradigm drawn.

**Instruction.** "Exercise 2. Two columns. **Column 1: something that happened to
Hannah or the people around her, with the little word from Unit 1.** **Column 2:
how things are *now* — present tense.** The first two in each are done. Four
minutes. Then compare: **is there anything you put in column 1 that hasn't got a
little word?**"

**CCQ.** "Column 2 — past or present?" → *Present.* · "Are we learning anything
new?" → *No — finding things we know.*

**Then, pairs, 2 minutes.** Compare. Then one lockstep question, thirty seconds:
"Column 1. `Mi`, `si`, `ci` — **what decides which?**" → *Who it's about — Hannah,
the colleagues, Hannah and her partner.* "Unit 1." Leave it there; stage 4 opens
from it.

**Correction stance.** Accuracy on the collection; a misplaced form is corrected by a
question — "who did it?" or "is that now or then?" — not by a rule.

### 4 · Recycle — then and now — 16 min · lockstep → solo → pairs · `A4` `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** The lesson's one Study block, for its one task-cluster. A change in your
life is told with **Unit 1's reflexive verbs, now in the past** — the choice of
little word is still Unit 1's — and finished with **how things are now**, in Unit
1's present.

**Part 1 — lockstep, 4 minutes.** Two board panels. **Elicit every line** — every
one is recycled.

**(a) The little word is Unit 1's. Who decides it?**

> `**Mi** sono trasferita a settembre.` — Hannah
> `I miei colleghi **si** sono presi una settimana.` — the colleagues
> `Io e il mio compagno **ci** siamo organizzati.` — Hannah and her partner
> `E tu? **Ti** sei trovato bene a Bologna?` — you
> but: `Sono rimasta in ufficio.` · `Siamo andati a Napoli.` — **no little word**

"Two questions, both from Unit 1. **Is the verb reflexive?** If not — no little
word, whatever the helper is. **If it is — who's the subject?** That picks `mi`, `ti`,
`si`, `ci`, `vi`." Nothing about `sono` or the endings: they are this week's, and
learners have them.

**(b) Then → now.**

> `A settembre **mi sono trasferita**.` → `Adesso **abito** in centro.`
> `Ad agosto **mi sono annoiata**.` → `Adesso **so** come funziona.`
> `Da allora **organizzo** l'anno in un altro modo.`

"Then: what happened. **Now: the present — Unit 1's present**, irregulars and all:
`so`, `vado`, `esco`, `faccio`, `devo`, `posso`." Point at `Da allora organizzo` and
say no more than "L3 — still true, so present."

**CCQ.** "*Marco and I moved*: `si siamo` or `ci siamo`?" → *`Ci siamo`.* · "*I went
out early*: `mi sono uscita`?" → *No — `uscire` isn't reflexive: `sono uscita`.* ·
"`Adesso mi sono trovata bene` — right for *now*?" → *No — `adesso mi trovo bene`.*

**Part 2 — solo, 6 minutes.** "Exercise 3. Part A: eight *then* sentences — who is in
brackets. Part B: six *now* sentences. **The last two in each part need a
decision.**"

**Part 3 — pairs, 6 minutes: the then-and-now chain.** Write this strip up:

> `trasferirsi · iscriversi · licenziarsi · separarsi · laurearsi · trovarsi bene /
> male · rendersi conto · pentirsi · organizzarsi · annoiarsi`

**Instruction.** "Partner A: one **true** sentence about your last two years with a
verb from the strip — `L'anno scorso mi sono iscritto a un corso di salsa.` Partner
B: **two questions back**, in `tu`: one about **then** — `Ti sei trovato bene?` — and
one about **now** — `E adesso, balli ancora?`. A answers both. Then swap. As many
rounds as you can in five minutes."

**CCQ.** "B's first question — `mi sei trovato` or `ti sei trovato`?" → *`Ti` — I'm
asking you.* · "B's second question — past or present?" → *Present — now.*

**Correction stance.** Exercise 3: accuracy, immediate. The chain: fluency — collect
`*si siamo`, dropped little words and *now* in the past for stage 8.

### 5 · Error clinic — 8 min · pairs → lockstep · `A8` `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** The unit's predicted errors for these two structures, side by side, so
learners decide *which* thing is wrong rather than apply the last rule practised.
One item is off-target on purpose.

**Instruction.** "Exercise 4. Eight sentences. Some have **one** mistake; some are
already right — I'm not saying how many. With your partner: decide, fix, and label
it — **little word**, **now / then**, or **other**. Five minutes."

**CCQ.** "Does every sentence have a mistake?" → *No.* · "Three labels — what are
they?" → *Little word, now/then, other.*

**Then, lockstep, 3 minutes.** All eight. Spend the time on items 3 and 5. Point at
the stage 4 board lines; do not re-explain.

**Correction stance.** Accuracy; metalanguage welcome.

### 6 · Activate 1 — *trova qualcuno che…* — 14 min · mingle → lockstep · `A5` `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** The first free use of both structures, as **questions** — which is
can-do goal 4. Every prompt forces a `tu` reflexive in the past (`Ti sei…?`), and the
follow-up rule forces the present.

**Instruction.** "Stand up. Six prompts in exercise 5 — find **one person for each**.
Ask in `tu`: *Ti sei trasferito negli ultimi due anni?* When someone says yes, write
their name — and then **one follow-up question, and it has to be about now**: `E
adesso…?` — *E adesso ti trovi bene? E adesso vai ancora al corso?* Write the answer
in two or three words. Ten minutes."

**CCQ.** "When can you write a name?" → *After a yes and a follow-up.* · "The
follow-up is about…?" → *Now — `E adesso`.*

**Standing rule** (on the board): **No name without an `E adesso…?`**

**Then, lockstep, 3 minutes.** "Tell me about one person you found." Expect *Sara si
è trasferita a marzo e adesso abita a Leeds* — third person reflexive in the past,
then the present. Accept everything; recast the little word without comment.

**Success criterion.** Each learner (1) asks **six** `Ti sei…?` questions with the
right little word, and (2) gets **at least four** names, each with an `E adesso…?`
answer written down.

**Correction stance.** Fluency. Collect for stage 8 — especially `*si sei
trasferito?` and `*sei trasferito?`.

### 7 · Activate 2 — *la mia svolta* — 20 min · lockstep → pairs → new pairs · `A6` `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** The lesson's goal, performed: one real change, told as **a connected
story** — then, in order, and now — to a listener who follows up. The scaffold comes
off for the last round.

**Setup (1 min).** "One change from the last year or two — job, home, study, a
relationship, a habit. **Real.** If you prefer not to tell a real one, borrow
Hannah's, Giulia's or Nadia's — but tell it as *yours*."

#### Rung 1 — *I do* (3 min, lockstep)

Say this aloud twice. Do not write it up.

> **Model.** "La svolta del mio anno? Il lavoro. A marzo mi sono licenziato dalla
> banca. Poi mi sono iscritto a un corso per insegnanti, e a giugno mi sono
> trasferito a Lecce con la mia compagna. All'inizio è stato difficile, ma alla fine
> ci siamo trovati bene. Adesso insegno in una scuola media e vado al lavoro in bici.
> Da allora sono un'altra persona. Non mi sono mai pentito."

"Eight sentences. **Which ones are *then*, and which are *now*?" → *The first five
then; `adesso`, `da allora` now.* "**What joins them?**" → *`poi`, `all'inizio`,
`ma`, `alla fine`, `adesso`, `da allora`.* "That's what makes it a story and not a
list."

#### Rung 2 — *we do* (8 min, pairs)

**Instruction.** "Your change, **out loud**, with the frames. Three minutes each.
**Listener: two follow-up questions at the end, in `tu`** — one about **then**
(`Ti sei pentito?` `Come ti sei trovata all'inizio?`), one about **now** (`E
adesso…?`). Speaker: answer both."

**Frames** (on the board):

1. `La svolta del mio anno? ______ .`
2. `A ______ mi sono ______ .`
3. `Poi ______ .`
4. `All'inizio ______ , ma alla fine ______ .`
5. `Adesso ______ e ______ .`
6. `Da allora ______ .`

**Prepared lexis:** the stage 4 strip, plus `poi · dopo · ma · però · perché · quindi
· così`.

**CCQ.** "Listener — how many questions, and about what?" → *Two: one then, one
now.*

**Correction stance.** Accuracy, narrowed to the little word and to *now* in the
present. Collect two for stage 8.

#### Rung 3 — *you do* (8 min, new pairs)

**Rub the frames off.** Leave the takeaway and the linkers.

**Instruction.** "New partner. Frames gone. **Five words of notes, maximum.** Tell
your change — five to eight sentences, joined up. Listener: same two questions. Then
swap."

**CCQ.** "How many sentences?" → *Five to eight.* · "Notes?" → *Five words.*

**Success criterion.** Read it out before rung 3.

| Role | The learner… |
| --- | --- |
| **Speaker** | **S1.** tells the change in **five to eight** sentences joined by at least **three** different linking expressions (`poi`, `all'inizio`, `alla fine`, `ma`, `adesso`, `da allora`…); **S2.** uses at least **three** reflexive verbs in the past, each with the right little word for its subject; **S3.** says at least **two** things that are true **now**, in the present, after the past (`adesso`, `da allora`). |
| **Listener** | **L1.** asks one follow-up about **then** with a reflexive in `tu` (`ti sei…?`); **L2.** asks one follow-up about **now** in the present (`E adesso…?`). |

**Correction stance.** Fluency. Say nothing during. Collect six for stage 8, at
least two of them little-word errors and one *now*-in-the-past.

### 8 · Delayed feedback + close — 6 min · lockstep · `A13`

**Instruction.** "Six sentences from the last two activities. **Two are good** —
worth stealing. With your partner, thirty seconds each: which two, and what's wrong
with the other four?" For little-word errors: "Who's the subject?" For *now* in the
past: "Is it still true?"

**What to listen for in stages 6–7** — the six usually come from these:

- the little word dropped: `*sono trasferita`, `*sei trasferito?`;
- the little word not following the subject: `*io e Marco si siamo…`, `*si sei
  trovato?`;
- a little word on a non-reflexive verb: `*mi sono uscita`, `*ci siamo andati`;
- *now* in the past: `*adesso mi sono trovata bene`, `*da allora ho abitato`;
- and the good ones: a clean `Ti sei pentito?`; `Da allora` + present.

**Homework.** "Exercise 6: *la mia svolta*, written. Eighty to a hundred words.
Three things it has to do are under it. Twenty minutes."

**Next lesson.** Say it out loud: "Next time is the unit's task. **Three things from
your year**, told to a partner who asks you two questions — then recorded. And a
150-word post. And the checkpoint. Today's story is one of your three things —
bring it."

## Exercises and answer keys `[ITA?]`

### Exercise 1 — *Il mio primo anno italiano*

**Part A — on the text.** Answer in English or Italian.

1. According to Hannah, when does the Italian year really start, and why?
2. What is a `ponte`?
3. What does Hannah do **now** in January and before July — and why?

**Key.** 1 **September** — schools reopen, everyone goes back to work, offices fill
up (`Riaprono le scuole, tutti tornano al lavoro`). · 2 When a holiday falls on a
Thursday, **many people take the Friday off too**, making a long weekend. · 3 She
**books her holidays in January** and **finishes all her paperwork before July**,
because in August everything closes (`Chiuso per ferie`, the city `un deserto`).
**3 marks.**

**Part B — on you.** No key; discussion, nothing marked.

4. Where you live, when does the year "start" — and when does everything close?
5. Tell your last year in four landmarks, the Italian way: *a settembre…, a
   Natale…, a Pasqua…, ad agosto…*

### Exercise 2 — Sort: then and now

| 1 · Then — reflexive verb in the past | 2 · Now — present |
| --- | --- |
| **mi sono trasferita** (Hannah) | **organizzo** l'anno in un altro modo |
| **mi sono resa conto** (Hannah) | **so** come funziona |
| | |

**Key.**

| Column | Instances | The point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · then | `mi sono trasferita` · `mi sono resa conto` · `si sono presi` (i colleghi) · `ci siamo organizzati` (io e il mio compagno) · `si sono trasferiti` (i vicini) · `si è svuotato` (il bar) · `mi sono annoiata` | **Seven**, four different subjects. The little word follows the subject every time — Unit 1. |
| 2 · now | `organizzo` · `so` · `prenoto` · `finisco` · `torno` · `mi chiede` · `rispondo` | **Seven.** `so` is a Unit 1 irregular. **Accept** the general-truth presents in paragraphs 1 and 3 (`comincia`, `riaprono`, `tornano`, `finiscono`, `lavorano`) **as present, not *now***: they are how Italy always is, not how Hannah is now. A learner who sorts them separately has seen the difference. |
| Not column 1 | `sono rimasta` · `siamo andati` · `è arrivato` · `hanno messo` · `è sembrata` · `ho scoperto` · `ho imparato` | Past, but **no little word**: not reflexive. This is the answer to the pair question in the instruction, and stage 4 panel (a) opens from it. |

### Exercise 3 — Then and now

**Part A — then.** Passato prossimo. Who is in brackets.

1. *(io — a woman)* trasferirsi a Bologna un anno fa
2. *(i miei vicini)* trasferirsi al mare ad agosto
3. *(io e il mio compagno)* organizzarsi per il ponte
4. *(tu — a man)* svegliarsi presto a Ferragosto?
5. *(Marco)* rilassarsi, finalmente, ad agosto
6. *(voi)* trovarsi bene a Napoli?
7. *Decide.* *(mia sorella)* andare in settimana bianca
8. *Decide.* *(i miei colleghi)* uscire presto il 14 agosto

**Key.**

| # | Answer | The Unit 1 decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Mi sono trasferita a Bologna un anno fa. | `io` → `mi` |
| 2 | I miei vicini si sono trasferiti al mare ad agosto. | `loro` → `si` |
| 3 | Io e il mio compagno ci siamo organizzati per il ponte. | `noi` → `ci` |
| 4 | Ti sei svegliato presto a Ferragosto? | `tu` → `ti` |
| 5 | Marco si è rilassato, finalmente, ad agosto. *(also: Marco finalmente si è rilassato)* | `lui` → `si` |
| 6 | Vi siete trovati bene a Napoli? | `voi` → `vi` |
| 7 | Mia sorella **è andata** in settimana bianca. | **`andare` is not reflexive** — no little word |
| 8 | I miei colleghi **sono usciti** presto il 14 agosto. | **`uscire` is not reflexive** — no little word (U1 L2 ex 2 item 9) |

**8 marks.** All-or-nothing on the little word; an ending error on an otherwise right
item is corrected but not penalised here — endings were L2's, and the L2 key has
them.

**Part B — now.** Write the *now* half in the present.

9. A settembre mi sono trasferita. Adesso ______ (abitare) in centro.
10. All'inizio mi sono trovato male. Da allora ______ (trovarsi) bene.
11. L'anno scorso io e Luca ci siamo separati. Adesso ______ (vedersi) solo a
    Natale.
12. A febbraio mi sono iscritta in palestra. Adesso ______ (andare) in palestra tre
    volte a settimana.
13. *Decide.* Mio fratello si è laureato a luglio. Da allora ______ (fare) un
    tirocinio a Roma.
14. *Decide.* Mi sono licenziata a gennaio. Adesso non ______ (dovere / svegliarsi)
    più alle sei.

**Key.** 9 abito · 10 mi trovo · 11 ci vediamo · 12 vado · 13 **fa** — still true,
so present; `ha fatto` would mean the internship is over · 14 **devo svegliarmi** *or*
**mi devo svegliare** — both, an equal pair, exactly as in U1 L2 stage 5; never
`*devo mi svegliare`. (`più` goes after the conjugated verb: `non devo più
svegliarmi` / `non mi devo più svegliare` are also both right.) **6 marks.**

### Exercise 4 — Error clinic

Some have **one** mistake. Some are correct. Fix the wrong ones and label them:
**little word** · **now / then** · **other**.

1. A settembre sono trasferita a Bologna.
2. Io e il mio compagno si siamo organizzati per il ponte.
3. Da allora ho abitato in centro.
4. Ad agosto i miei vicini si sono trasferiti al mare.
5. Il 14 agosto mi sono uscita presto dall'ufficio.
6. I miei genitori si è separati l'anno scorso.
7. Adesso mi trovo benissimo a Bologna.
8. Ho non ancora prenotato le vacanze di agosto.

**Key.**

| # | Verdict | Correction | Label, and why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | ✗ | **Mi** sono trasferita. | **Little word.** U1's rule 1, in the past: the pronoun is still obligatory. `sono` alone is *I am moved*. |
| 2 | ✗ | Io e il mio compagno **ci** siamo organizzati. | **Little word.** The subject is `noi`, so `ci`. `si` has been stored as "the reflexive word". |
| 3 | ✗ | Da allora **abito** in centro. | **Now / then.** Still true, so present. English *I've lived* pulls toward the past. |
| 4 | ✓ | — | Correct. `i miei vicini` → `si`, plural verb, plural ending. |
| 5 | ✗ | **Sono uscita** presto. | **Little word.** `uscire` is not reflexive. The little word has spread to every `essere` verb — a sign the reflexive pattern is strong, and worth saying so. |
| 6 | ✗ | I miei genitori si **sono** separati. | **Other** — the little word is right; the verb isn't. `genitori` is plural. Unit 1's "how many flatmates?" error, in the past. |
| 7 | ✓ | — | Correct. *Now*, present. |
| 8 | ✗ | **Non ho ancora** prenotato. | **Other** — `non` before the helper (L3). Here so that "it's always a reflexive error" is not a winning strategy. |

**8 marks**, verdict plus correction; 4 and 7 score only if left alone.

## Teacher notes

Unit-level error profile and pacing:
[`unit-02-teacher-notes.md`](unit-02-teacher-notes.md).

**Predictable errors**

1. **The little word dropped in the past.** `*Sono trasferita a Bologna`, `*Sei
   trasferito?` The learner now has `sono` and lets it carry the whole verb — Unit
   1's rule 1, resurfacing under a new tense. Expect it in stage 4 part 3 and all
   through stages 6–7. **Do:** "`mi` sono trasferita — Unit 1, still true", and
   nothing else. It is the single error this lesson most needs to catch.
2. **The little word not following the subject.** `*Io e Marco si siamo
   organizzati`; in questions, `*Si sei trovato bene?`. `si` has been stored as *the*
   reflexive word. Expect it in the survey above all, where every question is `tu`.
   **Do:** one question back — "who are you asking?" — and let them switch to `ti`.
3. ***Now* in the past.** `*Adesso mi sono trovata bene`, `*Da allora ho abitato in
   centro`. English present perfect for a state that is still true. Expect it at
   rung 2 frame 6 and in the `E adesso…?` answers. **Do:** "still true?" — and they
   switch to the present. Point at stage 4 panel (b).

**The `piacere` seed.** `piacere` chunks are recognition-only until `U8 L2`; do not
explain the construction. This lesson's text contains none; if a learner tells their
change with `mi è piaciuto` or `mi manca`, accept it as a phrase and say nothing.

**Correction stance per stage.** Stages 2 (part A), 3, 4 (exercise 3), 5 —
**accuracy**, immediate; metalanguage welcome at 5. Stages 1, 2 (part B), 4 (chain),
6, 7 rung 3 — **fluency**, delayed, stage 8. Stage 7 rung 2 — accuracy, narrowed.

**To shorten (−12 min).** Set stage 3 (the sort) as the first homework task (−6);
set exercise 1 part B as home reading and keep only the takeaway (−3); run stage 6
with four prompts, not six (−3). You lose the retrieval before stage 4, so open stage
4 by asking for the forms rather than writing them up. **Never cut stage 7 rung 3**:
it is the only place in the lesson where both contract structures are produced with
the scaffold gone, and without it both ledger rows lose their free-production
evidence. Neither structure is bolded, so the template's hard rule does not bind
here — but the ledger is what this slot is for, and a ledger with no free production
in it is a mention.

**To extend (+10 min).** Reverse the culture note: in pairs, learners tell an
Italian who has just moved to **their** country how the year works there — *a
gennaio…, ad agosto…* — and one thing that surprised them in their first year
somewhere new (`mi sono reso conto che…`). Same two structures, no preparation.

### The contract and "this unit's grammar"

Template README §2: L4 "is not more practice of this unit's grammar". Unit 2's row
names a U1 structure *in the past* — and the past is this unit's grammar. Read
literally, the README forbids half the contract; read under
[`grammar-progression.md` §Reading "extended to" entries](../../syllabus/grammar-progression.md#reading-extended-to-entries),
it does not. The phrase "in the past" is not one of the four markers that section
names, but the map's wording — *riflessivi, now in the past* — and the case are
identical: the operation (passato prossimo with `essere`) belongs to **U2 L2**, where
it was presented; this lesson practises **U1's** choices as the operation acts on
them.

**The deletion test, run.** Delete everything here that depends on Unit 1 — the
choice of `mi / ti / si / ci / vi`, whether a verb is reflexive, the present-tense
forms for *now* — and exercise 3, exercise 4 items 1–3 and 5–7, the survey questions
and both rung 3 criteria for the speaker have nothing left in them. What remains is
the auxiliary and the ending, which are the **medium** of every sentence and the
**object** of none.

## Self-study version — 55 minutes

Two stages are pair- or group-shaped — the survey (stage 6) and the svolta (stage 7)
— and both have real solo routes. Read the stage 6 row before you start: it asks you
to message someone.

| Live stage | Solo equivalent | Min |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Opener (pairs) | Say **out loud** what you did last August and last Christmas, and when your year really starts. | 3 |
| 2 Culture note (solo → pairs → lockstep) | Read the text twice: once for exercise 1 part A, once with a pen, circling every month. Check part A. Write part B's answers, two or three sentences each. Copy the takeaway. | 12 |
| 3 Sort (solo → pairs) | Identical, with the key. Then one written line: **why has `sono rimasta` got no little word?** (Answer: `rimanere` isn't reflexive.) | 5 |
| 4 Recycle (lockstep → solo → pairs) | Read both panels aloud, covering the right-hand side and saying who each little word is about. Exercise 3, then the key. Then the chain alone: for **five** strip verbs, say a true *then* sentence aloud, then ask yourself the two questions back **aloud in `tu`** — `Ti sei…?` and `E adesso…?` — and answer them. | 9 |
| 5 Error clinic (pairs) | Before you look at the key, write the label next to each item — little word, now/then, other. | 6 |
| 6 Survey (mingle) | **Predict-then-check** (`A5`). (a) Write how **two real people you know** would answer three of the prompts. (b) **Message one of them** — any language — asking two of the questions, and write their real answer up in Italian, in the third person, with an `E adesso…?` line: *Tom si è iscritto a un corso di spagnolo; adesso…*. (c) If you can't ask anyone, read the three profiles under exercise 5 and, for each, write the question you asked and the `E adesso…?` you would have asked. | 8 |
| 7 *La mia svolta* (`A6`) | Rung 2 **written**, all six frames. Rung 3 **spoken and recorded**, frames covered, five words of notes. Then play your recording, and at the end record **two follow-up questions to yourself** — one then, one now — and answer them. Check against the speaker criteria S1–S3. | 10 |
| 8 Delayed feedback (lockstep) | Listen to your rung 3 once with one question: **did every reflexive verb have the right little word — and is every *now* in the present?** Write any sentence where it slipped, corrected. | 2 |
| | **Total** | **55** |

**Why a real person, not an imagined one.** `A5`'s solo route is predict-then-check:
the thing being practised is asking a human being and dealing with the answer you
actually get. The profiles are the fallback, not the route.
