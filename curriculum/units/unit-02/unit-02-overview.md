**Why the split falls here, and not by the syllabus subgroups.** Two carve-outs,
both stated so they are decisions rather than drift:

- **A′ = the four outcome verbs that all take `essere`.** `è andato bene`, `sono
  riuscita`, `mi sono reso conto`, `mi sono pentito` are the unit's grammar wearing
  vocabulary's clothes — the same move as Unit 1 putting `vedersi` and `sentirsi`
  in L2. Taught in L3 they would arrive a lesson after the auxiliary rule they
  demonstrate; taught in L2 they are its best evidence, and `reso` joins the
  irregular-participle list.
- **`la fattoria` moves from subgroup B to L3.** It is the unit's false-friend item,
  and the false-friend strand is a fixed two-minute slot in L3 in every unit
  (`lexis-progression.md` §The false-friend thread). It is *heard* in L1's input,
  in a context that makes *factory* impossible, and *taught* in L3's slot. It
  counts inside the thirty, as the ITA-8 counting rule requires.

Outside the thirty, recognition only: the U1 `piacere` chunks recurring, `mi sembra`
(Seed 1, first seeded **here**, U2 L1) and `mi è piaciuto` — the last because a
"year in review" cannot be told without it, and a learner left to build it
produces `*ho piaciuto`. See §4 and the teacher notes.

## 4. Grammar focus

- **Main — passato prossimo (consolidation).** The three things an English L1
  actually gets wrong, in the syllabus's order:
  1. **auxiliary choice.** Taught as two learnable lists, not as a theory of
     motion and change: **every reflexive verb takes `essere`** (rule 1 — carried
     by U1's reflexives, which is why they come first), and **a short list of
     verbs of going, coming, staying and becoming takes `essere`** (rule 2 — the
     *essere list*: fifteen verbs in L2 stage 2, sixteen once stage 6 adds
     `riuscire`). Almost everything else takes `avere`. The verbs that take **both**
     — `cambiare`, `finire`, `cominciare`, `passare` — are taught in L2 stage 6 as
     one question: *has it got an object?*;
  2. **agreement with `essere`.** The participle behaves exactly like U1's
     `riservato`: `-o / -a / -i / -e`. With `avere`, the participle does not change
     with the subject (agreement with a preceding object pronoun, `l'ho vista`, is
     U6);
  3. **irregular participles**, taught as a **learnable list in sound families**
     (`-tto`, `-so`, `-sto`, `-rto / -nto`, and five oddities), not as a list of
     exceptions. The `-tto` and `-sso` families are the reason the pronunciation
     point is double consonants.
- **Secondary — past-time expressions and `già / appena / non ancora`.** L3.
  `[quantity] + fa`; `scorso` agreeing with its noun; `da allora` + **present** for
  something still true; `finora` + passato prossimo; `all'inizio`, `alla fine`,
  `di recente`. `già`, `appena` and `ancora` sit **between the auxiliary and the
  participle**, and `non` goes before the auxiliary: `non ho ancora finito`.
- **Deferred, and said out loud to learners who ask:** imperfetto (U4) — the
  unit's input contains none, deliberately; the contrast between the two pasts
  (U5); participle agreement with a preceding pronoun, `l'ho vista` (U6); `ne è
  valsa la pena` as anything other than a chunk (U6); why `mi è piaciuto` takes
  `essere` (U8); the modal + reflexive auxiliary switch, `mi sono dovuto alzare` /
  `ho dovuto alzarmi` (not in this course's production targets; see teacher
  notes).

## 5. Pronunciation / intonation point

**Double consonants.** L3 stage 7, via `A9`.

- The past-participle set makes length unavoidable: `fatto`, `detto`, `letto`,
  `scritto`, `rotto`, `successo`, `messo`. A learner who says `*fato` for `fatto`
  is understood from context; a learner who says `ano` for `anno` is not, and the
  stage says so.
- **Meaning-bearing pairs**, which is the test of whether a pronunciation point is
  worth a stage: `anno / ano` · `sonno / sono` · `cassa / casa` (the three the
  syllabus names) · `sette / sete` · `nonno / nono` · `coppia / copia` (`la
  coppia` is U1 lexis).
- **What the double letter is.** A **held** consonant, not a louder one, and the
  vowel before it is **shorter**. That second half is the part English speakers
  never get told, and it is what makes `fatto` sound right.
- **Practised in production, not only in listening** (the syllabus's words): step 3
  is pairs, and the final rung is the participles in sentences the learner is
  about to need in L4 and L5.

## 6. Culture note

**The shape of the Italian year.** L4 stage 2, via `A10`, **as the L4 input
text** — the template's ITA-11 rule, applied: the note is written so that both
contract structures are in it, and the recycling starts from it.

- **Content.** September as the year's real start (school, work, the `rientro`);
  Christmas running to the Epifania on 6 January; the `settimana bianca` in
  February; Easter and Pasquetta; the spring `ponti` (25 aprile, 1 maggio,
  2 giugno); August and Ferragosto, when cities empty and `chiuso per ferie` goes
  up. Told by an English speaker in their first Italian year, so the calendar is
  met as a sequence of surprises — which is how a learner will meet it.
- **In Italian, deliberately**, with five terms glossed and the takeaway written up
  in English. Unit 1's note was in English because its takeaway could not risk a
  confident misreading; this one could not be the L4 input text in English.
- **Behavioural takeaway.** (1) **When you tell your year in Italian, hang it on
  the landmarks** — `a settembre`, `a Natale`, `dopo Pasqua`, `ad agosto` — because
  that is how Italians tell theirs. (2) **Nothing administrative happens in the
  second half of August.** Book, sign and ask before the end of July, or after the
  first week of September.
- **Gives the unit's time anchors a calendar to attach to** (the syllabus's reason
  for the note), and gives L5's "my year" post its skeleton.

## 7. Production task

- **Written:** a 150-word "my year" post for a blog or social feed.
- **Spoken:** tell three things that happened this year, then answer two follow-up
  questions about them. Live: a partner asks the follow-ups. Solo: recorded,
  answering the scripted prompts in cue 4.
- **Success criterion** — the syllabus's, made countable for a learner checking
  their own work:
  1. **at least eight** passato prossimo verbs, including **at least two with
     `essere`** and correct agreement;
  2. **every event placed in time** with an explicit expression;
  3. the written piece reads as **a connected account** with **at least three
     linking expressions**, not as a list.
- **"Linking expressions", read for this unit.** The syllabus does not list them,
  and connettivi narrativi are U5's taught thread. The reading here: the set B
  anchors that sequence (`all'inizio`, `alla fine`, `da allora`, `finora`) plus the
  A2 baseline (`poi`, `dopo`, `ma`, `però`, `perché`, `quindi`, `così`). Any three
  distinct ones count. Nothing from U5's set is taught or required.
- **Scaffold and its removal.** L5 supplies the model (`A6` rung 1, cue 3) and a
  six-row planner (rung 2). The recorded attempt is made from **five written
  words** (rung 3). The written post is homework, from the same planner, against a
  four-row rubric.

## 8. Checkpoint

Built to the ITA-4 instrument
([`assessment/unit-checkpoint-template.md`](../../../assessment/unit-checkpoint-template.md),
Unit 2 row). 10 minutes inside L5 stage 7, marked in L5 stage 8. 25 marks in four
parts: **A** a written message, someone's year (4) · **B** a cloze text, 4 gaps on
the passato prossimo — auxiliary choice and agreement with `essere` — plus 2
recycled gaps on **riflessivi in the past** (6) · **C** lexis in use, subgroups A
(time anchors) and B (life events), including the false friend (5) · **D** a
40–60-word written turn, TA + TS, floor **5 passato prossimo verbs, ≥ 1 with
`essere`** (10). **Pass: ≥ 18/25, TA ≥ 3, TS ≥ 3 and ≥ 1/2 on the recycled
gaps.** A *not yet* **does not block** Unit 3: it writes a carry-forward entry
("PP auxiliary + agreement — returns U6 L4", ITA-4). Plus a self-rating against
the four can-do goals. Instrument and key:
[`unit-02-checkpoint.md`](unit-02-checkpoint.md).

**Where the recycled gaps come from.** ITA-4's parameter table names *riflessivi,
now in the past (from U1)* for U2. The template's checkpoint rule 4 says recycled
gaps come from a **bolded** row of the L4 contract — and Unit 2's contract row has
no bolded entry (§10). The ITA-4 table is the spec and wins (template checkpoint
note: "If the two disagree, ITA-4 wins"). Flagged in the Lesson Designer's ITA-20
report.

## 10. What this unit recycles, and what it seeds

From [`grammar-progression.md` §Per-unit L4 contract](../../syllabus/grammar-progression.md#per-unit-l4-contract),
which is binding.

- **Recycles** — Unit 2 row: *riflessivi in the past; presente set against the
  past* (U1, U1). **Neither entry is bolded.** The mandated second encounter of
  both structures is **U4 L4** (master table: riflessivi → habitual in the past;
  presente → set against imperfetto); U2 L4 is listed in the master table as an
  **extra encounter** for each. So:
  - no `recycles` entry in this unit carries `(mandated)` — marking one would put a
    false row into the course-wide grep the marker exists for (template README
    §3);
  - the L4 contract ledger is still compulsory and still complete: both rows have a
    written site, a spoken site and a count, because "extra" means *not
    load-bearing for the spiral*, not *optional in the lesson*.
- **"Riflessivi in the past" is read as an operation entry.** The phrase is not one
  of the four markers in `grammar-progression.md` §Reading "extended to" entries,
  but the map says *riflessivi, now in the past* and the case is identical: the
  operation (passato prossimo with `essere`) belongs to **this unit's L2**, where it
  is presented once; L4 practises **U1's** choices as the operation acts on them —
  which pronoun for which subject, whether a verb is reflexive at all — and never
  re-presents the auxiliary or agreement rules. Delete everything in L4 that
  depends on U1 and nothing is left.
- **Seeds**, every one of which a later unit depends on:
  - `mi sembra`, first seeded **here** (Seed 1, `U2 L1`); the U1 chunks recur; `mi è
    piaciuto` added as a chunk — all unanalysed, for **U8 L2**;
  - `trasferirsi`, `il trasloco` → **U3** (housing); `assumere`, `il colloquio`,
    `il tirocinio` → **U10** (work);
  - `appena` in its *just* sense; its narrative *as soon as* sense → **U5**;
  - `inaspettato` → **U5** (mishaps);
  - irregular participles and the `essere` list → **U6** (agreement with a
    preceding pronoun), **U9** (trapassato: auxiliary + participle the learner
    already has);
  - the time anchors → **U5 L4** (past-time expressions anchoring the anecdote —
    the structure's mandated second encounter).
