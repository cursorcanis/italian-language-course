### Marking conventions

| Mark | Means |
| --- | --- |
| `↗` | rising contour, carried by the last stressed syllable before the mark |
| `↘` | falling contour, carried by the last stressed syllable before the mark |
| `→` | level / suspended — the list or the sentence is not finished |
| `//` | pause, ~1 second |
| `///` | pause, ~2 seconds |
| **bold** | contrastive stress |
| `[...]` | stage direction, not read aloud |

## Cue 1 — `assets/audio/unit-02/u02-l01-anno.mp3`

**Serves.** L1 stage 4 (gist, normal take), stage 5 (detail, normal take, played
twice), stage 6 (noticing grid, slow take, transcript open). Also quoted by L3
stage 2 (paragraph [4]) and L3 stage 6 (paragraph [7]).
**Length target.** `normal` take **2:15–2:25**: ~255 words at ≈140 wpm is ≈1:50 of
speech, and the eight `///` and thirteen `//` pauses add ≈29 s. `slow` take
**2:45–2:55** at ≈110 wpm (≈2:19 of speech, same pauses). The overview's first
specification (~230 words, ~2:00) could not hold all 28 past forms, the eleven set A
items and the seeds in connected speech; the target was raised to fit the script,
as Unit 1 cue 1's was under ITA-14, and the overview and L1 were updated to match.
Work out any revision the same way; do not set a target the script cannot meet.
**Speakers.** Giulia, 30s, from Bologna, now in Milan — the same speaker as Unit 1
cue 1, a year later. Standard, with a light northern colour. Same voice talent as
U1 cue 1 if at all possible: learners are told it is the same person.
**Pace.** Two takes: `normal` (≈140 wpm) and `slow` (≈110 wpm). The slow take keeps
every pause and slows the words, not the pauses.

**Must contain** (from [`unit-02-overview.md` §2](unit-02-overview.md#2-input-text--dialogue-ita)):

- all 11 set A items;
- **exactly 28** passato prossimo forms, in the order of the L1 exercise 3 key —
  no more, because the grid key says 28 and a learner marking alone must be able
  to trust it;
- ≥ 8 with `essere`, across all four endings `-o / -a / -i / -e`;
- ≥ 6 irregular participles;
- ≥ 4 reflexives in the past;
- time anchors present but unmarked;
- `mi sembra` (Seed 1, first planted here), three U1 `piacere` chunks and `mi è
  piaciuto`, all unanalysed and unstressed;
- `la fattoria` in a context that makes *factory* impossible;
- **no imperfetto, no congiuntivo, no imperative with an attached pronoun.**

**Script**

> **[1]** Ciao Mark! Sono Giulia. // Ti ricordi? ↗ Un anno fa ti ho scritto della
> mia settimana a Bologna. // Beh, quest'anno è successo **di tutto**. ///
>
> **[2]** A gennaio mi sono licenziata: ho lasciato l'ufficio, i clienti, tutto. ↘
> // È stata una decisione difficile, → ma ho deciso di cambiare vita. ///
>
> **[3]** A febbraio mi sono iscritta a un corso di grafica, la sera. Mi è piaciuto
> tantissimo. // A marzo ho fatto un colloquio per un tirocinio in un'agenzia di
> comunicazione a Milano. È andato bene, → e ad aprile ho cominciato il tirocinio.
> ///
>
> **[4]** Così mi sono trasferita a Milano. // Il trasloco è stato un incubo: ho
> perso due scatole di libri → e ho rotto una lampada. ↘ Da allora abito in un
> monolocale piccolissimo, ma va bene così. // A maggio Sara e Chiara sono venute
> per il mio compleanno, → e abbiamo festeggiato sul balcone. ///
>
> **[5]** Poi, a settembre, è arrivata la svolta: l'agenzia ha deciso di assumere
> una persona → e ha scelto **me**! // Finalmente un contratto vero. ↘ ///
>
> **[6]** E non è finita! ↘ // A luglio mio fratello Luca si è laureato in
> ingegneria, dopo sette anni. Alla festa di laurea abbiamo ballato fino alle tre.
> ///
>
> **[7]** Una notizia meno bella: in primavera i miei genitori si sono separati,
> dopo trent'anni. // Mia madre è andata a vivere in campagna, in una vecchia
> fattoria con le galline. Mio padre è rimasto a Bologna. ↘ // Mi sembra giusto
> così. ///
>
> **[8]** Allora: mi piace la mia nuova vita, e il lavoro mi interessa davvero. //
> Però mi manca Bologna, e mi manca Sara. ↘ // E tu, Mark? ↗ Che cosa hai fatto
> quest'anno? ↘ Sei andato in Australia, alla fine? ↗ // Un abbraccio! ///

**Lesson checks run against this script (ITA-20).**

- **L1 exercises 1–4**: every key answer is in the paragraph its key cites —
  ex 1 items 1–6, ex 2 rows Jan–Sep, ex 3 all 28 rows, ex 4 items 1–10. Ex 4 item 6
  (`sono venute a trovarmi`) paraphrases [4] (`sono venute per il mio compleanno`) — the gap-fill tells Giulia's year in its own sentences, so this is
  by design; the phrase in the box is the one in the script.
- **L2 stage 1**: the 22 board forms all occur in this script, with the auxiliary,
  person and gender shown on the board; the six left out (`mi è piaciuto`, `è
  stato`, `ha deciso`, `è finita`, `abbiamo ballato`, `hai fatto`) are the 23rd–28th
  forms. **Re-checked by the Content Editor, 2026-11-09: confirmed**, in script
  order (`docs/unit-02-lesson-02-italian-review.md`). The ITA-21 edit to [3]
  (`in un'agenzia di comunicazione`) adds no verb.
- **L3 stage 2** quotes [4] `Da allora abito in un monolocale piccolissimo` and
  **L3 stage 6** quotes [7] `Mia madre è andata a vivere in campagna, in una
  vecchia fattoria con le galline` — both verbatim.

**Notes to voice talent.**

1. `[1]` `Ti ricordi? ↗` is a yes/no question carried by the rise alone — the same
   point U1 cue 1 made with `vuoi sapere…? ↗`. Keep it light and warm: she is
   picking up an old thread, not checking his memory. No pause after it: she runs
   straight on.
2. **Do not stress the participles or the auxiliaries.** Learners collect them in
   stage 6 from the slow take with the transcript open. A recording that leans on
   `sono` or `è` gives away the auxiliary question L2 is built to ask.
3. **Do not stress or set off the `piacere` chunks** — `mi è piaciuto`, `mi sembra`,
   `mi piace`, `mi interessa`, `mi manca`. They must pass as ordinary speech; a
   highlighted chunk invites the question the course does not answer until Unit 8.
4. `[5]` `ha scelto **me**!` carries contrastive stress — that is the whole surprise
   of the year. `[1]` `di tutto` takes a lighter stress.
5. `[6]` `E non è finita! ↘` falls: an exclamation, not a question.
6. `[8]` `Che cosa hai fatto quest'anno? ↘` has a question word, so it **falls**;
   `Sei andato in Australia, alla fine? ↗` has none, so it **rises**, on `-fi-` of
   `fine`. Two questions in a row with opposite contours, deliberately.
7. Double consonants as written, neither exaggerated nor lost: `fatto`, `successo`,
   `rotto`, `scritto`, `anno`, `tutto`. L3 comes back to this cue for them.

## Cue 2 — `assets/audio/unit-02/u02-l03-doppie.mp3`

**Serves.** L3 stage 7: step 1 (discriminate — part 1) and step 2 (mark — part 2).
Step 3 is the learners' own production; no audio.
**Length target.** **1:20–1:30.** Part 1: 12 words, each with a soft chime before it
and a `///` after — ≈40 s. Part 2: 10 short sentences with a `///` after each —
≈40 s. Two spoken part headings ≈5 s.
**Speakers.** One, standard, neutral. This cue is a measuring instrument, not a
performance.
**Pace.** `slow` for part 1, `normal` for part 2 (the sentences must sound like
speech, or the participles are not being heard as they will be met).

**Must contain.** Six meaning-bearing single/double pairs, both members of each,
in the mixed order fixed by L3 exercise 6 part A's key, including the three the
syllabus names (`anno / ano`, `sonno / sono`, `cassa / casa`) · ten sentences
carrying **seven participles with a double** (`fatto`, `successo`, `letto`,
`detto`, `rotto`, `scritto`, `messo`), **one with a single** (`preso`) and **two
non-participle controls** (`anni` double, `casa` single).

**Script**

> **[Part 1 — say:]** Parte uno. ///
>
> **[A soft chime before each item. Do not say the item number: in Italian the
> number would itself be a test item — item 7 is `sette`. The teacher reading live
> says the numbers in English.]**
>
> 1. sete /// 2. anno /// 3. copia /// 4. cassa /// 5. sono /// 6. nonno ///
> 7. sette /// 8. casa /// 9. ano /// 10. coppia /// 11. nono /// 12. sonno ///
>
> **[Part 2 — say:]** Parte due. ///
>
> 1. Ho fatto un colloquio. ///
> 2. È successo di tutto. ///
> 3. Hai letto il mio messaggio? ↗ ///
> 4. Mi ha detto di no. ///
> 5. Ho rotto la lampada. ///
> 6. Ho scritto a Mark. ///
> 7. Dove hai messo le chiavi? ↘ ///
> 8. Ho preso il treno. ///
> 9. Mi sono trasferita due anni fa. ///
> 10. Abbiamo trovato una casa nuova. ///

**Content check**

| Required | Where it is |
| --- | --- |
| Part 1 order = L3 ex 6 part A key | 1 `4B` sete · 2 `1A` anno · 3 `6B` copia · 4 `3A` cassa · 5 `2B` sono · 6 `5A` nonno · 7 `4A` sette · 8 `3B` casa · 9 `1B` ano · 10 `6A` coppia · 11 `5B` nono · 12 `2A` sonno — both members of all six pairs, never one straight after its partner |
| Part 2 = L3 ex 6 part B, items 1–10, in order | as above; key `tt · ss · tt · tt · tt · tt · ss · s · nn · s` |
| Participles with a double | 7 — `fatto`, `successo`, `letto`, `detto`, `rotto`, `scritto`, `messo` |
| Singles that stop "always double" winning | `preso` (8), `casa` (10) |

**Notes to voice talent.**

1. **Identical speed and volume for both members of every pair.** Any difference
   other than length — a louder `cassa`, a slower `sete` — makes part 1 solvable
   without hearing the double, which destroys the stage.
2. **A double is held, not louder.** And the vowel before it is **shorter**: `fat-to`
   with a short `a`; `fa-to` would have a long one. Make that vowel difference
   audible but natural — it is the half of the rule English speakers are never told.
3. `ano` is said exactly like every other item: plainly, no pause before it, no
   reaction. L3's teacher notes handle why it is there.
4. Part 2 is `normal` pace. Do not slow the participle down to help — learners
   will meet `fatto` at speed, and the point of step 2 is hearing it at speed.

## Cue 3 — `assets/audio/unit-02/u02-l05-tre-cose.mp3`

**Serves.** L5 stage 2 (`A6` rung 1 — the worked model) and exercise 1. The
self-study learner reads it aloud, both voices, timing themselves (L5 self-study
stage 2).
**Length target.** **1:25–1:35**, ~160 words: ≈1:10 of speech at an unhurried
≈135 wpm plus ≈20 s of marked pauses (five `///`, ten `//`). L5 says "it must land near 1:30" and
learners time themselves against it — do not speed up to get under it, and do not
pad it over.
**Speakers.**
- **Luca**, late 20s, from Bologna, now in Turin — Giulia's brother from cue 1, who
  graduated in July. Standard, relaxed.
- **Ilaria**, his friend, 20s–30s, standard. Two short questions only. In class a
  confident learner reads her lines (L5 Materials).

**Pace.** `normal`. One take.

**Must contain.** The task's target shape at the success criterion's standard and
no showing-off beyond it: **three things, each with a *when***; **eight or more**
passato prossimo verbs, **at least two with `essere`**; **two follow-up questions
that pick up what Luca said**, each answered in **two or more sentences**. No
structure the unit has not taught; no imperfetto. It reaches 12 past verbs,
because a model that only *just* reaches 8 teaches learners to count instead of
tell — but every verb is one a learner has used in L1–L4.

**Script**

> **[1] Luca:** Allora, tre cose del mio anno. ↘ // La prima: a luglio mi sono
> laureato in ingegneria — dopo sette anni, lo so. // Alla festa ho ballato tutta
> la notte. ///
>
> **[2] Luca:** La seconda: a ottobre ho trovato lavoro a Torino. // Ho fatto tre
> colloqui, → e alla fine un'azienda di trasporti ha scelto **me**. // A novembre
> mi sono trasferito, → e il trasloco è stato facile: due valigie e una bici. ↘
> ///
>
> **[3] Luca:** La terza è meno bella. // In primavera i miei genitori si sono
> separati. // È stato strano, → ma adesso stanno bene tutti e due. ↘ ///
>
> **[4] Ilaria:** E come ti trovi a Torino? ↘
>
> **[5] Luca:** Mi trovo bene. // Il mese scorso mi sono iscritto a un corso di
> arrampicata, → e così adesso conosco un po' di gente. // E la città mi piace: è
> elegante e tranquilla. ↘ ///
>
> **[6] Ilaria:** E tua madre? // È rimasta a Bologna? ↗
>
> **[7] Luca:** No, si è trasferita in campagna, in una vecchia fattoria. // A dicembre sono
> andato a trovarla, → ed è proprio felice. ↘ ///

**Content check**

| Required | Where it is |
| --- | --- |
| Three things, each with a *when* | graduated `a luglio` [1] · job `a ottobre`, moved `a novembre` [2] · parents separated `in primavera` [3]. In the answers: `il mese scorso` [5], `a dicembre` [7]. |
| Passato prossimo, in order (L5 ex 1 key) | `mi sono laureato`, `ho ballato`, `ho trovato`, `ho fatto`, `ha scelto`, `mi sono trasferito`, `è stato`, `si sono separati`, `è stato`, `mi sono iscritto`, `si è trasferita`, `sono andato` — **12**, of which **8 with `essere`** |
| Two follow-ups that pick up what was said | `E come ti trovi a Torino?` (picks up the move) · `E tua madre? È rimasta a Bologna?` (picks up the separation: a guess the listener can make from what Luca said, which is what a follow-up is) |
| Answers of two sentences or more | [5] three sentences · [7] two |
| Continuity with cue 1 | Luca's graduation (July, seven years, the party) and the parents' separation (spring; the mother moved to an old farm in the country, the father stayed in Bologna) match Giulia's message [6]–[7]. Ilaria's guess `È rimasta a Bologna?` is wrong for the mother and right for the father, which is why Luca's `No` is natural. |
| `mi piace` [5] | A U1 chunk; **not** a past verb and not counted (L5 teacher notes) |

**Lesson fix made against this script (ITA-20).** L5 exercise 1 part B's key said
"**seven** with `essere`". The twelve verbs it lists contain **eight** `essere`
forms (`mi sono laureato`, `mi sono trasferito`, `è stato` ×2, `si sono separati`,
`mi sono iscritto`, `si è trasferita`, `sono andato`). The key now says eight; the
twelve verbs and their order are unchanged.

**Notes to voice talent.**

1. **Sound like a person telling a friend, not like a reader.** `lo so` [1] is a
   self-deprecating aside; let it be one.
2. `[2]` `ha scelto **me**` — contrastive stress, as Giulia uses it in cue 1. The
   family resemblance is intended.
3. `[4]` `E come ti trovi a Torino? ↘` falls (question word). `[6]` `È rimasta a
   Bologna? ↗` rises (yes/no). Ilaria must sound **interested** — she is the model
   for the asker's role, and a flat asker teaches a flat follow-up.
4. `[7]` `andato a trovarla` — run `trovarla` together; do not stress `-la`.
5. Hit 1:25–1:35. Learners time themselves against this recording.

## Cue 4 — `assets/audio/unit-02/u02-l05-domande.mp3`

**Serves.** L5 stage 4 (self-study: answer two of its questions into the attempt 1
recording) and stage 6 (solo learners, and any pair short of an asker: play it
after the three things, answer into the pauses; self-study starts from a
**different** question than in stage 4).
**Length target.** **1:30–1:40**: a short opening line, then six numbered questions
(≈25 s of speech in all, with ten `//` beats), each followed by a **10-second**
answer gap (60 s). The gaps are the point of the
cue; do not shorten them.
**Speakers.** Ilaria from cue 3 — the same asker, so the solo learner meets the
voice they heard modelling the role.
**Pace.** `normal`, warm, unhurried.

**Must contain.** Six follow-up questions that work for **any** learner's year, in
`tu`, **gender-neutral** (the speaker cannot know who is answering, so no
`pentito / pentita`, no `soddisfatto / soddisfatta`), each one inviting a
**two-sentence** answer, and between them asking about **then** and **now** — the
two follow-up types L4 and L5 teach. Numbered aloud, so a learner can start from
any question.

**Script**

> **[0]** Che bello! // Ho qualche domanda per te. ///
>
> **[1]** Uno. // E com'è andata, alla fine? ↘ **[pausa 10 secondi]**
>
> **[2]** Due. // E adesso? // Come ti trovi, adesso? ↘ **[pausa 10 secondi]**
>
> **[3]** Tre. // Che cosa è stato più difficile, all'inizio? ↘ **[pausa 10
> secondi]**
>
> **[4]** Quattro. // E poi? ↗ // Che cosa è successo dopo? ↘ **[pausa 10 secondi]**
>
> **[5]** Cinque. // C'è una cosa che non hai ancora fatto? ↗ **[pausa 10
> secondi]**
>
> **[6]** Sei. // E la tua famiglia, i tuoi amici? // Che cosa hanno detto? ↘ **[pausa 10
> secondi]**

**Content check**

| Required | Where it is |
| --- | --- |
| About *then* | [1], [3], [4], [6] |
| About *now* | [2] (`come ti trovi`, present) · [5] (`non hai ancora fatto` — goal 3's *not yet*) |
| Gender-neutral throughout | yes — no participle or adjective refers to the listener |
| Each answerable in two sentences about any event | yes — none presupposes a particular kind of event |

**Notes to voice talent.**

1. **Keep the ten-second gaps exactly.** Silence on a recording feels long; to a
   learner answering in a second language it is short.
2. Say the number, a short beat, then the question — so a learner who starts the
   track at question 4 knows where they are.
3. `[4]` `E poi? ↗` rises — it is the prompt to go on, the same `E poi?` L1 and L2
   use. `Che cosa è successo dopo? ↘` then falls.
4. Sound curious, not like an examiner. The learner is meant to feel asked, not
   tested.
