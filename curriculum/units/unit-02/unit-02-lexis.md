# Unit 2 — Le parole: *eventi e svolte*

> **Italian signed off** (ITA-21, Italian Content Editor, 2026-11-09) — pass with

**This is your revision list.** Thirty items you are expected to **use**, fifteen
more you only need to **recognise**, and one false friend. Every item has an
example sentence you could plausibly say about your own life, and a "met in"
column showing where in the unit you actually used it — `L3 s5` means Lesson 3,
stage 5, `hw` means that lesson's homework, `cue 1` is the L1 audio, and `Ckpt`
means it is on the checkpoint.

**How to revise from this file.** Cover the English column, go down the list, and
say each item aloud in a sentence **about yourself, in the past** — not the
sentence printed here. The printed sentence is a model, not the answer. If you
cannot build your own sentence, you have recognised the word, not learned it.

The syllabus-level version of this set, with the frequency reasoning and the
interlock argument, is
[`lexis-progression.md` §Unit 2](../../syllabus/lexis-progression.md#unit-2--eventi-e-svolte).
This file is the study list. Citation forms match it exactly.

**How the thirty are split, and why not by the syllabus's subgroups.** The
syllabus groups the set as A (time anchors, 10), B (life events, 12) and C
(outcome and evaluation, 8). This unit teaches them in four lesson-sets, as
[`unit-02-overview.md` §3](unit-02-overview.md#3-lexis-set) explains: the four
outcome verbs that take `essere` are taught with the grammar in L2, and `la
fattoria` waits for L3's false-friend slot. The subgroup letter is kept on every
item, because the checkpoint samples by subgroup.

**One distinction worth owning:**

- **`licenziarsi` is what you do; `essere licenziato` is what is done to you.** `Mi
  sono licenziato` — *I quit.* `Sono stato licenziato` — *I was fired.* Giulia is
  careful to say which (L1 exercise 1 item 1), and so is the checkpoint (Part A
  item 2). Only the first is yours to produce in this unit.

**Two irregular participles live in this set**: `iscritto` (`-tto` family) and
`assunto` (`-nto`). Both are on L2's family table.

## Set A′ — the four `essere` outcome verbs · taught in L2 · 4 items

Syllabus subgroup **C**. In L2 rather than L3 because they are the unit's
**grammar** wearing vocabulary's clothes: all four take `essere`, two because they
are reflexive (rule 1) and two because they are on the list (rule 2).

| Italian | English | Example sentence | Met in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `andare bene` / `andare male` | to go well / badly | Il colloquio è andato bene, ma il trasloco è andato malissimo. | L1 cue 1, s8 · L2 s6 · L3 s4, s8 · L5 s3, s6 · cue 4 |
| `riuscire (a)` | to manage (to) | Alla fine sono riuscito a trovare casa vicino al lavoro. | L2 s6, s8, hw |
| `rendersi conto (di)` | to realise | A dicembre mi sono resa conto di una cosa importante: lavoro troppo. | L2 s6 · L4 s2, s3, s6 |
| `pentirsi (di)` | to regret | Ho cambiato lavoro e non mi sono mai pentito della scelta. | L2 s6 · L4 s4, s6, s7, hw · L5 s6 |

- **`riuscire` joins the rule-2 list** — it is the sixteenth verb on it (L2 stage
  6). `Sono riuscita`, never `*ho riuscito`.
- **`rendersi conto` → `reso`**, and it agrees: `mi sono reso conto` (a man), `mi
  sono resa conto` (a woman). `conto` never changes.
- **`pentirsi di` + what you regret.** `Non me ne sono mai pentito` is what an
  Italian will often say; you will hear it, and it is not yet yours to build
  (U6, `ne`).

## Set B — time anchors, and how it went · taught in L3 · 15 items

### B (i) — time anchors · L3 stages 2–3 · 10 items

Syllabus subgroup **A**. `non … ancora` is taught with `già` and **counted with
it** (`lexis-progression.md` §Unit 2).

| Italian | English | Example sentence | Met in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `l'anno scorso` | last year | L'anno scorso sono andata in Portogallo per la prima volta. | L2 hw · L3 s2, s6, s8, hw · L4 s4, s5 |
| `il mese scorso` | last month | Il mese scorso ho cominciato un corso di yoga. | L3 s2, s4, s8, hw · L5 s2, hw |
| `due anni fa` | two years ago | Mi sono trasferito qui due anni fa. | L1 cue 1 · L2 hw · L3 s2, s4, s8, hw · L4 s2, s4 |
| `da allora` | since then | Ho cambiato lavoro a marzo. Da allora vado al lavoro in bici. | L1 cue 1 · L3 s2, s4, s8, hw · L4 s2, s4, s5, s7, hw · L5 hw · Ckpt C1 |
| `all'inizio` | at first | All'inizio è stato difficile, ma poi mi sono trovata bene. | L3 s2, s4, s8, hw · L4 s4, s7, hw · L5 hw · cue 4 |
| `alla fine` | in the end | Ho fatto tre colloqui, e alla fine ho trovato un posto. | L1 cue 1, s8 · L2 s6 · L3 s2 · L4 s7 · L5 s2, hw · cue 4 |
| `di recente` | recently | Di recente mi sono iscritta in palestra. | L3 s2, s4 |
| `finora` | so far | Finora è andato tutto bene. | L3 s2, s4, s8, hw · L5 hw |
| `già` (+ `non … ancora`) | already (+ not yet) | Ho già trovato casa, ma non ho ancora fatto il trasloco. | L2 s6 · L3 s1, s3, s4, s8, hw · L4 s5 · L5 hw · cue 4 · Ckpt A3, C2, C3 |
| `appena` | just | Sono appena tornato dalle vacanze. | L3 s3, s4, s8, hw · Ckpt A (text) |

- **`[quantity] + fa`**, after, like *ago*: `tre mesi fa`, `una settimana fa`.
- **`scorso` goes after the noun and agrees with it**: `il mese scorso`, `la
  settimana scorsa`, `sabato scorso`.
- **`da allora` + present** when it is still true: `Da allora abito a Milano` —
  never `*da allora ho abitato`, which says you no longer live there. **`finora` +
  passato prossimo**: `finora è andato tutto bene`.
- **`già`, `appena`, `ancora` sit between the helper and the participle; `non` goes
  before the helper**: `non ho ancora finito`, `mi sono già iscritta`, `non mi sono
  ancora iscritta`.
- **"At first I didn't know anyone"** needs the imperfetto (`non conoscevo
  nessuno`), which is Unit 4. L3 and L4 accept it if you reach for it and never ask
  for it; every example in this file avoids it.

## Recognition only — not counted in the thirty, not tested

### The `piacere` family, and `mi sembra`

**Learn these as phrases.** They are deliberately **not explained** in this unit,
and they are not on the checkpoint. All are in L1's cue 1; the one-minute note at
the end of L1 stage 6 is their whole treatment.

| Chunk | English | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `mi piace` / `mi piacciono` | I like | From Unit 1. **Fixed chunk, deliberately not explained** — taken apart in **Unit 8**. |
| `mi interessa` | I'm interested in | From Unit 1. Same. |
| `mi manca` | I miss | From Unit 1. Same. `mi manca Bologna` — the thing you miss is the subject. |
| `mi sembra` | it seems to me | **New here** — this is the first time the course plants it (Seed 1, `U2 L1`). `Mi sembra giusto così` — *that seems right to me*. Same treatment: a phrase, Unit 8. |
| `mi è piaciuto` | I liked it (about the past) | **New here.** A year in review cannot be told without it, and a learner left to build it says `*ho piaciuto`. Learn it whole. **Why it has `è`, not `ho`, is Unit 8's question**; it is not evidence for L2's rule 2, and it never counts as a past verb in your task. |

## False friend — Unit 2 of 12

One per unit, two minutes in L3, and they are tested cumulatively at both
milestones. **Add it to your false-friend page, under `i parenti`.**

| Item | Actually means | It is **not** | That word is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `la fattoria` | farm | *factory* | `la fabbrica` |

You heard it first in Giulia's message, in a sentence built so that the wrong
reading is impossible: *"Mia madre è andata a vivere in campagna, in una vecchia
fattoria con le galline."* Countryside and hens are not a factory. Use the context,
not the warning.
