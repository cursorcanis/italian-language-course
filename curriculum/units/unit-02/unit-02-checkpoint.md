## Part A — Lettura · 3 minutes · 4 marks `[ITA?]`

**How it runs.** Read the message, then answer the four questions **in English or
Italian** — this part checks understanding, not writing. In class everyone reads
silently at once; the teacher calls *start* and *stop*.

> **Da:** Marta · **A:** Ben
>
> Ciao Ben! Com'è andato il tuo anno? Il mio è stato pieno. A febbraio mi sono
> licenziata dalla banca, e a marzo mi sono iscritta a un corso di cucina. Mia
> sorella Elena e mio cugino Marco hanno studiato ingegneria insieme, a Torino. A
> luglio, dopo cinque anni, finalmente si è laureata: grande festa a casa! E io?
> Ho appena fatto un colloquio in un ristorante, ma non ho ancora saputo niente.
> Incrociamo le dita! Un abbraccio, Marta

*Level A2, ~80 words. Informal message, `tu`. Every word is from set 2, set 1 or
the A2 base; `ho saputo` and `incrociamo le dita` were glossed and met in L3's
chat.*

**Questions**

1. *(gist)* What is Marta's message mainly about?
   (a) a job interview · (b) her year · (c) a graduation party
2. *(detail)* What did Marta do in February, and what did she do in March?
3. *(detail)* Does Marta know yet whether she got the job at the restaurant?
4. *(main structure)* Who graduated in July?
   (a) Elena · (b) Marco · (c) both Elena and Marco

**Key.** 1 **(b)** · 2 **February: she quit / left her job at the bank. March:
she enrolled on (started) a cooking course** — both halves needed · 3 **No — not
yet** (also accept *she's still waiting*, *she hasn't heard*) · 4 **(a)**. **1 mark
each, 4 marks.** Spelling and language not marked: a right answer in rough English
is right.

**Marking notes.**
- **Item 4 is the one that turns on the unit's grammar.** `si è laureata` — the
  participle ends in `-a`, so it is **one woman**: Elena. A learner who reads only
  `Elena`, `Marco` and `laureat-` cannot choose, because all three options name the
  people in the sentence before. (b) would need `si è laureato`; (c) would need `si
  sono laureati`. This is L2 stage 2's rule 3 — *with `essere`, the ending tells
  you who* — turned into a reading item. For the Italian Content Editor: the
  sentence deliberately does not name Elena again; please confirm it reads as
  natural Italian rather than as a puzzle.
- **Item 2.** *She was fired* scores 0 for February: `mi sono licenziata` is the
  set A pair `licenziarsi` / `essere licenziato` from L1 stage 3, and it means she
  quit. *She started cooking* without *course* is accepted for March.
- **Item 1.** (a) and (c) are both in the message, which is what makes them
  distractors; neither is what the message is *about*.

## Part B — Grammar in a text · 3 minutes · 6 marks `[ITA?]`

Paolo is writing to his friend Sofia. Write the **passato prossimo** of the verb in
brackets. One to four words per gap.

> Ciao Sofia, sono Paolo! Ti racconto il mio anno. A gennaio io e Giorgio abbiamo
> cambiato città: adesso abitiamo a Napoli. A febbraio **(1)** ______ (trovare,
> io) un posto in una scuola. A maggio i miei genitori **(2)** ______ (venire) da
> Torino, e mia madre **(3)** ______ (rimanere) qui due settimane. In estate io e
> Giorgio **(4)** ______ (andare) in Grecia. A settembre **(5)** ______
> (iscriversi, io) a un corso di vela. E Napoli? Io e Giorgio **(6)** ______
> (trovarsi) subito bene: la gente è simpaticissima. E tu? Un abbraccio, Paolo

*Level A2, ~78 words before the gaps are filled, ~90 after. Informal message, `tu`. One clause per idea. Paolo and
Giorgio are both men, and the text says so twice (`sono Paolo`, `io e Giorgio`).
The one past form outside the gaps (`abbiamo cambiato città`) is an `avere` verb
with an object — it models nothing a gap needs.*

**Key and alternatives.**

| Gap | Tests | Key | Also accept | Not accepted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | auxiliary — `avere`, with an object | **ho trovato** | — | `*sono trovato` |
| B2 | auxiliary `essere` (rule 2) + agreement `-i`, irregular | **sono venuti** | — | `*hanno venuto`, `sono venute`, `sono venuto` |
| B3 | auxiliary `essere` (rule 2) + agreement `-a`, irregular | **è rimasta** | — | `*ha rimasto`, `è rimasto`, `*è rimanuta` |
| B4 | auxiliary `essere` (rule 2) + agreement `-i` (`noi`, two men) | **siamo andati** | — | `*abbiamo andato`, `siamo andate`, `siamo andato` |
| B5 | **recycled** — riflessivi in the past, `io` | **mi sono iscritto** | — | `*sono iscritto`, `*si sono iscritto`, `*mi ho iscritto`, `mi sono iscritta` (Paolo is a man) |
| B6 | **recycled** — riflessivi in the past, `noi` | **ci siamo trovati** | — | `*si siamo trovati`, `*siamo trovati`, `ci siamo trovate` |

**1 mark each, all or nothing, 6 marks.** B1–B4 are the main structure; **B5–B6
are the recycled gaps, and pass condition 4 needs at least one of them.** Agreement
is part of the key on B2–B6 (ITA-4 Part B marking rule). Spelling and accents not
marked; a single `s` or `t` is not penalised (`*iscrito` scores if the rest is
right).

**Marking notes.**
- **B1** is there so that "always `essere`" is not a winning strategy. `trovare`
  has an object (`un posto`): `avere`, and nothing agrees.
- **B2–B4** are rule 2 — the `essere` list — and rule 3, the ending follows the
  subject. B3 is the L2 exercise 3 item 8 decision in miniature: `rimanere` is on
  the list, `rimasto` is irregular, and `mia madre` makes it `-a`.
- **B5 and B6 are Unit 1's choices inside Unit 2's tense.** The past is this unit's;
  **which little word** is Unit 1's: `io` → `mi`, `io e Giorgio` → `ci`. `*si
  siamo trovati` is the commonest wrong answer — the learner has stored `si` as
  "the reflexive word" — and it is exactly L4 exercise 4 item 2. A dropped pronoun
  (`*sono iscritto`) is L4 exercise 4 item 1.
- **B6 is `trovarsi bene`**, Unit 1 set A, so the gap also checks that a U1 item is
  still available. `Ci siamo trovati subito bene` and `Ci siamo trovati bene
  subito` are both fine if a learner moves the word — the key is the verb form.

## Part C — Words in use · 2 minutes · 5 marks `[ITA?]`

**C1–C3 and C5.** Write the missing word. No options are given.

1. Ci siamo trasferiti a Leeds nel 2022. Da ______ abitiamo vicino al parco.
2. — Hai ______ trovato casa? — Sì, già: ho firmato il contratto ieri.
3. Ho trovato un appartamento, ma non ho ______ firmato il contratto: la firma è
   lunedì.
5. Mio nonno ha una ______ in Toscana, con le galline e due cavalli.

**C4.** Which word is the odd one out **because of what it means**? Circle it, and
write **why** in one English word.

4. `il colloquio` · `assumere` · `il tirocinio` · `il trasloco`

**Key.** 1 **allora** · 2 **già** · 3 **ancora** · 4 **il trasloco** — *home*
(accept any one English word that says it is about the house, not work: *home*,
*house*, *moving*, *flat*; or a correct gloss: *removal*, *move*) · 5 **fattoria**.
**1 mark each, 5 marks.** Spelling not marked unless the word has become a
different word.

**What the five sample.** Subgroup A, time anchors (C1–C3: `da allora`, `già`,
`non … ancora`) and subgroup B, life events (C4, and the false friend C5). **Two
chunks** (`da allora`, `non … ancora`). C2 and C3 are the checkpoint's evidence for
**can-do goal 3**, as the overview says.

**Marking notes.**
- **C1** — `Da allora` + present, L3 stage 2: `abitiamo` is still true. Only
  `allora` fits; `Da sempre` contradicts `nel 2022`.
- **C2** — `Sì, già` in the answer fixes the question as a `già` question. `appena`
  is **not** accepted: *Have you just found a house? — Yes, already* is not an
  exchange. `mai` is not accepted either.
- **C3** — `la firma è lunedì` makes the signing future, so the contract is **not
  yet** signed: `ancora`. `già` contradicts `non`; `mai` (*never*) contradicts
  Monday's signing.
- **C4** — three are about work (U10 picks them up); `il trasloco` is the house
  move. `assumere` is the only verb, so *verb* would be a correct answer the key
  rejects — the words *because of what it means* are what make that fair, as in the
  Unit 1 checkpoint's C4.
- **C5** — **`fabbrica` is wrong**: hens and horses are a farm, and the sentence was
  written to make that plain. This is the unit's false-friend item, and the only
  one.

## Part D — Writing · 2 minutes · 10 marks `[ITA?]`

**Prompt** (read it, then write — two minutes, everyone at once):

> An Italian friend you haven't seen for a year sends you a message: *"So — what's
> new? Tell me about your year!"* Write a reply of **40–60 words**. Tell them:
> **(1)** three things that happened to you this year, **(2)** when each one
> happened, and **(3)** one thing that is different now because of it.

**Required elements** (this is the TA checklist): **(1)** three events · **(2)** each
event placed in time — a month, `fa`, `scorso`, a season · **(3)** one sentence
about **now**. Length 40–60 words.

**Elicitation floor:** **5 passato prossimo verbs, at least 1 with `essere`.** Below
the floor, TS cannot score above 2, however accurate the rest is.

**Scales** — from [`assessment/rubrics.md`](../../../assessment/rubrics.md):

- **TA — Task achievement, 0–5.** **You** score this, against the three required
  elements and the length. All three present and 40–60 words is a 3 at least;
  missing one is a 2. Full descriptors:
  [§Scale TA](../../../assessment/rubrics.md#scale-ta--task-achievement-05).
- **TS — the passato prossimo, 0–5.** **A partner** scores this, reading once:
  count the past verbs, and count how many have the right helper (`avere` /
  `essere`) **and** the right ending. Five or more, most of them right, at least one
  with `essere` — that is a 3 (pass). Full descriptors:
  [§Scale TS](../../../assessment/rubrics.md#scale-ts--range-and-control-of-the-target-structure-05).
  You do not score your own TS — a learner cannot reliably judge their own control
  of a structure learned this week, and the error is always optimistic.

**What counts as a past verb for TS.** Any passato prossimo, reflexive or not.
**`mi è piaciuto` does not count** — it is the L1 phrase, recognition-only — and it
is not an error either (L5 teacher notes).

**Not scored here:** general accuracy, spelling, the time words' own accuracy. A
reply that writes `*la settimana scorso` loses nothing in Part D; a reply that
writes `*ho andato` loses it in TS and only there.

**A model reply** — for the marker's reference, scoring TA 5 and TS 5 (59 words;
six past verbs, three with `essere`):

> Ciao Marco! Che anno! A febbraio ho fatto un colloquio e ho cambiato lavoro. A
> giugno mi sono trasferita a Leeds con il mio compagno, e ad agosto siamo andati
> in Sicilia. Il mese scorso mi sono iscritta a un corso di italiano, e ho già
> fatto il primo esame! Adesso vado al lavoro a piedi. Un abbraccio, Kate

*Band-3 and band-5 anchor answers for each unit's Part D are the Italian Content
Editor's to write (ITA-4 §Open; `rubrics.md` §Marker calibration). This model is a
reference, not an anchor.*

**Working alone, with no partner?** Score TA, leave TS blank, and write
**provisional** next to your result. A provisional result tells you everything
except whether the past verbs were right.

**Finding a marker** — the note L5's self-study points to. If your Unit 1 result
was also provisional, **this is the second in a row**, and ITA-4 says two
consecutive provisional results should trigger finding a marker. Before Unit 3,
give your Part D to one person who can read it once and do the TS count above —
another learner, a teacher, an Italian speaker, an online exchange partner. They
need no training: the count is the whole job. Write their TS in, and your result
stops being provisional.

## Your result, and what to do about it

**Write your result as a profile**, not a number:
`A __/4 · B main __/4 + recycled __/2 · C __/5 · D TA __/5, TS __/5 · total __/25 ·
pass / not yet`. A bare "19/25" tells you nothing you can act on.

**Pass** needs all four: **≥ 18/25**, **TA ≥ 3**, **TS ≥ 3**, **≥ 1/2 on B5–B6.**

| Result | Reading | Do this |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pass, **22–25** | Secure | Start Unit 3. Write down the two items you got wrong and look at them once more this week. |
| Pass, **18–21** | Pass | Start Unit 3, **and** redo the self-study for your weakest part (table below) before Unit 3's L3. |
| **Not yet** — any condition missed | Not yet | **Start Unit 3 anyway**, and write a **carry-forward entry** (below). Redo the self-study for the part that failed before Unit 3's L3. A checkpoint result never stops you starting the next unit. |

**Why "not yet" does not hold you back.** The passato prossimo comes back, already
scheduled: at **U3 L4** (`ho trovato`, `mi sono trasferito` — finding a flat), as
its **mandated** second encounter at **U6 L4** (agreement with a preceding pronoun,
`l'ho vista`), and in **U5**, where it is set against the imperfetto. A structure
that did not land in week one often lands at its next encounter, inside a new
theme, which is a better repair than repeating the same lessons.

**The carry-forward entry.** Write it on the first page of your notebook:

> *PP auxiliary + agreement (U2) — returns U6 L4. Closed when U6 L4 is done **and**
> the U6 checkpoint's recycled gaps (passato prossimo carrying participle
> agreement) are right.*

If the condition you missed was **B5–B6** — both recycled gaps wrong — write
instead: *Riflessivi in the past (U1, in U2's tense) — returns U3 L4, and as a
mandated encounter at U4 L4 (habitual past, `mi svegliavo`). Closed when U4 L4 is
done and the reflexives are right in a later checkpoint or Milestone 1.*

**If you have a Unit 1 carry-forward entry** (riflessivi in the present): it closes
**now** if B5 **and** B6 are both right — that is the condition Unit 1 wrote. If
not, keep it open and add U3 L4 to it.

**Two "not yet" results in a row** — Unit 1 and here — mean doing the repair set
for the earlier structure, the reflexives, before Unit 3's L2 (ITA-4 escalation;
the repair sets are not built yet). **Three in a row** means re-running the
placement check. One is normal.

**Where your marks were lost tells you which thing to redo:**

| Lost most marks in | Redo |
| --- | --- |
| Part A | L1 self-study stages 4–6, then L2 stage 2 question 4 — item 4 is about what the ending tells you |
| Part B, B1–B4 | L2 self-study stages 2, 3 and 5 — the three rules, exercise 1 rounds 2–3, exercise 3 items 8–10 — then exercise 6 again |
| Part B, B5–B6 | L4 self-study stage 4 — exercise 3 part A — and stage 5, the error clinic |

## Self-rating

Rate each goal **1**, **2** or **3**. The same three-point scale is used in all
twelve units.

**1** not yet · **2** with support, notes or a second attempt · **3** unsupported

| # | Goal | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | I can say what I've done over the past year — work, study, travel, moving — and place each event in time. | | | |
| 2 | I can tell someone about a significant change in my life in five to eight connected sentences, rather than as separate facts. | | | |
| 3 | I can say whether something has already happened, hasn't happened yet, or has just happened. | | | |
| 4 | I can ask someone about their recent past and follow up on what they tell me instead of moving to the next question. | | | |

**How to read your own ratings**, because this is the part a learner alone acts on:

- **Goal 1 at 3** is the expected outcome of this unit, and today's recording is
  the evidence. If it is a 2, listen to the recording again: the cause is almost
  always too few past verbs, which L5 exercise 4 says is fixed on paper — planner
  rows 4 and 5.
- **Goal 3** has objective evidence here (C2, C3 and Part A item 3). If you rated it
  3 and lost C2 or C3, trust the checkpoint, and redo L3 exercise 2.
- **Goal 2** is checked by the written post (rubric row 3) rather than here. Rate it
  after you have written the post, not before.
- **Goal 4 at 1 or 2 is normal and not a failure.** It needs another person, and no
  ten-minute item can test a follow-up. Unit 3 keeps asking you to do it. A learner
  who rates goal 4 at 3 without having asked a real person a real follow-up has not
  yet tested it.
