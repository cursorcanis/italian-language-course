## 1. If you are teaching this unit cold

- **What it is for.** **Consolidation, not first contact.** A solid-A2 learner has
  met the passato prossimo and uses it with `avere` for everything. The unit's job
  is to make the three things an English speaker actually gets wrong — the
  auxiliary, the agreement, the irregular participles — reliable enough to tell a
  year with, and to anchor every event in time.
- **The one thing that must land.** **Reflexive verbs and the `essere` list take
  `essere`.** English *have* + participle sits in every learner's mouth, and `*ho
  andato` / `*mi ho trasferito` are the errors that survive to B1 if they survive
  this unit. L2 teaches them as two **lists** (rule 1, rule 2) — not as a theory of
  motion — and leaves them on the board all lesson.
- **The stage that always overruns.** **L3 stage 2** — the time anchors, 15 minutes.
  It is the unit's secondary grammar point *and* its heaviest lexis stage, and
  learners want to tell their own `due anni fa` stories. Hold the fifteen minutes;
  stage 8 is where the stories go. L1 stage 3 (lexis set A, 13 minutes) is the other
  front-loaded lexis stage and runs long for the same reason (template README §9).
- **Most likely to be cut short.** **L3**, which carries five things — lexis set B,
  a reading, `già / appena / non ancora`, the false friend and the double
  consonants. Protect **stage 7** (the unit's only pronunciation stage) and **stage 8
  rung 3** (the only place goal 3 is performed without the scaffold).
- **What Unit 3 needs from this one.** **`ho trovato`, `mi sono trasferito`,
  `abbiamo visto` — said without thinking.** U3's L4 recycles the passato prossimo
  inside the housing theme, and its main point (relativi) is built on top of
  sentences learners must already be able to make. A *not yet* at the checkpoint
  does **not** hold the learner back (ITA-4); it writes a carry-forward entry that
  closes at **U6 L4**, the mandated return. Make sure every learner who gets one
  writes it down.

## 2. Error profile `[ITA?]`

The table `A8` error-clinic items are drawn from. The unit has **two** clinics —
L2 exercise 5 (the main structure) and L4 exercise 4 (the recycled ones) — and
every row below is in at least one of them; the right-hand column says where. All
ten are predictions for an **English L1** specifically.

| # | Error | The wrong sentence | Why an English L1 produces it | Where it shows | Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `avere` with a verb on the `essere` list | `*Ho andato in Spagna.` | English *I have gone* puts *have* in the sentence, so `ho` is the translation. | L1 s8–s9 (expected, not corrected), L2 onward, L5 | **L1: recast only** — "sei andata, sì" — and save two for L2's opener. | 2 | `avere` with a reflexive | `*Mi ho trasferito a Torino.` | The learner has the U1 pronoun and the English *have*, and combines them. | L2 s5, s8; L5 | Point at **rule 1**: "reflexive → `essere`, always. `*mi ho` never exists." `A8`: L2 ex 5 item 3. |
| 3 | Reflexive pronoun dropped in the past | `*Sono trasferita a Bologna.` · `*Sei trasferito?` | `sono` now feels like the whole auxiliary, and it pushes U1's pronoun out — U1's error 1, resurfacing under a new tense. | L1 s8, L4 throughout, L5 | "**`mi`** sono trasferita — Unit 1, still true." | 4 | Pronoun not following the subject | `*Io e Marco si siamo organizzati.` · `*Si sei trovato bene?` | `si` has been stored as *the* reflexive word. | 5 | No agreement with `essere` (the `-o` default) | `*I miei genitori sono tornato.` · `*Anna si è trasferito.` | English participles never change, so the learner never looks at the end of the word. | L1 s9 (expected), L2 s5, s8, L5 under load | "**Who** came back? How many?" — and point at rule 3. In L5, **say nothing during**: it costs under criterion 2 and rubric row 1, which is where it should cost. `A8`: L2 ex 5 item 5. |
| 6 | Agreement with `avere` | `*Giulia ha lasciata il lavoro.` · `*abbiamo lasciati` | **Appears only after rule 3 has landed** — the rule is over-extended. | 7 | Regularised irregular participle | `*prenduto`, `*succeduto`, `*scrivuto`, `*mettuto` | A correct rule (`-ere` → `-uto`) over-extended. | L2 s4 onward, L5 | Name the **family**, not the rule: "`-so`: preso." The family is what is retrievable under pressure. `A8`: L2 ex 5 items 6, 8. |

A sign the reflexive pattern is strong. | L4 s4–s7 | "Is `uscire` reflexive?" — one question. Say that it is a sign of strength. `A8`: L4 ex 4 item 5. |
| 9 | *Now* in the past after `da allora` / `adesso` | `*Da allora ho abitato in centro.` · `*Adesso mi sono trovata bene.` | English present perfect for a state that is still true (*I've lived here since*). Not ungrammatical — it says the wrong thing: that it is over. | L3 s2, s8; L4 s7; L5 | One question: "**still true?**" — and they switch to the present. Point at L3's two-line board pair. `A8`: L4 ex 4 item 3. |
| 10 | `non` / `già` / `ancora` in English order | `*Ho non ancora trovato casa.` · `*Ho trovato non ancora.` | *I have not yet found* maps word for word. | L3 s3, s8; L4; L5 | Write `Non ho ancora trovato casa` next to it, say both aloud. The shape `(non) + helper + già / ancora + participle` is shown, not explained. `A8`: L4 ex 4 item 8 (there on purpose, so "it's always a reflexive error" is not a winning strategy). |

**Three notes on using this table.**

- **Rows 1–2 and 5–7 are the main structure's error set** and they are the unit's
  real work. Rows 3, 4 and 8 are Unit 1's reflexive choices inside Unit 2's tense —
  L4's contract. Rows 9–10 are the secondary point.
- **Rows 6 and 8 appear only once the teaching has worked.** Say that to the group
  when they appear. A learner producing `*ha lasciata` has acquired rule 3; one
  producing `*mi sono uscita` has acquired the reflexive pattern. Telling them so is
  true and worth more than the correction.
- **A single consonant in a participle** (`*fato`, `*scrito`) is deliberately **not**
  a row: it is understood, it is the pronunciation point, and it is handled by
  recast at L3 stage 7 and after, never in a clinic.

### Two things learners will ask, and the answers

- **"Why is it `mi è piaciuto`, not `ho piaciuto`?"** — "It's a phrase; Unit 8." That
  is the whole answer, every time (see §4, the fixed row).
- **"What about `devo alzarmi` in the past?"** — The modal + reflexive auxiliary
  switch is **not a production target in this course**. If it comes up, the honest
  answer is a pair, mirroring U1's pair `mi devo alzare` / `devo alzarmi`: **`mi sono
  dovuto alzare`** (pronoun in front → `essere`) and **`ho dovuto alzarmi`** (pronoun
  attached → `avere`). Both are right; accept either; **`*ho dovuto mi alzare` and
  `*mi ho dovuto alzare` are not**. Do not drill it. If a learner wants one form to
  use, give them `ho dovuto alzarmi`: it keeps the helper they would expect.

## 3. Register decisions

This course runs a `tu`/`Lei` axis from Unit 1 onward, so any unit whose materials
use one and not the other is making a decision the learner will generalise from.
**Unit 2 is a `tu` unit**, deliberately: its theme is catching up with people you
know — friends, family, a partner, a classmate. Its decisions, written down:

| Material | Register | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cue 1, Giulia's voice message (L1) | `tu` | A message to a friend, the same relationship as U1 cue 1. The theme is informal and the input is too. |
| L2 cards, *Nadia e Pietro* | `tu` between partners, third person about others | Every question is about a third party (`Che cosa ha fatto Nadia?`), so no address choice arises — which keeps L2 on the auxiliary. |
| L2 paradigm, `lui / lei / Lei` row | **`Lei` named, not practised** | The row reminds learners that `Lei è andato / andata` agrees with the **person**, not with `Lei`'s grammatical feminine. One line, no exercise. |
| L3 chat, *Hai già trovato casa?* | `tu` | Two friends. The follow-up questions are the model for goal 4. |
| L4 culture note | first-person narration | Hannah addresses nobody. The **survey and the svolta are `tu`** (`Ti sei…?`), which is where row 4 of the error profile comes from. |
| L5 cue 3 and cue 4 | `tu` | A friend asking. Cue 4 is **gender-neutral** as well, because it is asking a learner it cannot see. |
| L5 written post | **`voi` to readers** | A blog or social post ends `E voi? Com'è andato il vostro anno?` — the plural "you" of a feed. The rubric does not mark register. |
| Checkpoint | `tu` throughout | Two messages between friends and a reply to a friend. No `tu`/`Lei` choice is marked. |

**What learners are told, if they ask about `Lei` in this unit:** the past works
the same way — `Lei è andata a Roma?` to a woman, `Lei è andato?` to a man — and the
choice between `tu` and `Lei` is Unit 1's rule, unchanged.

## 4. Known weak spots in this unit `[ITA?]`

The honest list. A unit with no weak spots listed has not been read carefully by its
author.

**Fixed row (Units 2–7), verbatim:**

> **The `piacere` seed.** `piacere` chunks are recognition-only until `U8 L2`; do
> not explain the construction.

1. **Where the chunks are in Unit 2, and the one that is riskiest.** In this unit the chunks are all in **L1's cue 1**: `mi piace`, `mi interessa`,
   `mi manca` from Unit 1, plus **`mi sembra`** (Seed 1, planted here for the first
   time) and **`mi è piaciuto`**. The one-minute note at the end of L1 stage 6 is
   their whole treatment; L2 stage 1 leaves `mi è piaciuto` off the board so it
   cannot become evidence for rule 2. If a learner asks, gloss it as a phrase, as U7
   L4 does: "`mi è piaciuto` — *I liked it*. A phrase. Unit 8 takes it apart." **`mi
   è piaciuto` is the riskiest chunk the seed has carried so far**: it looks like a
   passato prossimo and sits in a unit about the passato prossimo, so a teacher's
   instinct to explain it is strongest here. It never counts as a past verb in L5's
   criterion and it is never a checkpoint key.
2. **The checkpoint's recycled gaps cannot meet the template's rule.** Template
   checkpoint rule 4 says recycled gaps come from a **bolded** row of the L4
   contract; Unit 2's row has none (the mandated return of both structures is U4
   L4). The checkpoint follows ITA-4's parameter table — *riflessivi, now in the
   past* — because ITA-4 is the spec. Raised with the Chief under ITA-20; the
   overview §8 and the checkpoint both say so.
3. **L4's contract is the one place the template's "not more practice of this unit's
   grammar" rule bends.** The contract names U1 reflexives *in the past*, and the
   past is this unit's grammar. L4 reads it as an operation entry and runs the
   deletion test ([L4 §The contract and "this unit's grammar"](unit-02-lesson-04.md#the-contract-and-this-units-grammar)).
   A reasonable reviewer could read it the other way. If they do, the fix is in
   `grammar-progression.md`, not in L4.
4. **L2 is heavy.** Three rules, two paradigms, twenty-six irregular participles in
   five families, four outcome verbs and four verbs that take both auxiliaries, in
   one lesson. It is PPP for a reason, and the families make 26 into five things to
   remember — but it is the lesson where a weak group falls behind. Its self-check
   (exercise 6, below 10/14 → redo before L3) is the safety net, and it is only as
   good as the learner's honesty.
5. **No audio is recorded**, so every listening stage depends on the teacher reading
   aloud. For cues 1, 3 and 4 that is merely less good. **Cue 2 is a real risk**:
   the double-consonant discrimination is valid only if both members of each pair
   are said at identical speed and volume, and a teacher who is not a confident
   Italian speaker can invalidate L3 stage 7 without noticing. L3 says to rehearse
   it twice. That is mitigation, not a fix; cue 2 should be near the front of the
   recording queue, alongside U1 cue 3.
6. **`ano`.** Pair 1 of L3's double-consonant set is in the syllabus because the
   error is embarrassing rather than merely unclear. L3's teacher notes give the
   swap (`caro / carro`) for a setting where it is wrong, and the swap needs the
   Content Editor's approval (ITA-21).
7. **Cue 1 grew.** The overview first specified ~230 words and ~2:00; the script
   that holds all 28 past forms, the eleven set A items and the seeds in connected
   speech is ~255 words and ~2:20. The target was raised to fit the script, as Unit
   1's cue 1 was under ITA-14, and L1's timings were checked: stage 5 plays it twice
   in four and a half minutes inside an eleven-minute stage.
8. **Goal 4 has no checkpoint evidence**, for the reason U1's goal 3 had none: a
   ten-minute single turn cannot measure a follow-up. It is assessed in L4 stages
   6–7, L5 stages 4 and 6, and by self-rating. The cost is real and it is stated in
   the overview §1 rather than hidden.
9. **The checkpoint has never been sat**, and its Part D asks for 40–60 written
   words in two minutes, which is tight at A2. The construct-validity check leaves
   the timing unticked; the first live run should time each part.

## 5. Pacing reality

This duplicates each lesson's "to shorten" line **on purpose**: a teacher who is
already running late reads one file, not five. **Neither L4 contract row is bolded**,
so the template's hard rule (never cut the only production site of a mandated
structure) does not strictly bind here — but L4's ledger is what that lesson is for,
and the table still names its free-production stage as uncuttable.

| Lesson | Overruns at | Cut this | What you lose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **L1** | s3 (lexis set A, 13 min) and s8 (year ladder, 14 min) | **s9** (−9); run s8 rung 2 with the frames left up | The third-person attempts L2 opens with. If you cut s9, open L2 with the noticing grid and the two wrong forms in L2's prep line. **Never cut s6** — the noticing grid — L2's presentation is built out of it. |
| **L2** | s2 (guided discovery, 14 min) — discovery takes longer than telling | **s6 part 3** to homework (−4); one round fewer in s3 and in s4 (−4 together); s7 with four items, not eight (−2) | Exercise 4 moves to homework. **Never cut s8** — the only stage of real use; a PPP lesson without its third P is a worksheet. **Never cut s4** — the irregulars are a third of the main structure. |
| **L3** | **s2, every time** (time anchors, 15 min) | s4 as one pass with exercise 3 only (−4); drop s5's pair talk (−3); s8 rung 2 to six minutes (−3); a minute off s1 (−1) | Some reading practice and some evaluation talk — recoverable. **Never cut s7** (the unit's only pronunciation stage) or **s8 rung 3** (the only unscaffolded performance of goal 3). |
| **L4** | **s2** (culture note, 15 min) — it invites holiday stories in English | s3, the sort, as the first homework task (−6); exercise 1 part B as home reading, keep the takeaway (−3); s6 with four prompts, not six (−3) | The retrieval before s4 — so open s4 by asking for the forms instead of writing them up. **Never cut s7 rung 3**: it is the only place both contract structures are produced with the scaffold gone, and without it both ledger rows lose their free-production evidence. |
| **L5** | s3 (planner) and s6 (recording) both run long, and s6 must not | **s1** (−6); s3 to eight minutes (−3); s9's close to seven minutes (−3) | The before-and-after playback, the unit's best motivational moment, which carries no new language. **Never shorten s6**, and **never move the checkpoint out of the lesson** — a checkpoint set as homework is marked by nobody and acted on by nobody. |

**The standing instruction, if you are more than ten minutes behind in any lesson:**
cut a **production** stage's length, never its existence, and never cut a noticing
stage (L1 s6), a discovery stage (L2 s2), or the checkpoint. A shortened production
stage still produces. A deleted noticing stage silently breaks the next lesson,
which is a cost you pay tomorrow and cannot see today.
