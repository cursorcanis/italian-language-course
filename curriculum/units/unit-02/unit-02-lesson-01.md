## Stages in full

### 1 · Opener — *il tuo anno in tre parole* — 5 min · pairs → lockstep

**Purpose.** Put the theme on the table before any vocabulary, so that the lexis
stage answers a question the learner has already asked themselves.

**Instruction.** "With your partner. **Describe your last twelve months in three
words** — any three words, Italian or English. Then tell your partner *why* those
three. Ninety seconds each. I'll tell you when to swap."

**CCQ.** "How many words?" → *Three.* · "Do they have to be Italian?" → *No.*

**Then, lockstep, 90 seconds.** Ask three pairs for their partner's three words.
Write nothing yet.

**Correction stance.** None.

---

### 2 · Engage — my year on a timeline — 7 min · solo → pairs

**Purpose.** Make the learner find, in writing, where their Italian runs out when
they try to tell a year. The boxes are the lesson's shopping list.

**Instruction.** "On your own, five minutes. Draw a line across your page and
write the twelve months under it — start from **this month last year**. Above the
line, write **four things that happened** to you, at the right month. Italian if
you can. **When you can't, write the English and draw a box round it.** Don't worry
about grammar — I want the events."

**CCQ.** "Four things that happened, or four things you do every week?" → *Four
things that happened.* · "What do you do with the English?" → *Box it.*

**Then, pairs, 2 minutes.** "Show your partner your boxes. Did you box the same
kinds of thing?" They will have: *moved, started a course, changed job, graduated,
interview*. That overlap is the evidence that set A was chosen, not invented.

**Correction stance.** None.

---

### 3 · Lexis set A — 11 life events — 13 min · lockstep → solo `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** Deliver set A as sound first, spelling second, own-life example third.
These eleven are the events a year is made of, and **five of them are reflexive** —
the unit's grammar is hiding in the vocabulary on purpose.

**The set**, in the rough order a life goes. Put it on the board in this order.

| Italian | English |
| --- | --- |
| `iscriversi (a)` | to enrol, to sign up |
| `il tirocinio` | internship, placement |
| `laurearsi` | to graduate |
| `la laurea` | degree |
| `il colloquio` | (job) interview |
| `assumere` | to hire, to take on |
| `licenziarsi` | to resign, to quit |
| `trasferirsi` | to move (house, town), to relocate |
| `il trasloco` | the move — the boxes and the van |
| `separarsi` | to separate, to split up |
| `la svolta` | turning point |

**Instruction, part 1 (4 min, lockstep).** "Listen first, don't write. I'll say each
one twice. Repeat after me." For the five reflexives, say the infinitive and then
the `io` present: `trasferirsi — mi trasferisco`. "These five have the little word
from Unit 1. You already know what to do with them in the present."

**Instruction, part 2 (3 min, lockstep).** "Two questions. One: **which of these did
you draw a box around** in your timeline? Two: which **two** of them are a pair —
one is what *you* do, the other is what *a company* does to you?" → `licenziarsi`
and `assumere`. Then say one sentence and no more: "`Mi licenzio` — I quit. If the
company does it, it's a different verb, and we meet it later." (`essere
licenziato`, *to be fired*, is in the lexis file's notes, not taught today.)

**Instruction, part 3 (6 min, solo).** "On your own. Choose **four** of the eleven
and write one sentence about **someone you know** with each — **in the present or
as a plan**, not in the past yet. Mine: *Mia sorella si laurea a giugno.* *Il mio
coinquilino ha un colloquio domani.* Four sentences, six minutes."

**CCQ.** "Past or present?" → *Present, or a plan.* · "About you or about someone
you know?" → *Someone I know.*

**Why present, today.** The past is the form L2 teaches. A learner who writes
`*mia sorella ha laureato` here has been asked to do L2's work without L2's lesson;
a learner who writes `si laurea a giugno` has learned the word and practised U1's
reflexive while doing it.

**Teacher's job during part 3.** Circulate. Correct **only** the reflexive pronoun
on the five reflexive verbs — U1's rule, and fair game. If a learner reaches for
the past, say "good — that's next lesson; write it as a plan for now".

**Correction stance.** Accuracy, immediate, narrow — the reflexive pronoun only.

### 4 · Listen 1 — gist — 5 min · solo → lockstep

**Purpose.** One global question, so that learners hear the whole recording once
without a task heavy enough to stop them hearing it. `A1` pass 1.

**Instruction.** "Do you remember Giulia from Unit 1 — Bologna, yoga on Tuesdays,
asleep in front of the TV on Sunday? **This is Giulia one year later**, in a voice
message to Mark. Books down, pens down. One question, in English: **is Giulia's
life now better, worse, or just different?** Listen."

Play cue 1 at normal pace, or read the script aloud keeping the `///` pauses.

**CCQ.** "How many things do I want?" → *One.* · "English or Italian?" →
*Either.*

**Expected answer.** **Different — and mostly better.** The evidence: a real
contract (`finalmente un contratto vero`, [5]), `mi piace la mia nuova vita` and
`il lavoro mi interessa davvero` [8]; against it, she misses Bologna and Sara [8].
Accept "better" or "different" with a reason. Ninety seconds, then move on.

**Correction stance.** None.

---

### 5 · Listen 2 — detail — 11 min · solo → pairs → lockstep

**Purpose.** The real listening work. `A1` pass 2. One closed exercise and one
open grid, so fast listeners are not idle.

**Instruction.** "Now the detail. Exercise 1 is six true-or-false. Exercise 2 is
Giulia's year, month by month. You'll hear it **twice**, without stopping. On your
own first, then compare with your partner, then we check."

**CCQ.** "When do you compare — before or after you've written your own answers?"
→ *After.*

Play twice, no pause between — about four and a half minutes of audio. Two minutes in pairs,
then lockstep. When an answer is wrong, give the paragraph number and replay those
seconds rather than giving the answer.

**Correction stance.** Accuracy.

---

### 6 · Listen 3 + noticing grid — 12 min · solo → pairs · `A2` `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** Learners **collect** the passato prossimo out of the text before
anyone names it. L2 opens by sorting what this group found.

**Instruction.** "Transcript out. Listen once more — slowly — and read along.
**Every time Giulia talks about something that happened, there are two words
together: a short one like `ho`, `è`, `sono`, and a longer one like `lasciato`,
`trasferita`.** Write each pair in the grid, and who it's about. I've done the
first two. Don't worry about why. Just collect. On your own, then compare with your
partner and add what you missed."

Read the script at **slow** pace (or play the slow take).

**CCQ.** "Am I asking you to explain the two words, or find them?" → *Find them.*
· "About ten, or about twenty-five?" → *About twenty-five.* (There are 26 to find,
plus the 2 given.)

**Grid** — exercise 3 below, with its key.

**One minute, at the end of the stage, on two phrases** — say it exactly, and only
if someone has noticed them; if nobody has, say it anyway:

> "Two phrases in the message are **like `mi piace`** from Unit 1. `Mi sembra` — *it
> seems to me*. `Mi è piaciuto` — *I liked it*, about the past. **Phrases, not
> grammar.** Same rule as Unit 1: learn them whole; Unit 8 takes them apart."

Write them up as phrases, with the English, and move on. `mi sembra` is planted
here for the first time in the course (Seed 1); `mi è piaciuto` is here because the
unit's production task cannot be done without it, and a learner left to build it
says `*ho piaciuto`.

**Discipline.** If a learner asks **why** some are `ho` and some are `sono`, say:
**"That is exactly the question next lesson answers. Write your name next to it."**
And write it on the board. Answering it here turns L1 into L2 and throws the
noticing away.

**Correction stance.** Accuracy, on the collection only. No rule is stated, asked
for, or given.

---

### 7 · Controlled practice — supported gap-fill — 10 min · solo → pairs `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** First accurate use. The box supplies **complete** past phrases and the
learner chooses by **meaning**. Nobody builds a form: that is L2.

**Instruction.** "Exercise 4. Ten sentences about Giulia's year and a box of twelve
phrases. **Each phrase is used once; two are not needed.** Six minutes alone, two
comparing, then we check."

**CCQ.** "How many phrases?" → *Twelve.* · "How many gaps?" → *Ten.* · "So?" →
*Two are left over.*

**Correction stance.** Accuracy, immediate.

---

### 8 · Year ladder — 14 min · lockstep → pairs · `A6` rungs 1–2 `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** Gradual release. Turn eleven items and a text into the learner's own
spoken year, with complete phrases supplied so nobody has to build a past form yet.

#### Rung 1 — *I do* (3 min, lockstep)

Say this model aloud, twice. Do not write it up.

> **Model.** "Quest'anno è stato un anno pieno. A febbraio ho cambiato lavoro: ho
> fatto due colloqui e alla fine ho trovato un posto in una scuola di lingue. A maggio
> mi sono iscritta a un corso di italiano. Ad agosto sono andata in Sicilia con mia
> sorella. E a ottobre mi sono trasferita in un appartamento più grande. La cosa più
> bella? Il viaggio in Sicilia."

Then: "Six sentences. **What does almost every sentence start with?**" → *A month.*
"That's the only analysis today. Every event has a when."

#### Rung 2 — *we do* (11 min, pairs)

**Instruction.** "Your year, **out loud**, with your timeline from stage 2 and the
frames on the board. Your partner listens, then you swap. Two and a half minutes
each. **Listener: at the end, ask one question** — `E com'è andato?` or `E poi?` —
one, not five."

**Frames** (on the board; learners do not copy them):

1. `Quest'anno è stato un anno ______ .`
2. `A ______ ho ______ .`
3. `A ______ sono ______ / mi sono ______ .`
4. `Poi, a ______ , ______ .`
5. `La cosa più bella? ______ .`

**Prepared lexis — complete phrases, choose by meaning** (on the board):

> `ho cambiato lavoro · ho fatto un colloquio · ho cominciato un corso · ho trovato
> casa · ho fatto un viaggio a … · sono andato / andata a … · sono tornato / tornata
> a … · mi sono trasferito / trasferita · mi sono iscritto / iscritta a … · mi sono
> laureato / laureata · non è successo niente di speciale`
>
> `pieno · tranquillo · strano · difficile · bellissimo`

Say once: "Where there are two, **`-o` if you're a man, `-a` if you're a woman.**
That's all for today." That is prepared lexis, not a rule about which verbs take
which helper.

**CCQ.** "Speaking or writing?" → *Speaking.* · "Listener — how many questions?" →
*One.*

**Correction stance.** Rung 2 is accuracy, narrowed: correct only a phrase taken
from the board **wrongly** — `*sono trasferito` for `mi sono trasferito`, or the
`-o/-a` ending. Collect two things for stage 10.

---

### 9 · Activate — *l'anno di qualcun altro* — 9 min · pairs → lockstep · `A6` rung 3 `[ITA?]`

**Purpose.** Remove the scaffold — the frames come off — and stretch the task one
notch, into the third person. This is the stage that **creates the need for L2**:
learners will reach for `ha` where Italian wants `è`, and `-o` for a woman. Those
attempts are what L2's presentation is built out of.

**Rub out the frames. Write this strip instead:**

> `ha cambiato · ha fatto · è andato / è andata · è tornato / è tornata ·
> si è trasferito / si è trasferita · si è iscritto / si è iscritta`

One sentence: "**For someone else it's `ha`, `è`, `si è` — pick from the strip.**"
Do not explain the choice. Do not draw a table.

**Instruction.** "New partner. Frames gone. **Tell your new partner about the year
of the person you just spoke to**: *Anna a febbraio ha cambiato lavoro…* Ninety
seconds each. The strip, not your notes."

**CCQ.** "Whose year?" → *The person I just talked to.* · "Notes?" → *No — the
strip only.*

**Then, lockstep, 3 minutes.** Three or four reports: "One thing from Anna's year?"
Recast without comment — "ah, si è trasferita a marzo" — and write down what you
heard for stage 10, especially `*ha trasferito`, `*è andato` for a woman, `*ha
andato`.

**Success criterion.** The learner says at least **four** things about another
person's year, each with a month, and is understood. **Accuracy of the helper word
and ending is explicitly not the criterion** — say so if asked: "Next lesson is
about exactly that."

**Correction stance.** Fluency. Delayed, collected, stage 10.

---

### 10 · Delayed feedback + close — 4 min · lockstep · `A13`

**Purpose.** Deal with the fluency-stage errors without having interrupted them,
and set the homework L2 opens with.

**Instruction.** "Six things I heard. **Two are good** — worth stealing. Thirty
seconds with your partner: which two?"

Six items actually heard in stages 8–9, anonymised and verbatim: four with one
error, two correct. Where the error is the helper word or the ending, **give the
right form and do not explain it** — "`è andata`, not `ha andato`. Why — next
lesson."

**Homework.** Say it, and write it up:

1. "Finish the grid from exercise 3 — there are **twenty-eight** altogether. The key
   is in your file."
2. "Exercise 5: eight sentences about your year, from the board phrases."
3. "**Record forty seconds** on your phone: *il mio anno*. Don't script it. **Keep
   it** — you'll hear it again in Lesson 5."

**Next lesson opens with** the noticing grid and the wrong third-person forms from
stage 9. Bring the grid.

## Exercises and answer keys `[ITA?]`

### Exercise 1 — *Un anno di svolte*: vero o falso?

1. A gennaio l'ufficio ha licenziato Giulia.
2. Giulia ha fatto il corso di grafica la mattina.
3. Durante il trasloco ha perso delle cose.
4. Sara e Chiara sono andate a Milano per il compleanno di Giulia.
5. Luca si è laureato in tre anni.
6. Il padre di Giulia è andato a vivere in campagna.

**Key.** 1 **F** — *mi sono licenziata*: she quit, nobody fired her [2]. · 2 **F**
— *un corso di grafica, la sera* [3]. · 3 **V** — *ho perso due scatole di
libri* [4]. · 4 **V** [4]. · 5 **F** — *dopo sette anni* [6]. · 6 **F** — her
**mother** did; her father stayed in Bologna [7]. **1 mark each, 6 marks.**

*Item 1 is worth a sentence after the check: the difference between quitting and
being fired is the `licenziarsi` / `essere licenziato` pair from stage 3, and Giulia
is careful to say which.*

Put one phrase from the box in each gap. Each phrase is used **once**; **two are
not needed**.

> `è rimasto` · `è arrivata` · `ho cominciato` · `è andata`

1. A gennaio ______ : ho lasciato il lavoro in ufficio.
2. A febbraio ______ a un corso di grafica.
3. A marzo ______ un colloquio per un tirocinio.
4. Ad aprile ______ a Milano.

6. A maggio Sara e Chiara ______ a trovarmi.
7. A settembre ______ la svolta: un contratto vero.

**Key.** 1 mi sono licenziata · 2 mi sono iscritta · 3 ho fatto · 4 mi sono

gives it away: `padre`, `-a` does not fit. That is L2's rule, found by a learner
before it is taught.*

## Teacher notes

Unit-level error profile, register decisions and pacing:
[`unit-02-teacher-notes.md`](unit-02-teacher-notes.md). The three below are the ones
**this lesson** will produce.

**Predictable errors**

1. **`avere` with a movement verb.** `*Ho andato a Roma`, `*ho partito`. English
   *I have gone* has *have* in it, so `ho` is the translation. Expect it in stages 8
   and 9 from most of the group. **Do:** recast — "sei andata a Roma, sì" — and put
   two on the stage 10 board. Do **not** explain the auxiliary choice. It is L2's
   first rule and L2's discovery stage depends on it not having been given away.
2. **The reflexive pronoun dropped in the past.** `*Sono trasferito a Milano`. The
   learner has `sono` from the board and lets it do all the work — U1's rule 1,
   resurfacing. **Do:** point at the board phrase — `mi sono trasferito` — and say
   it. This one **is** fair to correct today: it is Unit 1's rule, not Unit 2's.
3. **The `-o` ending for everybody.** `*Anna si è trasferito`, `*mia madre è
   andato`. Expect it at stage 9 above all, when the strip offers both and attention
   is on the content. **Do:** write two down verbatim for L2's opener. That is what
   stage 9 is for.

**The `piacere` seed.** `piacere` chunks are recognition-only until `U8 L2`; do not
explain the construction. In this lesson that means `mi sembra`, `mi è piaciuto`,
`mi manca`, `mi piace` and `mi interessa` in cue 1: the one-minute note at the end
of stage 6 is the whole treatment.

**Correction stance per stage.** Stages 3, 5, 6, 7 — **accuracy**, immediate (stage
3 narrowed to the reflexive pronoun). Stages 1, 2, 8, 9 — **fluency**, delayed,
collected for stage 10; stage 8 corrects only misuse of a board phrase. Stage 4 —
none.
