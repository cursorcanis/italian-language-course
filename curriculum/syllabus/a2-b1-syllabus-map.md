This is the grid the rest of the course is built against. It fixes, for all
twelve units: theme, can-do goals, grammar focus, lexis set, pronunciation
point, culture note, production task, and what each unit recycles from earlier
units. Downstream work — lesson plans, activities, texts, assessments — is built
to this grid, not alongside it.

---

## How to read this map

### The container (from `docs/format-brief.md`, ITA-2)

12 units × 5 lessons = 60 guided lessons of 90 minutes (~90 guided hours), plus
45–60 minutes of non-optional self-study per lesson. Adult learner, L1 English,
entering at **solid** A2, roughly two sessions a week, ~7–8 months end to end.
Self-study-first, teachable live. Markdown in this repo.

### The lesson-slot convention

Every unit uses the same five-lesson shape. This is a **recycling and encounter
convention**, not a lesson design — the Lesson Designer owns what happens inside
each slot. Its purpose is to make every grammar encounter locatable as `U7 L4`
rather than "somewhere in unit 7".

| Slot | What it carries |
| --- | --- |
| **L1** | Input and theme entry. Lexis set A. The unit's new structures appear here **receptively only** — heard and read before they are analysed. |
| **L2** | Grammar focus: the unit's one heavy structure, analysed and practised under control. |
| **L3** | Lexis set B, skills work, the pronunciation/intonation point, and the unit's **secondary** (light) grammar point. |
| **L4** | Recycling and culture: named earlier structures re-worked inside *this* unit's theme, plus the culture note. |
| **L5** | Production task (free production) and the unit checkpoint. |

Three consequences worth stating, because downstream work depends on them:

- **Input before output** is structural, not a suggestion: L1 always precedes L2.
- A structure's three encounters are always **introduced at `L2` or `L3`**,
  **recycled at some later unit's `L4`**, and **freely produced at a still later
  unit's `L5`**. See `grammar-progression.md` for the full table.
- **L4 is not slack time.** It is where the spiral actually happens, and it is
  the slot most likely to get quietly converted into "more practice of this
  unit's grammar". If L4 stops recycling, the course stops being spiralled.

### The level ladder

CEFR names A2 and B1; the sub-bands below are this course's internal labels for
the run-up, and every can-do statement is tagged with one.

| Label | Meaning in this course | Units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A2** | Secure entry level. Re-entry and consolidation of what the learner already half-owns. | 1–2 |
| **A2+** | A2 structures used at greater length and with more control; first B1 structures appear. | 3–4 |
| **B1 lower** | B1 tasks performed with support, on familiar ground, with some breakdown under pressure. | 5–8 |
| **B1** | B1 tasks performed unsupported, including in semi-formal registers and on unfamiliar-but-everyday topics. | 9–12 |

Can-do statements are written learner-facing ("I can …") so a learner can
self-assess against them at the end of a unit without a teacher present.

### Assessment points

Full specification belongs to **ITA-4 (Assessment framework)**; this map fixes

| Point | Placed | Covers |

## Design decisions

These are the judgement calls behind the grid. They are stated here so that a
disagreement can be about the reasoning rather than about the table.

### 1. Theme-led, grammar spiralled underneath — and the interlock test

The board fixed theme-led sequencing. The operational consequence is the
**interlock** test: *a unit's grammar and its lexis must make each other
necessary*. If the unit's grammar could be taught on any other theme, the unit
is not designed yet — it is a grammar lesson wearing a theme.

Every unit here passes that test on purpose:

- U3 (housing) **needs** comparatives, because choosing between two flats is a
  comparison, and **needs** relative clauses, because a flat is described by
  what it has and where it is.
- U6 (market) **needs** direct pronouns and `ne`, because quantities are the
  one place in everyday Italian where you cannot avoid them: asking for two of
  something without repeating the noun is the transaction.
- U4 (how things used to be) **needs** `si` impersonale, because "people used to
  live differently" has no agent — that is precisely the form's job.
- U9 (favours) **needs** pronomi combinati and trapassato, and they meet in one
  of the most frequent utterances in spoken Italian: *I'd told you so*.
- U11 (requests and complaints) **needs** the conditional, because politeness
  distance in Italian is carried by it, and **needs** trapassato, because a
  complaint is always about what had already been arranged.

### 2. One heavy grammar point per unit — and what counts as heavy

**Cognitive load.** A unit carrying two new heavy structures teaches neither.
Every unit has exactly one main structure plus one light secondary point.

"Heavy" and "light" here are load, not importance:

- **Heavy** = a new form *system* with a new use system (pronomi combinati,
  congiuntivo, the PP/imperfetto contrast).
- **Light** = either (a) a **consolidation** point the learner already half-owns
  from A2, which is re-entry rather than introduction, or (b) a new form with
  one shape and one job (`si` impersonale, trapassato, `ci` pronominale).

This is why Units 1, 2 and 4 look "full": their main point is consolidation, and
consolidation of half-owned material is cheaper than introduction. It is also
why trapassato prossimo is a *light* point (§4 below) rather than a unit focus.

### 3. Additions to the brief's grammar list

The brief's list is covered in full. These seven items were added; each earns its
slot by **communicative payoff** — what it lets the learner *say* — not by
textbook completeness.

| Added | Unit | Why it earns a slot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pronomi relativi `che` / `cui` | U3 | B1 description means *extended* description. Without relatives a learner produces strings of short sentences, which is the single most audible A2 signature in otherwise decent Italian. `che` is A2 re-entry; `cui` is the new part, capped at `in cui / di cui / con cui / a cui`. |
| Imperativo (`tu / Lei / noi / voi`) + clitic attachment | U7 | Highest immediate payoff of anything not on the original list: directions, instructions, recipes, and every service encounter. It is also the natural home for **clitic attachment**, which pronomi combinati (U9) depends on. Teaching combinati without it would mean teaching attachment twice. |
| Periodo ipotetico della realtà (`se` + presente/futuro) | U10 | The B1 threshold structure for consequences and plans, and nearly free once futuro is in place — same clause, one connective. |
| Futuro epistemico (`sarà`, `avrà` for supposition) | U10 | Light touch, recognition-level. Very frequent in speech, and an anglophone learner who has only met futuro-as-future systematically *misparses* it as a prediction about the future rather than a guess about the present. |
| `ci` pronominale (`ci penso`, `non ci credo`, `non ci riesco`) | U11 | A small closed set of extremely high-frequency chunks that anglophone learners otherwise avoid entirely, because nothing in English cues them. |
| Connettivi / discourse markers as a taught thread | U5 (narrative), U12 (argumentative) | B1 is where **coherence**, not just accuracy, starts being assessed. Connectives are treated as a taught progression, not as incidental lexis. |
| `stare + gerundio` / `stare per` | U5 | Supports simultaneity and imminence in narrative, which is exactly where the PP/imperfetto contrast gets tested. |

### 4. Reorderings, and why

- **`si` impersonale moves early (U4), not late.** Formally it is one shape
  (`si` + 3rd person). The cost at B1 is *use*, not form. The "how things used
  to be" theme makes it necessary, and starting it at U4 gives it the widest
  spacing of any B1 structure in the course (U4 → U7 → U12). Its known risk —
  confusion with riflessivi, which share the surface form — is handled by
  distance: riflessivi are introduced at U1, three units earlier, and U4 L3
  addresses the clash head-on rather than hoping the learner doesn't notice it.
- **Pronomi combinati sit at U9, before futuro and condizionale.** Combinati
  depend on direct pronouns (U6) and indirect pronouns (U8) and on nothing else
  — not on any tense. Placing them straight after their prerequisites keeps
  those prerequisites fresh, and, critically, leaves three units in which to
  spiral them. Pushing them to U11–12 to "follow textbook order" would leave no
  room to recycle them at all.
- **Trapassato prossimo is a light point (U9 L3), not a unit focus.** Its form is
  free once imperfetto (U4) and participles (U2) are secure: imperfetto of the
  auxiliary plus a participle the learner already has. Its cost is knowing *when*
  to reach for it, and U9 and U11 give it two immediately usable homes —
  *I'd told you* and *I had booked a double room*.
- **Comparatives are folded into U3 as consolidation, not given a focus slot.**
  They are A2 re-entry, and the housing theme makes them necessary. Spending a
  main slot on them would cost a B1 structure a unit.
- **Futuro (U10) precedes condizionale (U11).** They share their irregular stems
  (`andr-`, `verr-`, `sapr-`, `sar-`). Teaching futuro first makes the
  conditional a change of ending on stems already learned, rather than a second
  set of irregular stems.

### 5. Deliberate compression at the end of the course, stated plainly

This is the one place where the twelve-unit container and the spiral principle
genuinely conflict, and it should be visible rather than buried.

**Distributed practice** says a structure's three encounters should widen in
spacing. Structures introduced at U11 and U12 cannot have that: there are not
enough units left. Condizionale (U11), congiuntivo (U12), futuro (U10) and
pronomi combinati (U9) all get three encounters in three different themes — the
spiral is intact — but the intervals are compressed rather than widening.

Three mitigations are built in, and one recommendation is escalated:

1. **Receptive seeding.** Congiuntivo chunks (`penso che sia`, `spero che vada`,
   `può darsi che`) appear in input texts from U5 onward as listed
   recognition-only items, never as production targets. The same trick carries
   the `piacere` family from U1 to its analysis at U8, where it demonstrably
   works. See `grammar-progression.md` §"Receptive seeding".
2. **Washback.** Milestone 2 weights the late structures, because learners study
   what gets tested. Designing the milestone to reward opinion-giving and polite
   request-making is how the course keeps teaching them.
3. **Unit 12 L4 is explicitly a whole-course recycling lesson**, not a congiuntivo
   drill.
4. **Escalated to Chief Italian Linguist:** if full widening spacing on
   congiuntivo and condizionale is wanted, the honest fix is **14 units, not a
   denser Unit 12**. Compressing more into the last unit would violate cognitive
   load to protect a spacing principle — trading one lens for another at a net
   loss. Flagged as a decision for the board, not made unilaterally here.

At B1, congiuntivo is a **first look** — recognition plus limited production
after a closed set of opinion verbs. The map is designed to deliver that, and
not to pretend it delivers mastery.

### 6. What this map deliberately does not decide

- **Lesson-internal staging, activity types, timings, answer keys, teacher and
  solo notes.** Lesson Designer owns these. The L1–L5 convention fixes *where a
  grammar encounter lands*, not how a lesson runs.
- **All Italian.** Every Italian item here is a specification of what a text must
  do; the Italian Content Editor writes or signs off the actual language.
- **Assessment internals.** Task types, durations, mark schemes and pass criteria
  are ITA-4. This map fixes only placement and coverage.
- **Recorded audio, images, packaging.** Out of scope for v1 per the brief.

### Unit 1 — Ricominciamo: io, la mia settimana, la mia gente

**Me, my week, my people** · Level **A2** · Lessons 1.1–1.5 · Checkpoint at L5

**Can-do goals**

- **(A2)** I can introduce myself and describe my daily and weekly routine in
  enough detail that someone gets a real picture of my life, not just a list of
  times.
- **(A2)** I can describe the people closest to me — what they're like and how I
  get on with them — using several different character adjectives.
- **(A2)** I can say what I like, need and miss using fixed expressions, even
  though I can't yet explain how they work.
- **(A2+)** I can ask someone the same questions back and keep a short
  conversation about routines going without stalling.

**Grammar focus**

- *Main:* **Riflessivi** (consolidation) — full paradigm, the reflexive as a
  routine-telling device, reciprocal use (`ci vediamo`, `ci sentiamo`).
  Alongside: **presente indicativo**, regular plus the high-frequency irregulars
  (`essere, avere, fare, andare, venire, uscire, dovere, potere, volere, sapere`).
- *Secondary:* **`piacere` family as unanalysed chunks** — `mi piace / mi
  piacciono / mi interessa / mi serve / mi manca`, taught as fixed expressions
  and explicitly **not** explained. This is deliberate seeding for U8, where the
  indirect pronoun is analysed. Learners meet the pattern for seven units before
  they are asked to build it.

**Lexis set** — Set 1: *Routine, carattere, rapporti* (30 items), in three
subgroups — reflexive routine verbs (11) · people and relationships (8) ·
character adjectives and how you get on (11). Full list:
[`lexis-progression.md` §Unit 1](lexis-progression.md#unit-1--routine-carattere-rapporti).
Plus a separate 5-chunk receptive set for the `piacere` family.

**Pronunciation / intonation** — **Word stress.** The penultimate-syllable
default, and the two departures that matter most at this level: `-ano` third
person plurals (`àbitano`, `telèfonano`, `svègliano`) where anglophone learners
reliably stress the wrong syllable, and truncated infinitives/futures.
Stress-shift minimal pairs are practised as meaning-bearing, not as decoration.

**Culture note** — **`tu` and `Lei`, and how Italians actually negotiate the
switch.** Who offers `diamoci del tu`, when it would be odd to, what titles
(`dottore`, `ingegnere`, `professore`) are doing, and why an anglophone
defaulting to `tu` reads differently from an anglophone defaulting to first
names in English. Establishes the register axis the whole course runs on and
sets up U7 (`Lei` imperatives) and U11 (formal requests).

**Production task (L5)** — **Spoken:** a two-minute audio self-portrait — who I
am, what a normal week looks like, who's around me. **Written:** a language
exchange profile (~120 words) covering the same ground for a reader.
*Success criterion:* the spoken piece runs two minutes without a stall longer
than five seconds, uses at least six reflexive verbs correctly in the present,
and describes at least two people with a character adjective each.

**Recycles** — Nothing: this is the entry unit. It re-enters the A2 baseline the
placement check confirms, and is the only unit in the course with no spiral
obligations. Its L4 slot recycles *within* A2 (present-tense question forms and
agreement) rather than from earlier units.

### Unit 2 — L'ultimo anno: raccontare il passato recente

**My last year** · Level **A2** · Lessons 2.1–2.5 · Checkpoint at L5

**Can-do goals**

- **(A2)** I can say what I've done over the past year — work, study, travel,
  moving — and place each event in time.
- **(A2+)** I can tell someone about a significant change in my life in five to
  eight connected sentences, rather than as separate facts.
- **(A2)** I can say whether something has already happened, hasn't happened
  yet, or has just happened.
- **(A2+)** I can ask someone about their recent past and follow up on what
  they tell me instead of moving to the next question.

**Grammar focus**

- *Main:* **Passato prossimo** (consolidation) — the three things anglophone
  learners actually get wrong, in this order: **auxiliary choice**
  (`essere` vs `avere`, including the verbs that take both), **agreement of the
  participle with `essere`**, and the **irregular participles** of the
  highest-frequency verbs, taught as a learnable list rather than as exceptions.
- *Secondary:* **Past-time expressions** — `fa`, `scorso`, `l'altro giorno`,
  `da allora`, `finora` — and the position and meaning of `già`, `appena`,
  `non ancora`.

**Lexis set** — Set 2: *Eventi e svolte* (30 items), in three subgroups —
time anchors (10) · life-event verbs and nouns (12) · outcome and evaluation
(8). Full list:
[`lexis-progression.md` §Unit 2](lexis-progression.md#unit-2--eventi-e-svolte).

**Pronunciation / intonation** — **Double consonants.** `fatto`, `detto`,
`successo`, `letto`, `rotto` — the past participle set makes this unavoidable
rather than abstract, which is why it sits here. Minimal pairs
(`ano/anno`, `sono/sonno`, `casa/cassa`) are treated as meaning-bearing, and the
length contrast is practised in production, not only in listening.

**Culture note** — **The shape of the Italian year.** August and the
`ferragosto` shutdown, `la settimana bianca`, the school and holiday calendar,
and why "what did you do this year" gets answered differently in Italy than in
the UK or US. Gives the unit's time anchors a real calendar to attach to.

**Production task (L5)** — **Written:** a 150-word "my year" post for a blog or
social feed. **Spoken:** tell three things that happened this year, then answer
two follow-up questions about them (solo: recorded, answering scripted prompts).
*Success criterion:* at least eight passato prossimo verbs, including at least
two with `essere` and correct agreement; every event placed in time with an
explicit expression; the written piece reads as a connected account with at
least three linking expressions, not as a list.

**Recycles** — **From U1:** riflessivi, now in the past (`mi sono trasferito`,
`mi sono iscritto`) — which is also the cleanest possible demonstration of
`essere` + agreement, so the recycled structure carries the new one.
**From U1:** the present tense, contrasted with the past to place *now* against
*then*.

### Unit 3 — Dove vivo: casa, palazzo, quartiere

**Where I live** · Level **A2+** · Lessons 3.1–3.5 · Checkpoint at L5

**Can-do goals**

- **(A2+)** I can describe where I live — the flat, the building, the
  neighbourhood — and say what I like and don't like about it.
- **(A2+)** I can compare two places and say which one suits me better and why.
- **(B1 lower)** I can join my descriptions into longer sentences instead of
  short ones, using `che` and `in cui / di cui / con cui / a cui`.
- **(A2+)** I can understand a rental advert and ask a landlord or agent the
  practical questions I'd actually need to ask.

**Grammar focus**

- *Main:* **Pronomi relativi `che` and `cui`** (new). `che` as subject and
  object is A2 re-entry; `cui` after a preposition is the new part, capped
  deliberately at `in cui`, `di cui`, `con cui`, `a cui`. `il quale` is shown
  once for recognition in formal written adverts and is not a production target.
- *Secondary:* **Comparativi e superlativi** (consolidation) —
  `più/meno … di` vs `più/meno … che`, `(così) … come`, `il più … di`, and the
  absolute superlative in `-issimo`. **`ci` locativo** (new, light) —
  `ci abito da tre anni`, `ci vado a piedi`. Also: prepositions with places and
  `preposizioni articolate`, as maintenance.

**Lexis set** — Set 3: *Casa, palazzo, quartiere* (30 items), in three
subgroups — the building and its parts (10) · renting and running costs (8) ·
describing and comparing places (12). Full list:
[`lexis-progression.md` §Unit 3](lexis-progression.md#unit-3--casa-palazzo-quartiere).

**Pronunciation / intonation** — **Stressed open and closed `e` and `o`**, plus
**list and comparison intonation**: the level-then-falling contour that marks
"this one, that one, and this is the one I'd choose". Comparison is the unit's
grammar, so its prosody belongs here rather than being left to chance.

**Culture note** — **Renting and the `condominio`.** `spese condominiali`, the
`assemblea condominiale`, the `amministratore`, what a `caparra` is and when you
get it back, and how north/south and city/small-town housing differ. Written for
someone approaching the Italian rental market from outside it, where the
expensive surprises are administrative rather than linguistic.

**Production task (L5)** — **Written:** a 150-word rental advert for a place the
learner knows, written to be read by a prospective tenant. **Spoken:** compare
two neighbourhoods and argue for one (2 minutes).
*Success criterion:* at least four relative clauses, of which at least two use
`cui` after a preposition; at least three comparative structures including one
`più … che`; the spoken argument reaches a stated conclusion rather than
trailing off into description.

**Recycles** — **From U2:** passato prossimo, used to tell how the learner found
and moved into the place (`ho trovato`, `mi sono trasferito`). **From U1:**
riflessivi (`trasferirsi`, `trovarsi`), and character adjectives extended to
describing places and neighbours.

### Unit 4 — Com'era una volta: infanzia e cambiamenti

**How things used to be** · Level **A2+** · Lessons 4.1–4.5 · Checkpoint at L5

**Can-do goals**

- **(A2+)** I can describe what my life was like as a child — where I lived,
  what I did, what the people and places around me were like.
- **(A2+)** I can say how something used to be done in general, without naming
  who did it.
- **(B1 lower)** I can talk for two or three minutes about how a place or a
  habit has changed, setting *then* against *now*.
- **(A2+)** I can follow someone else's account of their childhood and ask for
  more detail about it.

**Grammar focus**

- *Main:* **Imperfetto** (consolidation, taken deep) — forms including the four
  irregulars (`essere, fare, dire, bere`), and the three uses separated
  explicitly: **habitual** past, **descriptive** past (people, places, weather,
  age, states), and **background** to an event. The third use is deliberately
  introduced here without the foreground event, so that U5 has something to
  contrast against rather than two things to introduce at once.
- *Secondary:* **`si` impersonale** (new) — `si viveva`, `si andava`, `si
  mangiava`. Introduced here because "how people used to live" has no agent,
  which is exactly this form's job. **The riflessivo clash is addressed head-on
  in L3**: `si alzava` (he/she got up) vs `si andava` (people went) — not left
  for the learner to trip over later.

**Lexis set** — Set 4: *Infanzia, abitudine, cambiamento* (30 items), in three
subgroups — childhood and its places (12) · habit and frequency (8) · change,
loss and nostalgia (10). Full list:
[`lexis-progression.md` §Unit 4](lexis-progression.md#unit-4--infanzia-abitudine-cambiamento).

**Pronunciation / intonation** — **`gli` /ʎ/ and `gn` /ɲ/** — `famiglia`,
`figli`, `compagno`, `bagno`, `sogno`. Both are absent from English and both are
carried by high-frequency childhood and family vocabulary, so the unit's lexis
forces the practice. Plus the flat, unhurried contour of reminiscence, which is
audibly different from narrative.

**Culture note** — **`Una volta si stava meglio`: the weight of "once upon a
time".** The `boom economico`, internal migration from south to north, the
nonni-as-childcare arrangement that still structures many Italian families, and
why `ai miei tempi` is both a genuine social observation and a running joke.
Explains why the unit's grammar is something Italians reach for constantly.

**Production task (L5)** — **Spoken:** a three-minute monologue, "my life when I
was ten" — place, routine, people, what the world was like. **Written:** a short
nostalgia piece (~150 words) on one thing that has disappeared.
*Success criterion:* three minutes sustained; imperfetto used for all three of
its taught uses at least once each; at least two `si` impersonale generalisations;
no passato prossimo used where imperfetto is required (the single diagnostic
error this checkpoint looks for).

**Recycles** — **From U3:** comparatives, now across time rather than across
places (`si viveva meglio di adesso`) — the same structure with a new job.
**From U3:** relative clauses, used to describe remembered places.
**From U1:** the present tense, set directly against the imperfetto as *then vs
now*, and riflessivi, now habitual in the past (`mi svegliavo`).

### Unit 5 — Storie e imprevisti: raccontare un episodio

**Telling what happened** · Level **B1 lower** · Lessons 5.1–5.5 · Checkpoint at L5

**Can-do goals**

- **(B1 lower)** I can tell a story about something that happened to me: set the
  scene, say what happened, and say how it ended.
- **(B1 lower)** I can say what was going on when something interrupted it.
- **(B1 lower)** I can hold a listener's attention across a two-to-three-minute
  anecdote by linking events, instead of starting each sentence from scratch.
- **(A2+)** I can react to someone else's story in a way that shows I'm
  following it and keeps them talking.

**Grammar focus**

- *Main:* **Passato prossimo vs imperfetto** (new contrast — the heaviest single
  point in the first half of the course). Background vs foreground, state vs
  event, the verbs whose meaning shifts between the two (`sapere`, `conoscere`,
  `dovere`, `volere`, `potere`), and the `mentre` / `quando` split.
- *Secondary:* **`stare + gerundio` in the past** (`stavo uscendo quando…`) and
  **`stare per`** (`stavo per dirtelo`). **Connettivi narrativi** as a taught
  set: `poi`, `allora`, `così`, `a un certo punto`, `all'improvviso`,
  `alla fine`, `per fortuna`, `purtroppo`.

**Lexis set** — Set 5: *Imprevisti e reazioni* (30 items), in three subgroups —
narrative signposts and connectives (10) · things that go wrong (12) · reactions
and outcomes (8). Full list:
[`lexis-progression.md` §Unit 5](lexis-progression.md#unit-5--imprevisti-e-reazioni).

**Pronunciation / intonation** — **Narrative prosody**: chunking and pausing,
the suspended rise before a turn in the story, the fall that signals the point
has landed. This is the unit where intonation stops being decoration and starts
carrying the task: an anecdote delivered on a flat contour fails as an anecdote
even when every verb form is right.

**Culture note** — **`Fare una figuraccia`.** `bella figura` / `brutta figura` as
a social frame, why the self-deprecating mishap story is a standard Italian
conversational move, and how an audience is expected to react. Directly serves
the production task: the learner is being taught a genre, not just a tense.

**Production task (L5)** — **Spoken:** tell a real anecdote about something that
went wrong (2–3 minutes), shaped for an audience. **Written:** the same story
retold in 180 words.
*Success criterion:* both tenses used contrastively, with at least two clear
background-plus-event pairs; at least five different narrative connectives; the
spoken version has an audible beginning, turn and end rather than stopping when
the learner runs out of material.

**Recycles** — **From U2:** passato prossimo, now in its contrastive role rather
than as a standalone past. **From U4:** imperfetto, same. **From U1:** riflessivi
inside narrative (`mi sono accorto`, `mi sono spaventato`). **From U2:** past-time
expressions, used to anchor the anecdote before the narrative connectives take
over.

### Unit 6 — A tavola: mercato, spesa, cucina

**At the table** · Level **B1 lower** · Lessons 6.1–6.5 · Checkpoint at L5,
then **Milestone 1**

**Can-do goals**

- **(B1 lower)** I can shop at a market or a counter: ask for quantities, ask
  what's good today, and deal with the price and the change.
- **(B1 lower)** I can avoid repeating a noun I've already mentioned — "I'll
  take it", "I'll take two of them" — instead of saying the noun every time.
- **(B1 lower)** I can explain how to make a dish I know, step by step, clearly
  enough for someone else to follow it.
- **(A2+)** I can say what I think of a food and compare it with something else.

**Grammar focus**

- *Main:* **Pronomi diretti** (`lo, la, li, le`, plus `mi, ti, ci, vi`) and
  **`ne` partitivo** (new), taught together because the market transaction needs
  both and because the choice between them *is* the learning point
  (`li prendo` vs `ne prendo due`). **Agreement of the past participle with a
  preceding direct object** (`l'ho comprata`, `ne ho presi due`) is taught here,
  where the recycled passato prossimo makes it concrete.
- *Secondary:* **Quantifiers and partitives** — `un po' di`, `qualche`,
  `alcuni/e`, `troppo`, `abbastanza`, `la maggior parte di`, and the
  partitive `del / della / dei`.

**Lexis set** — Set 6: *Mercato, quantità, cucina* (30 items), in three
subgroups — market and transaction (11) · quantities and containers (7) ·
cooking process and qualities (12). Full list:
[`lexis-progression.md` §Unit 6](lexis-progression.md#unit-6--mercato-quantità-cucina).

**Pronunciation / intonation** — **Hard and soft `c` and `g`**: `chilo`,
`cuocere`, `aggiungere`, `ghiaccio`, `cucina`, `ciliegia`. The food lexis is
saturated with the contrast, which is why it sits here. Plus the brisk,
falling-terminal intonation of a market request, which sounds abrupt to an
anglophone ear and is not.

**Culture note** — **Market, counter and supermarket.** How a `banco` transaction
actually runs, `campanilismo` and regional food identity, coffee rules (what,
when, standing or sitting), and Italian mealtime timing against northern
European expectations. Explains the register of the production task: brisk is
polite here.

**Production task (L5)** — **Spoken:** a market role-play (buy four items in
different quantities, ask about two, handle the total) *and* explain a dish the
learner knows well, step by step. Solo path: recorded, against a scripted
vendor audio. **Written:** the recipe written out for someone else to cook from.
*Success criterion:* at least six direct-pronoun or `ne` substitutions with no
noun repeated unnecessarily; at least two correct participle agreements; the
recipe is followable — a reader could execute it without asking a question.

**Recycles** — **From U2:** passato prossimo, now carrying participle agreement
with a preceding pronoun — the recycled structure is what makes the new one
teachable. **From U3:** comparatives, used to choose between products.
**From U3:** relative clauses (`il formaggio che mi hai consigliato`).
**From U1:** the present tense in its fullest transactional use.

> **Milestone 1** sits after this unit. Coverage: Units 1–6 — past narrative,
> description and comparison, direct pronouns and `ne`, transactional speaking.
> Specification in ITA-4.

### Unit 7 — Muoversi e sbrigare: trasporti, strada, uffici

**Getting around and getting things done** · Level **B1 lower** ·
Lessons 7.1–7.5 · Checkpoint at L5

**Can-do goals**

- **(B1 lower)** I can give someone clear directions and step-by-step
  instructions, both to a friend and to a stranger.
- **(B1 lower)** I can handle a counter transaction at a station, post office or
  public office: say what I need, ask what I have to do, and understand the
  answer.
- **(B1 lower)** I can understand and explain public rules and notices — what's
  allowed, what's forbidden, what you have to do first.
- **(B1)** I can ask for something to be repeated or put more simply without
  losing the thread of the conversation.

**Grammar focus**

- *Main:* **Imperativo** (new) — `tu`, `Lei`, `noi`, `voi`, affirmative and
  negative, including the irregular `tu` forms (`va'`, `da'`, `fa'`, `di'`,
  `sta'`) and the `Lei` forms that are formally subjunctive (`mi dica`,
  `si accomodi`, `scusi`, `senta`) and are taught here as lexical imperatives,
  not as a first congiuntivo. Plus **clitic attachment**: `prendilo`,
  `non lo prendere` / `non prenderlo`, `mi dica`, `si accomodi`.
- *Secondary:* **`si` passivante** as an extension of U4's `si` impersonale —
  `si affittano appartamenti`, `qui si parla inglese`, `non si accettano carte` —
  with the agreement rule that distinguishes it. Plus **prepositions of place and
  movement** and the direction lexis.

**Lexis set** — Set 7: *Trasporti, strada, sportello* (30 items), in three
subgroups — transport and tickets (9) · directions and street geometry (10) ·
offices, forms and rules (11). Full list:
[`lexis-progression.md` §Unit 7](lexis-progression.md#unit-7--trasporti-strada-sportello).

**Pronunciation / intonation** — **Command vs request intonation**, and
**stress under clitic attachment**: the stress stays on the verb stem when a
pronoun is attached (`prèndilo`, not `prendìlo`), which anglophone learners get
wrong by default. This matters beyond this unit: the same rule governs pronomi
combinati in U9.

**Culture note** — **Bureaucracy as a system, not an obstacle.** `codice
fiscale`, `residenza`, `marca da bollo`, `sportello` culture, appointment
booking, and why the queue works the way it does. The most practically valuable
culture note in the course for the learner profile in the brief — someone
living in or moving to Italy meets this before they meet anything else.

**Production task (L5)** — **Spoken:** give a newcomer directions to a place and
then talk them through a bureaucratic errand, start to finish. **Written:** the
same errand as a numbered set of instructions, plus three public notices
rewritten in plain Italian.
*Success criterion:* imperatives used correctly in **both** `tu` and `Lei` across
the task, with at least three attached pronouns; the instructions are complete
enough that a reader could follow them without asking a question; at least two
`si` passivante notices produced correctly.

**Recycles** — **From U6:** direct pronouns, now attached to imperatives — a new
operation on a structure that is one unit old, which is deliberate, because
attachment is easiest to learn while the pronouns themselves are still fresh.
**From U4:** `si` impersonale, extended to `si` passivante in a completely
different theme. **From U3:** `ci` locativo (`ci si arriva a piedi`,
`ci vada in metro`). **From U3:** prepositions of place, now with movement.

### Unit 8 — Corpo e salute: come sto

**Body and health** · Level **B1 lower → B1** · Lessons 8.1–8.5 · Checkpoint at L5

**Can-do goals**

- **(B1 lower)** I can describe how I feel physically, say what hurts, and say
  how long it's been going on.
- **(B1 lower)** I can tell a doctor or a pharmacist what happened and
  understand the advice I'm given.
- **(B1)** I can say what I like, need, miss and what bothers me — and explain
  it — instead of relying on fixed phrases.
- **(B1 lower)** I can tell someone else's health story: what was wrong, what
  they did about it, and how it turned out.

**Grammar focus**

- *Main:* **Pronomi indiretti** (new) — `mi, ti, gli, le, ci, vi, gli/loro`, the
  stressed forms (`a me, a te`) for contrast and emphasis, and **the `piacere`
  family analysed at last**: `piacere, mancare, servire, bastare, sembrare,
  dispiacere, far male, dare fastidio`. The seven-unit seeding from U1 is
  cashed in here: learners have been *using* `mi serve` since Unit 1 and now find
  out what it is. The direct/indirect contrast (`lo chiamo` vs `gli telefono`) is
  taught as a verb-by-verb property, which is where anglophone learners need it.
- *Secondary:* **Adverbs in `-mente`** and their formation, plus the impersonal
  `fa male` / `mi fa male` pattern and duration with `da`.

**Lexis set** — Set 8: *Sintomi, cure, benessere* (30 items), in three
subgroups — symptoms and the body (10) · treatment and the health system (10) ·
wellbeing and the `piacere`-family verbs (10). Full list:
[`lexis-progression.md` §Unit 8](lexis-progression.md#unit-8--sintomi-cure-benessere).

**Pronunciation / intonation** — **Contrastive stress on the stressed pronoun**:
`a ME non piace` vs `non mi piace`. This is the unit's grammar made audible —
the stressed form exists *for* contrast, so it is taught with the prosody that
makes the contrast real. Plus the intonation of describing discomfort and of
responding with sympathy, which is a register an anglophone learner tends to
under-mark in Italian.

**Culture note** — **The `SSN`, the `medico di base` and the pharmacy as first
stop.** How registering works, what the `tessera sanitaria` does, why the
pharmacist is a genuine first port of call, and the folk-health frame
(`colpo d'aria`, `cervicale`, `la digestione`) that an anglophone learner will
hear constantly and otherwise misread as a joke.

**Production task (L5)** — **Spoken:** a full consultation role-play — describe
symptoms, answer the practitioner's questions, understand and read back the
advice. Solo path: recorded against a scripted practitioner audio. **Written:**
a message to a friend reporting what was wrong and what the doctor said.
*Success criterion:* at least six indirect pronouns used correctly, including
two `piacere`-family verbs with a plural subject (`mi fanno male`,
`mi servono`); the advice is read back accurately enough to show it was
understood, not guessed.

**Recycles** — **From U5:** the passato prossimo / imperfetto contrast, in its
natural clinical shape — *I had a temperature, so I went to the doctor*.
**From U6:** direct pronouns, now contrasted with indirect ones rather than
practised alone. **From U1:** the `piacere` chunks, now analysed — the seeding
pays off. **From U5:** narrative connectives, carrying the symptom history.
**From U5:** `stare + gerundio`, in its most natural home — describing what was
going on when the symptom started (`stavo tornando quando…`).
**From U7:** `Lei` imperatives, heard from the practitioner (`si accomodi`,
`respiri`, `mi dica`) — recognition, then use in the role-play.

### Unit 9 — Favori, regali, rapporti

**Favours, gifts and what we owe each other** · Level **B1** ·
Lessons 9.1–9.5 · Checkpoint at L5

**Can-do goals**

- **(B1)** I can ask someone for a favour, accept or turn one down, and thank or
  apologise in a way that sounds normal rather than translated.
- **(B1)** I can refer back to a person and a thing at the same time — "I told
  him about it", "lend it to me" — without repeating either.
- **(B1)** I can say that something had already happened before something else
  did.
- **(B1 lower)** I can smooth over a small misunderstanding with a friend in a
  message.

**Grammar focus**

- *Main:* **Pronomi combinati** (new) — `me lo, te lo, ce lo, ve lo, glielo`,
  and `me ne / gliene`. Placed here, immediately after both prerequisites (direct
  U6, indirect U8), so those are still fresh and so the structure has three
  remaining units to spiral through. Includes the vowel change `mi → me` and the
  fused `glie-` forms, and attachment to imperatives and infinitives, which U7
  already established.
- *Secondary:* **Trapassato prossimo** (new, light) — imperfetto of the auxiliary
  plus a participle the learner already owns. Introduced here because the theme
  produces its two highest-frequency uses naturally:
  *I'd already told you* and *I'd asked him before you did*.
  Also: light-touch **reported speech in the past** (`mi ha detto che…`),
  recognition-level only, as the frame trapassato lives inside.

**Lexis set** — Set 9: *Favori, regali, rapporti* (30 items), in three
subgroups — asking, offering, lending (11) · gifts, invitations and thanks (10) ·
misunderstanding and repair (9). Full list:
[`lexis-progression.md` §Unit 9](lexis-progression.md#unit-9--favori-regali-rapporti).

**Pronunciation / intonation** — **Clitic clusters as single phonological
words**: `me lo`, `te la`, `glielo` /ʎe/, and elision in `te l'avevo detto`.
Learners who produce combinati as two separate stressed words are
comprehensible but audibly foreign; this is the unit where that gets fixed.
Plus the intonation of a request that leaves the other person room to refuse.

**Culture note** — **Reciprocity: favours, gifts, invitations.** What to bring
and what not to, `l'onomastico` alongside the birthday, who pays and how the
question is settled, `figurati` / `ci mancherebbe` as the expected answer to
thanks, and how `fare bella figura` shapes gift-giving. The theme is a social
system, and the grammar is what the system runs on.

**Production task (L5)** — **Spoken:** a two-way favour exchange — ask for one,
be asked for one, grant one and decline one gracefully. **Written:** a matched
pair of messages — one thanking someone properly, one apologising for something
small and putting it right.
*Success criterion:* at least five combined pronouns used correctly, including
at least two `glie-` forms; at least two uses of trapassato where the sequence
genuinely requires it; the refusal is polite enough to be usable — a flat `no`
fails the task even if the grammar is correct.

**Recycles** — **From U6 and U8:** direct and indirect pronouns, now combined —
this unit *is* the interlock of the two. **From U7:** clitic attachment to
imperatives, now with clusters (`dimmelo`, `me lo dica`). **From U2 and U4:**
passato prossimo and imperfetto, which together build the trapassato. **From
U4:** imperfetto, as the background to a favour story. **From U5:** narrative
connectives, holding the misunderstanding-and-repair account together.

### Unit 10 — Lavoro e progetti: quello che verrà

**Work and what's next** · Level **B1** · Lessons 10.1–10.5 · Checkpoint at L5

**Can-do goals**

- **(B1)** I can talk about my work or study, describe what a job involves, and
  say what kind of work I'm looking for.
- **(B1)** I can talk about my plans and make predictions about the next few
  years.
- **(B1)** I can say what will happen if something else happens.
- **(B1)** I can tell the story of my working life so far, in order and with
  reasons for the moves I made.

**Grammar focus**

- *Main:* **Futuro semplice** (new) — regular forms plus the irregular stems
  (`andr-, avr-, sar-, dovr-, potr-, sapr-, vedr-, verr-, vorr-, farà, darà,
  starà`), taught as a stem list because those same stems are reused unchanged
  for the conditional in U11. Uses: plans, predictions, promises, and the
  present-tense alternative (`domani parto`) that learners must be able to
  choose against.
- *Secondary:* **Periodo ipotetico della realtà** — `se` + presente/futuro, and
  the permitted combinations. **Futuro epistemico** (light, recognition-first) —
  `sarà vero?`, `avrà trent'anni`, `saranno le otto`: the future form used to
  guess about the *present*, which anglophone learners systematically misparse.

**Lexis set** — Set 10: *Lavoro, contratti, obiettivi* (30 items), in three
subgroups — jobs, contracts and conditions (12) · training and career movement
(8) · goals, timelines and likelihood (10). Full list:
[`lexis-progression.md` §Unit 10](lexis-progression.md#unit-10--lavoro-contratti-obiettivi).

**Pronunciation / intonation** — **Future stems and the `r` cluster**: `andrò`,
`verrò`, `saprò`, `potrò` — the tapped/trilled `r` in a consonant cluster, which
is where anglophone learners' `r` most often collapses. Plus final-vowel stress
on the first and third person singular (`andrò`, `andrà`), which is the only
thing distinguishing them in speech.

**Culture note** — **The Italian labour market from outside it.** `contratto a
tempo determinato / indeterminato`, `partita IVA`, `precariato`, the `concorso`
system for public jobs, the real weight of personal networks, and north/south
mobility. Written for someone who will be reading Italian job adverts and needs
to know what the categories actually mean.

**Production task (L5)** — **Written:** a cover letter or professional profile
(~200 words) for a real or plausible job. **Spoken:** two minutes on where the
learner sees themselves in five years, including at least one conditional
consequence.
*Success criterion:* at least eight future forms including three irregular
stems; at least two well-formed `se` sentences; the career narrative is in a
clear order with at least two stated reasons, and uses past narrative correctly
alongside the future.

**Recycles** — **From U5:** the passato prossimo / imperfetto contrast, in its
free-production home — a career history *is* an extended past narrative.
**From U3:** comparatives, comparing job options and conditions.
**From U3:** relative clauses (`un lavoro in cui si viaggia`).
**From U6:** `ne`, with quantities and statistics (`ne assumono pochi`), and the
quantifiers and partitives that go with them.
**From U8:** avverbi in `-mente`, describing how one works and how a process
runs.
**From U4 and U7:** `si` impersonale / passivante, for workplace norms and job
adverts (`si richiede`, `si offre`), which is the register these are written in.

### Unit 11 — Con garbo: viaggi, ospitalità, richieste

**Asking nicely** · Level **B1** · Lessons 11.1–11.5 · Checkpoint at L5

**Can-do goals**

- **(B1)** I can book accommodation or travel and sort out a change to a booking.
- **(B1)** I can make a request politely enough for a formal situation, and
  soften advice so it doesn't come out as an order.
- **(B1)** I can complain about a problem with a service and ask for it to be put
  right, in speech and in writing.
- **(B1)** I can say what I'd like, what I'd prefer, what I'd do and what someone
  else should do.

**Grammar focus**

- *Main:* **Condizionale presente** (new) — regular forms on the **same stems as
  the futuro from U10**, which is why the units are in this order. Four uses,
  taught as distinct jobs rather than as one label: **polite request**
  (`potrebbe`, `le dispiacerebbe`), **wish** (`vorrei`, `mi piacerebbe`),
  **advice** (`dovresti`, `io al tuo posto`), and **softened opinion**
  (`direi che`, `sarebbe meglio`) — the last of which sets up U12.
- *Secondary:* **`ci` pronominale** (new, light) — the closed high-frequency set
  `ci penso`, `ci credo`, `ci riesco`, `ci tengo`, `ci provo`, `ci vuole` —
  taught as chunks, because anglophone learners avoid them entirely otherwise.

**Lexis set** — Set 11: *Prenotazioni, ospitalità, reclami* (30 items), in three
subgroups — booking and accommodation (11) · politeness formulae and softeners
(11) · problems, complaints and remedies (8). Full list:
[`lexis-progression.md` §Unit 11](lexis-progression.md#unit-11--prenotazioni-ospitalità-reclami).

**Pronunciation / intonation** — **Politeness carried by contour.** A rising or
level terminal on a request versus a falling one; how the conditional plus a
falling contour still reads as a demand; and voiced vs voiceless `s`
(`casa` / `cosa`, `sbaglio`, `disdire`). This is the unit where an anglophone
learner discovers that their Italian sounds blunter than they mean it to, and
that the fix is prosodic as much as grammatical.

**Culture note** — **Host and guest.** What an Italian host expects and provides,
`agriturismo` and the family-run `struttura`, tipping norms (and how different
they are from US expectations), and — importantly for the production task — how
a complaint is actually made in Italy: firm, specific, and not apologetic, which
is not the anglophone default.

**Production task (L5)** — **Spoken:** a two-part role-play — make a booking with
three constraints, then call back to report a problem and ask for a remedy.
Solo path: recorded against scripted reception audio. **Written:** a formal
complaint email asking for a specific refund or fix.
*Success criterion:* at least six conditional forms spanning at least three of
the four taught uses; the complaint states what was booked, what happened, and
what is being asked for, in that order; the register holds — no `tu` leaks into
the formal exchange.

**Recycles** — **From U10:** futuro, whose stems the conditional reuses, and
travel plans stated in the future before being negotiated in the conditional.
**From U10:** the real conditional (`se non c'è posto, cerchiamo altro`).
**From U7:** `Lei` imperatives, now set against conditional requests so the
learner can *choose* a politeness level rather than having one.
**From U8:** indirect pronouns, everywhere in this theme (`le confermo`,
`mi farebbe un favore`). **From U9:** combined pronouns (`me lo conferma?`,
`gliel'ho già detto`) and trapassato, in its best home in the whole course —
*I had booked a double room*. **From U3:** `ci` locativo, extended here to `ci`
pronominale. **From U6:** direct pronouns, in free use.

### Unit 12 — Opinioni: notizie, dibattito, convincere

**Opinions** · Level **B1** · Lessons 12.1–12.5 · Checkpoint at L5, then
**Milestone 2**

**Can-do goals**

- **(B1)** I can give my opinion on something in the news and back it up with
  reasons and examples.
- **(B1)** I can disagree with someone politely and respond to what they
  actually said, instead of just restating my own position.
- **(B1)** I can express doubt, hope and what seems likely to me, using
  `penso che`, `spero che`, `può darsi che` with the right verb form after them.
- **(B1)** I can write a 200-word opinion piece that holds together as an
  argument rather than reading as a list of points.

**Grammar focus**

- *Main:* **Congiuntivo presente after verbs of opinion** (new — a **first look**,
  not mastery). Scope is capped deliberately: regular forms plus the
  high-frequency irregulars (`sia, abbia, faccia, vada, possa, debba, venga,
  dica, sappia`), after a closed list of triggers — `penso/credo che`,
  `mi sembra/mi pare che`, `spero che`, `dubito che`, `è possibile/probabile
  che`, `può darsi che`, `è giusto che`. Explicitly included: the
  **`penso che` vs `penso di` + infinitive** choice (same subject), because it is
  the escape hatch a B1 learner should be able to use deliberately.
  Explicitly excluded: congiuntivo after conjunctions (`benché`, `prima che`),
  congiuntivo imperfetto, and the unreal conditional — see
  `grammar-progression.md` §"Deliberately deferred".
- *Secondary:* **Connettivi argomentativi** — `tuttavia`, `d'altra parte`,
  `anzi`, `piuttosto che`, `in conclusione` — and **concession with `anche se` +
  indicative**, which is given as the B1-appropriate alternative to `benché`
  rather than leaving a gap the learner fills with English structure.

**Lexis set** — Set 12: *Media, opinione, dibattito* (30 items), in three
subgroups — opinion and stance formulae (11) · media and reliability (9) ·
argumentative connectives and evaluation (10). Full list:
[`lexis-progression.md` §Unit 12](lexis-progression.md#unit-12--media-opinione-dibattito).

**Pronunciation / intonation** — **Argument prosody**: emphatic stress on the
word carrying the claim, the pause that sets up a connective, and the
concessive contour of `sì, però…`. Plus holding and yielding the floor —
directly relevant because Italian debate tolerates overlap that anglophone
speakers read as interruption, and learners need to be able to both do it and
not be stopped by it.

**Culture note** — **How Italians argue.** The main newspapers and their
leanings, the television talk-show format, `la polemica` as a recognised genre,
and the key pragmatic point for this learner: overlapping speech in an Italian
discussion is usually engagement, not rudeness. Without this note, the
production task teaches learners to be run over politely.

**Production task (L5)** — **Spoken:** a four-minute structured opinion on a
current topic — position, two supported reasons, one acknowledged counterargument
and a response to it — followed by live disagreement from a partner. Solo path:
recorded, responding to two scripted counterarguments. **Written:** a 200-word
opinion piece.
*Success criterion:* at least five correct congiuntivo forms after taught
triggers, and at least one deliberate `penso di` + infinitive where the subject
is the same; the counterargument is genuinely answered rather than restated; at
least four argumentative connectives; the written piece has a stated position in
the first two sentences and a conclusion that follows from what precedes it.

**Recycles** — This is the whole-course recycling unit, and **L4 is reserved for
that** rather than for more congiuntivo. Named: **from U11**, condizionale for
softening (`sarebbe meglio`, `direi che`); **from U10**, futuro for prediction
and futuro epistemico for speculation about a news story; **from U10**, the real
conditional for consequences; **from U4 and U7**, `si` impersonale and passivante
for generalising and for news register (`si dice che`, `si prevede che`);
**from U9**, combined pronouns in free use; **from U3**, relative clauses and
comparatives, carrying the argument's structure; **from U6**, `ne`
(`ne parlano tutti`); **from U11**, `ci` pronominale (`non ci credo`,
`ci tengo`); **from U5**, narrative connectives, now extended into argumentative
ones.

> **Milestone 2** sits after this unit, modelled on CELI/CILS B1 task types.
> Specification in ITA-4.
